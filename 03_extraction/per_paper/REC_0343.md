## 2. Problem Statement
"This paper presents a sophisticated vision-aided flocking system for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), which is able to operate in GPS-denied unknown environments for exploring and searching missions, and also able to adopt two types of vision sensors, day and thermal cameras, to measure relative motion between UAVs in different lighting conditions without using wireless communication." [p.1]
"To realize autonomous flight in GPS-denied environments, a UAV should be able to sense the surrounding environment using on-board sensors." [p.5]

## 3. Motivation
"This paper is inspired by flocking behaviors in nature via vision exploring in obstacle-rich environments. Different from conventional systems, this paper adopts a day camera and a thermal camera to conduct flocking using visual sensing in various illumination with autonomous navigation capabilities." [p.1]
"Naturally, flocking performs a collective behavior that individuals use limited environmental information organizing in an ordered motion, so that they remain together as a group." [p.1]
"Since our flocking system is designed to operate in cluttered environments, like urban and forest, a feature-based 2-D SLAM algorithm is implemented [23]." [p.5-6]

## 5. Proposed Method
"an integrated framework of tracking-learning-detection on the basis of multifeature coded correlation filter has been developed. To achieve long-term tracking, a redetector is trained online to adaptively reinitialize target for global sensing. An advanced flocking strategy is developed to address the autonomous multi-UAVs' cooperative flight. Light detection and ranging (LiDAR)-based navigation modules are developed for autonomous localization, mapping, and obstacle avoidance." [p.1]
Reason for the tracker design: "the system adopts discriminative-based tracker for tracking due to its robust adaption of target's appearance changing. Because of the relative motion of the target to the camera, it is hard to keep a consistent view of the target, which results in target's appearance variation." [p.5]
Distance and bearing from imagery: "the target has physical size L and image with size of l. According to the triangle property, we have the relationship of d/L = f/l ... attitude angle is theta = arctan(qc/f)" [p.5]; results "transform to the waypoints for trajectory generation" [p.5].
Navigation: "we install a LiDAR for each individual UAV to sense the surrounding information for localization, map generation, and obstacle avoidance" [p.5-6]; the SLAM steps are enumerated as line filtering, ellipse extraction from tree trunks, nearest-neighbour scan matching, pose estimation from scan-to-scan transformation, pose-graph construction with a local sliding window, and "use the G2O library to optimize the pose graph." [p.6]
Planning: "We adopt an online motion planning method [18] for UAV exploration ... The online path planning employs an improved A* searching algorithm to find a collision-free path from a start point to a destination point on the local grid map." [p.7]; "We employ Reflexxes Type IV Motion Library [24] to generate 3-D trajectories for UAVs." [p.7]
Follower strategy fallback: "If camera tracking is failed, we switch to the SLAM estimation, but tracking error may drift over time." [p.8]; the disagreement threshold is "the max tolerance error is 1.41 m" [p.9].
Method-category note: HYBRID (vision-based relative sensing plus LiDAR SLAM and A* planning).
## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: "in-house-built quadrotor UAVs, which can fly about 15 min with 1.5 Kg extra payload by using an 8000 mAh LiPo battery" [p.7]
- Sensors: "an LiDAR sensor: Hokuyo UTM-30LX [see Fig. 8(a)], which can provide 0.1-30 m measurement range, 270 deg sensing angle, and 40 Hz scanning rate. The drone is able to use two types of vision sensors: an Optirs PI450 thermal camera and a PointGrey BFLY-PGE-12A2C-CS day camera." [p.7]
- Compute: "The UAV has a customized flight control computer and an AscTec Mastermind mission computer. The flight control computer has been used to perform low-level feedback control and trajectory tracking. The mission computer is employed to integrate the visual sensing system with the navigation system to realize high-level command and control" [p.7]; the onboard processor is "i7-3612QE (4 x 2.1 GHz) CPU and 4G RAM running in Ubuntu 14.04" [p.9]
- Middleware: "Software integration has been implemented by using a middleware: robot operating system (ROS) due to its good modularity and current resource handling. The aforementioned algorithms have been implemented in individual ROS nodes" [p.7]
- Rate budget: "the frequency of laser scan is limited to 20 Hz, so that all the algorithms can run in full speed (SLAM, 3-D mapping, path planning, and visual tracker are running at 20, 20, 10, and 25 Hz, respectively) without overload." [p.9]
- Imagery: "The images captured by the on-board cameras are normalized with resolution of 382 x 288. The cameras are calibrated in advance for target state estimation and also stabilized by using gimbals." [p.7]
- Mapping: "The point clouds provided by the LiDAR are employed to generate a 3-D occupancy grid map centered in the UAV, which is divided evenly into cubes to represent the environment. Each cube can be labeled in two statuses: occupied or unoccupied." [p.6]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: three scenarios - "The UAV team operates at an indoor scene in size of 35 x 35 m." (Indoor1, thermal) [p.7]; "A day camera has been employed in the second round under the same scenario (Indoor2)." [p.8]; "a challenging outdoor experiment with a day camera ... abbreviated as Outdoor conducted in a small forest with size of 50 x 50 m" [p.8]
- flight configuration: "Initially, UAVs take off 3 m apart and with 1.5 m height. Their distance intervals range from 3 to 8 m during flight." [p.7]; "The waypoints are predefined in the initial stage and only the team leader knows its task information. On the other hand, a follower tracks its leader and dynamically plans its path for the tandem flocking." [p.7]
- sequence sizes: "The total number of the frames is 3300 and the UAVs take off around the Frame #1200." (Indoor1) [p.7]; outdoor - "In total, 5000 frames have been collected with a resolution of 644 x 482. Because of time limit, we only label frames with #5x and the final labeled images are 1000." [p.8]
- flocking trials: "Three UAVs are used for visible experiment. UAV #1 is the leader, and UAV #2 and #3 are the followers, with origin points (7, 0), (3.5, 0), and (0, 0). Two UAVs are used for the thermal experiment with leader UAV #1 and follower UAV #T2, and their starting points are (7, 0) and (3.5, 0)." [p.9]
- baselines: "Selected state-of-the-art algorithms including TLD [12], CMT [25], CF2 [26], FCT [27], and DAT [28], are applied for comparison." [p.7]
- metrics: "Tracking accuracy is evaluated using center error and overlap ratio [29]." [p.7]; plus SLAM map alignment quality and the follower position traces in Fig. 11(e)-(f) [p.9]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Sensor-modality ablation: the same tracker is run on thermal (Indoor1) and daylight (Indoor2) imagery, plus an outdoor forest sequence [p.7-8].
Failure analysis per baseline is given: "DAT totally loses target at Frame #1627. This is because DAT relies on a color feature which is unstable in thermal images." [p.7]; "CF2 and FCT cannot present good performance in overlap rate after the UAV takes off. It is understandable for CF2 because it does not consider scale change. Although FCT tracks the scale of the target, it cannot give a robust scale estimation when the target is far away from the camera." [p.7]; "TLD presents false positives because of the attraction of outliers ... The failure of TLD may be caused by poor resolution of thermal images." [p.8]; "CMT recaptures the target at Frame #2622, but cannot provide a good tracking, because keypoint-based global matching may not be stable in thermal image." [p.8]
Redetection behaviour is measured by recapture frame: proposed algorithm recaptures at Frame #1977 versus TLD at Frame #2080 (and unstable from #2080 to #2600) in Indoor1 [p.8].
Indoor2: "The UAV team takes off at Frame #1090 and all the trackers keep tracking before the leader disappears at Frame #1416. The proposed algorithm can promptly recapture the target when the target reappears in image and it is stable through the experiment. In contrast, the other trackers cannot recover even the target is reappearing." [p.8]
Outdoor: "CF2 loses target at Frame #405 because it is attracted by the background with similar appearance to the target. CMT and TLD also drift after Frame #428. FCT loses the target at Frame #1167 because of the cluttered background." [p.8]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"While for the result of UAV #2 and #3 in Fig. 11(b) and (c), the SLAM has drifted. That is mainly because the other moving UAVs interfered with the feature-based scan matching." [p.9]
"If camera tracking is failed, we switch to the SLAM estimation, but tracking error may drift over time." [p.8]
"it is difficult to estimate an accurate pose of the target in spatial. However, our tracker provides a robust target sensing which can guide the follower flying to the target. An approximate estimation of waypoints is acceptable for our autonomous flocking system." [p.5]
Comment recorded by the authors on the online maps: "the maps in Fig. 11(a) to (d) are generated online, which means dynamic obstacles and outliers are also shown here. So it is reasonable that the paths pass through the obstacles in the map." [p.9]
