## 2. Problem Statement
"Cooperative localization is an essential task in multiple uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) networks, especially in environments with partial global navigation satellite system (GNSS) denial." [p.1]
"However, localization accuracy suffers under nonlinear dynamics and inaccurate knowledge of process and measurement noise statistics." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"By effectively integrating low-precision inertial navigation system (INS)-derived priors, inter-UAV relative distances, and sparse anchor-based measurements, the extended Kalman filter (EKF) can be employed for UAV localization." [p.1]
"To address these challenges, this article proposes a structurally integrated attention Kalman filter called SIA-KalmanNet for accurate and robust state estimation." [p.1]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context

## 5. Proposed Method
"By combining state-space models with attention-based neural networks in the Kalman flow, the estimator preserves the data efficiency and interpretability of traditional model-driven methods while implicitly learning second-order covariance matrices from data." [p.1]
"Specifically, two networks are devised to learn the Kalman gain: the self-attention residual masked network (SA-RMN) and the self-cross attention residual masked network (SCA-RMN)." [p.1]
"SCA-RMN introduces masked cross-attention to adaptively fuse forward state features and backward historical correction information to obtain a more precise state estimate." [p.1]
"Furthermore, a practical training strategy based on sliding window and causal masking is developed to ensure efficient training and real-time inference." [p.1]
Method-category note: HYBRID - the model-based EKF information flow is retained and only the Kalman gain is learned by attention modules; the paper states the design "mirrors the recursive information propagation of the EKF" [p.6].

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Simulated multi-UAV network only; no physical aircraft. "At the initial time step, the UAV network consists of 18 UAVs flying within a 3-D space of 500 x 500 x 500 m, including 8 anchor UAVs with GNSS capability and 10 UAVs without GNSS" [p.8]
- Sensors: "The dataset contains inertial navigation data sampled at 100 Hz, relative-distance measurements between neighboring UAVs sampled at 5 Hz, GNSS observations for anchor UAVs sampled at 5 Hz, and ground-truth position labels." [p.8]
- Compute: "All experiments are conducted on a server with an AMD Ryzen 7 PRO 4750U 1.7-GHz CPU and 16-GB memory, using the PyTorch framework." [p.8]
- Networks: SA-RMN (lightweight, one multihead self-attention module) and SCA-RMN (two self-attention modules plus one masked multihead cross-attention module) [p.6-7]
- Output mapping: "we introduce an adaptive bounded mapping for the network output K_i,t = alpha * tanh(K_hat_i,t)" [p.7]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: SIM
- environment: A 3-D 500 x 500 x 500 m flight volume; each UAV transitions between GNSS-available and GNSS-unavailable states "according to a state transition matrix P = [0.9 0.1; 0.1 0.9]" [p.8]
- baselines: "the EKF as described in Section II-C, KalmanNet [22] using Architecture #1 under setting C2 (KalmanNet A1) and Architecture #2 under setting C4 (KalmanNet A2), as well as KalmanFormer [46]" [p.9]
- dataset: "we construct a localization dataset for a UAV network based on public trajectory data [50]"; after resampling at 1 Hz, segmentation yields "27 short trajectories per UAV and a total of 5400 time steps", partitioned into "21 trajectories for training, 2 for validation, and 4 for testing" [p.8]
- noise model: "The GNSS observation noise standard deviation for anchor UAVs is 10 m, and the relative-distance observation noise standard deviation between neighboring UAVs is 3 m" [p.8]
- training: "150 epochs using the ADAM optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a batch size of 32. Sequence inputs are processed with a sliding window of length 8 and stride 4, and the attention mechanism employs four heads." [p.8]
- metrics: "the accuracy of UAV positions is evaluated using Euclidean distance error and root-mean-square error (RMSE)" [p.8]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Two architectures are compared against four baselines: "even the simpler SA-RMN demonstrates competitive performance, achieving localization accuracy comparable to the three KalmanNet configurations" [p.9].
Robustness is probed via four degradation settings S1-S4 (INS drift, NLOS, combined): "In SCA-RMN, RMSE increases by 31.0% from S1 to S2, but only by 15.7% from S1 to S3. In SA-RMN, the corresponding increases are 35.1% and 16.5%." [p.11]
"These findings confirm that drift errors dominate accuracy loss, while both models remain resilient to NLOS effects, with SCA-RMN consistently achieving lower errors across all scenarios. Overall, SCA-RMN exhibits superior robustness, whereas SA-RMN trades performance for efficiency." [p.11]
