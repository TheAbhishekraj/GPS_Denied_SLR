## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
Zhang, Xian, Zhao, and Zhang, "Autonomous Flight Control of a Nano Quadrotor Helicopter in a GPS-Denied Environment Using On-Board Vision," IEEE Transactions on Industrial Electronics, vol. 62, no. 10, pp. 6392-6403, Oct. 2015. DOI: 10.1109/TIE.2015.2420036 [p.1]. Affiliations: School of Electrical Engineering and Automation, Tianjin University, China [p.1].

## 2. Problem Statement
"a nano quadrotor helicopter which weighs about only 45 grams and has a diameter less than 0.15 m is employed for the autonomous flight control development in GPS-denied environments" [p.1]. The challenge is achieving stable hovering and trajectory tracking without external motion capture or GPS, given "very limited payload ability" [p.1].

## 3. Motivation
"few of previous works have reported the autonomous control ability of a nano UAV which weighs less than 50 grams in GPS-denied indoor and outdoor environments, and does not require any help from an external ground motion capture system" [p.2]. Optical flow sensors (e.g., PX4FLOW at ~20 g) are "still too heavy to be mounted on our 45 grams quadrotor" [p.2].

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
REAL_WORLD - indoor and outdoor flight tests conducted "without any help from GPS or ground motion capture system" [p.8]. Indoor ground truth via ceiling camera (0.005 m accuracy) [p.8]; outdoor tests under wind disturbance (~1.5 m/s) [p.10].

## 5. Proposed Method
Modified visual SLAM (VSLAM) for pose estimation fused with low-cost IMU via Kalman filter [p.1, p.2]. Nonlinear robust feedback (RISE) controller for outer-loop position control, with inner-loop attitude control [p.4]. System identification via least-squares for attitude dynamics [p.5]. VSLAM initialized automatically using barometer altitude data to resolve scale ambiguity [p.6].

## 6. System Architecture
Onboard: micro wireless monocular camera (downward-facing), low-cost IMU, micro barometer, autopilot board, 1.9 g telemetry radio [p.2-3]. Ground station: Linux laptop running C/ROS middleware executes VSLAM; position/velocity sent wirelessly to onboard autopilot which fuses with IMU [p.3]. Control: nonlinear RISE outer-loop + PID-like inner-loop [p.4].

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL_WORLD
- environment: Indoor arena (white tile floor with structured cloth for texture) and outdoor [p.8, p.10]
- ground_truth: Ceiling camera (0.005 m accuracy) indoors [p.8]; no external GT outdoors [p.10]
- duration: ~90 s hovering, ~100 s tracking (2 laps of 2.5 m x 1.1 m rectangle) [p.8-9]
- baseline: PID outer-loop controller for comparison [p.11]

## 8. Key Quantitative Results
Indoor hover: RMS error 0.0519 m (x), 0.0428 m (y), 0.039 m (z); >85% within 0.075 m radius [p.9]. Indoor tracking: RMS 0.1619 m (x), 0.1428 m (y), 0.0215 m (z); max error +/-0.25 m (x), +/-0.5 m (y) [p.9]. Outdoor hover (RISE): RMS 0.1089 m (x), 0.1322 m (y), 0.0424 m (z); >70% within 0.2 m [p.10-11]. Outdoor hover (PID): RMS 0.2824 m (x), 0.2687 m (y); only 10.83% within 0.2 m [p.11].

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
RISE vs PID comparison outdoors: RISE achieves "much better control performance than the PID position controller" [p.11]. Barometer-based automatic VSLAM initialization eliminates manual scale setup [p.6]. Structured cloth on floor provides texture for feature extraction [p.8].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"the very strict payload ability limitation" prevents mounting heavier sensors like laser range finders or PX4FLOW [p.5-6]. VSLAM "ineffectiveness when it works in a self-similar scenario, it can be easily confused by repetitive stripes or patterns" [p.6]. Low-altitude flight over uniform tiles risks tracking loss [p.6].

## 11. Future Work
"Future work will be extended to autonomous flight control in a more confined non-constructed environment" [p.11].
