## 2. Problem Statement
"In global satellite navigation system (GNSS)-denied environments, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation based on visual–inertial odometry (VIO) has been widely studied. However, existing VIO methods still suffer from some practical problems, such as image enhancement oversaturation and unreasonable weighting in backend optimization. Therefore, this article presents monocular VIO with point-line fusion and backend adaptive optimization to improve the positioning accuracy and robustness of UAV navigation systems." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"However, GNSS signals can be rendered vulnerable and unavailable in GNSS-denied environments such as indoor and underground spaces. In recent years, UAV navigation in GNSS-denied environments based on sensors such as visual, inertial measurement unit (IMU), and ultra-wideband (UWB) has been extensively studied [4], [5], [6]. However, visual sensors can exhibit certain disadvantages—such as scale errors and sensitivity to light changes—and IMU measurement errors can accumulate over time. Consequently, multisensor fusion technologies have become the mainstream for UAV navigation [7], [8], [9]." [p.1]
"However, monocular visual-inertial SLAM technology based on point features is not particularly robust and can be sensitive to illumination changes, making it difficult to achieve high-precision reliable positioning and navigation in weakly textured and complex environments." [p.1]
"Today, the main problems with VIO for UAV navigation in GNSS denial environments are low accuracy and low robustness." [p.2]

## 5. Proposed Method
Monocular VIO with point-line feature fusion and two-step adaptive-weight backend optimization (adaptive gamma correction front end, improved EDLines line extraction, IMU preintegration, sliding-window factor graph) — VIO
"In the front end, we proposed an adaptive gamma image correction algorithm for image preprocessing to avoid image oversaturation, which is more conducive to image extraction and matching. Instead of the traditional line segment detector (LSD) line feature extraction algorithm, we employed an improved EDLines algorithm to enhance the efficiency of line feature extraction... In the backend, we proposed a tightly coupled nonlinear adaptive optimization method based on a two-step approach to address the issue of unreasonable static weights." [p.1]
"we proposed a monocular VIO with point-line feature fusion and adaptive nonlinear optimization in the backend for UAV navigation." [p.13]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Structured three-level ablation with the loop-closure thread toggled [p.9-10]: (1) adaptive gamma correction only, on top of PL-VINS ("Ours (gamma)") — "Compared to PL-VINS, Ours (gamma) exhibits smaller localization errors in the majority of sequences, thereby demonstrating the validity of our approach"; (2) improved EDLines replacing LSD ("Ours (w/o reopti)") — "shows little variation in positioning error, indicating that the improved EDLines algorithm is comparable to LSD in terms of positioning accuracy. Combined with Table I, it is concluded that the advantages of the improved EDLines algorithm are mainly reflected in the faster online feature extraction"; (3) adaptive weighted reoptimisation ("Ours (w/ reopti)") — "has a significant improvement in positioning accuracy, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed optimization method with adaptive weighting". Sensitivity of the weighting itself is shown via the relationship between feature reprojection-error RMSE and the point/line weighting factors (Fig. 8) [p.9].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"Although the proposed method achieved relatively good results in different environments, the proposed method still suffers from inaccurate scaling and error accumulation. On the one hand, the scaling factor in the monocular vision inertial odometer may change in different environments, resulting in inconsistent global scaling and poor positioning accuracy. On the other hand, the trajectory may diverge due to the accumulation of errors in the inertial sensors over time. In addition, it is relatively fragile and susceptible to interference during initialization." [p.13-14]
Motivating limitation of the prior art that the paper also inherits as a design constraint: "in most cases, histogram equalization can alter image brightness and extend the pixel threshold range, resulting in image overcorrection and gray-level merging, making it difficult to control the corrective effect." [p.2]

## 11. Future Work
"Therefore, to address the above issues, we intend to introduce new sensors, such as UWB sensors, into the system. We employ an adaptive fusion approach integrating vision, inertial, and UWB to mitigate scaling inaccuracies and error accumulation associated with monocular vision inertial odometry. By utilizing this approach, we aim to enhance both the positioning accuracy and system robustness of UAV navigation, especially in complex environments with GNSS-denied." [p.14]

## 12. Contribution Type
METHOD
Five stated contributions [p.2]: (1) the adaptive gamma image-correction algorithm; (2) the improved EDLines algorithm replacing LSD with adaptive gradient thresholds, iterative short-line merging and adjacent-line elimination; (3) the tightly coupled two-step nonlinear adaptive backend optimisation with a real-time adaptive information-matrix weighting model; (4) collection of multiple complex real-world scenes (weak texture, lighting variation, large scale); (5) validation on the public EuRoC dataset and real-world scenarios against state-of-the-art methods.

## 15. Notes
- Strong evaluation design for this corpus: one public benchmark (EuRoC, seven sequences, three scenes) plus nine real-world indoor sequences across three scenario families, each tested with loop closure both off and on — 32 combinations of method x sequence x loop-closure state.
- The ablation cleanly separates the three claimed contributions, and the paper reports the honest finding that the improved EDLines mainly buys speed rather than accuracy ("comparable to LSD in terms of positioning accuracy") [p.10].
- Quantitative tables (I, II, III, IV, V, VI) are table objects and their cells are absent from the archived page text; only the aggregate improvements (32.3 % and 33.8 %) and qualitative claims are quotable. Any per-sequence ATE figures must be taken from the PDF tables directly.
- Monocular scale inconsistency and IMU error accumulation remain the acknowledged core weaknesses, and the authors' own remedy is to add UWB — a useful cross-link to the UWB cluster in this review [p.13-14].
- Collected with the Intel RealSense D455 (left camera + IMU) and validated against VICON motion capture, i.e., the ground-truth instrument is external and high grade [p.11].
- The paper does not report runtime, CPU/GPU, or onboard-computer type, so the real-time claim rests on algorithmic complexity arguments (O(n) vs O(n^2)) rather than measured latency [p.9].

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
Monocular camera (Intel RealSense D455 left-eye camera) + built-in IMU, tightly coupled; no UWB or GNSS in the solution, with VICON motion capture as external ground truth [p.11]
### 18.2 Best algorithm
Monocular VIO with adaptive-gamma preprocessing, improved EDLines point-line fusion, IMU preintegration, and two-step tightly coupled nonlinear optimisation with reprojection-error-driven adaptive weighting of point and line features in the information matrix [p.1, p.7, p.9]
### 18.3 Best accuracy (metric + value + unit + [p.N])
ATE improvement of 32.3 % on average over PL-VINS on the EuRoC dataset and 33.8 % on average over PL-VINS across three real-world scenario families; absolute ATE values are TABLE_ONLY (Tables III-VI) [p.1, p.13]
### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
VINS-MONO (point-feature VIO) and PL-VINS (point-line VIO with LSD and static weights); the proposed method has the lowest localisation error in the sequences tested, both with and without loop closure, including dim/bright/light-to-dark lighting, weak texture, and the 30 x 12 m large-scale complex scene [p.10-13]
### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy
Indoor environments with good point-line structure but challenging illumination or texture: EuRoC factory and room scenes, a 10 x 10 m room with four lighting regimes, wall-facing weak-texture passes, and the 30 x 12 m complex scene; both handheld and drone-mounted data collection; closed-loop detection on or off [p.9-12]
### 18.6 Trade-offs
Adaptive weighting and the second nonlinear reoptimisation add computation in exchange for accuracy, while the improved EDLines buys speed at equal accuracy; the deep preprocessing option (neural gamma correction) was rejected as too slow for real-time SLAM ("it was difficult to apply it to a visual-inertial SLAM system in real time") [p.2, p.10]
### 18.7 Generalization limits
Indoor only [p.1, p.9-12]; monocular scale may change between environments, breaking global scaling [p.13]; IMU error accumulation can cause divergence over long trajectories [p.13]; sensitive to initialisation conditions [p.13-14]
