## 2. Problem Statement
"However, reliable indoor UAV operation fundamentally depends on robust localization and navigation capabilities. Unlike outdoor environments, indoor scenarios typically suffer from unavailable or severely degraded Global Positioning System (GPS) signals" [p.1].

## 3. Motivation
"Existing indoor localization approaches mainly focus on recovering continuous metric coordinates and performing navigation on geometric maps[5]. Chen et al. [6] transformed BIM semantic and geometric information into a graph structure for UAV navigation, improving the interpretability of indoor path planning. However, the approach primarily focused on navigation planning and assumed that the UAV location was already known, failing to address localization ambiguity caused by repetitive corridors and visually similar rooms in GPS-denied indoor environments." [p.1].

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
INDOOR
"To support UAV localization and navigation in GPS-denied indoor environments, these building elements are abstracted into a semantic-topological graph composed of node set V and navigable edge set E" [p.2].

## 5. Proposed Method
Method name: BIM-assisted UAV topological localization and navigation framework
Category: HYBRID
"The proposed framework transforms BIM into a lightweight semantic-topological graph and converts UAV image observations into candidate BIM node sets using spatial semantic recognition and OCR-based textual cues. A topology-aware recursive localization mechanism is then introduced, integrating BIM adjacency constraints and historical states to continuously infer the most probable topological node. Furthermore, semantic-cost A* path planning is employed to verify whether the estimated topological locations can effectively support indoor UAV navigation tasks." [p.1].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"The current experiment was conducted on a relatively small topology graph consisting of 16 nodes. Although the results demonstrate the feasibility and robustness of the proposed recursive topological inference mechanism, real-world smart-building applications may involve hundreds of interconnected nodes with more complex semantic ambiguities." [p.4].

## 11. Future Work
"Future work will compare it with representative visual localization methods, including feature-based approaches, visual SLAM, and deep-learning-based place recognition. The evaluation will consider localization accuracy, robustness, computational efficiency, and navigation consistency in GPS-denied indoor environments." [p.4].
