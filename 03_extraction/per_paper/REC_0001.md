## 2. Problem Statement
Indoor UAV localization is hard because GPS is unavailable and interiors are visually repetitive. Quote: "Unlike outdoor environments, indoor scenarios typically suffer from unavailable or severely degraded Global Positioning System (GPS) signals[3]. In addition, repetitive corridors, visually similar rooms, weak textures, dynamic occlusions, and illumination variations further increase the difficulty of UAV localization in indoor environments[4]." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
Prior indoor approaches recover metric coordinates on geometric maps and do not resolve node-level ambiguity. Quote: "However, the approach primarily focused on navigation planning and assumed that the UAV location was already known, failing to address localization ambiguity caused by repetitive corridors and visually similar rooms in GPS-denied indoor environments." [p.1-2]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
INDOOR. Quote: "indoor scenarios typically suffer from unavailable or severely degraded Global Positioning System (GPS) signals[3]." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
BIM-assisted topological localization: BIM is converted to a semantic-topological graph (16 nodes / 15 edges); UAV images become candidate BIM nodes via spatial semantic recognition + OCR room-sign matching; a topology-aware recursive (Bayesian) mechanism infers the most probable node; semantic-cost A* validates navigability. method_category: **OTHER** (semantic topological localization; not SLAM/VIO — no metric state estimation is performed). Quote: "The proposed framework transforms BIM into a lightweight semantic-topological graph and converts UAV image observations into candidate BIM node sets using spatial semantic recognition and OCR-based textual cues." [p.2]

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
18.1 Best sensor combination: monocular camera + BIM semantic-topological graph (OCR room-sign matching). Quote: "converts UAV image observations into candidate BIM node sets using spatial semantic recognition and OCR-based textual cues" [p.2]
18.2 Best algorithm: semantic classification (PyTorch) + topology-aware recursive Bayesian localization + semantic-cost A*. Quote: "A topology-aware recursive localization mechanism is then introduced by integrating BIM adjacency constraints and historical states to infer the most probable indoor location." [p.1]
18.3 Best accuracy: valid-frame node accuracy **100%** (and 95.95% localization coverage over 74 frames). Quote: "Results demonstrate that the proposed framework achieves 95.95% localization coverage and 100% valid-frame node accuracy, showing strong robustness and interpretability for indoor UAV localization and navigation." [p.1]
18.4 Baseline comparison: NO_BASELINE — no comparative method is run; comparison is deferred to future work [p.4].
18.5 Conditions: indoor teaching building; 3 image-sequence paths (corridor-to-room, room-to-room, stair-to-stair), 74 frames; 16-node/15-edge BIM graph; offline workstation evaluation. [p.3-4]
18.6 Trade-offs: Quote: "Since the proposed method performs localization in a lightweight semantic-topological state space rather than dense metric maps, the computational complexity is expected to remain significantly lower than traditional dense SLAM-based localization approaches." [p.3]
18.7 Generalization limits: validated only on a small 16-node graph; multi-floor and denser semantic ambiguity untested. Quote: "real-world smart-building applications may involve hundreds of interconnected nodes with more complex semantic ambiguities." [p.4]
