## 2. Problem Statement
"In this paper the problem of autonomous navigation of aerial robots through obscurants is considered. As visible spectrum cameras and most LiDAR technologies provide degraded data in such conditions, the problem of localization is approached through the fusion of thermal camera data with inertial sensor cues." [p.1]
"However, despite this rapid progress, GPS-denied Degraded Visual Environments (DVEs) represent a hard limit for autonomous systems as the sensor information inside them can be severely degraded." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Aerial robots have been widely used in a variety of applications, including those of infrastructure inspection [1-6], precision agriculture [7-9], search and rescue [10-13] and more." [p.1]
"Although in some types of DVEs, e.g., darkness, the problem can be resolved using onboard illumination or [14] active sensors such as LiDARs, more complex cases including fog- and smoke-filled environments remain particularly hard. In such environments, traditional cameras or LiDARs used for Simultaneous Localization And Mapping (SLAM) provide ill-conditioned information therefore not allowing to reliably estimate the pose of the robot and build the map of its surroundings." [p.1]
"This solution successfully enabled autonomous navigation through obscurants, an ability critical for many important applications such as search and rescue in underground mines that can quickly become filled with smoke in emergency conditions." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
"a thermal-inertial sensor fusion approach is proposed that relies on the data extracted from a FLIR Tau 2 Long-Wave IR (LWIR) which are then software time-synchronized with the data from an IMU." [p.1-2]
"The camera intrinsics, as well as the camera-to-IMU extrinsics are calibrated and an Extended Kalman Filter based camera-inertial odometry using direct photometric feedback is deployed." [p.2]
"A 3D model view of the developed robotic solution is depicted in Figure 2. The aerial robot corresponds to a widely adopted quadrotor platform (DJI Matrice 100) which has been extended with a multi-modal perception solution and extensive onboard computational processing capabilities." [p.3]
Calibration is treated as a prerequisite: "Reliable operation of the proposed thermal-inertial localization system relies on appropriate calibration of camera intrinsics, as well as an accurate estimate of the extrinsic transformation" between camera and IMU [p.3].
The estimator is ROVIO-style direct photometric VIO: the filter state propagation and update equations are tabulated as "ROVIO STATE PROPAGATION & FILTER UPDATE STEPS. State Propagation Step - Equations (3) ... Filter Update Step - Equations (4) ... standard EKF update step is directly performed to derive the new estimate of the robot belief for its state and the tracked features." [p.4, Table I].
"the raw data of this work are released online as an open dataset to support further community efforts" [p.5].
Method-category note: VIO (thermal camera and IMU fused in an EKF with direct photometric measurement model).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: "a widely adopted quadrotor platform (DJI Matrice 100)" [p.3]; "The overall system weighs approximately 2.9kg." [p.3]
- Sensors: "the multi-modal perception solution consists of a FLIR Tau2 LWIR camera, a stereo visible light camera pair based on Point Grey Chameleon3 cameras, and a VectorNav VN-100 IMU." [p.3]
- Visible-camera role: "the data from these cameras are not employed for the purposes of localization in the experiments presented in this work, but are only used for sparse stereo depth estimation, as it was shown that their data are inadequate to perform reliable pose estimation." [p.3]
- Compute: "a powerful Intel NUC7i7BNH, which is responsible to run the proposed thermal-inertial odometry pipeline alongside position control." [p.3]
- Control: "a Linear Model Predictive Control strategy has been deployed ... This control law derives reference roll, pitch, yaw and thrust values which are then fed through the ROS middleware to the low-level controller of the DJI Matrice 100 quadrotor platform." [p.3]
- Data conditioning: "as the FLIR Tau2 raw camera data are 14 bit, rescaling to an 8-bit intensity image was conducted based on a sliding window algorithm which adapts to the maxima of the derived thermal reading values of the local environment." [p.4]
## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: "a test in a smoke-filled machine shop was conducted. The experimental area was filled with very dense smoke using a Hurricane 1800 Flex glycerin-based fog-machine. The smoke density level was such that it could be verified from the intense white-out effect in the normal camera images that light scattering was very strong." [p.4]
- task: "the robot was tasked to perform an automated trajectory, demonstrate its ability for GPS-denied autonomous flight through obscurants such as fog and smoke, and collect data that can be relevant to search and rescue, surveillance and other intelligence-related operations." [p.4]
- deliberate degradation check: "we first verified that LiDAR data coming from a state-of-the-art system (Velodyne PUCK Lite) become increasingly unreliable (sparsified and noisy point clouds), which is also the case for a visible spectrum camera (white-out of the captured frames)." [p.1]
- baselines: comparative stereo visual-inertial localization inside the same estimation framework: "the failed (drifted) estimates provided by stereo visual-inertial localization using the same estimation framework" [p.5]
- dataset: "it is noted that the raw data of this work are released online as an open dataset to support further community efforts http://www.autonomousrobotslab.com/thermal-inertial-dataset.html" [p.5]
- metrics: trajectory estimation quality against the visible/stereo alternative (figure-based), plus qualitative human-detection outcomes [p.4-5]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Modality comparison is the core ablation: LiDAR (Velodyne PUCK Lite), visible-spectrum camera and thermal LWIR camera are all assessed in the same smoke-filled conditions, with only the thermal channel remaining informative [p.1, p.4-5].
Thermal feature tracking is probed and found weaker than visible-light feature tracking: "This methodology has demonstrated high-performance results at high-speed motions using a normal camera, however the performance is relatively weaker in the thermal camera primarily due to the fact that significant parts of the environment are in thermal equilibrium and therefore cannot provide such strong feature information." [p.3-4]
Additional thermal-domain processing is demonstrated qualitatively: optical flow using the Lucas-Kanade method and saliency detection based on spectral residuals, extended to the thermal domain [p.4-5].
