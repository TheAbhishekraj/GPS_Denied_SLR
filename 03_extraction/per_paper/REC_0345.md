## 2. Problem Statement
"This paper presents an algorithm for localizing an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) in GPS denied environments." [p.2]
"The challenge addressed in this paper is how to provide position when information from the GPS receiver is momentarily lost." [p.2]

## 3. Motivation
"incorporating robustness to GPS failure is widely accepted as important, particularly in law enforcement and defence applications." [p.2]
"Integration of information from an on-board camera and a body mounted inertial measurement system is one of the most promising approaches to navigate in environments without GPS information." [p.2]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
MIXED
The method targets "GPS denied environments" [p.2], is validated in Gazebo simulation and field trials [p.6-7], and assumes a pre-built geo-referenced terrain map with locally planar ground [p.2].

## 5. Proposed Method
"Localization is performed with respect to a pre-built map of the environment represented using the distance function of a binary mosaic, avoiding the need for extraction and explicit matching of visual features." [p.2]
"Edges extracted from images acquired by an on-board camera are projected to the map to compute an error metric that indicates the misalignment between the predicted and true pose of the UAV." [p.2]
"A constrained extended Kalman filter (EKF) framework is used to generate an estimate of the full 6-DOF location of the UAV by enforcing the condition that the distance function values are zero when there is no misalignment." [p.2]

## 6. System Architecture
"The UAV platform of choice for team VICTOR is a custom-built hexa-rotor helicopter" equipped with "a Pixhawk flight controller unit and an on-board ARM computer", in-built IMU, on-board GPS with compass, RTK GPS module, laser range finder, PX4FLOW optical sensor, a 5 MP gimbal perspective camera and a downward fish-eye camera [p.3].
High-level software runs on "an on-board NVIDIA Jetson TX1 computer" using ROS [p.3].

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: BOTH
- environment: Gazebo simulation and outdoor field trials over a pre-built arena/terrain map [p.6-7]
- baselines: onboard odometry/Pixhawk motion estimate is plotted/used; no external algorithm baseline is quantified [p.6]
- dataset: custom simulation and field data [p.6-7]
- metrics: pose error against known simulation ground truth and RTK-GPS field reference [p.6-7]
Ground truth: simulation "Ground-truth is known" [p.6]; field "RTK GPS unit ... is used only for ground-truth" at 1 Hz [p.6-7].

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
The EKF can use "a motion model based on the velocities and angular velocities of the UAV or with the UAV motion modelled as a random walk" [p.2]. Spurious edge measurements are rejected by "an innovation gate (with a 2 sigma bound)" [p.6]. No formal ablation table is printed in the extracted text.

## 10. Stated Limitations
"One major limitation of the work presented is the assumption that the map of the environment that the UAV traverses is planar." [p.7]
Field ground truth is limited: RTK-GPS updates are "only available at a 1Hz frequency" and "it is not reliable and depends on environment conditions" [p.7].

## 11. Future Work
"Our future work will focus on incorporating information from other sensors mounted on the UAV for example the optical flow sensor." [p.7]
"We also aim to explore the utility of tightly coupling the information from an IMU and other sensors in a single EKF framework." [p.7]
Relaxing the planar-map assumption using a full 3-D distance map is also named [p.7].
