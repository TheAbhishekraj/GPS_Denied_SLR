## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
C. Cheng, X. Li, L. Li, and L. Xie, "Distance-based Autonomous Docking for UAV-MP in GPS-denied Environment," in Proc. 34th Chinese Control and Decision Conference (CCDC), 2022, doi: 10.1109/CCDC55256.2022.10034409.

## 2. Problem Statement
"In this paper, we study the docking problem between a UAV and a mobile platform (MP) by estimating the relative position through distance information and its own displacement, and design the approaching trajectory for eliminating the need of any global positioning systems or other infrastructures. The main challenges of this problem are to estimate the relative position between the UAV and the MP and design controllers for the navigation trajectory control." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"How to navigate a UAV to land on a MP is always a popular research topic." [p.1]
"Depending on the positioning methods, there are two main types of autonomous docking: absolute positioning (GPS, Beidou, MCS) and self-search navigation (V-SLAM) [5-8]. The former is based on the signal strength of the base setup which determines its accuracy in absolute coordinate systems and will lead to deviations or failures in navigation when the signal is limited. On the other hand, the latter requires a large amount of search until the MP appears within the FOV of the UAV, often requiring significant computing power and time." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
Distance-based relative positioning with saturated negative-feedback approach navigation — HYBRID
"our work proposes a distance measurement-based navigation and positioning algorithm for UAV and MP in GPS-denied environment. The UAV and MP are ranged via a UWB node, and the UAV is displaced by its own visual odometer and IMU." [p.2]
"we propose an integrated navigation control scheme based on ranging sensor and odometer, which is divided into three phases: hovering, approaching and landing." [p.2]
Method notes: hybrid because two modalities are combined — UWB two-way time-of-flight ranging (inter-agent distance) fused geometrically with the UAV's own visual-inertial displacement.

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Multi-rotor UAV plus a mobile platform (MP); the MP dynamics model is reused from omnidirectional mobile robots ("MY wheel, MY wheel-II, Swedish wheel and Omni-wheel") [p.2]
- Sensors: "a UWB node is installed respectively on UAV and MP and distance measurement is performed by TWTOF (Two-way time of fight)." [p.2]; UAV displacement "measured by the onboard camera and IMU of the UAV" [p.2]
- Compute: NOT_REPORTED (the computed control command u(k) is "sent to the onboard flight controller") [p.3]
- Communication: UWB ranging link between the UAV node and the MP node; no other infrastructure [p.2]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
NOT_REPORTED. No ablation study. The only variation tested is a second scenario with a different MP starting coordinate, to check feasibility "in all directions" [p.5]. Analytical sensitivity is limited to the closed-form navigation-time bound kmax (Eq. 11), which shortens as αUmax grows relative to √2·vmax [p.4].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"It is clear that the relative positioning obtained is vaguely imprecise. However, a vague orientation is still much more efficient than a blind search." [p.3] — relative localization is admitted to be coarse, and the method relies on the UAV flying closer until the MP enters the camera FOV.
"It is important to note that the applicable range of the autonomous docking method in this paper depends on the range of the selected UWB sensors." [p.2] — the operating envelope is bounded by UWB ranging range.
"The solution is verified by simulation." [p.2] and "The feasibility of the scheme is verified by numerical examples." [p.5] — no physical flight validation in this paper.

## 11. Future Work

## 15. Notes
- Simulation-only control-theoretic contribution: no RMSE/CEP reported; the success criterion is whether the MP enters the UAV FOV.
- Constant-altitude assumption: "Assuming that the UAV can maintain its altitude" [p.2].
- "Assumption 1. It is assumed that during the circular motion of the UAV with radius R, the effect on the measured relative position is minimal, i.e., the MP's position changes are so tiny as to be nearly negligible." [p.3] — the estimator assumes a near-static MP during the circular ranging manoeuvre.
- Relative position is recovered from three range circles with a centre-of-mass fix (Eq. 6), which the paper itself calls "vaguely imprecise" [p.3].
- Algorithm 1 (UAV-MP docking algorithm) takes inputs α, R, d̄ and outputs u(k+1), q̂0 [p.4].
- Terminal landing (image-recognition of the landing point) is out of scope by design [p.3-4].

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
UWB ranging node on the UAV + UWB ranging node on the MP, combined with the UAV's onboard camera and IMU for displacement measurement [p.2]
### 18.2 Best algorithm
Circular-motion distance-based relative localization (three-circle geometric intersection with centre-of-mass fix, Eq. 5-6) feeding a saturated negative-feedback approach controller with clockwise/counter-clockwise search (Eq. 7-10) [p.3-4]
### 18.3 Best accuracy (metric + value + unit + [p.N])
NOT_REPORTED — no localization error metric is given. The only stated performance criterion is the docking condition ‖pU(k) − pM(k)‖ ≤ d̄, reported as met in both simulated scenarios (Figures 6-11) [p.2-5]
### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
NO_BASELINE — absolute-positioning and V-SLAM docking approaches are reviewed [p.1] but none is simulated for comparison; the paper reports only that all simulations "support the obtained theoretical results" [p.5]
### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy
Noise-injected simulation: MP moving at <2 m/s with random direction/size changing every 1 s; UAV at maximum 5 m/s and 4 m altitude; α = 0.95; UWB noise ±0.1 m; odometer noise ±0.01 m; both MP start positions tested [p.4-5]
### 18.6 Trade-offs
Minimal deployment cost and infrastructure independence are traded against coarse relative localization ("vaguely imprecise") and a hard dependence on UWB ranging range; the MP needs no odometer and no GPS [p.2-3]
### 18.7 Generalization limits
Simulation only [p.2, p.5]; constant-altitude flight assumed [p.2]; MP treated as a near-static mass point during the circular positioning manoeuvre [p.3]; explicit dependence on "the range of the selected UWB sensors" [p.2]; terminal landing handled by other work [p.4]
