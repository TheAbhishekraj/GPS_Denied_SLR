## 2. Problem Statement
"This paper presents the development of a framework enabling a drone to autonomously navigate and explore in GPS denied environments impacted by visual obscurants." [p.1]
"However, some difficult environments are characterized by partial to no GPS signal, limiting the drone's ability to localise itself. Moreover, visual obscurants affect the UAV's performance and behaviour by restricting its visibility, adding complexity to some subsystems such as target detection." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"The use of autonomous Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) continues to increase for a wide range of applications, such as wildlife monitoring, planetary exploration, and emergency Search and Rescue (SAR). Autonomous UAVs typically rely on Global Positioning System (GPS) usage and most operations require correct flight conditions with good visibility (no rain, fog nor smoke)." [p.1]
"The study illustrates this by formulating the navigation and target detection problem as an autonomous Sequential Decision Problem (SDP) with uncertainty caused by degraded visibility and lack of GPS." [p.1]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
INDOOR
"The navigation task was created following the model of a classic SAR operation in a cluttered indoor environment with visual obscurants." [p.1]
"The simulated environment was created following the model of a cluttered and GPS-denied environment." [p.6]

## 5. Proposed Method
"The SDP is modelled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) and tested using the Adaptive Belief Tree (ABT) algorithm." [p.1]
"The framework includes target detection under uncertainty from a vision-based camera." [p.1]
"The TAPIR toolkit [23] was used to model the POMDP formulation. In this toolkit, the online POMDP solver used is the ABT solver, and a ROS interface is included." [p.5-6]
"The discount factor was selected to be gamma = 0.93. This value was chosen after multiple tests and experiments." [p.6]
Detection module: "The package used to detect Aruco markers was aruco ros, a package developed by the Ava group of the University of Cordoba in Spain [24]." [p.5]
Method-category note: VISION_OBJECT (target detection under uncertainty drives the decision process).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Simulated quad-rotor UAV flown through PX4 software-in-the-loop; no physical aircraft is flown in the reported tests. "The autopilot is also assumed to include the take-off, landing and return home functions." [p.4]
- Sensors: "The target detection subsystem and observations are operated using a vision-based camera facing downward." [p.4]
- Compute: "The framework integrates Gazebo, Robot Operating System (ROS), Software In The Loop (SITL) and PX4 firmware to recreate a SAR scenario in simulation." [p.1]
- Decision stack: TAPIR POMDP toolkit with a ROS interface to PX4 flight control software [p.6]
- State/action model: state S = (S_a, S_t) with agent position and yaw and target position; "Seven actions were selected for the UAV to interact with its environment"; "the actions were restricted to pure translation, and no rotations were used." [p.4]
- Pose uncertainty: "The observation of the agent's state was modelled ... From the experiments, the maximum standard deviation found was 0.08 meters, and the standard deviation used to model the pose estimation uncertainty was round up to 0.1 meters." [p.5]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: SIM (pose uncertainty is, however, "modelled from real-life flight experiments" [p.1])
- environment: "Two maps of different sizes were created, with a flying area of 5x5 and 10x10 meters respectively, and a maximum flight altitude of 2.5 meters. Each map is also composed of several obstacles in the form of columns. Using Gazebo libraries, particles were also generated to simulate the presence of smoke. In this research, the smaller testing environment comprised three obstacles, while the larger comprised nine obstacles." [p.6]
- target: "The selected target for these experiments is an Aruco marker, which is located at a fixed position of (2.0, 1.0, 0.2) for the 5x5m map, and (3.5, 3.5, 0.2) for the 10x10m map." [p.6]
- baselines: NO_BASELINE (the comparison is between visibility levels and altitude ranges of the same framework) [p.7-8]
- repetitions: "Each series of test were run 30 times, with a mean time step's duration of 2.0 seconds." [p.7]
- flight-time caps: "the number of actions for the agent was limited to a 100, restricting the maximum flight time at around 3.3 minutes" (5x5 m) and "limited to 300 to give more time for the agent to explore the larger environment" (10x10 m) [p.7-8]
- dataset: NOT_REPORTED
- metrics: success rate, crash rate, timeout rate and number of steps to find the target [p.7-8]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Visibility level (normal vs smoke) and commanded altitude range (high ~2.1-2.5 m vs low ~1.3-1.7 m) are the two controlled factors [p.7].
"The framework performed perfectly without the presence of smoke. In these conditions, the UAV was able to stay in the flying area, avoid collisions with obstacles, and detect the Aruco marker at each run." [p.7]
"For the second case study, the success rate drastically decreased as the UAV was above the cloud of smoke, causing the target to be heavily occluded." [p.7]
"As shown by Table 3, the success rate increased from 23.33% in the second case study to 56.6% in the third case study." [p.7]
"the mean time step's duration increased to 2.35 seconds due to the larger range of possible actions" on the 10x10 m map [p.8]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"The scalability of the framework was demonstrated, but limitations were shown in presence of visual obscurants, with a target detection rate of 56.6% and 50% for the small and large map respectively. The detection rate was already improved by lowering the range of altitude of the UAV, but is still far from the efficiency shown in normal visibility." [p.8]
"the range of altitude included in the reward function was manually modified between test cases. The agent was able to modify its altitude within the selected range, but the UAV was not able to change this range by itself, limiting the autonomous abilities of the drone." [p.8]
"In both series of tests, the flight time was limited because of an issue that caused the UAV to get stuck in an area, with the same series of actions repeating itself." [p.8]
"the target detection module, limited by the sole use of a vision-based sensor, was too impacted by degraded visibility." [p.8]

## 11. Future Work
"Future work includes modelling the agent's ability to detect changes in visibility and categorizing 'low visibility' and normal visibility conditions. ... Future work will also include automating the change of range of altitude, and the implementation of the LIDAR/Inertial odometry. Finally, a thermal camera would be added onboard the UAV, with sole purpose to detect targets in visually-degraded environments." [p.8]
