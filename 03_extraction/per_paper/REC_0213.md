## 2. Problem Statement
"We propose a framework for a team of UAVs to cooperatively explore and find a target in complex GPS-denied environments with obstacles. The team of UAVs autonomously navigates, explores, detects, and finds the target in a cluttered environment with a known map." [p.1]
"In contrast, using a team of UAVs to find a target in cluttered and GPS-denied scenarios with efficiency and cooperation is still a challenging problem." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"The use of multi-rotor Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) for Search and Rescue (SAR) and Remote Sensing is rapidly increasing. Multi-rotor UAVs, however, have limited endurance. The range of UAV applications can be widened if teams of multiple UAVs are used." [p.1]
"Examples of such environments include indoor scenarios, urban or natural canyons, caves, and tunnels, where the GPS signal is limited or blocked." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
"The framework is based on a probabilistic Decentralised Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (Dec-POMDP) which accounts for the uncertainties in sensing and the environment. The team can cooperate efficiently, with each UAV sharing only limited processed observations and their locations during the mission." [p.1]
"The framework is able to consider the uncertainties present in the formulation, by using the proposed Ob-Dec-POMDP as the underlying model, in order to control computational cost without compromising the benefits stemming from a multi-agent system." [p.8]
"In order to navigate and localise UAVs in a GPS-denied environment, the proposed system uses a framework which combines a close range detector and a baseline map." [p.8]
Solver: TAPIR online POMDP solver, C++ implementation [p.5].

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Multi-rotor UAVs simulated in software-in-the-loop. "PX4, an open source software developed by the Dronecode Project [33], is widely used as the flight control unit (FCU) in several UAV applications" [p.5]
- Sensors: IMU odometry from PX4; "There is a multi-ranger sensor that uses 5 laser sensors to measure the distance in the directions front, back, left, right, and up of the UAV." [p.5]; a downward-facing monocular camera observes the target [p.5-6]
- Compute: ROS/Gazebo simulation stack; communication between UAVs is modelled over WiFi [p.6]
- Positioning subsystem: "One of the main parts of the positioning subsystem is the Octomap server which uses the 3D occupancy voxels to represent the obstacles and boundaries within the flying area." [p.5]
- Localisation source: "The IMU odometry from the PX4 provides the basic estimate position of the UAV, the Octomap server then sends position estimate data to the motion planner module and the observation processing module in the occupancy map." [p.5]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: SIM
- environment: "The simulation environment consists of ROS, Gazebo, Octomap, Rviz as visualisation tool, and PX4 at software in the loop (SITL) mode as UAV" [p.6]; two scenarios with target locations P1/P2 (scenario one) and P3/P4 (scenario two) [p.6]
- fail criterion: "If the UAVs system can not find the target within a maximum flight time of tmax = 600 seconds, the system reports a failure to find the target." [p.6]
- baselines: three cooperation strategies - "Independent: this strategy sets all three UAVs as separate independent agents"; "Divided: in this strategy, the entire search area was divided into three equal zones"; "Informed: the three UAVs work jointly, and exchange messages as described in the Section IV." [p.7]
- runs: "For each multi-UAV system, 40 simulations were performed, with same initial positions of UAVs and two separate target positions." [p.7]
- dataset: NOT_REPORTED (simulated worlds only)
- metrics: time to find the target (search time) and target detection rate / success rate [p.7-8]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Two reward functions are contrasted: "Option A sets equal cost for all the search actions, and Option B is designed for a more aggressive exploration e.g. less hover." [p.6]
"the motion planner prefers the less costly action (Forward) rather than the more costly action (Hover). This type of decision helped the UAVs to explore more area and reduce the search time." [p.8]
"The Independent strategy performed poorly and its performance widely fluctuated compared to the other two categories (Divided and Informed)." [p.8]
"the Informed setting kept a consistent high successful rate in all simulations, while the rate of the Divided generally dropped from 97.5% of P1 to 72.5% of P4." [p.8]
Scalability caveat: "the performance gains for a large group are likely to be marginal, while collision risk is likely to increase" [p.7]
