## 2. Problem Statement
"Autonomous navigation of unmanned vehicles in GPS-denied environments is a challenging problem, especially for small ground vehicles and micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) which are characterized by their small payload, short battery lifetime and limited processing resources." [p.1]
"In this paper, we propose a hybrid visual-inertial odometry solution that minimizes the computation load by dividing the mission into two interchangeable stages." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Stereo vision positioning has been introduced as a scale-free positioning technique, but it is computationally expensive. Monocular vision systems aided by inertial measurement unit (IMU) are more computationally efficient but it suffers from IMU random biases and scale errors." [p.1]
"However, visual processing is still a computational bottleneck that affects the practical application of visual inertial tracking in small-size battery-powered unmanned vehicles." [p.2]
"The smaller the vehicle, the less the processing power available and the shorter the baseline distance between cameras which cause degeneration to a monocular mode whenever the visual features are at a distance that is significantly larger than the baseline between stereo cameras. Therefore, we believe having a system that can dynamically switch between a monocular and stereo mode could give a great benefit to autonomous vehicles navigation." [p.2]

## 5. Proposed Method
"Firstly, a stereo vision stage in which a loosely coupled integration between stereo cameras and IMU is performed. In this stage, an extended Kalman filter (EKF) is used to automatically and dynamically estimate IMU biases. Once the IMU is calibrated, a monocular stage is activated where the system is downgraded into single camera getting the motion scale from the calibrated IMU." [p.1]
"Stereo mode is activated in the beginning until a reliable accurate estimate of the IMU errors is obtained and stable EKF convergence is achieved. Then, the system switches to IMU-aided monocular mode benefitting from its lower memory and computation requirements." [p.3]
"Motion scale generated by the IMU is tested every few seconds and when the error in the motion scale goes beyond a certain threshold the system switches back to the stereo mode and feed its estimation to the EKF to re-estimate IMU biases." [p.3]
Stereo stage algorithm: "we used the SOFT odometry method [5], which ranks as one of the highest accuracy visual odometry solution on KITTI vision benchmark suite [16]. The name SOFT stands for Stereo Odometry algorithm relying on Feature Tracking [17]." [p.3]
Monocular stage: "We applied ORB feature detector [18] and then we tracked the features using Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi [3] sparse optical flow technique. ... Once we have point correspondences we used RANSAC 5-points [19] ... and then compute the essential matrix using the Epipolar geometry constraints." [p.2]
"Since the scale in monocular vision is unobservable, we get the motion scale from the IMU prediction model." [p.2]
Method-category note: VIO (visual odometry fused with IMU inside an EKF).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Handheld/vehicle-mounted sensor rig in the reported tests; no UAV airframe is flown. The camera is mounted on the "platform" shown in Fig. 4 [p.4]
- Sensors: ZED Mini stereo camera with integrated IMU [p.1, p.4]; Intel RealSense T265 tracking camera used as a comparison system [p.1, p.4]
- Compute: A laptop is used as the processing host: "The laptop was used to synchronize and time-tag all incoming data (IMU and the cameras)." [p.4]
- EKF rate: "The implemented EKF uses a full 3D IMU dynamic model [14] to generate its predicted trajectory estimation of the position, velocity and orientation of the moving platform at a rate of 100Hz. At a lower rate (15 Hz) a stereo vison system that processes images sequence from couple of cameras is developed." [p.3]
- Filter structure: "a full 3D IMU positioning solution within an EKF module that considers the vehicle's non-holonomic constrains" [p.3]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
The stereo-to-monocular switch is the core ablation: "After convergence, the system automatically switched to the monocular mode visual odometry solution and continue for 40 seconds resulted in the blue trajectory illustrated in Figure 8. The red trajectory in Figure 8 is the full SOFT stereo solution which has 20 cm end-of-loop position error." [p.4]
"During the experiment, 5 accuracy tests have been performed on the IMU generated scale to verify its consistency with that estimated scale from stereo vision. No discrepancy has been detected as the error was smaller than the selected threshold, and the overall error at the end of the tested loop was 0.5 meters." [p.4]
"the estimated gyroscope Z bias behavior shows how feeding the EKF a bad initial guess can lead to an interval of instability before convergence." [p.4]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"Although the calibrated IMU-aided monocular vision solution is slightly less accurate than the SOFT stereo vision solution, given the huge saving in computation by switching to monocular solution instead of stereo solution, the advantage of the proposed hybrid scheme is clear." [p.4]
"Although stereo vision does not suffer from scale ambiguity, the stereo vision processing is more expensive as it processes two input video streams which consume larger memory and power leading to shorter battery lifetime and limited operating range." [p.2]
