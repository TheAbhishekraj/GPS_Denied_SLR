## 2. Problem Statement
"The autonomous swarms of drones have literally shaken the fences of the sphere of search and rescue operations, precision farming, and inspection of infrastructures. Well, there is research that corroborates that [1], [2]. The explanation for why such swarms perform better than the single drones is simply due to the multi-agent interactions that enable these swarms are capable of perform complex tasks. However, this is highly dependent on good real-time localization. The Global Positioning System, or GPS, normally does that. The classical localization schemes fail in GPS-denied environments like the city canyons, indoor environments, dense woodland and intentional jamming, undermining swarm coordination and mission achievement [3], [4]. Additional systems such as inertial navigation systems (INS), ultra-wideband (UWB) ranging and visual odometry have also been thought of, but these systems do not have sensor drift, scalability, and are computationally intensive, as well as sensitive to environmental problems [5], [7]." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Despite the relative positioning algorithm improvements [3], collective navigation algorithm improvements [4], and even flocking-inspired algorithm improvements [5], the existing approaches still have issues with complex situations in terms of balance of accuracy, scalability and computational efficiency [8], [9]. The specific surroundings are especially severe regarding such challenges. Urban canyons make the multipath extreme and aggravate the localization based on RF [19], [20], and the total inaccessibility of GPS signals in the indoor space, and this situation necessitates alternative localization paradigms." [p.1-2]

## 5. Proposed Method
Quantum-Enhanced Drone Swarm Localization (QEDSL) — METHOD
"This paper brings in a model that uses quantum stuff to boost the accuracy and how well the drones stick together. The idea here mixes quantum entanglement for syncing up between drones. It also throws in quantum-enhanced Kalman filtering to fuse sensor data better. That way, they get positions figured out more precisely and tougher against messes." [p.1]
The method employs quantum entanglement for inter-drone clock synchronization and a Quantum-Enhanced Kalman Filter (QEKF) for multi-sensor fusion across the swarm, aiming to improve localization accuracy and computational efficiency in GPS-denied conditions. [p.2-4]
