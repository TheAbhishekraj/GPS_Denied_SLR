## 2. Problem Statement
Inertial-only positioning drifts unboundedly without GNSS. Quote: "The dependency of the GNSS signal to navigate is common because most inertial systems of aircrafts cannot guarantee a trustable positioning information during too much time, since the errors accumulate continuously, mainly for small and low cost systems[4]." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
GNSS can be unreliable from atmosphere or deliberate interference, especially over long ranges. Quote: "A recurrent application of these techniques is work as a backup to Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS), since these systems can be unreliable in some situations, sometimes due to atmospheric conditions [2] or even due to intentional interference [3]." [p.1]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
JAMMED / untrustable-GNSS is the scenario class, tested via simulated GNSS denial on a long outdoor route. The paper states navigation "independently of GNSS information" without a jamming/spoofing attack; closest enum: **NOT_REPORTED** for an explicit denial mechanism, with the setting being long-range outdoor flight without GNSS reliance. Quote: "aiming to perform a long-range navigation, independently of GNSS information." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
Landmark Recognition algorithm used as the primary GNSS-free positioning source, integrated with the ArduPilot flight controller via MAVLink on a Raspberry Pi; adaptive landmark-candidate selection bounds the search set per flight segment. method_category: **VISION_OBJECT** (visual landmark recognition for positioning). Quote: "Additionally, an algorithm that uses landmark recognition as the main resource to perform an autonomous navigation flight, independently of GNSS, is proposed and tested, showing successful results in a long range simulated flight" [p.1]

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: low-cost fixed-wing/multirotor UAV platform (simulated); Quote: "The platform considered for this work is based on low-cost solutions, with open hardware and software components" [p.1-2]
- Sensors: downward-looking (nadir) camera; inertial sensors. Quote: "Since in this project, for the whole flight, the camera will be in nadir position, looking down" [p.3]
- Compute: Raspberry Pi as processing unit. Quote: "a Raspberry Pi [10] computer was selected as the processing unit aiming low-cost and flexibility" [p.1]
- Software/communication: ArduPilot suite + MAVLink between flight controller and image processing. Quote: "the MAVLink protocol [9] is used in this work to establish the connections between the flight controller and the image processing system" [p.1]
- Modules: Integration Module (flight-controller bridge) + Image Processing module. Quote: "the system has been split in two modules, the integration module and the image processing module" [p.1]

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
18.1 Best sensor combination: nadir camera + inertial sensors + Raspberry Pi processing with ArduPilot control. Quote: "a Raspberry Pi [10] computer was selected as the processing unit aiming low-cost and flexibility" [p.1]
18.2 Best algorithm: Landmark Recognition with adaptive landmark-candidate selection over the route (HMaxErr / GSMaxErr bounding), flying on ArduPilot. Quote: "an adaptive selection of the landmark candidates was proposed aiming to only feed the algorithm with the landmarks that can really be in the captured images." [p.3]
18.3 Best accuracy: average coordinate-estimation error **lower than 6 meters** over a 20.7 km route at 400 m altitude. Quote: "where the system has been able to estimate the coordinates during the whole route, with an average error lower than 6 meters." [p.1]
18.4 Baseline comparison: NO_BASELINE.
18.5 Conditions: real-time simulation; single long-range route (20.7 km, 400 m altitude); nadir camera; landmarks required within camera field of view. Quote: "Since in this project, for the whole flight, the camera will be in nadir position, looking down, the calculation of DGround can be simplified, only using the flight height (h) and the camera field of view" [p.3]
18.6 Trade-offs: landmark-candidate count vs recognition performance. Quote: "depending on the distance between two CPs and the number of landmarks, the amount of APs can still be big, reducing the performance of the Landmark Recognition algorithm." [p.3]
18.7 Generalization limits: results limited to simulation and one scenario; weather effects untested. Quote: "since the current results are limited to the simulation and restricted to a specific scenario, some drawbacks may not be clear as, for example, the effects of weather conditions in the Landmark Recognition" [p.5]
