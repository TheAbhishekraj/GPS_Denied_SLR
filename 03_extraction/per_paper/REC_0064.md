## 2. Problem Statement
"This research paper addresses the crucial challenge of the non-GPS drone in outdoor surveillance scenarios and proposes a packet loss-based positioning approach, wherein a drone estimates its current position by utilizing wireless packet loss from reference points packet transmission by employing a multilateration technique. Moreover, we introduce three heuristic lightweight navigation algorithms, leveraging packet loss-based positioning and enabling a drone to navigate toward its intended destinations." [p.1]
"The simulation results prove that by relying solely on packet loss computation, the drone without a precise positioning system can identify its location and recover to its nearest destination position without the necessity of complex sensors." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
Packet-loss-rate multilateration positioning plus three heuristic navigation algorithms (Heading-Based Navigation, Clockwise-Sequential Navigation, Pre-Decision Navigation) — COOPERATIVE
"we seek to develop an alternative lightweight positioning and navigation methodology solely relying on packet loss measurement. In contrast to conventional signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) methods that often necessitate sensor fusion and additional resources, our approach prioritizes simplicity and efficiency as the packet loss rate is more straightforward to measure and monitor in real-world scenarios" [p.2]
"we address the challenge of resolving unknown positions by combining trilateration and triangulation, thus so-called multilateration." [p.4]
Packet-loss-to-distance hypothesis: "The packet loss computation begins to estimate the distance once the transmission is completed, for which a higher packet loss value indicates a significantly longer distance. In this manner, the estimated distance can be represented correspondingly to a signal measurement from a GPS receiver." [p.4]; formally "a more significant distance indicates a higher possibility of failure, hence a higher packet loss rate, PLRi(t) ∝ Pr(loss) ∝ disti(t)" [p.4].
Method-category note: positioning is cooperative — n >= 3 reference drones at known vertices broadcast packets to the target drone; the reference set P = {1,...,n}, n >= 3 with n = 4 in evaluation [p.4, p.9].

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Unspecified drones: one target drone plus a set of n reference drones "positioned at the corner of a rectangular surveillance area as reference points" acting as packet transmitters [p.4]
- Sensors: Deliberately minimal — wireless packet transceivers only; heading information is assumed only for the HBN scenario ("drones equipped with gyroscope sensors") while the CSN/PDN scenarios assume "drones thoroughly have no direction information" [p.4]; "without the necessity of complex sensors" [p.1]
- Compute: Offline/simulated Java implementation with SVD from the JAMA linear-algebra library ("The simulations in our study are conducted using Java, and SVD is implemented through the Java Matrix (JAMA) linear algebra library [45]") [p.9]; a discrete-event simulation with four event types (MoveEvent, PositioningEvent, NavigateEvent, DoneEvent) [p.9]
- Communication: Wireless packet broadcast from each reference point; PLRi(t) = (Ti − Ri(t))/Ti over 1 s transmission windows, Ti = 100 packets per broadcast [p.4, p.9]
- Navigation controller: PID-style velocity regulation driven by the estimated position difference delta'(t) = rho'(t) − phi, with a discrete velocity ladder (25, 10, 5, 3, 1 m/s) selected as the first vc satisfying ||delta'(t)|| > vc [p.9]
- Formulation: 2-D Euclidean space only (rho(t) = (x(t), y(t))); position solved from the linear system Ax = b built from multilateration of packet-loss-derived distances [p.4-5, p.9]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: SIM
- environment: A synthetic 1 km x 1 km quadrangular surveillance area: "The distance between vertices of the surveillance area is set at 1000 m such that the form position alpha = {(0 m, 0 m), (0 m, 1000 m), (1000 m, 0 m), (1000 m, 1000 m)}, demonstrating the intended destination position phi at the centroid of the surveillance area" [p.9]; abstract frames the application as "outdoor surveillance scenarios" [p.1]
- trials/runs: One representative demonstration per algorithm for trajectories and angle-shifting analysis, plus "five sets of simulations for each algorithm" averaged for the navigation-round count k, all with the same Table 2 parameters [p.12-13]
- baselines: The three proposed heuristics are compared against each other (HBN, CSN, PDN) on navigation precision, complexity (number of rounds k) and operation time [p.3, p.12-13]; TDOA and RF fingerprinting are discussed as prior techniques but not simulated [p.2-3]
- datasets: NOT_REPORTED (synthetic; environmental noise modelled as eta ~ Uniform(0,1)) [p.9]
- key simulation parameters (Table 2) [p.9]: n = 4 reference points; initial flight command C(0,1) = (pi/4 rad, 47.1 m/s, 15 s) giving destination phi ≈ (500 m, 500 m); selective velocities vc in {25, 10, 5, 3, 1} m/s; Ti = 100 packets per broadcast; threshold tau = 5 m; eta ~ Uniform(0,1); beta = gamma = pi/4 rad

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Algorithm-level comparison rather than component ablation. Three axes are examined: (1) trajectory shape over time (Fig. 5), where HBN and PDN produce near-straight routes and CSN produces a "completely non-straight path" due to its fixed beta = pi/4 clockwise search [p.10-11]; (2) directional angle shifting (Fig. 6) as a proxy for power/actuation cost, where "HBN excels in minimizing angular rotation as evidenced by the flat line" while CSN shows the widest angular range and PDN a non-pattern rotation tied to estimation accuracy [p.11-12]; (3) navigation-round count k averaged over five runs per algorithm, where HBN is lowest, PDN second, CSN highest — with the caveat that CSN can approach PDN when the ideal direction aligns with the primary orientation [p.12-13]. Parameter sensitivity is discussed qualitatively: smaller gamma reduces k but "the increases in possible points coordination iterations lead to an increase in time complexity, gamma ∝ 1/j" [p.13]. No sensitivity analysis is reported for rmax, packet counts Ti, or the noise distribution eta.

## 10. Stated Limitations
"Theoretically, a limited packet transmission range directly impacts the effectiveness of position estimation where the more disti(t) between any reference point i in P, the less accuracy of the reliable estimation of rho(t). Given that disti(t) > rmax, the estimation position rho'(t) is no longer considered accurate since rmax represents the range at which packet loss is at 100%... As a result, some solutions x may yield two or more possible coordinate points, indicating non-unique intersections on radius PLRi(t) for all i in P. Moreover, there may be some cases in which no solution x satisfies the linear system Ax = b, indicating there is no absolute intersection point coordinate." [p.13]
"it is crucial to acknowledge that packet loss is not exclusively determined by distance as it is subject to be influenced by various environmental factors within the wireless communication environment, i.e., signal interference, network propagation characteristics, or the level of network congestion." [p.13]
"it is apparent that our proposed approach, while showing promise in simulations, may encounter practical challenges in real-world settings due to the complexities introduced by the multifaceted nature of wireless communication environments. Consequently, there is a need for further research and practical implementation to refine and adapt the algorithm for real-world applications." [p.13]
"Fundamentally, the effective operation of the multilateration approach hinges on the constant precision of the reference points' positions, as any deviation or loss of accuracy can introduce errors in determining the target drone's position." [p.13-14]
"it is also crucial to acknowledge the vital role that range estimation accuracy plays in shaping the precision of our packet loss-based positioning and navigation methodology." [p.14]

- The evaluation is 2-D (position is (x, y) only) with a waypoint-return objective (return to phi within tau = 5 m), so this record measures return-to-point performance, not trajectory-following accuracy [p.4-5, p.9].

- The packet-loss-to-distance mapping is a hypothesis validated only in simulation with Uniform(0,1) noise; the authors explicitly recommend linear regression to establish the correlation and cite prior empirical evidence that packet loss grows with distance (Aguayo [39], Carpenter [40], Tantayakul [41]) [p.3, p.13].
- Novelty positioning: this extends the authors' earlier drone-swarm formation work [25], upgraded from formation-keeping to absolute positioning via multilateration [p.3-4].
- All quantitative claims are internal to the Java discrete-event simulation (JAMA SVD); no hardware, no field data, and no GNSS reference comparison [p.9-13].

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
Wireless packet transceivers only, with four fixed reference drones at known vertices; a gyroscope is used only in the heading-informed scenario (HBN), while CSN/PDN assume no directional information at all [p.4, p.9]
### 18.2 Best algorithm
Heading-Based Navigation (HBN) — the recommended variant when heading is available: lowest navigation-round count, fastest termination inside tau = 5 m, minimal angular rotation; when no heading is available, Pre-Decision Navigation (PDN) gives the lowest average error at the cost of a second-best k [p.10-13]
### 18.3 Best accuracy (metric + value + unit + [p.N])
Average navigation error reduced from 11.69 m to 5.17 m over the mission (a 55.78% reduction), with HBN terminating at a residual of 2.69 m and the demonstrated terminal position (503.3 m, 502.6 m) inside the tau = 5 m threshold [p.10, p.12]
### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
Internal comparison among the three heuristics: HBN best on speed and k, PDN best on average error and error stability, CSN worst (highest average k, longest duration, 29.32 m error swing); TDOA and RF fingerprinting are critiqued qualitatively but never simulated as baselines [p.2-3, p.12-13]
### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy
2-D, 1 km x 1 km surveillance square, four corner reference drones, 100-packet broadcasts per second, eta ~ Uniform(0,1) noise, tau = 5 m, discrete velocity ladder 25/10/5/3/1 m/s, beta = gamma = pi/4 [p.9-10]
### 18.6 Trade-offs
Lightweight sensing (packet loss only, no ranging or vision hardware) is traded against coarse, noisy positioning that depends entirely on the packet-loss-to-distance hypothesis and on rmax tuning; HBN is fastest but needs heading information, PDN is most accurate but pays pre-computation, CSN is simplest but slowest and least stable [p.2, p.12-13]
### 18.7 Generalization limits
2-D formulation only (no altitude) [p.4-5]; simulation-only with synthetic uniform noise and no field validation [p.9-13]; requires >= 3 fixed, precisely known reference drones, so it does not generalise to infrastructure-free single-UAV operation [p.4, p.13-14]; estimation degrades sharply once disti(t) exceeds rmax (non-unique or no solutions) [p.13]; velocity parameters are outside realistic multirotor envelopes [p.9]
