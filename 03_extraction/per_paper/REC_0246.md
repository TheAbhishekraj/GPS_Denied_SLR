## 2. Problem Statement
"This paper presents the development, instrumentation, system integration, algorithm development, and flight testing of two UAVs for autonomous search and rescue in GPS-denied environments." [p.1]
"In order to compensate for the lack of partial or complete loss of GPS signals, this research focuses on the implementation of a Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) technique for the localization and mapping of the GPS-denied areas to enable autonomous navigation of UAVs in such areas." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"It is of utmost importance to rescue people from natural disaster zones as fast as possible. However, sometimes this is hard to achieve due to the inherited risk involved with sending humans to disaster zones." [p.1]
"GPS may be partially lost in certain locations due to high-rise buildings or other obstructions and collapsed/damaged areas. GPS signal may also be unavailable due to GPS spoofing. In addition, GPS signal is not available in indoor environments." [p.1]
"However, most of the work on using SLAM for autonomous navigation of vehicles is limited to unmanned ground vehicles or ground robots." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
"The first UAV uses a Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithm to map the environment and localize itself within it. As it explores the disaster zone, it is designed to scan the environment for victims using a computer vision algorithm. Once the victims are identified, a second UAV is designed to be deployed to distribute rescue packages to the identified victims." [p.1]
Mapping algorithm: "One such solution is called RTAB-Map [5]. The algorithm is a graph-based SLAM approach that has been integrated into robot operating" system frameworks [p.2].
Detection algorithm: the paper "talks about the performance of SLAM using visual imagery as its single input as well as the performance of YOLOv3 deep learning algorithm to identify victims" [p.2].
Method-category note: SLAM (visual SLAM with a CNN detector as a secondary module).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: Two custom-built quadcopters ("search vehicle" and "rescue vehicle") assembled from commercial off-the-shelf parts rather than prebuilt multicopters [p.3-4, p.8]
- Flight control: Pixhawk autopilot; an APM2.6 PixHawk Power Module supplies the avionics [p.4]
- Onboard computer (search UAV): "The main onboard computer selected is the UpSquared Pentium Quad Core 08/128" [p.4]; the rescue UAV uses "a different onboard computer, Jetson Xavier from NVDIA" [p.6]
- Sensors: Intel RealSense T265 tracking camera (173 deg FOV, 848x800) [p.6]; Intel D435i depth camera with integrated IMU (depth FOV 87 x 58 deg, range 0.3-3 m) [p.6]; Terabee TeraRanger rangefinder (60 m range, 12 g) [p.6]
- Propulsion/power: 4-in-1 50A Skywalker ESCs, four Flash Hobby D3536 brushless motors, counterrotating propellers, "3S LiPo 5,000 mAH battery, which is rated at 11.1 V" giving "10 minutes of flight time" [p.3-4]
- Payload mechanism: "The payload drop mechanism on the rescue UAV is used to drop rescue packages from the vehicle to the identified victims within a disaster zone." [p.6]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Lighting is the only variable tested for mapping: "It was found that the presence of white or featureless background (e.g., empty white walls) interfered with the mapping accuracy as shown by empty sections shown in Fig. 25." [p.7]
"It was discovered that the algorithm would only be able to map out the section where there is the most lighting. After that, the algorithm's localization feature stopped initiating and never restarted, which resulted in no more surrounding features being recorded and updated." [p.7]
Detection robustness was probed with partial-body imagery: "The results indicated that YOLOv3 was equally able to detect human leg. This experiment shows that YOLOv3 works well with the detection of full body as well as different body parts." [p.7]
