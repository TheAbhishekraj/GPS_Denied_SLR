## 2. Problem Statement
"Locating drones in GPS-denied environments is a challenging task, particularly for hardware platforms with limited computational resources and sensors." [p.1]
"In this paper, we propose a real-time drone positioning system that uses a Vision-UWB fusion framework." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"A common approach for positioning a drone within the environment is to cooperate with an unmanned ground vehicle (UGV) by estimating the drone's relative position on the UGV frame." [p.1]
"However, these fusion systems are limited by their range and are susceptible to lighting conditions, occlusion, and motion blur, which can significantly limit their applicability and increase error accumulation [12], [13]." [p.1]
"However, these sensing systems still face challenges due to the limited size, payload, and power supply of drones that prevent the use of high-end" sensors [p.1-2].

## 5. Proposed Method
"The fusion system involves two steps: 1) processing the state estimation using UWB ranging measurements and vision information separately, and 2) fusing the estimations from UWB and vision to provide the final state estimation for the drone." [p.1]
UWB branch: closed-form multi-lateration solved by least squares - "Then, taking the derivative of Equation 5 regarding to P^T_t and equating it to zeros yields P^T_t = (M^T M)^-1(M^T H)." [p.2]
Vision branch: "to utilize the information from the Realsense camera effectively, we applied the YOLOX model [24] to detect the drone" [p.2]; the model is custom-trained on drone images plus an open dataset [p.3].
Fusion: "we propose a light-coupled fusion framework that leverages the complementary inf[ormation]" [p.2] - particle-filter based combination of the two estimates [p.2-3].
Method-category note: HYBRID (UWB ranging fused with vision-based detection).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: "1) a DJI Mavic 2 equipped with a Decawave UWB tag, 2) a Husky robot equipped with 4 UWB beacons and a Realsense camera." [p.3]
- Sensors: Decawave UWB tag on the drone and four fixed UWB beacons on the Husky; Intel RealSense camera on the Husky. "Note that the UWB units used in our system are commercial products that generally provide an accuracy of 10-20 centimeters of range measurement." [p.3]
- Ground truth: "we used an accurate localization system, named OptiTrack [28], as the ground truth of location", with "three tags of the OptiTrack system mounted on the top of the DJI drone" [p.3]
- Compute: "The tracking program is running on an ASUS laptop with Nvidia Geforce GTX 1660 Ti Mobile GPU and Intel Core i7-10750H CPU." [p.3]; YOLOX was trained "on the desktop with a single Nvidia Geforce RTX 3080 Ti" [p.3]
- Control: "Both the DJI drone and Husky robot are controlled manually in our experiments." [p.3]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: "The DJI drone was flown in a workshop at a height between 1.2m to 2.1m while the Husky robot followed its movement on the ground." [p.4]
- baselines: the individual sensing branches - UWB ranging estimation alone and YOLOX detection alone [p.4, Table II]
- dataset: "we trained our own YOLOX model for drone detection using the drone images collected in our environments and an open dataset [27]" [p.2]
- metrics: "average errors and standard deviation of the drone's position estimation at different flying heights" [p.4]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Height-dependent behaviour of each branch is analysed: "For UWB estimation, it can provide accurate state estimation when the flying height is low (i.e., less than 1.6m), but the performance degrades as the height increases due to the small baseline between UWB beacons compared to the distance of beacons-to-tag" [p.5].
"Small vibrations on the UWB tag can introduce significant errors in the final state estimation." [p.5]
"The ground robot will completely lose track of the drone once it is outside the camera's FOV." [p.5]
"According to the comparison, the fusion system has achieved a good balance between UWB estimation and YOLOX object detection." [p.4]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"However, it is worth noting that the tracking results from the developed fusion framework mostly make a trade-off between different information resources, and further improvements can be made to enhance the overall performance of the system." [p.4]
Also recorded: "YOLOX object detection is limited by factors such as the camera's FOV, lighting variations, and" computational limits [p.5].
