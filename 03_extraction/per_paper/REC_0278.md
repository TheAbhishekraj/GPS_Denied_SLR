## 2. Problem Statement
"This study presents a methodology to address these challenges, enabling the maintenance of coordinates and navigation for UAVs to traverse jammed or completely out-of-coverage zones, thereby avoiding the need for emergency landings or Return-to-Home (RTH) protocols common in current UAV systems." [p.1]
"Despite this integration, maintaining reliability in heavily jammed or GNSS-denied zones (common in military contexts) remains a critical challenge for UAVs." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"With the rapid advancement of autonomous flight technology, there is an increasing demand for higher precision and advanced navigation techniques, with permissible distance errors often restricted to a few meters." [p.1]
"In UAV operations, civilian GPS signals - broadcasted at low power over wide bandwidths without dedicated security - are highly susceptible to interference, spoofing, and complete signal loss in complex environments." [p.1]
"Traditional filtering algorithms often prove insufficient in modeling complex, non-linear error accumulations during extended outages." [p.2]
Countermeasure gap: "Current countermeasures, such as fiber-optic tethers, are constrained by short operational ranges (< 20 km) and payload limits." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
"The proposed approach leverages the TransGAN model, a framework typically employed for data analysis comprising a Generator and a Discriminator. In this context, the model processes sequential real-world coordinate data." [p.1]
"Under normal GNSS operation, TransGAN is trained as a high-precision prediction model utilizing velocity and coordinate data as inputs. Conversely, during GNSS outages or interference, the trained TransGAN model is utilized to generate coordinates, thereby maintaining navigation capabilities for the UAV." [p.1]
"TransGAN is an advanced architecture that entirely eschews Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), employing Transformer architectures for both the Generator and Discriminator to efficiently process spatial-temporal data sequences." [p.2]
Two stated contributions: "Domain Adaptation of TransGAN: We fundamentally redesign the vision-based TransGAN architecture to effectively process and generate 1D multi-dimensional spatio-temporal sequences (Longitude, Latitude, Altitude, Velocity)." [p.1]; "Customized Loop-back Inference Pipeline: We propose a novel continuous prediction mechanism that completely bypasses the Critic during the inference phase. By integrating an Adaptive Step Adjustment and a Savitzky-Golay filter, our custom pipeline successfully mitigates cumulative INS drift" [p.1].
Inference detail: "during this inference phase, the Discriminator (Critic) and its REAL/FAKE scoring mechanism are completely deactivated. The trajectory reconstruction relies exclusively on the trained Generator via a continuous loop-back mechanism" [p.3].
Method-category note: HYBRID (generative deep learning substitutes for GNSS/INS fused position when GNSS is lost).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: UAV with "the Pixhawk flight controller to acquire and process input data. Real-world trajectory coordinates are interpolated from peripheral modules, specifically the GPS and IMU." [p.3]
- Sensors: GPS module (NEO-M8N) and IMU; velocity along 3 axes is the inference-time input [p.3-4]
- Compute: Edge feasibility assessed for "onboard UAV edge devices, such as NVIDIA Jetson Nano or Raspberry Pi" [p.5]
- Model size: "The lightweight design of our Transformer-based architecture requires approximately 0.355 million parameters. The computational cost is evaluated at 0.0007 Giga-FLOPS (GFLOPS)" [p.5]
- Inference rate: "the average inference speed for generating a single spatial coordinate update was measured at 2.69 milliseconds (ms) per step" [p.5]
## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: "a real-world GPS trajectory acquired in central Hanoi via a NEO-M8N module. The 75-minute flight yielded over 3,000 coordinate points at a 1 Hz update frequency." [p.4]; adverse segments were then simulated: "specific segments of the processed trajectory were intentionally subjected to simulated interference and complete signal outages" [p.4]
- scenario partitioning: interference case - "The 1,200-point dataset is partitioned into a Training Set (initial 900 points comprising optimal GNSS and INS data), a Signal Interference Set (subsequent 100 points), and an Orientation Set (final 200 points)." [p.4]; complete-loss case - "The dataset is partitioned into a Training Set (initial 2,100 points), a Complete Signal Loss Set (subsequent 100 points), and an Orientation Set (final 273 points)." [p.4]
- baselines: "TransGAN's performance was benchmarked against Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Wavelet Neural Network (WNN), and Kalman Filter (KF) models [15], [16], [17]." [p.4]
- dataset: single handcrafted trajectory; "we acknowledge that it limits the immediate generality of our conclusions" [p.4]
- metrics: "TransGAN consistently yields significantly lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Standard Deviation (SD) for spatial coordinates compared to the baseline methods." [p.4]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Two outage regimes are contrasted - signal interference (degraded GNSS plus INS) and complete signal loss (INS only): "In stark contrast to the interference scenario, the Complete Signal Loss Set assumes a total GNSS blackout. Here, Gaussian noise is injected into the retained INS signals to emulate realistic sensor inaccuracies, and the TransGAN model must reconstruct the trajectory relying exclusively on these noisy INS signals and the last known valid coordinates." [p.4]
Noise injection doubles as regularisation: "the dynamic injection of Gaussian noise into the INS data serves a critical dual purpose in our methodology: it not only emulates realistic environmental sensor degradation but also acts as a continuous data augmentation technique." [p.4]
Observed residual artefacts: "LAT maintains the highest graphical alignment, despite minor secondary peak suppression (0.0075-0.0082) caused by the Savitzky-Golay smoothing algorithm. LON accurately captures the primary directional movement, though a slight leftward shift suggests marginal underestimation." [p.4]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"While the current framework achieves acceptable operational accuracy, its precision remains constrained by inherent sensor errors, environmental factors, and a simplistic INS architecture." [p.5]
"Given the limited size of the initial handcrafted dataset, training a Transformer-based GAN architecture inherently raises valid concerns regarding overfitting and model memorization." [p.4]
"While this single handcrafted trajectory dataset effectively serves as an initial proof-of-concept to validate the TransGAN coordinate generation framework, we acknowledge that it limits the immediate generality of our conclusions." [p.4]
"While altitude exhibits comparatively higher deviations, this limitation can be mitigated via future hardware Kalman Filter (KF) integration." [p.4]

## 11. Future Work
"Future iterations will integrate advanced multi-axis IMUs (accelerometers and gyroscopes) to diversify input data and mitigate these structural limitations, ultimately targeting high-precision, robust navigation with error margins restricted to a mere few meters." [p.5]
"First, enhancing the hardware and data foundation by integrating multi-axis IMU sensors to minimize device errors, alongside expanding the training dataset across diverse topographies (e.g., urban canyons, forests, and mountainous terrains) to improve model generalization. Second, algorithmic refinement will involve exploring advanced generative frameworks, such as TimeGAN and Diffusion Models, to synthesize smoother and more realistic temporal sequences. Finally, future efforts will transition to real-world deployment by embedding the model directly into UAVs for field testing in complex signal environments, such as military or densely populated zones." [p.5]
Also: "the system's applicability can be extended by modulating the generated data into I/Q signals for external GPS receivers and developing spoofing signal generation for electronic warfare countermeasures." [p.5]
