## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
Zheng, Xie, and Li, "Multi-UAV Collaboration and IMU Fusion Localization Method in Partial GNSS-denied Scenarios," IEEE Access, vol. 11, 2023. DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3315870 [p.1]. Affiliations: Nanjing University of Information Science and Technology; National Mobile Communications Research Laboratory, Southeast University, Nanjing, China [p.1].

## 2. Problem Statement
"the simultaneous movement of the UAVs and the UE brings about problems of UAV coordinate deviation and range interference, resulting in poor accuracy and stability of UE localization" [p.1]. Target: locate mobile user equipment in disaster scenarios where "satellite rejection and base station damage" interrupt GNSS [p.1].

## 3. Motivation
"When it comes to disaster rescue, quickly and accurately locating the trapped people will not miss the golden rescue time" [p.1]. Existing work assumes stationary UEs or accurate UAV coordinates, both unrealistic in partial GNSS-denied emergency scenarios [p.1-2].

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
SIMULATION - MATLAB simulation with 3 UAV base stations in a 1000 x 1000 x 200 m3 test region; UAVs hover at 150 m altitude; GNSS deviation of +/-1.5 m horizontal, +/-0.5 m vertical injected [p.9-10]. "Partial" denial: UAVs can still receive satellites at altitude, but ground UEs cannot [p.3].

## 5. Proposed Method
Three-stage fusion localization: (1) Multi-UAV collaborative coordinate correction via gradient descent to eliminate initial UAV position bias [p.1, p.5-6]; (2) Density Peaks Clustering (DPC) preprocessing to screen abnormal ranging data [p.1, p.7-8]; (3) Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) fusing multi-UAV ranging with user-side IMU for mobile UE localization [p.1, p.9].

## 6. System Architecture
UAV emergency network: 3 UAVs as mobile anchors providing ranging; 4th UAV as relay to ground base station [p.3]. User-side IMU provides acceleration/angular velocity for dead-reckoning [p.2-3]. Ranging via TOF or RSSI modules on UAV airborne base stations [p.2]. UKF state vector includes user position, velocity, acceleration [p.9].

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: SIMULATION
- environment: MATLAB, 1000 x 1000 x 200 m3, 3 UAVs at 150 m altitude [p.9]
- noise model: UAV coordinate deviation +/-1.5 m (H), +/-0.5 m (V); ranging error N(0,1); observation noise std 1.5, process noise std 0.2 [p.9-10]
- metrics: RMSE and CDF of localization error [p.10]
- baselines: EKF, CT (constant turn-rate), PSO, raw coordinates, Kalman correction [p.10]

## 8. Key Quantitative Results
UAV coordinate correction: collaborative method improves 2D accuracy by ~45.8% vs raw, ~27.5% vs Kalman [p.9]. Table 2: Collaboration horizontal error 0.919 m vs Kalman 1.267 m vs Raw 1.697 m [p.9]. UKF vs EKF: "positioning error was reduced by 21.4% compared with the commonly used extended Kalman filter" [p.1]. Table 3: Low-altitude RMSE - UKF 1.41 m, EKF 1.65 m, CT 2.37 m; High-altitude - UKF 2.84 m, EKF 3.90 m, CT 6.06 m [p.12].

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Gradient descent vs PSO for coordinate correction: PSO "converged early and stopped optimization" at fitness ~15, while gradient descent "had fast convergence speed and low complexity" [p.10]. DPC screening effective under range anomaly conditions [p.10]. Low-altitude outperforms high-altitude due to reduced ranging sensitivity [p.12].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"sensitive to ranging error, a high-precision ranging module is needed to ensure localization accuracy in high-altitude scenarios. This will increase the power consumption and cost of the UAV system" [p.12]. "dense forests and buildings will also seriously reduce the ranging accuracy" [p.12]. Vertical accuracy not improved by collaboration (UAVs at same height) [p.9].

## 11. Future Work
"the multi-UAV collaboration and IMU fusion localization algorithm can also apply to short-range dynamic IoT localization" [p.12]. Implicit: extend to non-emergency IoT scenarios and improve ranging hardware [p.12].

## 12. Contribution Type
METHOD - simulation-based multi-UAV + user-side IMU fusion localization with DPC preprocessing and UKF for partial GNSS-denied emergency rescue.
