## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
Y. Xianjia, L. Qingqing, J. P. Queralta, J. Heikkonen, and T. Westerlund, "Cooperative UWB-Based Localization for Outdoors Positioning and Navigation of UAVs aided by Ground Robots," Turku Intelligent Embedded and Robotic Systems (TIERS) Lab, University of Turku, Finland, 2021.

## 2. Problem Statement
"Accurate navigation and localization, required for precise data collection in many industrial applications, often relies on RTK GNSS. These systems, able of centimeter-level accuracy, require a setup and calibration process and are relatively expensive." [p.1].

## 3. Motivation
"As UAVs become ubiquitous across different domains and application areas [5], having access to more flexible and lower-cost solutions to precise UAV navigation can aid in accelerating adoption and widespread use." [p.1].

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
MIXED
"With fast deployment and wide setup flexibility, the proposed system is able to accommodate different environments and can also be utilized in GNSS-denied environments." [p.1].

## 5. Proposed Method
Method name: Cooperative UWB-based relative localization
Category: UWB
"This paper addresses the problem of accurate positioning and navigation of UAVs through cooperative localization. Inexpensive ultra-wideband (UWB) transceivers installed on both the UAV and a support ground robot enable centimeter-level relative positioning." [p.1].

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: "a single ground robot and a UAV" [p.3]; "ClearPath Husky outdoor platform" [p.3]; "custom UAV" [p.3]
- Sensors: "four UWB responder transceivers" on ground robot [p.3]; "two UWB transceivers and an Intel RealSense tracking camera T265" on UAV [p.3]
- Compute: "PX4 autopilot firmware" [p.3]
- Communication: "UWB ranging measurements" [p.1]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
"We observe that the positioning error consistently decreases as the anchors become more separated." [p.3].

## 10. Stated Limitations
"VIO is more accurate but only during the first few seconds of flight, before it rapidly loses accuracy and diverges when the UAV altitude increases." [p.3].

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
"UWB transceivers installed on both the UAV and a support ground robot" [p.1].
### 18.2 Best algorithm
"least squares estimator (LE)" [p.2].
### 18.3 Best accuracy
Navigation error <10 cm with fixed anchors separated more than 10m [p.3].
### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
"the cooperative UWB approach particularly outperforms both VIO and GNSS estimations in terms of vertical accuracy." [p.3].

"if UWB systems based on fixed anchors separated more than 10m are utilized" [p.3].
### 18.6 Trade-offs
