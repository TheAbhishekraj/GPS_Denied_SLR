## 2. Problem Statement
"This paper discussed cost-effective vision-based obstacle avoidance for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating in GPS-denied environments. The system combines the You Only Look Once (YOLO) architecture with stereo vision cameras (OAK-D Lite), a Raspberry Pi computer, and a flight controller unit (Pixhawk-Cube)." [p.1]
"Navigating safely through obstacles becomes challenging for UAVs in GPS-denied environments. To address this issue, the drone is configured in altitude hold mode, and the system is trained on the Common Objects in Context (COCO) dataset, enabling it to recognize objects and analyze the surrounding areas to identify free spaces." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"However, navigating UAVs in environments where GPS signals are limited or unreliable, such as indoor or urban environments, presents challenges for safe and efficient operations. So, obstacle avoidance is critical to UAV navigation to prevent collisions and ensure safe operations." [p.1]
"There are still challenges remaining in terms of obstacle avoidance accuracy, utilization of system resources, and optimized path planning that needs to be addressed. The motivation behind this novel technique is to overcome the limitations of GPS and provide UAVs with the capability to navigate safely and autonomously in GPS-denied environments by relying on visual perception." [p.1]
Cost argument: "The proposed solution leverages a stereo vision camera and a Raspberry Pi onboard unit. It offers a low-cost solution that is compact and efficient." [p.1-2]; prior methods criticised because "these methods often required computationally intensive image processing on off-board ground computers" [p.2].

## 5. Proposed Method
Real-time YOLO object detection over stereo depth for threshold-triggered reactive obstacle avoidance and velocity-based navigation — VISION_OBJECT
"Obstacle avoidance is a two-step process: initially, the system detects nearby obstacles and reduces the drone's speed to prevent collisions. We employ the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection algorithm due to its swift and precise real-time object detection capability." [p.2-3]
"By analyzing the output of YOLO, we can determine the presence of obstacles in the scene. Additionally, leveraging the disparity information from the stereo vision camera, we can compute the depth or distance of the detected obstacles. If the calculated distance is within 2 meters, the system triggers obstacle avoidance by reducing the drone's speed to avoid collision with the obstacle." [p.3]
Stereo ranging equation (1): "D = (B * f)/(d + (P/2 − p))" combining baseline B, focal length f, disparity d, sensor width P and object-to-centre disparity p [p.4].
Free-space logic: "The predictions from the YOLO model can be used to determine free space for navigation... When an object is detected, the cells surrounding the object are examined to identify available space for the drone to navigate." [p.3]

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: In-house-built drone, "payload capacity of 1kg", "endurance of 10-15 minutes" [p.2]
- Sensors: OAK-D Lite stereo camera, forward-facing (Table II specs: colour 13 MP 4208x3120 at up to 35 FPS with DFOV 81 deg; stereo depth 640x480 at up to 120 FPS with DFOV 86 deg) [p.2, p.4]; IMU for velocity estimation [p.3]
- Compute: "a Raspberry Pi 4B with 4 GB RAM, 32 GB memory card, 40-pin GPIO, a powerful quad-core with processing speed of 1.2 GHz, and a 64-bit CPU is employed as a secondary onboard controller" [p.2]
- Flight control: Pixhawk Cube flight controller; drone operated "in altitude hold mode... navigated using velocity mode estimated from the IMU sensor" [p.2-3]; "Serial communication is employed to send control commands between the Raspberry Pi and Pixhawk telemetry" [p.2]
- Power: "power is supplied by a universal battery eliminator circuit (UBEC) connected to the same 5000 mAh, 12 V, 30 Amp lithium-polymer (LiPO) battery that powers the flight controller via a Pixhawk cube power module 2" [p.2]
- Trigger chain: camera range value relayed to Pixhawk, with "a threshold sensor value of 4m is set" at the system level, while the avoidance experiments trigger at 2-2.5 m (see section 10 — the two stated thresholds are inconsistent in the source) [p.2-4]
- Detection stack: YOLO grid-cell detection with Non-Maximum Suppression ("NMS includes selecting the bounding box with the highest probability score as the starting point and then suppressing other bounding boxes with a lower Intersection over Union (IoU)"), applied frame by frame, trained on COCO [p.1, p.3]

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL
- environment: "This evaluation took place at the TiHAN Testbed, Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad (IITH) in Hyderabad, India." with "human-sized obstacles represented by a human mannequin" [p.4]; the indoor/outdoor character of the testbed site is not specified in the text, so no environment enum is asserted
- trials/runs: One documented end-to-end flight (takeoff to 2 m altitude, obstacle approach, roll-right avoidance manoeuvres, landing) plus ranging trials: "placing an obstacle at a known distance of 5-10 meters and gradually reducing meters until it was below 2.5 meters. We conducted 15 experiments for each distance interval" [p.5]
- baselines: NO_BASELINE — no alternative obstacle-avoidance method is implemented or compared; related work (SLAM, A*, APF, RRT, VFH+, reinforcement learning) is reviewed qualitatively [p.2]
- datasets: COCO (training only) [p.1]; no evaluation dataset or recorded-data release is reported
- operating parameters: constant 2 m flight altitude outside takeoff/landing; relative drone speed 2 m/s; avoidance speed 1 m/s; avoidance trigger observed at 2.49-2.5 m; roll adjustments of 1 m right, a further 1 m right, forward 1 m, then roll left 2 m [p.4-6]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Not reported. There is no component ablation (e.g., detection-only vs depth-only avoidance), no threshold sweep, no comparison of YOLO variants, and no sensitivity analysis of the velocity ladder. The only repeated-measures evidence is the 15-experiments-per-distance-interval ranging test reporting "less than 1% error" [p.5]. Trajectory fidelity is examined only qualitatively via the velocity/altitude/roll plots, where "there is a slight deviation between the actual and the desired route taken" attributed to "external disturbances, variations in wind conditions, control system dynamics, or limitations in the accuracy of the drone's sensors" [p.4-5].

## 10. Stated Limitations
No dedicated limitations section exists. Limitations stated or evidenced in the text:
- Route-tracking deviation: "there is a slight deviation between the actual and the desired route taken... stem from several factors, including external disturbances, variations in wind conditions, control system dynamics, or limitations in the accuracy of the drone's sensors." [p.4-5]
- Threshold inconsistency in the source: the system description sets "a threshold sensor value of 4m" [p.2] while the method, abstract and flown experiment trigger avoidance at 2-2.5 m [p.1, p.3, p.4] — copied verbatim and flagged as an internal inconsistency.
- Two-dimensional sensing: "a flight system for constant altitude flight and collision avoidance using a two-dimensional stereo camera. Which improved the field of view by not accumulating data over multiple frames, enabling the detection of different parts of the environment." [p.5] — single-frame depth use, constant-altitude flight, no 3-D mapping.
- Resource ceiling: "Despite the limited computational resources, the algorithms showcase effective performance" on the Raspberry Pi, with CPU at 93% during operation (Table I) [p.4].
- Single-obstacle-class evaluation: experiments used "human-sized obstacles represented by a human mannequin" [p.4].

## 15. Notes
- The reported numbers need careful handling in synthesis: the abstract states a 10% error rate / 90% detection rate for detection-and-avoidance, while the ranging experiments report "less than 1% error" — these are different quantities (detection success vs stereo-distance error) but the paper does not separate them cleanly; both are quoted with pages above.
- Threshold inconsistency: 4 m system threshold [p.2] vs 2 m method/abstract threshold [p.1, p.3] vs 2.5 m trigger in the flown experiment [p.4]. Flagged for the human reviewer; do not silently pick one.
- The demonstrated avoidance manoeuvre is entirely reactive and one-sided: "In this experiment, most turns were towards the right to avoid the obstacle positioned on the left" [p.4] — single-obstacle, known-scenario testing.
- The velocity metric is ambiguous in the source: "The drone's relative speed is configured at 2 m/s" (abstract) versus "relative speed of 2 m/s for the drone and obstacle avoidance speed of 1 m/s" (conclusion) [p.1, p.6].
- Reported FPS utilisation "15-20%" [p.4, Table I] is phrased unusually (a rate expressed as a percentage); copied verbatim per Rule E5 and flagged.
- No positioning/localisation accuracy of any kind is reported — consistent with this record's role in the review as a reactive-avoidance, low-cost-platform data point rather than a localisation result.
- IIT Hyderabad's TiHAN testbed is named as the evaluation site; the same group's prior rangefinder-based work is cited as [23] (GlobConPT 2022), showing continuity of the research line.

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
OAK-D Lite stereo camera (forward-facing, 120 FPS stereo depth stream) + IMU velocity estimation on an altitude-hold platform; no GPS, no LiDAR, no rangefinder in the avoidance loop [p.1-3]
### 18.2 Best algorithm
Frame-by-frame YOLO object detection (COCO-trained) with NMS, grid-cell free-space assessment, stereo-disparity ranging via D = (B*f)/(d + (P/2 − p)), and a reactive roll-based avoidance state machine with a 2-2.5 m trigger [p.3-4]
### 18.3 Best accuracy (metric + value + unit + [p.N])
Detection rate 90% ("error rate of 10%") for detect-and-avoid within the 2 m threshold; stereo distance-measurement error below 1% over 15 trials per distance interval in the 5-10 m range; no localisation accuracy reported [p.1, p.5]
### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
NO_BASELINE — the only reference points are the resource-utilisation figures (93% CPU on a Raspberry Pi 4B) and the qualitative review of SLAM/A*/APF/RRT/VFH+ alternatives, none of which is run for comparison [p.2, p.4]
### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy
TiHAN Testbed flights at constant 2 m altitude, relative speed 2 m/s (1 m/s during avoidance), human-mannequin obstacles, obstacle placed at known distances 5-10 m and reduced below 2.5 m, avoidance by right-hand roll adjustments of 1 m [p.4-6]
### 18.6 Trade-offs
Cost and compute footprint (Raspberry Pi + OAK-D Lite, 485 MB RAM in use) are traded for a reactive, threshold-based avoidance strategy with no map, no localisation and single-obstacle behaviour; the 93% CPU load indicates little headroom for richer perception on this hardware [p.2, p.4]
### 18.7 Generalization limits
Constant-altitude 2-D flight only [p.5]; evaluated with a single obstacle type (mannequin) in one testbed campaign [p.4]; right-biased avoidance behaviour in the demonstrated run [p.4]; no localisation, mapping or path replanning, so it does not by itself constitute GPS-denied navigation beyond obstacle clearance [p.3]; threshold definitions inconsistent in source (4 m vs 2-2.5 m) [p.2, p.4]
