## 2. Problem Statement
"Reliable navigation in Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-denied environments remains a critical challenge for fixed-wing Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs)." [p.1]
"This paper presents the development, implementation, and experimental validation of a unified Error-State Extended Kalman Filter (ESEKF) designed for fixed-wing UAV navigation under GNSS-denied and GNSS-degraded scenarios." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"In such conditions, Inertial Navigation Systems (INSs) must be aided by heterogeneous sensors to mitigate drift and ensure long-term accuracy." [p.1]
"However, GNSS signals are vulnerable to blockage, multipath, interference, and intentional jamming, making GNSS-denied" operation unavoidable in contested environments [p.1].

## 5. Proposed Method
"The proposed framework integrates an INS with magnetometer, barometer, pitot tube with pseudovanes, Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO), and Computer Vision (CV) Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based position estimates within a unified estimation architecture." [p.1]
"A comprehensive mathematical description of the ESEKF is provided, with special attention given to the modeling of sensor systematic errors, which are explicitly included in the augmented error-state vector." [p.1]
"The estimator performance is evaluated through a flight experiment using a custom fixed-wing UAV platform, where the contribution of each aiding sensor is assessed via systematic sensor deactivation under GNSS outage conditions." [p.1]
Method-category note: HYBRID (INS propagation fused with magnetic, barometric, aerodynamic and vision-based aiding).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: custom fixed-wing UAV [p.1], flying at "approximately 770 meters above mean sea level, with an average ground speed of 55 m/s" [p.8]
- Sensors: "The sensor suite onboard the UAV comprised: (a) an Advanced Navigation Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems (MEMS)-based Spatial IMU; (b) a three-axial Drotek RM3100 magneto-inductive sensor; (c) a Cube Orange autopilot comprising an MS5611 digital barometer; (d) a CUAV SKYE2 pitot/external temperature probe; (e) an in-house-developed monocular camera-based vision navigation system; and (f) a CUAV C-RTK 9Ps GNSS receiver" [p.8]
- Ground truth: "The Ground-Truth (GT) values for position and velocity were obtained from the CUAV C-RTK 9Ps GNSS receiver, while for attitude, we employed the native solution running inside Cube Orange." [p.8]
- Vision navigation: "we developed a custom factor graph-based solution that can benefit from additional sensors and reliable prior information from the INS. This allows the system to achieve real-scale monocular VIO and partially mitigate the fundamental monocular limitation of unobservable scale." [p.8]

## 7. Experimental Setup

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Systematic sensor deactivation is the core ablation: magnetometer off, barometer off, pitot/pseudovanes off, VIO off and CV off [p.9].
"First, the analysis of the magnetometer-denied ESKEF's outcomes shows that, differently from what we expected, no significant loss of performance was noticed w.r.t. the fully-aided ESKEF." [p.9]
"the removal of the pitot/pseudovanes aiding from the ESKEF actually improved the vertical channel and roll/pitch navigation performance" [p.9]
"the paramount importance of the barometer and CV sensors for mitigating position drift in the INS horizontal and vertical channels, respectively" [p.9]
Caveat recorded by the authors: the apparently good CV-denied horizontal RMSE "were a direct consequence of the error cancellation that took place as a function of the 'zigzag' trajectory performed by the UAV during the experiment" [p.9]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"The results also indicated that magnetometer aiding may be redundant under the tested conditions, suggesting limited observability of the corresponding augmented calibration states" [p.9]
"the ESEKF was unable to properly (and separately) observe/calibrate the nine extra states that were augmented to account for the magnetometer systematic errors (even when the GNSS was active)" [p.9]
possible causes of pitot-related degradation: "the filter inability to properly estimate wind velocity fluctuations in the absence of GNSS signals" [p.9]

## 11. Future Work
"Future work will focus on a formal observability analysis of the complete filter structure and on the systematic evaluation of TC measurement models for all auxiliary sensors to improve robustness and consistency in extended GNSS-denied missions." [p.9]
