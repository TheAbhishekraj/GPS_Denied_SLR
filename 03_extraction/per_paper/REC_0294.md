## 2. Problem Statement
"In this paper, a navigational system is presented for a multi-rotor UAV in a GNSS-denied environment. The global positioning problem is solved by taking a local reference frame and estimating the UAV's position and velocity w.r.t it, using an onboard monocular camera and barometer." [p.1]
"The navigational problem is solved by designing a required velocity function and tuning the system's response to this input by a PID controller." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"A good receptivity of GNSS signals during autonomous navigation is of prime importance. Due to this, navigation becomes close to impossible with places having low receptivity or GNSS-denied zones." [p.1]
"To achieve this, almost all UAVs rely on a combination of multiple GNSS systems for accurate positioning and hence navigation." [p.1]
"The results of both simulations and actual flight tests show the effectiveness and reliability of the presented work, which enables a UAV to perform full automated missions from takeoff, waypoint navigation and landing, in complete absence of any GNSS signals." [p.1]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
MIXED
"GNSS-denied outdoor navigation" is the keyword given by the authors [p.1]; the system is also described for GNSS-denied/loss patches within otherwise GNSS-aided missions [p.1, p.7].

## 5. Proposed Method
Feature-tracking visual odometry with barometric altitude: "Feature tracking is used to calculate the displacement along the x and y axes. The displacement along the z-axis or altitude is obtained from the readings of the barometer of the autopilot." [p.3]
Displacement derivation: "The average position of features in the nth frame is compared with their average position in the (n-1)th frame, to estimate the displacement along the x and y axes." [p.4], with the altitude-dependent projection correction of Eq. (10) and the pixel-to-metre conversion of Eq. (12) [p.4].
Velocity filtering: "The velocity estimates given by the image processing algorithm is highly oscillatory in nature. It is fed to a low-pass filter which smoothens the velocity output." [p.4]
Navigation controller: a required-velocity field plus PID - "To obtain the desired velocity for the UAV to reach the destination, a positional error term is calculated" [p.5]; "The PID controller outputs the optimum control input in the form of linear accelerations w.r.t the inertial frame, in x, y and z directions" [p.5], converted to roll, pitch and thrust [p.5].
Mode switching: "the GNSS location and velocity estimates of the last 5 seconds of GNSS aided flight are stored and the displacement from the takeoff point is continuously tracked. This data is used to switch to Non-GNSS navigation when GNSS signals are weak or lost. A switch is made if the number of satellites <6 or if a GNSS glitch occurs." [p.5]
Method-category note: OPTICAL_FLOW (monocular feature-tracking odometry fused with barometric altitude).

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: "Tarot 650mm quadcopter" with "Pixhawk 2.1 Cube" autopilot running "ArduPilot's ArduCopter" [p.5, Table II]
- Sensors: "Camera: GitUp Git2P (RGB, monocular, diagonal FOV: 90 deg)"; "Gimbal: Tarot T4-3D (3-axis)"; barometer of the autopilot for altitude [p.3, p.5]
- Compute: "Onboard Computer: Nvidia Jetson Nano" [p.5]; "It has a Quad-Core ARM Cortex-A57 processor which has been proved to be sufficient for our algorithm." [p.6]
- Mounting: "The gimbal is controlled by the autopilot and is commanded to always point the camera downward facing." [p.5]
- Interfaces: "The onboard computer is interfaced to the camera using a USB cable and to the autopilot using the serial interface." [p.5]
- Runtime: "The complete software (position and velocity estimation and navigational controller) runs at ~20fps and ~16fps at resolutions of 640 x 480 and 1280 x 720 respectively." [p.5]
## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: BOTH
- environment: "Several experiments were conducted at varying camera resolutions and terrain types." ## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Feature detector choice: the paper benchmarks Shi-Tomasi against SURF and adopts Shi-Tomasi: "Plot of actual trajectory (red) and estimated trajectory for Shi-Tomasi (green) and SURF (blue)" with the processing times of Table I [p.3].
Resolution versus throughput: "Higher resolution resulted in lower error on altitudes above 70m, however it resulted in a lower frame rate." [p.5]
Controller gain re-tuning between simulation and flight is documented in Table III (e.g., k 0.1 after simulation versus 0.1-0.28 in flight; Kd 0.03 versus 0.02; Ki 0.02 versus 0.015; a 4 versus 5) [p.6].

## 10. Stated Limitations
Constraints reported by the authors: "Lowering the speed" is needed at lower frame rates [p.5]; the altitude estimate depends on the barometer; and the follow-on work lists relaxations still required - "Integrating the present algorithm with IMUs along with rangefinder/altimeter to increase accuracy and relaxing zero-yaw constraint to increase practicality." [p.8]
Computational framing: "our solution is computationally lighter as neither it requires multiple flight computers as in [7] & [8] nor it needs a significantly powerful CPU as in [9]." [p.6]

## 11. Future Work
"Some extensions to this work are planned for future, aimed at increasing the reliability of GNSS-denied navigation: Introduction of an EKF based switching between GNSS aided and GNSS denied navigation. Using pre fetched satellite images of terrain with GPS locations and matching them with terrain images taken during flight as a corrective measure to significantly reduce error during long distance flights. Integrating the present algorithm with IMUs along with rangefinder/altimeter to increase accuracy and relaxing zero-yaw constraint to increase practicality." [p.8]
