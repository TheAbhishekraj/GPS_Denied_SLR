DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3684630
ABSTRACT: The deployment of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in tasks such as urban surveillance, disaster assessment, and logistics necessitates enhanced autonomous navigation capabilities. Traditionally, the localization of UAVs relies on Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS). However, in urban canyons, indoor environments, or bad weather conditions, GNSS signals are prone to obstruction and interference, leading to degraded navigation performance. To overcome the limitations, the development of GNSS-denied alternative localization techniques has become an important topic. In this paper we present an UAV visual localization and heading estimation in GNSS-denied environments by matching the UAV overhead imagery with satellite maps. The proposed NavCLIP constructs an image database using satellite data and retrieves the best-matched by querying with aerial images. A homography matrix is then employed to estimate the UAV location and yaw angle. Due to the lack of publicly available datasets, this work collects an aerial and satellite image dataset (UAV-Satellite Dataset) for training and validation. The experimental results show strong localization performance on the Google Earth dataset, while UAV flight experiments exhibit larger localization errors under the real-world conditions. In addition, the proposed technique improves over the GeoCLIP baseline and achieves low heading estimation error. Code and dataset are available on GitHub at https://github.com/PHChenGit/NavCLIP
