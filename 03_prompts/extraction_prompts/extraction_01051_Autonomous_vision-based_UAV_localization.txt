DOI: 10.1088/2631-8695/ae305b
ABSTRACT: Autonomous vision-based Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) offer considerable potential in GPS-denied environments, where localization and path planning must rely solely on onboard visual perception systems. This review systematically analyses recent advancements in vision-based UAV localization and optimization-driven path planning, particularly under GPS-denied constraints. We first examine the capabilities and limitations of various onboard vision sensors, emphasizing their roles in data acquisition, environmental perception, and system-level integration. We then review visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) frameworks, with a focus on pure visual SLAM approaches, including both feature-based and direct methods, and their adaptability to UAV flight scenarios. Furthermore, we analyze optimization-based path planning methods, categorized into gradient-based and heuristic-based algorithms, evaluating their ability to generate collision-free paths under UAV-specific motion constraints. Based on a critical analysis of existing research, this review identifies key gaps and proposes future directions, including improving UAVs’ robustness to visual degradation, enhancing localization under dynamic and partially observable conditions, and emphasizing real-world validation in 3D dynamic environments. These steps are essential for bridging the gap between simulated performance and real-world autonomy. © 2026 IOP Publishing Ltd.
