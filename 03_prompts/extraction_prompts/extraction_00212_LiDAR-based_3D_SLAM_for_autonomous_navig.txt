TITLE: LiDAR-based 3D SLAM for autonomous navigation in stacked cage farming houses: An evaluation
AUTHORS: Jiang J.; Zhang T.; Li K.
YEAR: 2025
VENUE: Computers and Electronics in Agriculture
DOI: 10.1016/j.compag.2024.109885
ABSTRACT: Autonomous navigation in challenging environments, especially in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-denied stacked cage farming houses (SCFHs), presents considerable challenges for modern inspection robotic systems. These settings are semi-structured, enclosed, and characterised by narrow spaces, multilevel metallic cages, irregular obstacles, and repeating feature aisles, rendering traditional localisation techniques unreliable. Three-dimensional (3D) light detection and ranging (LiDAR) simultaneous localisation and mapping (SLAM) has demonstrated its efficacy in various environments. This paper evaluated the performance of 15 representative 3D LiDAR SLAM methods using data collected from a laboratory, field SCFH, and simulation setting. Methods were categorised into three groups: 1) LiDAR-only methods, 2) LiDAR-inertial methods, and 3) LiDAR-visual-inertial methods. For comparison with heterogeneous sensor localisation methods, two-dimensional (2D) LiDAR SLAM method, stereo visual-inertial odometry (VIO), and wheel odometry system were also assessed. The positioning accuracy, map quality, and resource consumption were evaluated during the same movements. An SCFH-like simulation environment was constructed in Gazebo to calculate the absolute pose error (APE) between the estimation and ground truth across the three different travel speeds. SCFH experimental results identified limitations in 3D LiDAR SLAM, such as pose drift and mapping failures, despite its strong performance in laboratory tests. Methods that directly register raw point clouds to maps outperform feature-extraction-based methods. In tests over four aisles, Faster-LIO achieved the highest positioning accuracy, with a translation error of 0.0592 %, followed by VINS-Fusion and Fast-LIO2. Faster-LIO also showed the highest mapping accuracy, with mapping errors of 6.31 cm in the single-aisle and 4.21 cm in the two-aisle scenario at a 5 cm map resolution. However, in the simulation environment, Fast-LIO2 outperformed Faster-LIO, achieving APEs of 12.8 cm at 0.2 m/s in the single-aisle and 27.1 cm at 0.4 m/s in the two-aisle scenario. VINS-Fusion required to 6–7 times more computational resources than Faster-LIO for comparable accuracy and struggled with backward motion. © 2024
