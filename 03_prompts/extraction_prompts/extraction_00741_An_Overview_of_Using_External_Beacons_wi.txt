TITLE: An Overview of Using External Beacons with UAV for Navigation, Localization, and Information Exchange

DOI: 10.1109/ICCR64365.2024.10927537
ABSTRACT: UAVs (Unoccupied Aerial Vehicles) work in high-risk environments where disruptions and failures impede their progress. A UAV must exhibit a degree of robustness to these disruptions in order to conduct operations satisfactorily. Like all current vehicular robots, UAVs rely on GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) and onboard sensor data to perform navigation, communication, and execute critical actuated behaviors. However, these UAVs may often travel to regions where signal availability is sparse, and incidents arise when critical information such as location and telemetry from primary onboard sensors, ground control, or satellites is unavailable. In such cases, external beacon networks are the solution to address this information scarcity. In critical situations, these beacons can sometimes be the only source of information for an agent that could prevent catastrophic agent or mission failure. This study provides a comprehensive overview of external beacons supporting UAV operations. A taxonomy of sensors, beacons, and marker systems is provided, and four primary categories of beacon systems are discussed. Potential limitations in applying the solution and critical factors for proper implementation are also explored.
