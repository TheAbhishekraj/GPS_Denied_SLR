AUTHORS: T. Anagnostopoulos

ABSTRACT: Flying carpets are adopted by thematic parks (i.e., Disneyland) and the hospitality industry (i.e., luxurious hotels in Dubai) worldwide. Such devices assess artificial intelligence as a service to provide safe and enjoyable indoor navigation to youths under the unobtrusive supervision of their parents. In this paper, indoor navigation problem is treated as a reinforcement learning Temporal-Difference (TD) learning problem adopting a Q-learning algorithm. Such an algorithm can avoid spatial obstacles (i.e., walls’ dead ends and/or swimming pools) during indoor navigation in certain places within thematic parks and/or hotels’ playgrounds. Concretely, a simple variation of the Q-learning algorithm is adopted, namely one-step Q-learning, since it does not require a model of the environmental dynamics thus being a more flexible and less complex algorithm. Certain experiments are performed and analyzed to support the adoption of one-step Q-learning as an efficient algorithm for facing the indoor navigation problem in smart entertainment places of interest.
