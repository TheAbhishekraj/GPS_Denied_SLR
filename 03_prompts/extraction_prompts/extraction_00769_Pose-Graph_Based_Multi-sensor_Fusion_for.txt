ABSTRACT: As the commercialization of indoor mobile robots continues to expand, robust localization and mapping (SLAM) technology for GPS-denied indoor environments has become increasingly important. Single-sensor SLAM systems face inherent limitations in stable localization due to indoor-specific challenges such as illumination changes, low-texture surfaces, reflective materials, and wheel slip. This paper proposes a multi-sensor SLAM system that fuses a stereo camera, 2D LiDAR, wheel odometry, and IMU through GTSAM-based pose graph optimization. Deep learning-based SuperPoint feature detection ensures stable visual odometry in low-texture and varying illumination conditions, while a diagonally arranged dual 2D LiDAR configuration provides 360-degree omnidirectional environment perception. A dynamic noise model that reflects each sensor’s reliability in real time, combined with DBoW2-based loop closure detection, enables global correction of accumulated drift. Experiments conducted on a Scout Mini platform achieved positioning accuracy of RMSE $\mathrm{1. 2 4 - 2. 3 7 ~ c m}$ in a laboratory environment and $\mathrm{1 6. 3 - 1 6. 5 ~ c m}$ in a corridor environment, representing approximately 8-fold improvement over ORB-SLAM3 stereo. The proposed system demonstrates that robust and practical indoor localization across diverse environments is achievable through complementary multi-sensor fusion without requiring costly 3D LiDAR.
