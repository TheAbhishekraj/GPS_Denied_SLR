TITLE: I-NAV: Inverse Navigation for questionnaire-based geolocation in GPS-denied environments
AUTHORS: Qazi S.; Alejandro J.; Murshed R.U.; Evans T.S.; Lippert J.; Saquib M.
YEAR: 2026
VENUE: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT
DOI: 10.1016/j.array.2026.100978
ABSTRACT: Accurate geolocation remains challenging in areas with limited Global Positioning System (GPS) availability, such as dense forests, rural settings, urban canyons, and disaster zones. To overcome this, we introduce Inverse Navigation (I-NAV), a geolocation framework explicitly designed for decision-making in GPS-denied environments. Unlike conventional methods that determine optimal routes from known origins to destinations, I-NAV inversely identifies probable user locations based on contextual data from questionnaires such as recently passed landmarks, travel-time constraints, and directional cues. Consequently, it can be utilized in critical applications, including public health monitoring, drone surveillance, search-and-rescue operations, and emergency response. Applying a modified Dijkstra's algorithm, I-NAV generates all feasible paths at a high resolution that is reachable within a specified travel budget. Within a Bayesian inference framework, we introduce an Adaptive Gaussian Likelihood Model that combines a reachability prior with travel-time and directional observation likelihoods to produce a posterior probability distribution over candidate locations, effectively capturing uncertainties in travel conditions. Our experimental results demonstrate that I-NAV's deterministic isochrone component achieves spatial coverage comparable to established routing services such as OpenRouteService, while the probabilistic component outperforms several baseline localization approaches across multiple evaluation metrics. © 2026 The Authors
