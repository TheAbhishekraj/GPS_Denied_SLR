ABSTRACT: This paper introduces a Destination Guidelines mechanism for a Smart Blind Stick designed to facilitate indoor movement, tackling the shortcomings of current solutions for individuals with visual impairments. Traditional approaches such as beacon-dependent systems demand substantial infrastructure investment, while methods relying on computer vision struggle with environmental inconsistencies. In contrast, the proposed approach incorporates ceiling-positioned Infrared (IR) LED routes with an ESP32-CAM component and wireless connectivity principles. Through sequential refinement phases that specifically confronted ambient illumination interference, a filter that blocks visible light was incorporated, achieving accurate and consistent pathway identification under various lighting conditions. The system transmits image data via Wi-Fi, calculates the user's orientation in relation to the IR pathway, and communicates directional guidance through Bluetooth audio. Evaluation within a home setting demonstrated consistent functionality, providing a financially viable alternative to established methods without sacrificing dependability. Future work includes integrating processing capabilities for autonomous operation and implementing functionality to detect reverse directional movement.
