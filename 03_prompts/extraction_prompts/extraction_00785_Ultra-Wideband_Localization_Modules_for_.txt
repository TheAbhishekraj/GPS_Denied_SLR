TITLE: Ultra-Wideband Localization Modules for Autonomous Lawn Mowers: A Cost-Effective Feasibility Study
AUTHORS: S. Sharma; T. S. Thind; P. Gupta; H. Ahmad; R. Rout; S. K. Sharma
YEAR: 2025
VENUE: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT
DOI: 10.1109/ICCICA67008.2025.11337516
ABSTRACT: In this paper we propose a novel technique that is cost-effective and can help lower the overall cost of autonomous lawnmower at the expense of acceptable accuracy. This work explores the application of Ultra-Wideband (UWB) technology for localization in an autonomous lawn mower, serving as an alternative to existing solutions such as LiDAR and RTK-GPS (Real-Time Kinematics - GPS). The approach employs UWB Time-of-Flight (ToF) sensors, with multiple stationary anchors positioned along the boundary of the lawn. A mobile node placed on the lawn mower receives distance measurements from these anchors, and multilateration is used to estimate the mower's position within the defined area. The system is implemented using a microcontroller that communicates with MATLAB in real time via a serial COM port. Initial testing demonstrated accurate position estimation, including under non-line-of-sight (NLOS) conditions where vision-based systems like LiDAR typically face limitations. Preliminary experiments suggested that the average percentage error in the position is 8.74%, which can be reduced further. Latency of 80 millisecond to 1 second was observed in updating the position due to delays in the multilateration computation. Overall, the system demonstrates potential as a cost-effective and reliable localization method for small-scale outdoor autonomous platforms.
