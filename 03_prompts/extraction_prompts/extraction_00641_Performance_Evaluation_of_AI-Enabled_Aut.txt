TITLE: Performance Evaluation of AI-Enabled Autonomous Drone Navigation Systems Using Computer Vision
AUTHORS: Gali S.; Zabeen S.A.; Manasa J.; Yasmin S.; Manisha V.; Nagamani V.
YEAR: 2026
VENUE: Proceedings of 3rd International Conference on Machine Learning and Autonomous Systems, ICMLAS 2026
DOI: 10.1109/ICMLAS67792.2026.11483799
ABSTRACT: The current state of AI and computer vision has reached a long way in the area of drone navigation, being able to navigate at full in a dynamic environment and locations with low GPSs. This form of traditional navigation is limited within the fringes of environmental vagaries, dynamically moving obstructions, and real-time decision-making demands over and beyond the set logic of satellite-based navigation. In this paper, the AI-based navigation methodologies will be exposed to deep analysis, which relies upon an assessment by the computer vision of the perception, flight control, path planning, and obstacle avoidance. The analysis system is based on the key performance indicators: accuracy of object detection, flight stability, accuracy of navigation decisions, latency and safety reliability. ConvNets and vision transformers are the deep learning architectures that have been discovered in object detection in real-time, depth estimation, and scene understanding. Based on the experiment findings, accuracy of the mean object detection within the above 95percent range of precision and recall values has been obtained both indoors and outdoors. The combination of visual perception with inertial and depth sensing with fusion adaptive sensor technology has enhanced the localization of nearly 35% drift when worse off than the approach that is based on vision alone. The system flight stability demonstrates the improvement of 30 percent in attitude control uniformity in case of dynamic movement of the drone. Although the autonomous decision-making and path planning modules are guided by a series of AI algorithms in relation to any moving obstacles or other environmental limitations, the navigation decision accuracy of 94.1% is achieved, with an average inference latency of 38 milliseconds, and this system can perform under the real-time operation capability. Dynamic obstacle avoidance maintains safety even in cluttered and unpredictable conditions in an astonishing 94.3% of the engagements. Trajectory optimization in experimental proof features have reported that the time to complete a mission has been reduced by 22 percent by the achievement of a smoother and energetically deep path. All these findings meant that AI-incorporated vision enhanced significantly the quality of functioning in the area of exploration of an autonomous drone system in action. The study includes a vivid critique of the issues presented in the context of scaling, computing efficiency, and uncertainty management, whereby simultaneous recommendations on how to improve the situation were put forward in further work of establishing safe, and sound operation of unmanned aerial vehicles. These appraisals will play an important role in the future research which will see the utilization of Intelligent UAV Navigation Systems in complex and real world scenarios. © 2026 IEEE.
