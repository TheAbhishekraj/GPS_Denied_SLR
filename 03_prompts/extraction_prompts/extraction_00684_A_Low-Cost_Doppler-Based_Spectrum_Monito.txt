TITLE: A Low-Cost Doppler-Based Spectrum Monitoring and Interference Localization System for Public Safety in GPS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: Huang C.H.
YEAR: 2026
VENUE: Journal of Aeronautics, Astronautics and Aviation
DOI: 10.6125/JoAAA.202604_58(4).01
ABSTRACT: Global navigation satellite systems (GNSS), such as global positioning system (GPS), are widely used for unmanned aerial system (UAS) navigation; however, GNSS signals are vulnerable to blockage, jamming, and spoofing in disaster response, critical infrastructure protection, and conflict scenarios, posing significant public safety risks. This study proposes a low-cost Doppler-effect-based spectrum monitoring and interference localization system for operation in GPS-denied environments. Unlike conventional multi-antenna direction-finding approaches, the proposed system employs a single receiver integrated with a mechanically rotatable platform to perform three-dimensional azimuth (360°) and elevation (90°) scanning. Signal Doppler frequency (SDF), Received signal strength indicator (RSSI), and Signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) are jointly analyzed, while a Savitzky-Golay (SG) filter is applied to suppress environmental noise and enhance frequency-shift observability. In addition, Binary phase-shift keying (BPSK) and Quadrature phase-shift keying (QPSK) features are utilized to improve Doppler-based interference discrimination. Experimental results demonstrate real-time estimation of signal source azimuth and elevation under GPS-denied conditions. Beyond UAV tracking and recovery, the proposed platform enables early warning and spectrum occupancy monitoring in restricted or regulated areas, highlighting its applicability to public safety and defense-related applications. © 2026, The Aeronautical and Astronautical Society of the Republic of China.
