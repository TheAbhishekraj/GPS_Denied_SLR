ABSTRACT: Advancements in computational power and artificial intelligence have facilitated the development of autonomous vehicles and robots. Reliable navigation systems are essential for mobile robots, but traditional map-based methods face challenges in dynamic indoor environments. This study explores mapless navigation using Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) algorithms, comparing them in terms of sample efficiency, cumulative reward, and reward-shaping strategies. A simulated environment was developed using Webots, OpenAI Gym, and StableBaselines3 to conduct three experiments using different reward functions and action spaces. Results indicate that DRL algorithms with discrete action spaces consistently outperform those with continuous action spaces. Among them, Deep Q-Network exhibits superior performance and sample efficiency. The findings highlight the significance of appropriate reward function design and action space selection for optimal performance. This study contributes to developing efficient mapless navigation policies for indoor scenarios, with applications in warehouse automation, domestic service robots, and social environments.
