TITLE: Stereo Vision SLAM for Autonomous Uncrewed Surface Vessels in GPS-Denied and Uncharted Ports
AUTHORS: N. P. Santos; V. T. Viegas; V. Lobo

VENUE: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT
DOI: 10.1109/OCEANS66983.2026.11617078
ABSTRACT: Uncrewed Surface Vessels (USVs) are increasingly employed as force multipliers in naval operations, but their autonomy depends on reliable navigation in Global Positioning System (GPS)-degraded environments such as cluttered ports. This paper investigates stereo-vision-based Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) as a passive navigation alternative for maritime scenarios. A Robot Operating System 2 (ROS 2)based framework was developed using a high-resolution stereo camera and a custom dataset acquired at the Lisbon Naval Base (Base Naval de Lisboa, BNL). The resulting BNL dataset is divided into two sequences, BNL A and BNL B, based on their complexity. A comparative SLAM evaluation was conducted using Real-Time Appearance-Based Mapping (RTAB-Map) and the ZED Software Development Kit (ZED SDK). Quantitative benchmarking against a GPS reference trajectory used exclusively for evaluation, based on planar (XY) localization error metrics, shows that RTAB-Map consistently outperforms the ZED SDK in localization accuracy. On BNL A, RTAB-Map achieved a mean planar error of 5.36 m and a planar Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of 6.05 m, whereas the ZED SDK yielded a mean planar error of 8.29 m and a planar RMSE of 8.61 m. On the more challenging BNL B sequence, RTAB-Map obtained a mean planar error of 11.20 m and a planar RMSE of 12.00 m, compared to 13.98 m and 15.24 m, respectively, for the ZED SDK. These results indicate that stereo visual SLAM (V-SLAM) can support navigation in GPS-challenged port environments, while highlighting the need for improved robustness over long trajectories and under maritime-specific visual degradations.
