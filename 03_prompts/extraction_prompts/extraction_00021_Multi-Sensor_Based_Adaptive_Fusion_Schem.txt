TITLE: Multi-Sensor Based Adaptive Fusion Scheme for Position Estimation of Multirotor UAV Systems in GPS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: Busse L.; Street L.; Kumar M.
YEAR: 2025
VENUE: Regional Student Conferences, 2025
DOI: 10.2514/6.2025-99640
ABSTRACT: The use of Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) has seen significant growth across a wide range of applications over the past decade. UAVs are now commonly used for outdoor tasks such as surveillance, monitoring, inspection, delivery, and aerial photography, where a Global Positioning System (GPS) is available and in most cases sufficient for localization, as large distances to obstacles make high-precision position estimation less critical. In contrast, indoor environments present a challenge for UAV localization, as GPS signals are unavailable, making reliable positioning a primary concern in such areas. This work focuses on the implementation of a novel Kalman Filter based estimation technique that delivers reliable position information to a coaxial octorotor UAV in GPS-denied environments using inexpensive LIDAR technology. Accurate position estimation of a UAV is a primary challenge for navigation in a GPS-denied environment. Use of environmentally embedded sensors (such as a motion tracking system) is a popular choice. However, their use is not feasible in many applications outside of a controlled environment. The use of onboard sensors for localization is desirable in these conditions and is an active area of research where a variety of onboard sensors, each with its own limitations and capabilities, such as LIDAR, cameras, optical flow, and other proximity sensors have been used. However, developing a robust localization method that works under a variety of operating conditions is still a challenge. The main contribution of this paper is to introduce an Adaptive Kalman Filter (AKF) framework for precise position estimation of a multirotor UAV system in GPS-denied environments solely utilizing LIDAR and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) technology. The LIDAR sensors used in this research include a Unitree 4D LIDAR L1 in combination with a Garmin LIDAR-Lite V3. The system was developed on a Nvidia Jetson Orin Nano and a Cube Orange Flight Controller with PX4 Autopilot software. Point-LIO 3D SLAM was used to map the environment and obtain position information. The Robot Operating System (ROS) was used for data communication between the sensors, the onboard computer, and the flight controller. Lastly, the system and its performance were validated using a high-precision OptiTrack Motion Capture system as a ground truth reference. The Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) analysis demonstrated that the fusion and predictive capabilities of the AKF algorithm significantly improved the accuracy of position estimation of the GPS-denied UAV. © 2025, American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics Inc, AIAA.
