ABSTRACT: This paper presents a structured survey and comprehensive synthesis of influential research on autonomous drones, focusing on their development, applications, and performance evaluation. The studies span multiple domains, including medical delivery, agricultural automation, military surveillance, swarm coordination, urban navigation, and last-mile logistics. Techniques such as deep learning, fuzzy logic, reinforcement learning, SLAM, and LLM-based adaptive navigation have been applied and evaluated using both simulations and field deployments. Representative parameters include navigation accuracy, latency, battery life, payload capacity, flight time, and delivery efficiency. To complement this analysis, we highlight real-world drone deployments such as Zipline (medical logistics), Wing (urban parcel delivery), and Matternet (hospital supply chains), thereby linking experimental findings to operational viability. Emerging trends include the rise of AI-driven autonomy, integration with mobile and cloud applications, GPS-denied navigation, and use in fire detection, infrastructure inspection, and healthcare delivery. However, gaps remain in privacy-preserving data usage, AI responsibility, benchmarking standards, and commercial viability assessments. This survey identifies opportunities for future research toward scalable, context-aware, and ethically aligned deployment of autonomous drones.
