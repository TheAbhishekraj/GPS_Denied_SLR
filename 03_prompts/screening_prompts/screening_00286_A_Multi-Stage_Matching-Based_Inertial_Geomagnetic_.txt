TITLE: A Multi-Stage Matching-Based Inertial/Geomagnetic Integrated Navigation Algorithm for UAVs in GNSS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: Y. Cheng; L. Zhang; X. He; H. Tian; D. Han; Y. Lv; C. Fan
YEAR: 2025
ABSTRACT: In near-Earth space, magnetic field information may exhibit similarity across different locations, implying that single-point positioning cannot be achieved merely by using geomagnetic information. However, a correspondence exists between the correlation of geomagnetic fields and the continuity of motion trajectories. Therefore, building upon the initial navigation information provided by inertial navigation systems (INS), high-precision navigation and positioning can be realized by performing correlation matching between real-time measured geomagnetic sequences along the trajectory and pre-stored geomagnetic maps. High-precision navigation without GNSS presents significant challenges, and geomagnetic field-aided inertial navigation serves as one potential solution. The classical Magnetic Contour Matching (MAGCOM) algorithm’s effectiveness depends on its search area, which is typically fixed, resulting in limited algorithm flexibility. Meanwhile, the traditional Iterative Closest Contour Point (ICCP) algorithm demands high sensor performance, exhibits poor noise tolerance, and suffers from computational complexity and inadequate real-time performance. To address these limitations, this paper proposes an inertial/geomagnetic integrated navigation algorithm based on geomagnetic maps. The complete algorithm consists of four key stages: initial position determination, coarse matching, fine matching, and integrated filtering. Firstly, the point on the contour line closest to the INS-indicated starting position is selected as the search center. This approach significantly reduces subsequent computational workload while ensuring matching reliability. During coarse matching, contour constraints are employed to limit the search area, thereby improving matching efficiency. The Mean Absolute Difference (MAD) algorithm serves as the correlation criterion for matching. Although coarse matching reduces the computational burden, residual errors from inertial calculations persist and may degrade positioning accuracy. The fine matching process further constrains inertial navigation error accumulation through additional contour line constraints. The refined matching results are then input as position observations into a State Transformation Extended Kalman Filter (ST-EKF) to correct system parameters, with the final output representing the algorithm’s ultimate navigation solution. To evaluate the algorithm’s performance, experimental validation was conducted on a UAV platform. The tests were performed along predefined flight paths within a designated area at altitudes of 450 meters, 260 meters, and 70 meters, respectively. The obtained average position estimation errors were 72.0 meters, 25.1 meters, and 21.9 meters, meeting the requirements for typical UAV autonomous navigation and positioning. The proposed algorithm demonstrates superior performance over two classical geomagnetic matching navigation algorithms in most evaluation metrics, offering a viable solution for long-endurance positioning in GNSS-denied environments.
