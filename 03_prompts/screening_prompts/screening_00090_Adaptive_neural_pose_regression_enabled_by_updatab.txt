TITLE: Adaptive neural pose regression enabled by updatable graphics-based digital twin for robust UAV localization in GPS-denied civil infrastructure
AUTHORS: Matiki T.; Narazaki Y.; Chowdhary G.; Spencer B.F., Jr.
YEAR: 2026.0
ABSTRACT: Autonomous inspection of civil structures using unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) offers a safer and more efficient alternative to manual methods; however, in GPS-denied environments, the absence of global positioning makes reliable navigation challenging and requires the use of onboard sensing. Conventional approaches such as Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) estimate motion by tracking local features, but are susceptible to drift accumulation over time, even though loop closure can partially reduce this effect. Recent data-driven methods directly regress UAV poses from images, but their performance depends on large, accurately labeled datasets that are difficult to obtain in practice. To address this limitation, this study presents an updatable Graphics-Based Digital Twin (GBDT) that provides a globally referenced structural prior and generates synthetic image-pose datasets for training Siamese neural pose regressors for absolute pose estimation. The network embeds synthetic GBDT renders and real inspection images into a shared feature space, promoting reliance on geometric structure rather than appearance. To reduce the synthetic-to-real gap, geometric cues are emphasized through preprocessing while appearance variations are suppressed, and a geometric constraint loss enables indirect supervision on real images without ground-truth poses. By regressing poses relative to GBDT template views with known global poses, the method avoids cumulative drift and maintains consistent global alignment. Wireless communication between the UAV edge device and a base station enables real-time inference. Experiments on a custom-built drone demonstrate stable flight and improved global localization compared to the Intel T265 VIO-SLAM and RTAB-Map. As the GBDT is incrementally updated using inspection imagery, localization accuracy improves over successive deployments, enabling increasingly reliable autonomous UAV-based monitoring. Code is available at https://github.com/tmatiki2/GBDTpose/tree/main. © 2026
