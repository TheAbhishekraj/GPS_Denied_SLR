TITLE: Inertial-based Autonomous Navigation and Gravity Measurement System assisted by LDV
AUTHORS: G. Chunfeng; M. Haiyang; Y. Xudong; X. Pengbo; L. Yiming; W. Guo
YEAR: 2025
ABSTRACT: To address the challenges posed by limited positioning and gravity detection accuracy in environments where GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System)is obstructed, such as mountains, jungles, and canyons, as well as situations where satellite signals are denied, this paper improves the quartz flexible accelerometer measurement module of the traditional laser gyro rotary modulation inertial navigation system. A LDV (Laser Doppler Velocimeter) with a speed measurement accuracy better than 0.05 per mileage is introduced to assist the inertial navigation system in navigation. A temperature control module for the acceleration measurement unit with a precision better than 0.05 °C has been developed, and research on the attitude fluctuation compensation technology of the rotating shaft system has been carried out to ensure the high-precision output of the attitude, velocity and position of the inertial navigation system. The integrated system can use the carrier attitude information provided by the IMU, the specific force information provided by the gravity sensor and the carrier velocity information provided by the laser velocimeter to calculate the spatial gravity anomaly in real time. The results of vehicle-mounted experiments show that the positioning error of the integrated system in a 30 km vehicle test is less than 10 m, and the internal consistency accuracy of vehicle-mounted dynamic gravity measurement reaches 0.70mGal, realizing long-endurance navigation and positioning and dynamic continuous measurement of gravity information without relying on GNSS at all. The relevant achievements can be used for autonomous navigation and positioning under GNSS-denied conditions, and can effectively realize the refined construction of the local Earth's gravity field.
