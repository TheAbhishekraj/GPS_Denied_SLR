TITLE: Multi-Sensor Fusion SLAM for Interceptor UAVs in GNSS-Denied Environments: A Review
AUTHORS: Liu B.; Shi J.; Gong Y.; Li C.; Li H.
YEAR: 2026.0
ABSTRACT: With the rapid development of interceptor unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), they have become core equipment for counter-UAV missions. Interceptor UAVs exhibit high dynamic performance and high-speed maneuverability, yet maintaining stable operation in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-denied environments remains a major challenge. Conventional simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) systems are extremely susceptible to motion distortion, which can lead to system failures. This paper presents an in-depth and systematic overview of multi-sensor fusion SLAM technologies for interceptor UAVs. Traditional SLAM and learning-based SLAM methods are categorized and summarized according to sensor types and the adoption of learning techniques, and their performance and limitations under interception scenarios including high-speed flight, maneuvering flight, and alternating low-and-high altitude flight are analyzed. This review identifies key technical gaps in existing studies, including discrepancies between simulation and real-world scenarios, limited onboard computing resources, and poor model interpretability. Furthermore, it outlines promising future research directions for interceptor UAV applications, such as learning-based cooperative SLAM and distributed swarm intelligence-based SLAM. This review aims to provide comprehensive research references for researchers and engineers, and facilitate the deployment and implementation of full-scene, high-precision and high-reliability SLAM technologies on new-generation interceptor UAVs. © 2001-2012 IEEE.
