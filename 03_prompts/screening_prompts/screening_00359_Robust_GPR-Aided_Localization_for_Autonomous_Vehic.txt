TITLE: Robust GPR-Aided Localization for Autonomous Vehicles in GPS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: F. M. Bahr; M. Stolz; A. Elashry; D. Watzenig
YEAR: 2025
ABSTRACT: Accurate localization in GPS-denied and perceptually degraded environments remains a critical challenge for autonomous systems. This paper presents a robust sensor fusion framework that integrates Ground Penetrating Radar (GPR) to exploit subsurface features as stable, environment-invariant signatures for navigation. The core of our approach is a sophisticated GPR processing pipeline that enhances standard geo-physical techniques including time-zero correction, background removal, and migration to render raw radar data suitable for high-precision localization. This enables our Correlation 1D Matching algorithm, which robustly correlates processed live GPR traces with a pre-constructed reference map. The GPR-derived position estimates are fused with Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) and wheel encoder data within an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) framework to achieve drift-corrected navigation. Experimental validation on the public CMU-GPR dataset across three distinct real-world environments demonstrates consistent sub-meter localization accuracy, achieving a Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) as low as 0.50 m. Our method achieves an order-of-magnitude improvement over a drift-prone inertial-odometry baseline, providing reliable, all-weather navigation for challenging underground settings.
