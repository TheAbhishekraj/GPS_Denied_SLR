Abstract—This paper proposes a homography-based visual
servo control approach for the stabilization of underactuated un-
manned aerial vehicles in GPS-denied environments. A virtual
homographymatrixisproposed,andanewvisualerrorvectorisde-
ﬁnedtoobtainthepassivity-likevisualerrorequation.Inaddition,a
homography-based online translation velocity estimator and a non-
linear backstepping visual servo control approach are addressed to
promote fast maneuvering without linear velocity feedback. Unlike
conventional homography-based visual servo control solutions that
only guarantee the local stability in the equilibrium, the proposed
controlschemecangettheglobalstabilityinlargeoffset.Simulation
and experimental tests with a realistic quadrotor subject to the
measurement noise are carried out to verify the robustness and
performance of the control approach. In addition, the autonomous
tracking experiment when the target speed is unpredictable are
also reported.

rich environmental information. It has the advantages of being
small in size and lightweight and is very easy to integrate into

Generally speaking, the use of vision to assist drones to
operate autonomously in GPS-denined environments can be di-
vided into vision-based state estimation and visual servo control.
Vision-based state estimation uses computer vision methods to
obtain the UAV’s pose, velocity, and other states. Its advantage
is that it is not coupled with the control loop and is suitable for
long-distance application scenarios such as autonomous navi-
gation. Typical vision-based state estimation methods include
vision-inertial odometry (VIO) [7]–[9], optical ﬂow [10], [11],
and so on [12]. The visual servo control directly inputs image
characteristics or reconstructed pose information to the control
loop, which is suitable for small-scale application scenarios
such as self-stabilizing hovering and precise positioning. Since
the scope of this paper is on visual servo control, we will
focus on reviewing the existing visual servo control methods
for underactuated UAVs.
Using feedback features to classify the various visual servo
control of UAVs is straightforward. There are two main ap-
proaches: position-based visual servoing (PBVS) and image-
based visual servoing (IBVS). PBVS uses the reconstructed
pose as the feedback feature and designs the control law in
Cartesian space, which has the advantages of a large conver-
gence range [13], [14]. However, it depends on the accuracy
of the camera calibration and the precise geometric model of
the target. IBVS uses visual features (such as points, lines,
image moments, etc.) as feedback input into the control loop,
without the need to reconstruct poses, but it suffers two key
problems. Firstly, the depth of each visual feature is independent

which effectively improves the convergence rate in the vertical

the visual servo control of other composite UAVs, such as aerial
manipulators [14], [18]. These approaches do not require an
accurate geometric target model or a well-calibrated camera,

in the system dynamics, it will lead to complex nonlinear control
problems. The second is the virtual camera approach ﬁrst men-
tioned in [19]. It obtains the passive-like visual error kinematics
by using perspective image moments, and various methods have
been used to design the control law. For example, backstepping
control [20], adaptive output-feedback control [21], geometric
hierarchical control [22], [23], model predictive control [24]–
[26], learning-based control [27], observer-based control [28],
etc. In particular, the design methods of controllers in [27]
and [28] give us a good inspiration. Although the visual error
equation based on the virtual camera method has the advantages
ofsimpleformandisnotrelatedtoimagedepth,itneedsaccurate
image feature matching, and the loss of features will greatly
affect the stability of the system.
An alternative approach is the homography-based visual ser-
voing (HBVS), commonly used for fully actuated systems [29]–
[31]. The homography matrix relates the planar target in the
camera frame as seen from two conﬁgurations of the camera.
Various approaches can be used to obtain the homography
matrix, i.e., points and lines features matching based [33],
intensity based [34] and learning based [35]. Although the
homography matrix is represented by the rotation matrix, dis-
placement, normal vector, etc., it is very challenging to decouple
these geometric parameters because of the existence of multiple
solutions [36]. Therefore, there are few works focused on the use
of HBVS on the under-actuated system. A homography-based
visual servoing of a ﬁxed-wing UAV for automatic approaching
and landing is proposed in [37], but system dynamics were
not considered and stability analysis was lacking. In [36], a
gyrometer-aided HBVS solution was proposed for underactu-
ated UAVs. It can achieve stable hovering without linear velocity
measurement. However, only local stability is proved based on
Lyapunov analysis. No matter whether it is a fully actuated
or an underactuated system, there is very few work that can
prove the global stability of the HBVS control, especially when
considering the system dynamics. Only [30] and [31] proved al-
most global asymptotic stability, but for an almost fully actuated
autonomous underwater vehicles.
This study is consistent with these efforts. Compared with
the existing HBVS solutions, an important original result of the
proposed HBVS control approach is to obtain a passive-like
error equation by deﬁning a new virtual homography matrix.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section II,
the system modeling and problem formulation are given. In
Section III, we ﬁrst state the existing HBVS control for fully
actuated vehicles, and then we propose a novel HBVS control
approach for underactuated UAVs with an online linear velocity

the performance and robustness of the proposed approach. In
Section V, various experimental results such as reaching a
stationary target and tracking of a moving target on a quadrotor
UAV are provided. Finally, we present some conclusions and an
overview of possible future works.

VI. CONCLUSION
We propose a homography-based visual servo control ap-
proach for the stabilization of underactuated unmanned aerial
vehicles in GPS-denied environments. By deﬁning a virtual ho-
mography matrix, a passive-like error equation can be obtained.
A backstepping-based control approach that uses full dynamics
to estimate its translation velocity online is proposed. The con-
troller can ensure global asymptotic stability. Two simulation
experiments under the condition of an ideal and a slop target
plane with noise are tested to verify the performance of the
proposed HBVS control. The experimental results show that
the proposed scheme can not only achieve fast stabilization
to the target pose, but also can effectively track the unknown
moving target. In addition, using homography as a feedback
feature can effectively reduce the constraints on the target in the
ﬁeld of view, thereby increasing the robustness of the system.
In spite of all these improvements, there are still some problems
worthy of improvement or analysis. One problem that may need
to be analyzed is that when the target is in unknown motion,
the stability of the system is difﬁcult to guarantee. Although the
robust follow-up of the moving target is achieved in this article,
it lacks rigorous theoretical analysis. In addition, considering
the stability control method under the angular dynamics of the
UAVs is a direction for further research.
