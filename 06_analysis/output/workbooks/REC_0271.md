Abstract — Autonomous navigation of unmanned vehicles in 
GPS-denied environments is a challenging problem, especially 
for small ground vehicles and micro aerial vehicles (MAVs) 
which are characterized by their small payload, short battery 
lifetime and limited processing resources. Stereo vision 
positioning has been introduced as a scale-free positioning 
technique, but it is computationally expensive. Monocular vision 
systems aided by inertial measurement unit (IMU) are more 
computationally efficient but it suffers from IMU random biases 
and scale errors. In this paper, we propose a hybrid visual-
inertial odometry solution that minimizes the computation load 
by dividing the mission into two interchangeable stages. Firstly, 
a stereo vision stage in which a loosely coupled integration 
between stereo cameras and IMU is performed. In this stage, an 
extended Kalman filter (EKF) is used to automatically and 
dynamically estimate IMU biases. Once the IMU is calibrated, a 
monocular stage is activated where the system is downgraded 
into single camera getting the motion scale from the calibrated 
IMU. The proposed solution has been tested using the popular 
IMU-enabled ZED-Mini tracking camera. We compared our 
stereo vision solution against the IMU-aided monocular solution 
and the results showed accurate positioning with the advantage 
of less computation. Further analysis is provided where we 
compared our solution with the built-in solutions of the ZED 
Mini camera and the Intel Realsense T265 tracking camera. 
Keywords—Visual Odometry, Inertial Navigation Systems 
(INS), Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), Sensor Fusion, 
Autonomous Navigation. 
I. 
INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND
Having unmanned small vehicles with the ability of 
autonomously navigate the world became a must in a wide 
spectrum of applications, from security surveillance, boarder 
control, building inspection, film making, to even space 
missions and planet exploration and the application list is 
getting larger by the day. However, the nature of these 
applications imposes many constraints on the navigation 
solutions in terms of payload, battery and processing power 
yet, these constraints goes tighter for the small-scale vehicles. 
Navigation becomes a more complex challenge under 
unavailability of space-based Global Navigation Satellite 
Systems (GNSS) [1]. From these constraints came the need 
to use small and power efficient sensors like inertial 
measurement units (IMUs) and cameras [2] [3]. IMUs and 
cameras have their own limitations. However, IMU and 
cameras have complementary characteristics that make the 
integration of the two sensors a promising direction.  
IMU is an important sensor used in navigation applications 
where full 3D position, velocity, and orientation is calculated 
incrementally using dead-reckoning [4]. Dead-reckoning 
suffers from large drifts that grow overtime due to IMU 
stochastic errors and biases. Therefore, the standard approach 
is to use a low cost Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems 
(MEMS) IMU fused with other positioning sensors to control 
IMU drifts and to provide precise positioning at affordable 
cost.  Practically, to make IMU measurements usable, an 
accurate estimation of IMU stochastic errors must be 
performed.  Traditionally, GNSS has been the common 
choice to fuse with IMUs [1] [4]. However, GNSS may not 
be available in all scenarios and, hence, integration of IMU 
with other sensors becomes necessary. 
Camera-based solution is one of the best replacements of 
GNSS for its availability, low cost and small size and weight. 
Moreover, IMUs provide complementary characteristics to 
vision sensors in the form of the higher input rates and higher 
accuracy to track rotations at high dynamics, which made it 
better choice for estimating fast motions. Many recent 
researches tested visual inertial integration which delivered 
promising results [5] [6]. However, visual processing is still 
a computational bottleneck that affects the practical 
application of visual inertial tracking in small-size battery-
powered unmanned vehicles.  
Estimating motion from images is known as ego motion 
estimation [7]. The process of estimating the camera ego 
motion from its input frame sequence [8] is a special case of 
“structure from motion” and this process could be referred to 
by the term Visual Odometry (VO). VO is implemented by 
tracking the motion of visually recognized features in image 
sequences. VO solutions can be classified into two main 
common groups [9]; monocular methods and stereo vision 
methods. In monocular visual odometry algorithms [10], use 
a single camera feed is used to estimate the new camera 
position and orientation. The weakness of this class of 
solutions is that a single camera can only estimate the 
translation and rotation between two image frames up to an 
unknown scale. This scale is necessary to transform the 
estimated camera motion to physical real-world motion. 
Consequently, monocular visual odometry are always 
integrated with other sensors like IMU [11] to estimate this 
unknown scale and to provide real-world positioning. 
However, uncalibrated IMU with large stochastic noise and 
errors make the precise estimation of this unknown scale a 
challenging problem.  
In stereo visual odometry algorithms [7] [12], the visual 
feature physical depth in the real-world is directly measured 
by triangulation using images from two or more cameras and 
then construct the camera motion in physical real-world 
domain. Although stereo vision does not suffer from scale 
ambiguity, the stereo vision processing is more expensive as 
it processes two input video streams which consume larger 
memory and power leading to shorter battery lifetime and 
limited operating range. 
Even with the availability of stereo camera onboard, having 
a monocular vision-based navigation solution is more

In this paper, we address the accuracy and computation 
challenges of visual inertial odometry by proposing a hybrid 
and adaptive fusion scheme that minimizes the processing 
requirements by dividing the navigation mission into two 
interchangeable phases. The core fusion algorithm used in 
this work is the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) which is 
widely used for its simple implementation and suitability to 
real-time applications [2] [13]. The proposed system operates 
through two phases. Firstly, A stereo vision-based navigation 
phase in which a loosely coupled integration between the 
camera and IMU solution is performed where the vision 
solution used as an update to the EKF to estimate the INS 
errors. The second phase is a monocular phase where we 
downgrade our vision system into only one camera-based 
system with minimum computations and get the motion scale 
from the calibrated INS solution. The system has been 
realized and tested using the ZED-Mini camera tracking 
system and results showed that the proposed hybrid adaptive 
fusion scheme can provide accuracy that is comparable to full 
stereo vision tracking with the advantage of less 
computations. 
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II 
describes the proposed solution. In section III we present the 
experimental work. While section 5 is devoted to the 
experimental results. Section IV concludes the paper. 
II. PROPOSED SOLUTION
The proposed fusion scheme benefits from the accuracy and 
scale-free positioning estimate of stereo vision-based system 
to update a full 3D IMU positioning solution within an EKF 
module that considers the vehicle’s non-holonomic 
constrains. Stereo mode is activated in the beginning until a 
reliable accurate estimate of the IMU errors is obtained and 
stable EKF convergence is achieved. Then, the system 
switches to IMU-aided monocular mode benefitting from its 
lower memory and computation requirements. Motion scale 
generated by the IMU is tested every few seconds and when 
the error in the motion scale goes beyond a certain threshold 
the system switches back to the stereo mode and feed its 
estimation to the EKF to re-estimate IMU biases. 
The implemented EKF uses a full 3D IMU dynamic model 
[14] to generate its predicted trajectory estimation of the
position, velocity and orientation of the moving platform at a
rate of 100Hz. At a lower rate (15 Hz) a stereo vison system
that processes images sequence from couple of cameras is
developed. The developed stereo vision uses SOFT SLAM
library [5]. While using the SOFT estimation as a position
update, EKF convergence is watched. Once converged, the
system automatically switches to the monocular mode using
the calibrated IMU position prediction (aided by non-
holonomic constraints) to generate the motion scale leading
to a full 3D monocular visual-inertial solution that is scale-
free. Switching to monocular mode frees extra resources for 
the other tasks like motion planning or mapping. The 
Flowchart of the proposed VIO system is shown in Figure 1. 
To sustain the accuracy of the system, the following 
mechanism could be implemented. Every few seconds, we 
switch on the stereo SOFT odometry solution and test the 
EKF estimated motion scale against the SOFT odometry 
solution for one iteration. If there is large discrepancy 
between the two estimations, the system automatically 
switches off the monocular estimation and feed the SOFT 
odometry updates to the EKF to re-converge and re-estimate 
the IMU biases. The remaining of this section is devoted for 
describing both SOFT stereo VO and the monocular VO, we 
refer the reader to [15]  for more detailed explanation of the 
EKF implementation.

A. SOFT Visual Odometry System
For the stereo visual odometry stage, we used the SOFT
odometry method [5], which ranks as one of the highest 
accuracy visual odometry solution on KITTI vision 
benchmark suite [16]. The name SOFT stands for Stereo 
Odometry algorithm relying on Feature Tracking [17]. As can 
be seen in the proposed solution in Figure 1, the estimated 
change of position and orientation estimated from the SOFT 
is fed to the EKF as an update to optimally estimate the IMU 
biases and errors. The main drive that motivated every 
decision in designing the SOFT odometry algorithm is 
enabling real-time performance and optimizing the 
processing requirements. The algorithm is composed of four 
primary stages as shown Figure 2. The first stage is the 
feature detection and selection in which the idea of bucketing 
is applied. Bucketing technique divides the image into 50x50 
pixel-sized buckets. In every bucket, corner and blob masks 
are applied followed by non-maximum suppression step to 
have four class lists of carefully selected strong features. 
Then, these features go through circular matching process in 
which the features are tested if they are matched in two 
consecutive frames. Only the features that passes this test is 
taken into account. Using this small subset of the image 
features reduces the computational time for all the following 
stages.

With the feature first appearance a unique identifier is 
assigned for it and the age is initialized with zero. In each 
iteration the position of the feature point is refined on a 
subpixel level with respect to the feature initial position, 
using the same descriptor during the entire feature lifetime 
reduces the drift. The third stage is the rotation estimation. In 
this stage, the feed from the left camera is used as an input to 
the RANSAC 5 points module [5] [9] to estimate the rotation 
matrix then apply the spherical linear interpolation (SLERP) 
step that refines the rotation estimation. Finally, the 
translation estimation stage is implemented. Having all the 
correspondences determined, the 3D point cloud estimation 
of the previous view is projected into the image plane of the 
current view, and then the translation is calculated iteratively 
by minimizing a cost function.

IV. 
CONCLUSION 
In this paper, we presented a system that fuses both visual 
and inertial sensors in a hybrid and adaptive scheme. The 
solution uses a high precision visual odometry algorithm 
(SOFT) as an update for the EKF to have an accurate 
estimation of IMU biases. Then the system minimizes the 
processing power requirements by switching to a monocular 
visual odometry mode, which frees resources for the 
additional tasks. The presented solution could help in 
navigating unknown environments and the obtained accuracy 
encourages an embedded implementation targeting small 
autonomous battery-operated vehicles.  Our trajectory 
estimation was compared to the soft odometry estimation, 
ZED mini and Intel Realsense T265 tracking solutions and 
for 50 seconds update outages, the overall position estimation 
error was less than 2m.
