A Graph-Optimization-Based Tightly Coupled Multi-Source Positioning 
Method for UAVs in GNSS-Denied Environments
Abstract
To address UAV positioning in GNSS-denied environments, this study proposes a tightly coupled 
multi-source positioning method that integrates IMU, vision, and UWB. First, a structured visual 
feature optimization method is introduced to enhance feature uniformity and robustness through 
adaptive thresholding, density balancing, and the fusion of grayscale-gradient information. Second, 
a graph-optimization-based tightly coupled framework is constructed, incorporating UWB-IMU 
joint initialization and UWB differential residual–enhanced global constraints. By combining an 
adaptive sliding window with an IMU pre-integration compensation mechanism, the framework 
balances accuracy and real-time performance while mitigating temporal asynchrony issues. 
Experimental validation shows that the optimized visual features achieve an 11.8% increase in ORB 
matched points and a 5% improvement in accuracy compared with ORB, while demonstrating 
significantly better distribution uniformity than both SIFT and ORB. Finally, tests on the EuRoC 
dataset reveal that the proposed positioning method achieves an RMSE below 0.047 m across five 
sequences, with MAE reduced by 80.9% and 21.9% compared with VINS-Fusion and ORB-SLAM3, 
respectively. In real flight experiments, the method achieves an RMSE of 0.480 m under high-
dynamic conditions and 0.286 m under steady-state conditions, validating its sub-meter accuracy 
and applicability in real-world environments.
Keywords: Denied Environment; Unmanned Aerial Vehicle; Positioning; Multi-Sensor Fusion
1 Introduction
In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have seen widespread application in military 
reconnaissance, disaster relief, and other fields. The success of their autonomous flight and mission 
execution is contingent upon the utilization of precise and reliable positioning systems. Presently, 
Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) function as the predominant positioning methodology 
for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). However, in complex electromagnetic environments or 
adversarial scenarios, GNSS signals are highly susceptible to blockage, interference, or spoofing 
attacks. This hinders UAVs from acquiring precise positional data, jeopardizing flight safety and 
exerting a substantial negative influence on mission success rates. Consequently, achieving high-
precision positioning for UAVs in GNSS-denied environments has become a critical technology 
and research focus for ensuring safe flight operations and mission success.
Visual localization has been demonstrated to be an effective positioning method for UAVs in 
GNSS-denied environments. For instance, SLAM facilitates high-precision positioning in small-

employed for long-range, wide-view scenarios. This approach utilizes a global estimation method 
that involves the comparison of real-time imagery with terrain databases [4-5]. The accuracy of 
visual positioning is contingent upon the precision of feature matching and relative pose estimation 
between successive frames in the front-end processing. This is imperative for the suppression of 
positioning drift. Common front-end methods include feature point-based approaches, optical flow-
based approaches, direct methods, and deep learning-based methods [6-8]. Optical flow methods 
are predicated on the assumption of constant brightness, thereby enabling the tracking of pixel 
motion. For instance, SVO2.0 [9-10] integrates sparse direct methods with local optimization to 
enhance stability in large-scale scenes. However, both the optical flow and direct methods are 
sensitive to luminance variations and are prone to failure due to noise, blurring, and abrupt lighting 
changes. Deep learning-based approaches, exemplified by DeepVO[11] and TartanVO[12], employ 
convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to learn motion from image sequences. However, these 
approaches are heavily reliant on substantial datasets and substantial computational resources, 
which compromises their effectiveness in real-time operations on unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). 
Consequently, feature-based methods remain the predominant front-end approach for UAV visual 
localization.
Feature point methods are a class of algorithms that estimate the motion state of a carrier by 
extracting feature points from consecutive frames and matching them. This methodology has been 
demonstrated to extract feature points that are resistant to rotation and translation, thereby ensuring 
a high degree of robustness. Among the various visual localization algorithms grounded in feature 
point methods, ORB-SLAM [13] is a noteworthy exemplar. This system establishes a 
comprehensive monocular real-time localization framework by integrating loop detection and graph 
optimization techniques based on ORB features. Its successor, ORB-SLAM2 [14], extends support 
to stereo and RGB-D inputs, further broadening the system's applicability. However, the mission 
areas of drone operations frequently encompass low-texture scenes, such as sandy terrains, fields, 
and water surfaces. This phenomenon results in the presence of sparse feature points, which, in turn, 
leads to challenges in matching. Consequently, the continuity and robustness of trajectory 
estimation are severely compromised. Concurrently, the coexistence of bright skies and shadowed 
surfaces in images engenders local grayscale distortion, thereby significantly degrading the 
matching accuracy of descriptors based on grayscale difference encoding. Therefore, enhancing 
feature perception capabilities in low-texture environments and improving descriptor robustness 
under dynamic lighting conditions are critical for achieving high-precision visual positioning in 
UAVs.
Given the visual-only positioning's inability to meet high-precision requirements under highly 
dynamic conditions, multi-source fusion positioning is currently the preferred approach. Inertial 
measurement units (IMUs) are capable of providing high-frequency, continuous attitude

The present paper endeavors to address the challenge of high-precision positioning for UAVs 
in GNSS-denied environments. To this end, a multi-source positioning method has been designed 
that integrates IMU, visual, and UWB information. For the visual component, the proposal calls for 
an optimized structured visual feature approach that effectively mitigates the susceptibility of visual 
observations to low-texture repetitive structures and dynamic lighting variations. In light of the 
substantial observational discrepancies among IMU, visual, and UWB sensors, this paper puts forth 
a graph-optimization-based positioning method that integrates IMU/visual/UWB data. This method 
unifies the integration of visual reprojection residuals, IMU pre-integration, and UWB ranging 
information. Within the framework of a factor graph optimization model, a joint optimization model 
incorporating multi-source residual terms is constructed to achieve tightly coupled state estimation 
of visual, inertial, and ranging information.

3.2 Optimized Factor Diagram Modeling Method Under Multi-Source Observation 
Constraints
Factor graphs, which serve as graphical representations of optimization, express 
dependencies among state variables and residual constraints from sensor observations. They 
offer excellent scalability and interpretability. Fig. 6 shows the factor graph structure used in 
this paper, in which system states are represented by variable nodes. Adjacent states are 
connected via IMU pre-integration factors, forming continuous temporal constraints. The UWB 
ranging factor and visual reprojection factor serve as external observation constraints, providing 
global positioning and reprojection information, respectively. The UWB factor calculates 
residuals based on the Euclidean distance between the current frame and multiple base stations. 
This achieves fusion by integrating ranging observations with weights. However, due to 
temporal offsets between ranging data and visual keyframes, ranging data is usually associated 
with the nearest keyframe.

This paper uses a sliding window strategy to estimate and fuse initial conditions. During the 
initialization phase, static initialization is first performed based on multi-frame uwbranging. 
Then, velocity and attitude estimation is performed via IMU integration. Finally, keyframe 
states are progressively constructed. Convergence of the initial state estimation requires 
satisfying two sets of conditions. First, state convergence requires accumulating at least ten 
valid observations within the sliding window to achieve attitude estimation convergence (with 
the quaternion model length approaching one and an error below 0.05), maintaining stable 
ranging residuals within 0.2 m for five consecutive frames, and stabilizing the IMU bias. 
Second, visual activation requires the successful detection of at least one pair of co-viewed 
keyframes with stable feature matching counts that exceed the threshold. Once these conditions 
are met, the system transitions to backend graph optimization. The graph structure is 
constructed from the initial state, and inertial, odometry, and visual factors are progressively 
incorporated to achieve tri-modal joint estimation.

6 Conlusion
(1) A multi-source fusion positioning method integrating IMU, visual modules, and UWB 
ranging modules is proposed. This approach fully leverages the complementary advantages of the 
three sensor types across temporal-frequency, error characteristics, and spatial constraint 
dimensions: IMUs offer high frequency and excellent short-term stability; visual modules possess 
strong environmental perception capabilities and are well-suited for relative motion observation; 
UWB signals feature low energy density, broad spectrum coverage, strong anti-interference 
capabilities, and can provide global high-precision positioning constraints.
(2) A structured visual feature optimization method is proposed. By employing a locally 
texture-aware adaptive FAST detection mechanism and a density-driven spatial balancing strategy, 
while integrating gradient and grayscale information in descriptor construction, CLAHE 
enhancement and Top-K direction sparse coding are introduced. This enhances the spatial 
distribution balance and directional robustness of feature points, thereby improving the accuracy 
and stability of visual observations during UAV positioning.
(3) A graph optimization positioning method integrating IMU/vision/UWB is proposed. An 
UWB-IMU initial state estimation strategy based on extended Kalman filtering is adopted, 
enhancing the system's convergence speed and accuracy during high-dynamic initial phases. Within 
the graph optimization framework, a UWB differential ranging residual factor and an inter-frame 
state compensation mechanism based on IMU pre-integration were designed to enhance the system's 
global constraint capability and observation time synchronization. Adaptive sliding window 
scheduling further improves real-time performance and optimization efficiency. Unified modeling 
and tightly coupled joint estimation of multi-source information were achieved, effectively boosting 
the positioning performance of UAVs in anti-access environments.
