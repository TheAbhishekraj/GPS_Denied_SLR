Cooperative Search Area Optimization using Multiple
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles in a GPS-denied Environment
Sohum Misra†, Srijanee Biswas†, Ali A. Minai
∗, Rajnikant Sharma†
Abstract— This paper addresses the problem of
maximizing surveillance area coverage using multiple
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in an obstacle-
laden and Global Positioning System (GPS)-denied
environment. The UAVs should achieve this objec-
tive using the shortest possible routes while staying
inside the designated search space and avoiding the
obstacles. To attain a desired area coverage, we divide
the NP-hard multi-objective optimization problem of
planning optimal routes for all UAVs into 3 parts:
(a) optimizing search area coverage, (b) performing
obstacle avoidance, and (c) using Cooperative Local-
ization (CL) for state estimation. We demonstrate the
eﬃciency of our algorithm through extensive simula-
tions.
Index Terms— Unmanned Vehicles; Path Planning;
Multi-cost Optimization; Cooperative Localization;
Obstacle Avoidance
I. Introduction
Multiple UAVs are increasingly being used to coop-
eratively accomplish tasks such as search and surveil-
lance [1]–[3]. They are especially helpful in situations
that pose physical and logistical challenges for humans,
such as border patrolling [4], topographical area surveil-
lance, reconnaissance [5]–[7], etc. Most existing local-
ization algorithms, however, rely upon GPS which can
often be intermittent, unreliable or jammed, as in the
case of deployment in hostile regions. The loss of pose
information may result in complete loss of inter-UAV co-
ordination, thereby rendering GPS-dependent algorithms
ineﬀective. Thus, it is essential to develop algorithms
for GPS-denied localization. Such algorithms depend
on available sensor measurements such as range and/or
bearing from points of interest in the environment called
landmarks (LMs). Techniques such as Simultaneous Lo-
calization and Mapping (SLAM) rely on extracting fea-
tures from an unknown environment [8], which might be
challenging at higher mission altitudes. In such cases,
sharing sensor data between multiple UAVs maximizes

the information required for navigation. This is addressed
using CL in [9], [10] with a team of Unmanned Vehi-

LMs in the environment.
Achieving coordination between multiple UAVs can be
diﬃcult due to constraints such as sensing range, number
of UAVs, computational complexity, speed and scalability
of the algorithm. Several authors address problems like
perimeter or cooperative search for ﬁxed-wing UAVs in
an obstacle-dense environment [6], [7], [11]. Schleich et.
al [12] proposed a decentralized area coverage approach
to control a ﬂeet of UAVs. Persistent monitoring using a
single UAV is solved in [13] by minimizing target revisit
time in an obstacle-free environment, which is addressed
in [14] for rectangular search area with grid-like struc-
ture. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Ant Colony
Optimization (ACO) and other evolutionary algorithms
have been used extensively [15]–[17] to address routing of
multiple UAVs. While the precise mathematical or the-
oretical formulation for such algorithms is diﬃcult, they
prove to be advantageous as they are inherently scalable
in comparison to global optimization techniques [18].
The solution quality of PSO improves signiﬁcantly with
computation time [15], [19], i.e., it generates a mono-
tonically decreasing cost. However, during initialization,
it randomly generates the particle positions which can
result in longer time to converge or particle initialization
within obstacles or outside the surveillance region. ACO
is a meta-heuristic based optimization technique that is
inspired by the foraging of ants. Its structure inherently
favors parallelism: the positive feedback loop in its design
expedites the discovery of good solutions and enables
its use in dynamic environments [16], [20]. ACO has
been widely used for solving Traveling Salesman Problem
(TSP) based routing [21], [22]. These approaches, how-
ever, consider each UV visiting a waypoint (WP) exactly
once per tour and no WPs are revisited by any other UV,
i.e., there is no overlap between the paths of multiple
UVs. To the best of our knowledge, none of these tech-
niques address a surveillance area maximization problem
in an obstacle-laden and GPS-denied environment.

In this paper, we propose an eﬀective routing solution
for multiple UAVs ﬂying at a ﬁxed altitude through a
constrained environment such that they can maximize
surveillance over a given region. This approach takes ad-
vantage of the evolutionary algorithms mentioned earlier
and builds upon the method described in [9] for search
area optimization, routing and navigation. Furthermore,
we design a controller using Artiﬁcial Potential Fields
(APFs) [23], [24] to avoid obstacles or danger zones in the
region. APF is simple and elegant, and has low compu-
tation cost compared to consensus-based techniques [25],
[26] and Rapidly exploring Random Trees (RRT) [27].
Unlike algorithms such as RRT, APF can modify routes
on-the-go, i.e., a priori path planning is not required.
The main contributions of this paper are:
• A novel initialization method using a modiﬁed PSO
implemented on a multi-core CPU for faster conver-
gence without violating the feasibility criteria.
• Proposal and comparison of two routing solutions
that provide a strict or fuel-eﬃcient surveillance
scheme while also minimizing path length of each
UAV.
• Ensuring that the routing algorithm works in GPS-
denied environments containing obstacles by com-
bining Cooperative Localization (CL) and obstacle
avoidance techniques
The paper is organized as follows: Section II explains
the system architecture, Section III describes the prob-
lem methodology, associated cost functions and algo-
rithms, Section IV covers the simulation results and
explains the performance of the proposed algorithm.
Section V concludes the paper by summarizing the work
and discussing possible scopes of improvement.
II. System Architecture
Fig. 1 shows the block diagram of the system architec-
ture. The dashed and solid lines denote oﬄine and real-
time computation, respectively. Before the mission, PSO
optimizes and distributes the WPs that the UAVs should
visit for maximizing coverage. Then, ACO assigns a set
of WPs to each UAV and also allocates the shortest paths
between them. The WP and LM positions are stored in
a global map and are used by the controller and the
bearing sensors respectively. The bearing sensors provide
the UAVs with their azimuth bearing information w.r.t
LMs that are located within their Field-of-View (FOV).
This is used by an Estimated Kalman Filter (EKF) to
improve the state estimates due to the motion of each
UAV. The controller uses this and the static obstacle map

to compute a turn rate ωi for each UAV such that they
can reach the next WPs on their respective paths while
avoiding obstacles. The APF controller performance is
subject to tuning.

A. Search Area Optimization
PSO is a population based stochastic optimization al-
gorithm that has been extensively used in the literature.
For brevity, we refrain from mentioning the generalized
concept of PSO, which is elucidated in [19] (Section
III.A). Here, we discuss the particle initialization (Al-
gorithm 1) and cost function deﬁnitions that are unique
to our implementation of PSO.
The performance and convergence rate of PSO is
dependent upon the initialization of particles. To avoid
particle initialization in unfeasible regions, we propose a
novel PSO initialization technique by deﬁning the posi-
tion (X, Y ) of particles using uniform grid initialization
as shown in Algorithm 1. By associating a higher weight
(λ1 in Eqn. 11) to CFenv which represents the feasibility
criterion, we guarantee that it is never violated. The
GCAs are placed in this grid structure such that they
overlap to some extent initially as per user’s choice
(Fig. 2). The PSO can shift these WPs and the over-
lapping regions can change if it helps in maximizing the
overall coverage. Note that our solution for initialization
only improves the initial distribution of WPs in feasible
space without adversely aﬀecting the inner workings of
the algorithm. This is due to the fact that we are not
modifying the method used by PSO for optimization
other than enabling it for parallelization.

A classical PSO updates the global minimum cost any
time a particle in the swarm reaches a solution having
the lowest cost. To enable parallel pooling and cluster
processing, we needed to overcome the interdependence
of the local and global cost at a given time instant
while the algorithm is looping through the number of
particles in the swarm. Saving all local costs and states
independently and updating it at every time step, once
the algorithm loops through every particle in the swarm
enables us to utilize the parallel pooling architecture.
This makes our algorithm scalable and signiﬁcantly
faster without using GPU or Cuda processing techniques.
While this method can potentially lead to a slightly
higher cost per iteration, the inherently random nature of
PSO coupled with the presence of multiple local maxima
and minima in the optimization plane results in it having
no adverse eﬀect on the optimization cost in practice. It
scales linearly on time as a function of available clusters

C. Cooperative Localization for GPS-Denied Navigation
Cooperative Localization is a technique in which the
pose of multiple agents are estimated by sharing infor-
mation from their internal (IMU, encoders, etc.) and
external sensors (e.g., camera, laser, etc.) [9], [10]. Since
our goal is to survey and secure a given region, it is safe
to assume that the coordinates of this region are known.
Therefore, we can place known LMs in the search space.
The UAVs need paths to at least 2 LMs to estimate their
location and orientation. In a GPS-denied environment,
the UAVs can rely upon various sensors to estimate
their pose w.r.t. each other and LMs to uninterruptedly
estimate their states and continue routing through their
desired course. In this work, we assume that the UAVs
can see each other within a given sensing range using
on-board vision sensors and incorporate a bearing-only
CL strategy for state estimation. We use more than 2
LMs to improve the quality of the estimated states. This
strategy is detailed in [9].

V. Conclusion and Future Work
In this paper, we address the problem of maximiz-
ing surveillance using multiple UAVs in a GPS-denied
environment containing obstacles using a combination
of algorithms. To the best of our knowledge, imple-
mentation of Cooperative Localization in tandem with
Artiﬁcial Potential Field has not been addressed before in
literature in order to meet this objective. Two alternative
solutions were proposed based on user’s choice for fuel-
eﬃcient or strict surveillance based routing. We also
showed the performance and robustness of our technique
using simulation results.
This work can be extended in the future for larger
surveillance regions and higher number of UAVs and/or
obstacles. The scalability of the WP placement algorithm
can be established by running multiple Monte Carlo
simulations. Sensing constraints can be imposed such
that a UAV chooses the next WP on its route dynam-
ically so as to keep the graph connected. This way, we
can use observability as an active constraint and ensure
that the conditions for continuous-time observability is
maintained at all times while avoiding obstacles. Obsta-
cle avoidance using the APF technique is sensitive to
tuning and might get stuck in a local minima. Owing
to this, alternate obstacle avoidance techniques can be
explored in the future. We have not benchmarked our
approach against other relevant evolutionary optimiza-
tion algorithms, but this is planned as part of future
research. We also plan to use the observation heatmaps
to actively generate WPs in cooler regions so that the
overall coverage area can be improved.
