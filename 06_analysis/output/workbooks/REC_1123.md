A Novel Scene-Matching Positioning Approach for
Low-Altitude UAVs Considering Elevation
Diﬀerences in Satellite Images
Xiaoguo Zhang , Tianyu Li, Zhengdong Wang, and Yujin Kuang , Graduate Student Member, IEEE
Abstract—The precise localization of uncrewed aerial vehicles
(UAVs) is a critical prerequisite for mission success in smart
urban environments driven by the Internet of Things (IoT).
Traditional Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-based
positioning schemes are vulnerable to signal interference and
spooﬁng attacks, highlighting the importance of visual scene
matching as a robust solution for anti-jamming localization.
However, this technology faces challenges such as sensitiv-
ity to perspective variations and trajectory discontinuities. To
address these limitations, this article presents a novel visual
scene matching-based UAV localization framework. First, a
spatiotemporally constrained candidate map retrieval strategy
is proposed, which enables eﬃcient and accurate candidate
retrieval by coupling single-frame probability estimation with
a hidden Markov model (HMM). Second, a pose recovery
algorithm based on cross-frame elevation diﬀerence constraints is
introduced, and by leveraging feature matches between adjacent
UAV frames and between UAV frames and satellite images,
the elevation information of satellite images is estimated and
2-D–3-D correspondences between UAV images and satellite
images are established. This constructs a 3-D constraint model
that enables the UAV to obtain 3-D coordinates independent of
perspective variations, thereby maintaining localization accuracy
under oblique views. Finally, a factor graph joint optimization
framework is developed to fuse local relative poses with satellite-
based absolute geographic poses, thereby ensuring smooth and
stable localization performance. Extensive experiments on public
and real-world datasets demonstrate that the proposed method
signiﬁcantly improves localization accuracy and validates its
eﬀectiveness in supporting collaborative IoT agent operations in
GNSS-denied environments.
Index Terms—Elevation recovery, factor graph optimization,
geolocalization, Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-
denied, satellite image, uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs).

II. RELATED WORKS
In this section, we divide existing visual geolocalization
methods into two main categories, those based on 2-D RS
images and those that incorporate satellite image elevation.
A critical review is provided to summarize the strengths and
limitations of each category.
A. Visual Geolocalization Based on 2-D RS Images
Achieving visual geolocalization for UAVs in GNSS-denied
environments requires establishing feature correspondences
between UAV images and RS images. Early studies, such
as [20] and [21], used traditional handcrafted features such
as SIFT and HOG to perform UAV localization. Such
approaches work when real-time captured images and exist-
ing images are obtained with the same visual sensors in
most cases; however, due to the large domain gap between
UAV and satellite images—caused by diﬀerences in sensor
modalities, acquisition time, viewing angles, and illumination
conditions—traditional features often fail to establish suﬃ-
cient correspondences, limiting their practical applicability [1],
[22]. In response, recent studies have leveraged deep-learning-
based feature extractors and matchers, such as Superpoint [6],
SuperGlue [23], and LightGlue [7], to obtain suﬃcient and
accurate matching points in heterogeneous image matching
scenarios. For example, wildnav [8] demonstrated a vision-
based UAV geolocalization framework using learned local
features to directly estimate geographic coordinates. Similarly,
Wang et al. [10] introduced an image moment-based method
for selecting aerial photographs suitable for geolocation, eﬀec-
tively ﬁltering out images unsuitable for localization. These
methods leverage deep-learning-based feature extraction and
matching to establish local correspondences between UAV
and satellite images, compute the homography matrix H, and
directly derive the UAV’s geographic coordinates from H.
However, the above methods typically assume that the initial
position of the drone is known in advance, which signiﬁcantly
limits their scalability in real-world applications. To overcome
this constraint, many recent studies [14], [24], [25], [26]
have adopted coarse-to-ﬁne strategies. For example, GLVL
[15] uses a two-stage visual localization framework that ﬁrst
uses a large-scale retrieval module to identify satellite regions
resembling the UAV’s ﬂight scene, followed by a ﬁne-grained
matching module to accurately estimate the UAV’s coordi-
nates, thereby achieving real-time and precise localization.
Similarly, Qiu et al. [24] use the NetVLAD global descriptor
[11] to retrieve visually similar satellite images for a given
UAV image, and then apply SuperPoint and SuperGlue to
extract ﬁne-grained feature correspondences. These correspon-
dences are used in conjunction with the geographic metadata
embedded in the satellite images to localize the UAV. However,
these methods rely on direct retrieval from a large satellite
image database, resulting in unstable retrieval accuracy and
are also prone to errors in locally self-similar scenarios.
In addition, SLAM, a widely adopted technique in robotic
navigation and localization, has also been used in many UAV
localization tasks [27], [28], [29], [30]. However, visual SLAM

B. Visual Geolocalization Incorporating Satellite Image
Elevation
Regardless of whether image retrieval or SLAM-based
methods are used, a major limitation remains: the assumption
of a nadir-viewing UAV camera, which relies solely on 2-D
feature constraints derived from satellite images [8], [14], [15].
However, maintaining a strictly downward camera pose during
real-world UAV ﬂights is often impractical due to complex
environmental conditions and dynamic ﬂights. Under oblique
viewpoints, the lack of elevation information in purely 2-D
satellite features leads to signiﬁcant localization errors. To
address this limitation, several studies [17], [18], [19] have
incorporated terrain data, including DEM and DSM, to recon-
struct 3-D satellite features. This allows 3-D-to-2-D matching
and makes the localization process more robust under oblique
UAV viewing angles. Nevertheless, DEM tends to introduce
large errors in urban scenes due to their low resolution [17],
which undermines their suitability for high-precision localiza-
tion. Although DSM provides high-resolution elevation data,
their reliance on prior data acquisition using UAVs or other
platforms incurs substantial costs and signiﬁcantly limits their
scalability in applications.

B. DCMU Mechanism Based on HMM
To perform geolocalization of UAV images using satellite
maps, this article proposes an adaptive candidate map retrieval
strategy based on the HMM. Unlike conventional frame-
independent retrieval strategies, our method introduces an
HMM-based temporal update mechanism that dynamically
reﬁnes the candidate submap set, substantially improving
retrieval eﬃciency. Speciﬁcally, the satellite map is ﬁrst

database, while SuperPoint is used to extract local features for
ﬁne-grained matching. During online localization, the HMM
is leveraged to model UAV state transitions and observation
likelihoods, enabling dynamic ﬁltering of candidate submaps.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND ANALYSIS
In this section, we present the experimental results of the
proposed method on the RealUAV [35] and SEULoc datasets.
Among them, SEULoc is a dataset collected by us in real-
world scenarios. Fig. 5 shows three experimental scenarios
selected from the RealUAV dataset. We compare our method
against both the absolute and relative localization methods.
The absolute localization method, referred to as ElevPnP, uses
our proposed elevation recovery algorithm to estimate the ele-
vation of feature points extracted from satellite images, thereby
constructing a 3-D point set. The UAV pose is then estimated
by solving the PnP problem using these 3-D satellite feature
points. For the relative localization method, we adopt the
classic SLAM algorithm ORB-SLAM3 (monocular mode). To
align the SLAM-estimated trajectory with the correct scale and
the geographic coordinate system, we use ElevPnP to estimate
the geographic coordinates of the ﬁrst two keyframes during
the initialization stage. A scale correction is then applied based
on the geodetic distance between these keyframes. All the
localization results are transformed into the Universal Trans-
verse Mercator (UTM) coordinate system for evaluation and
comparison. Furthermore, to better demonstrate the eﬀective-
ness of the proposed elevation recovery algorithm in improving
localization accuracy, we perform additional comparisons with
a traditional homography-based method on oblique view data
from the SEULoc dataset. All the experiments are conducted
on a computer equipped with an Intel Core i9-13900HX CPU,
32GB DDR5 RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU.
A. Metrics
To comprehensively evaluate the performance of diﬀerent
visual localization methods, we adopt the open-source bench-
marking tool evo [36], which calculates the absolute trajectory
error (ATE) by comparing the estimated UAV trajectory with

of localization accuracy, ﬁve key metrics are derived from
the ATE distribution: maximum error (Max), minimum error
(Min), mean error, root mean square error (RMSE), and
standard deviation (Std). These metrics oﬀer a robust and
comprehensive evaluation of the performance of various visual
localization methods.

V. CONCLUSION
This article addresses the challenge of UAV localization
in GNSS-denied environments within IoT-enabled intelligent
environments and proposes a novel visual geolocalization
approach. The proposed method uses a spatiotemporally
constrained candidate map retrieval strategy to adaptively
constrain the satellite image search space based on real-
time UAV poses, enabling eﬃcient and accurate candidate
map ﬁltering. In addition, it introduces an elevation recov-
ery mechanism that operates independently of DEM or
DSM, by leveraging covisibility across adjacent UAV frames
and their feature correspondences with satellite imagery to
autonomously extract elevation information. This design eﬀec-
tively addresses localization failures that arise under oblique
viewing angles. Finally, a factor graph joint optimization
framework is developed to collaboratively reﬁne both the
UAV’s absolute geographic pose and its local relative trajec-
tory, signiﬁcantly improving the smoothness and stability of
localization while mitigating the pose discontinuities typically
observed in traditional single-frame-based approaches. Exten-
sive experiments conducted on the RealUAV and SEULoc
datasets demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed
method in terms of both accuracy and stability. Compared
with established baselines such as ORB-SLAM3 and ElevPnP,
the method notably reduces positioning errors and suppresses
pose instability in complex low-altitude urban environments.
Speciﬁcally, during transitions from vertical to oblique camera
views, the method achieves an 89.5% improvement in posi-
tioning accuracy over conventional approaches, demonstrating
its eﬀectiveness in enabling collaborative operations of IoT
agents in GNSS-denied conditions.
To meet the demands of future UAV and multiagent collab-
oration in IoT applications, future work will explore an edge
collaborative optimization framework. By integrating federated
learning with distributed factor graphs, this framework aims to
facilitate the sharing of positioning information among UAV
clusters across edge nodes, thereby enhancing positioning
eﬃciency and scalability in large-scale collaborative scenarios.
In addition, inertial sensors are also being considered for
integration into our positioning system to further enhance its
robustness.
