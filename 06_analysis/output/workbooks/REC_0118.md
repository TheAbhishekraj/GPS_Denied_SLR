A Vision-Aided Navigation System by
Ground-Aerial Vehicle Cooperation for UAV in
GNSS-Denied Environments
Zi-Hao Wang, Te Zhang, Kai-Yu Qin, Bo Zhu
School of Aeronautics and Astronautics, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China
Abstract—Modern UAVs are expected to be able to ﬂy safely
through the area where GNSS signal is weak or even could not
be received. In order to realize it, the intuitive idea is by adding
some airborne sensors and processor chips, which is, however,
not practically feasible for the UAVs that have limited payload
capability and online-computing resources. To address this issue,
this paper presents a technical solution for building autonomous
navigation system based on the concept of Ground-Aerial Vehicle
Cooperation. The key features of the solution lie in four aspects:
1) A local reliable self-organizing network is constructed for
transmitting control commands and estimated pose information
between the UAV and a reusable ground robot; 2) A small and
lightweight monocular camera is equipped on the UAV to achieve
visual simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) consider-
ing the limitation of UAV’s carrying capacity; 3) The modiﬁed
ORB-SLAM2 system equipped on the ground robot rebuilds a
global dense map and reconstructs the 3D environment based on
the Octomap; and 4) The low-cost, commercial ground robots
and rotor craft were chosen to facilitate other researchers to
build a similar platform quickly. Four in-door ﬂight experiments
are conducted to verify the effectiveness of the technical solution.
Other potential and extendable applications of the cooperation
system include: real-time reconstruction of environmental maps,
collaborative obstacle avoidance and path planning, and motion
synchronization control.

I. INTRODUCTION
With the rapid development of artiﬁcial intelligence tech-
nology, autonomous vehicle systems including UAVs and
ground robots are expected to play a more imporant role in
various military and civil applications. In the past decade, the
autonomous localization problem of vehicles under GNSS-
denied environments has received increasing attention, and
several localization solutions have been proposed. Some of
the solutions suggest to adopt speciﬁc stationary devices such
as RFID transceivers [1] and Infrared transceivers [2], wich
are high-cost and inﬂexible. Other suggest to employ a WIFI
network, which can cover most indoor environments [3], but
it is diffﬁcult to reach a relatively high precision and maintain
a long-term stability. There are also researchers proposing to
instal onboard sensors which has some advantages over other
approaches. For instance, the spatial information included in
the images can effectively assist autonomous localization, and
a higher measurement accuracy can be easily achieved by
adopting a high-tech visual sensors and graphical processing
algorithm. In addition to navigation application, visual system
can be used to achieve some other potential applications. For
UAV applications, a local map can be built by visual SLAM.
The map is important for some higher-level autonomous
control, such as obstacle avoidance and planning path [4].
The Multiple Autonomous Robotic Systems Lab at University
of Minnesota focuses on quadrotor’s navigation, cooperative
mapping, and semi-dense mapping [5] [6]. In the ﬁeld of
unmanned vehicles, the visual odometer imformation obtained
from the visual SLAM can be used to achieve data fusion
among multiple sensors. The team led by Kumer at University
of Pennsylvania has studied the SLAM techniques based on
the fusion of inertial navigation and vision [7] [8]. For other
ground robots, visual SLAM has been used to generate a map
of the environment to achieve the identiﬁcation, searching and
navigation task. For example, the Autonomous System lab at
Zurich Federal Polytechnic Institute has developed a location
and depth reconstruction algorithm based on vision systems
[9] [10].
Roughly speaking, there are two typical visual navigation
schemes. One scheme adopts an onboard computing platform,
which is applicable to the vehicles that have no obvious restric-
tions on the weight and size of cameras or processing chips,
for instance, ground robots and large-size UAVs. Another
scheme is non-airborne with a computing platform located on
a moving vehicle, which is suitable for small-size UAVs. It is
practically important to study the latter scheme in its own right,
by noting the fact that in most situations, the time interval for
UAV ﬂight in the absence of GNSS signal is relatively short
with respect to the time interval for a complete ﬂight mission,
and that it is not necessary to carry a large computation
load during a whole ﬂight. However, in the latter scheme, an
additional moving vehicle (or a ground station) is required
to cooperate with the UAV. In recent years, some reseachers
have developed several vision-aided techniques for ground-air
robots cooperation applications. By motion synchronization
and cooperative control of multiple vehicles [11] [12], the
performance limitations of a single robot can be overcome.
U.S. Army Research Laboratory has studied the collaboration
of ground-air robots in complex environments to accomplish
the goal of cooperative navigation and target detection [13].
The Robotics and Perception Group at University of Zurich
have developed aerial robots assisted by ground robots to
actualize the navigation tasks in the environment of mobile
obstacles [14].

In order to solve the problem of autonomous navigation
of small-size UAVs in GNSS-denied environments, we de-
veloped and veriﬁed a vision-aided guidance system using
the air-ground robot cooperation. A ground robot is used to
accomplish the ﬂight mission of the quadrotor in the absence
of GNSS signals. Noting that the ground robot has a good load
capacity and can work for a long time, we equipped it with the
RGB-D visual sensor and a laptop as a computing platform
of our system. A lightweight monocular camera installed on
the unmanned rotor craft generates measurements for pose
estimation. Several in-door ﬂight experiments demonstrated
that the system can accomplish the navigation goal, and motion
synchronization as well as the rebuilding of a 3D environment
map are realized. By the developed system, we won the second
prize at the Third China Graduate Future Aircraft Innovation
Competition. Some typical experimental videos have been
uploaded to online: https : //youtu.be/ MLb7GeNj0M.
The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In section
II, we introduce the requirement analysis, design ideas and
implementation methods of the system. In section III, we
describe the design of 4 indoor experiments and give the
detailed analysis. In section IV, we conclude our system with
a summary and look forward to its future application.
II. SYSTEM DESIGN
A. Motivations
We studied the small qurdrotors in the market and analyzed
their ability of autonomous navigation in the GNSS-denied
environments. The Inspire quadrotor produced by DJI can
achieve accurate indoor hovering and pose estimation through
a single camera and ultrasonic sensor on its bottom. Besides,
DJI launched an onboard computer for developers named Man-
ifold, which is a powerful computing platform with the Nvidia
Tegra K1 that contains ARM Cortex-A15 Processor and 192
CUDA cores.The lightweight quadrotor Bebop 2 produced
by Parrot also has the capability of autonomous localization.
Furthermore, Parrot launched an airborne platform named
SLAMDUNK, which is an open development kit for Linux-
based drones and robotic platforms for obstacle avoidance,
indoor navigation and 3D mapping. It is also powered by an
Nvidia Tegra K1 mobile processor, carrying sensors such as
ultrasonic, binocular ﬁsh-eye cameras and the IUM.However,
small quadrotors can not handle the issue of carrying capacity
at the present. The extra loads will seriously increase mini
quadrotors weight and shorten their ﬂight time.
To solve the above problem, we propose a scheme to achieve
the navigation of small UAVs based on the concept of ground-
aerial vehicle cooperation. The designing idea of our system
mainly focuses on the following three aspects:
• Self-organizing network: The air-ground robots need a
complete set of data links to achieve the interaction of
data and instructions. Based on Robot Operating System
(ROS), data interaction between the quadrotor and laptop
is realized through wireless LAN and between the ground
robot and laptop through a wired network.
• Vision-aided navigation method: The monocular SLAM
method has the advantages of simple structure, low price,
small size and easy installation on UAVs. Although
monocular SLAM method has scale uncertainty, it is the
scale uncertainty that makes it convert freely in scenes
of different sizes. Therefore, we chose the monocular
camera as the front-end of our system.
• Motion control strategy: Robust motion control strategy is
the basis for realizing the coordination of heterogeneous
robots. The PID control method that we employed in our
system does not need the accurate model of the system. It
has a strong adaptability for all kinds of small quadrotors.
It is also relatively insensitive to the change of the
controlled object and suitable for industrial production.
B. Framework
• Overall scheme: The ground robot is equiped with high-
performance computing and storage device with optical
sensors, which are utilized to navigate and locate. The
UAV only carries its own light-weight monocular camera,
IMU and atmospheric pressure sensor without additional
sensors. The location information of the UAV is computed
and determined by the ground robot. The robot assists
the UAV in navigation by using a laptop to transfer data
and commands through the network between them. As
shown in Fig. 1, we give the overall structure diagram of
our technical framework.
• Measuring system: The UAV transfers its ﬁrst-perspective
vision data to the ground robot and the computing
platform will estimate the pose of the UAV. An RGB-
D SLAM technique is employed to achieve the self-
locating and map rebuilding by using a depth sensor.
Those information will provide the UAV with a priori
map under complicated circumstances.
• Communication system: To establish a reliable communi-
cation between the UAV and robot, we use wireless LAN
and wired network to construct an efﬁcient air-ground
independent communicating network. The accurate navi-
gating information is transferred to the UAV in real-time.
This design will also achieve independent communicating
network for air-ground vehicles in other forms such as
one-to-many and many-to-many.

G. Modiﬁed PTAM Algorithm on UAV
PTAM [17] is a landmark framework in the development of
visual SLAM. It realizes the parallelization of the tracking and
mapping process using non-linear optimization at the back-
end. Meanwhile, it introduces the key frame so that researchers
do not have to handle every image anymore but only the key
images. The original PTAM algorithm has some limitations.
The most typical is the drift caused by the uncertainty of the
scale. On the basis of PTAM, Jakob Engel et al proposed
scale ﬁlter for the uncertainty problem of the monocular scale
[18], and solved the problem of scale uncertainty of monocular
cameras. The estimation part of our system draws on the
modiﬁed PTAM algorithm. Fig. 4 (a) shows the ﬂow chart
of the algorithm.
H. Modiﬁed ORB-SLAM2
When the environmental map is known, the ground robot
can obtain accurate positioning information by reading its own
tire encoder in a relatively short time. However, considering
unknown envi ronments and long working time, ground robots
also need to use other methods to solve accumulated errors
of inertial device and encoder. Therefore, using the RGB-D
interface of ORB-SLAM2, our ground robot achieved real-
time localization and mapping by equipping with Microsoft
KinectV2.
Considering that the original ORB-SLAM2 only provides
a sparse feature point cloud, which is not conducive to
actual environment exploration and perception. We designed
a modiﬁed ORB-SLAM2 with 3D reconstruction based on
dense point clouds. We use the pose information obtained
by the key frame to splice the dense point cloud which is
obtained by Kinect V2. Considering the burden of calculation,
we down sample the dense point cloud while splicing. Finally,
we transform the space point cloud into the octomap for
3D reconstruction, which provides a priori environmental
information for missions such as path planning. Fig. 4 (b)
shows the modiﬁed algorithm.

However, there are still some weaknesses in our system.
First of all, PTAM algorithm has a poor robustness against
rotation. Once the aircrafts yaw rotates too fast, the algorithm
can be easy to fail. We need to restrict the yaw angle in a
smaller range (10 deg or less) and then use the roll action
to achieve the steering of our quadrotor. Secondly, in scenes
like corridors where the distance of front feature points are
relatively long and there are white walls on both sides, the
system may fail easily. Facing such a situation where visual
information is extremely poor, we may consider adding sensors
like lidar and ultrasound. Third, the low chassis of the ground
robot we currently used in our system makes it easy to fail
when the robot passing through uneven terraces such as steps
or potholes.
IV. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we proposed a vision-aided guidance system
based on the idea of cooperative air-ground robots. The
contributions of this paper are as follows. 1) We proposed a
vision-aided navigation framework for UAVs based on ground
robots. 2) A scheme of ﬂight pose estimation and trajectory
measurement system for UAVs was given. 3) A construction of
scalable network for air-ground coordinated robots was given.
4) We employed a modiﬁed ORB-SLAM2 framework with
real-time dense point cloud map construction and rebuilding
of 3D environment. 5) We chose low-cost, commercial ground
robots and rotor craft, which will facilitate other researchers
to build a similar platform quickly.
