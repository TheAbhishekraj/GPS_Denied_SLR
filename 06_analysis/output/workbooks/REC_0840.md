ABSTRACT
Precise cross-domain matching between Unmanned Aerial
Vehicle (UAV), imagery and satellite maps is essential for
navigation in GPS-denied environments but remains chal-
lenging due to drastic variations in scale and rotation. This
paper proposes a geometry-aware matching framework that
decouples the process into specialized extraction, descrip-
tion, and navigation modules. By incorporating a local patch-
ing strategy and the Grid-based Random Sample Consensu
(GRANSAC) algorithm, the framework effectively mitigates
geometric biases and spatial feature clustering. Experimen-
tal results on the MDAS dataset demonstrate that our method
significantly outperforms SIFT[10] and SuperPoint[1] in both
accuracy and robustness, providing reliable localization pre-
cision for autonomous UAV flight.
Index Terms— Keypoint Matching, Cross-domain Im-
agery, Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Navigator.
1. INTRODUCTION
Visual geo-localization serves as a critical navigation alter-
native for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating in
signal-compromised environments.
By aligning real-time
aerial observations with georeferenced satellite databases,
UAVs can estimate their position without relying on GPS.
However, bridging these heterogeneous domains presents
substantial challenges due to the fundamental disparity be-
tween orthographic satellite imagery and perspective UAV
views.
Drastic variations in scale, arbitrary flight orienta-
tions, and appearance heterogeneity (e.g., seasonal changes)
complicate the establishment of reliable correspondences.
Traditional handcrafted algorithms, such as SIFT [10],
often suffer from severe performance degradation under these
non-linear radiometric differences.
While deep learning-
based methods like SuperPoint [1] have advanced feature
extraction, standard convolution operations often lack the in-
herent rotation invariance required for omnidirectional UAV
flight.
Conversely, detector-free architectures like LoFTR
[12] increase computational costs that are prohibitive for
resource-constrained onboard hardware. Furthermore, even
when valid features are extracted, standard geometric veri-
fication methods like RANSAC [15] remain susceptible to
uneven spatial distributions of keypoints, leading to biased
geometric solutions that fail to represent the entire scene.
To address these limitations, this paper proposes a mod-
ular, geometry-aware cross-domain matching framework. By
decoupling the localization and description stages and intro-
ducing spatial constraints into the geometric verification pro-
cess, the proposed system facilitates stable navigation under
various challenging conditions. Three main contributions of
this paper are:
• Geometry-Aware Cross-Domain Descriptor: A de-
scriptor design incorporating multi-scale feature fusion
and local patch sampling to enhance stability under sig-
nificant rotation and scale variations.
• Grid-based Random Sample Consensus (GRANSAC):
An enhanced RANSAC [15] algorithm that enforces
spatial uniformity via grid-based sampling, thereby
mitigating estimation errors arising from biased dense
sampling in localized regions.
• Cross-Domain Navigation Dataset: A heterogeneous
benchmark dataset constructed by precisely aligning
Google satellite imagery with the MDAS aerial dataset
for evaluating navigation robustness.

3.2. Descriptor Generator
To bridge the domain gap caused by disparate sensor re-
sponses and viewpoints, the descriptor module maps het-
erogeneous features into a shared, domain-invariant latent
space.
Multi-scale Patch Sampling.
Leveraging the hierarchical
features of an EfficientNetV2-b2 backbone, we adopt a multi-
stage extraction strategy. For each keypoint pi, we concur-
rently crop fixed-size local feature patches (5×5) from the fi-
nal three backbone stages (resolutions 1/8, 1/16, 1/32). This
hierarchical sampling captures a comprehensive representa-
tion ranging from fine-grained geometric textures to broad
semantic context, effectively handling the scale variations be-
tween UAV and satellite platforms.
Fusion and Encoding.
Unlike single-pixel sampling, our
5 × 5 spatial patches preserve local structural arrangements,
providing higher discriminative power against environmen-
tal noise. During fusion, these multi-scale patches are con-
catenated and processed by a convolutional fusion block to
enable cross-scale information interaction. Finally, a Multi-
Layer Perceptron (fMLP ) projects the fused features into a
unified, geometric-invariant descriptor space fi ∈R256. This
process yields robust descriptor sets Fuav = {fuav,i}N
i=1 and
Fsat = {fsat,j}M
j=1 capable of withstanding extreme cross-
domain discrepancies.
3.3. Navigator
The Navigator integrates visual perception with motion con-
trol for precise localization.
Pipeline and Database. As shown in Fig. 2, to minimize
onboard computation, we construct an offline georeferenced
database by processing satellite imagery (Isat) to extract
descriptors Fsat linked to GPS coordinates.
During on-
line flight, the UAV only extracts descriptors for the current
frame, enabling efficient vector-based retrieval against this
pre-indexed database.
Grid-based Random Sample Consensus (GRANSAC).
Standard RANSAC suffers from geometric biases when key-
points cluster on dominant structures. We propose GRANSAC
to enforce spatial uniformity. Instead of random sampling,
the image plane is partitioned into G × G grid cells. The
algorithm prioritizes sampling 4 points from distinct non-
empty cells to form the hypothesis model Mk, ensuring the
estimated homography covers the global scene geometry.
Hypotheses are verified via parallelized GPU processing to
meet real-time latency requirements.
Dynamic Strategy. The system employs a predict-correct
mechanism. If the GRANSAC-estimated coordinates align
with the trajectory prediction, the global position is updated.
In feature-less regions where visual matching fails, the navi-
gator automatically switches to inertial dead reckoning, while
a dynamic cache pre-fetches database sectors to ensure unin-
terrupted long-range operation.

We adopt a two-stage training strategy to decouple geometric
localization from semantic description.

4.2. Evaluation Metrics
To evaluate the proposed framework, we utilize six metrics
covering visual perception, geometric accuracy, and system-
level navigation.
Repeatability (Repeat@k): Measures the keypoint extrac-
tor’s ability to consistently re-detect features across domains
within k ∈{3, 5, 10, 20} pixels.
Precision (Precision@k): Evaluates the purity of matches,
defined as the ratio of correct matches (reprojection error ≤
k ∈{3, 5, 10, 20}) to the total predicted matches. We report
AUCPrecision to capture aggregate stability.
Recall (Recall@k): Measures the completeness of matching,
defined as the ratio of correct matches to the total ground-truth
correspondences.
Homography Estimation (Homography@k): Represents
the geometric success rate. A task is successful if the average
corner error ≤k ∈{3, 5, 10, 20} pixels.
Average Cross-Track Error (ACTE): Quantifies flight sta-
bility by measuring the mean Euclidean distance between the
UAV’s trajectory and the planned path.
Final Goal Distance (FGD): Measures the terminal position-
ing accuracy at the end of a mission.

Quantitative Analysis.
Experimental results demonstrate

unable to perform the navigation task due to its inability to

5. CONCLUSION
This work presents a geometry-aware matching framework
for robust UAV navigation in GPS-denied environments. By
decoupling feature extraction from description and incorpo-
rating Hard Negative Mining, the system bridges the domain
gap caused by drastic scale and rotation. Crucially, the multi-
scale descriptor filters out outliers, providing reliable visual
anchors even under significant appearance shifts.
Experiments on the MDAS dataset confirm that our
method outperforms baselines, achieving an 83% success
rate in unseen environments.
Active visual re-localization
effectively suppresses inertial drift, while the proposed
GRANSAC algorithm ensures globally consistent geomet-
ric verification, offering a reliable solution for autonomous
localization under significant flight attitude changes.
