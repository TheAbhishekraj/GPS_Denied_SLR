Heterogeneous Swarm of Drones Navigating in

Kamil Canberk Atik1∗, Enes Erdogan1∗, Ahmet Yusuf Yahsi2, Feyzullah Kara3,
Baris Yalcin4, G¨urkan Cetin4 and Nazim Kemal Ure1
Abstract—This paper presents a methodology for enhancing
the localization performance of drone swarms by collaborative
propagation of the estimates of drones equipped with higher cost
sensors to the drones with lower-cost sensors. Although recent
advances in visual-inertial odometry (VIO) enabled good perfor-
mance in GPS denied environments, the localization performance
is still highly dependent on the accuracy of inertial sensors.
Moreover, environmental effects that corrupt visual inputs also
contribute to the error rate of VIO estimates thus localization
in noisy environments with low-grade sensors is still widely an
open problem. We show that this problem can be alleviated
when drones are navigating in the form of a swarm, and the
relative distance measurements between drones are available
through ultra-wideband (UWB) signals. In particular, we develop
a collaborative Kalman filtering and optimization framework,
where the drones with lower grade sensors receive updates from
drones that have access to higher-quality measurements. We
validate our approach in a simulation environment with realistic
visual representations and show that the proposed methodology
can significantly improve the localization performance, especially
for the scenarios where the camera measurements are corrupted
with high noise. We further present results in simplistic flight
tests to demonstrate the applicability of our approach to real
hardware.
Index Terms—optimization, swarm drone, localization, VIO
I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are a key autonomous
systems technology that found a wide range of applications,
ranging from warfare [1] to transportation, agriculture, and
healthcare [2]. As the scale and complexity of missions
increase, single UAV operations become ineffective and as
a result multi-UAV metholodogies are gaining popularity in
most recent applications [3], [4]. For instance, in surveillance
missions, multiple drones can cover a much larger area in a
limited amount of time.

Localization accuracy is an important aspect of the success
of autonomous multi-drone missions. High accuracy local-
ization prevents UAVs from colliding with each other and
improves the overall mission efficiency. In such missions,
every drone can either localize itself independent of the other
UAVs or improve its localization accuracy by extracting state
estimates of their teammates through communicating with
each other. The concept of fusing information from multiple
vehicles is known as cooperative localization, which is a
research topic that gained popularity with the emergence
of multi-drone missions [5], [6]. Multi-drone localization
poses several challenges, such as handling communication
constraints and improving robustness [7]. One of the central
issues in building multi-drone perception systems is addressing
the trade-off between the cost of sensors and localization
accuracy. Due to the high cost of advanced sensors, it is often
infeasible to equip all drones with high quality sensors in a
large-scale swarm. On the other hand, equipping all drones
with low-cost sensors usually result in sub-par performance.
The central research question we are concerned about in this
paper is, whether it is possible to only equip a few drones in
the team with high-quality sensors and leverage cooperative
localization algorithms to compensate for the errors that low-
quality perception of drones equipped with low-cost sensors.
Our main contribution is a cooperative localization method-
ology, which fuses the estimates of high-cost and low-cost
drones with ultra-wideband (UWB) signal measurements [8]
using a sequential Kalman filtering algorithm coupled with
an optimization-based localization algorithm. We demonstrate
the potential of the proposed approach through extensive sim-
ulations conducted in the AirSim [9] environment. Although
the high-cost drones are equipped with high-quality inertial
measurements units (IMUs) and cameras, low-priced drones
are only armed with low-quality cameras and IMUs. Results
show that, especially under noisy visual inputs, even using
advanced VIO algorithms (such as VINS-Fusion [10]) cannot
obtain a sufficient level of localization accuracy for the low-
cost drones without the aid of cooperative localization. On the
other hand, using cooperative localization significantly boosts
the accuracy of low-cost drone estimates. The overall results
show that it is possible to obtain high accuracy localization for
the team of drones equipped with varying levels of sensors
through UWB signals. We conclude the study with results

II. RELATED WORK
Drone navigation algorithms frequently use the Global
Navigational Satellite System (GNSS) to improve their local-
ization accuracy. However, many drone applications involve
operations in GNNS-denied environments (for instance see
DARPA Subterranean Challenge [11]). For example, Kalman
Filter Inertial Navigation Systems (INS/KF) [12] presents the
methodology for tracking position of moving objects without
multi-sensor data fusion. In such applications, visual-inertial
odometry (VIO) based methods [13] are widely utilized for
UAV navigation. The baseline VIO methods considered in
this work are ROVIO [14], OKVIS [15], VINS-MONO [16],
and VINS-Fusion. ROVIO is an algorithm that can track
three-dimensional landmarks and recognize characteristics on
images using multiple cameras and an Extended Kalman
filter. Despite employing a monocular camera, VINS-Mono
has a smaller root mean square error than OKVIS, which is
optimized for stereo VIO algorithms. In Section III, we briefly
review VINS-Fusion, which is an extension of VINS-Mono
[16]. Meanwhile, compared to the other baselines, VINS-
Fusion has the lowest root mean square error value, which
results in superior performance.
The motivation for this research is based on the idea that
by sharing information among swarm members, the accuracy
of VIO can be improved. UWB allows for the transmission of
relative distance and position between drones. Swarm control
and estimation methods can be centralized or decentralized
[8]. All processes take place on a single computer in a cen-
tralized manner. Therefore, information is transmitted across
swarm members. As a result, each vehicle is intensely reliant
on the central station. As a result, when communication
between the hub and swarm members [7] becomes unreliable,
the algorithms fail. We took a decentralized strategy in this
work; the reasons for this are analyzed in Section III.B.1.
A decentralized visual-inertial-UWB approach is proposed
in [17] that they reach the centimeter-level precision, and
cooperative localization for scene reconstruction is proposed
that consists of both ground robots and aerial vehicles in [18].
By combining the VIO and trilateration results in a sequen-
tial collaborative Kalman filter, the suggested new technique
improves drone localization accuracy.
III. BACKGROUND AND METHODOLOGY
This work focuses on cost reduction for drone swarms,
where establishing high localization accuracy might be ex-
pensive due to high-cost associated with equipping all drones
with advanced sensors. The main idea behind our approach is
to utilize information gathered from small number of high-
cost drones to improve the localization accuracy of large
number of low-cost drones. Our strategy can be explained
into two parts: i) sharing navigational data between high-
cost and low-cost drones and ii) fusing these data on low-
cost drones. First, high-cost drones send relative distance and
location information generated by UWB sensors to low-cost
drones. Second, the optimization-based collaborative Kalman
filter algorithm fuses distance and location data to increase
low-cost drone navigation accuracy. Our approach requires
availability of at least four high-cost drones in the swarm.
A. VINS-Fusion
VIO methodologies utilize two sensors to estimate the
ego-motion: i) IMU, which consists of an accelerometer, a
gyroscope, and a magnetometer and ii) an RGB camera that
helps with the detection of features, construction of optical
flow, and estimation of motion. VIO fuses these two sensor
measurements to compute reliable estimations of position
and orientation. Therefore, the accuracy of VIO algorithms
depends directly on sensors and algorithm quality. Hence,
expensive sensors and complex algorithms are needed to
achieve high accuracy navigation with VIO methodology.
VINS-Fusion is an extension of VINS-Mono, which can
fuse a variety of sensors [19], such as GPS, IMU, and
stereo cameras. In this work, IMU and the stereo camera
integration model are integrated and VINS-Fusion is used as
the baseline VIO algorithm. The algorithm is composed of the
tightly coupled accurate initialization process and nonlinear
optimization-based method that uses fused pre-integrated IMU
measurements and visual features by Kalman filter. VINS-
Fusion offers a loop-closure algorithm and a 4-DOF pose
graph optimization to maintain global stability [19].
B. Collaborative Drones
1) Swarm Drone Systems: A swarm is a group of drones
that fly together while ensuring that they avoid colliding
with one another. A swarm of drones can be homogeneous,
where each drone has the same sensor configuration and
capabilities, or heterogeneous, where each drone is equipped
with different sensing and actuation capabilities. We focus
on heterogeneous drone swarms in this study, which can be
further classified into two types: hard heterogeneous [20] and
soft heterogeneous [21]. Unlike hard heterogeneous systems,
where swarms consist of drones with varying capabilities and
goals, soft heterogeneous systems consists of drones with
varying capabilities cooperating to achieve a common goal.
In thıs work, we consider a soft homogeneous swarm, where
drones have varying sensing capabilities and are working
together to achieve a common goal (such as surveying a
particular area). Fig. 1 portrays a representation of the swarm
considered in this work.
In terms of mode of operation, swarm drone systems
can be classified as either centralized or decentralized. In
centralized frameworks, only one station collects, computes
the information generated from each drone and shares the
results among all the swarm members. In decentralized
framework, the computational load is distributed among the
members of swarm. In this way, a swarm member can operate
without connecting to a compulsory central station. This is
an advantage over the centralized swarm system, however it
comes with the downside of sacrificing accuracy and stability

Localization information can be improved by applying our
proposed algorithm, even if high grade sensors are not avail-
able in all swarm members. Our proposed algorithm makes use
of the optimization based Kalman filter to correct the position
of the low-cost drones by using the information obtained
from high-cost drones. Distance measurements between high-
cost and low-cost drones and positions of high-cost are fused
with an optimization method (Simulated Annealing algorithm
[23]) to yield an estimation regarding the position of the
low-cost drone. The position estimation obtained from op-
timization algorithm is then used in Kalman Filter’s update
phase as measurements, where the reliability of measure-
ments decreases as the distance increases. We assume that
estimations obtained from high-cost drones are more accurate
than estimations of the VINS-Fusion algorithm. Therefore,
process covariance matrix Q is initialized with higher val-
ues compared to measurement covariance matrix R. In this
way, low-cost drones position can be corrected accurately by
putting more trust to measurements from high-cost drones.
After initialization of the state error covariance matrix Pt|t−1,
where t denotes the discrete time, VINS-Fusion outputs are
utilized as process state estimates ˆxt|t−1. Jacobian of the
process state is the state transition matrix F, which is used
for computing a priori state error covariance Pt|t−1, which
in turn determines the reliability between update and process
state in Kalman gain calculation. Multiplication of Kalman
gain K and residual error, which is the difference between
the measurement states and process states is added to process
state to compute posterior estimate covariance matrix ˆxt|t−1.
Process state is initialized as same as the state estimation
whenever measurement is available.
