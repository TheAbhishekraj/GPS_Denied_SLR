Robust Autonomous Flight and Mission Management for MAVs in
GPS-denied Environments
Yingcai Bi1, Menglu Lan1, Jiaxin Li1, Kun Zhang2, Hailong Qin2, Shupeng Lai2 and Ben M. Chen2
Abstract— This paper addresses the problem of autonomous
ﬂight in GPS-denied environments. We present our systematic
solution with integrated hardware and software design, robust
state estimation and mission management. A micro aerial vehi-
cle (MAV) equipped with 2D laser scanner, camera, on-board
computer and ﬂight controller is designed and constructed to
realize our ﬂight missions. The 2D laser-based localization and
Kalman ﬁlter-based sensor fusion provide a robust state esti-
mation for the MAV. We also design a task tree-based mission
management method to handle a complete mission with multiple
tasks. Moreover, vision-based detection algorithms complement
various tasks, such as target searching and precision landing.
Extensive experimental results demonstrate the robustness and
effectiveness of the overall integrated system. It was also
validated by our championship winning in Singapore Amazing
Flight Machine Competition (SAFMC) 2017.
I. INTRODUCTION
Micro aerial vehicles (MAVs), or drones are widely ap-
plied on aerial photography, infrastructures (e.g. bridge,
power line) inspection, onsite mapping and others. In most
cases, a human pilot is needed to control the drone manually
with a transmitter, or autonomous mission is only partially
possible under the circumstance of satisfactory outdoor GPS
availability. However, a large variety of applications for
MAVs would rely on the autonomous capabilities in GPS-
denied environments to handle difﬁcult, dirty, dangerous
jobs. A typical scenario is a post-disaster search and rescue
mission, in which MAVs must ﬂy among buildings with GPS
blocking or even inside buildings without any GPS signal.
To this end, a fully functional system incorporating with
robust guidance, navigation and control algorithms is in high
demand. What’s more, an efﬁcient software architecture and
mission management structure is of vital importance.
Development of autonomous MAVs is one of the most
active research areas in recent years. Early success on
autonomous indoor ﬂight based on 2D laser scanner were
reported in [1] and [2]. They both emphasized the local-
ization and planning components which were adapted from
previous research on ground robots to complete autonomous
indoor exploration. Later on, [3] showed accomplishment
of autonomous ﬂight with a camera as primary sensor. It
was the very ﬁrst systematic design to deal with vision
based autonomous navigation. Recent attempts [4], [5], [6]
and [7] speciﬁcally handled different situations with various
platforms.

While extensive research has been conducted to design
different control structures for MAVs, the key points for
autonomous ﬂy to be discussed are state estimation and
path planning. State estimation for GPS-denied environments
consists of localization and sensor fusion. In this case, the
solution for localization is a typical Simultaneous Localiza-
tion and Mapping (SLAM) problem. 2D laser-based SLAM
([8], [9]) has been well studied and demonstrated to be a
high-accuracy and robust solution. It is an excellent solution
if we assume the environment in the vertical direction does
not change a lot (2.5D environment). This is also valid if the
MAV ﬂies with a ﬁxed height. Besides, a multi-level strategy
[10] can be adapted if the assumption is violated. To control
the MAV, sensor fusion is necessary to boost different sensor
outputs to high-frequency consistent 6D pose estimation.
Kalman ﬁlter-based methods ([11], [12]) are widely applied
to manage this.
Due to the limited size and constrained payload of the
MAVs, most of motion planning methods adopt two-layered
approaches ([5], [13]), which decouple the vehicle dynamics
and geometric constraints. Normally, a global planner is used
to ﬁnd a free path ﬁrst and then a trajectory generation pro-
cedure is involved to handle the vehicle dynamics. In [13], a
standard A* algorithm was performed in an octree-based map
to generate a collision-free corridor. Then they formulate the
corridor constraints into a set of convex position constraints,
and generate the dynamical feasible trajectory within the
corridor using numerical optimization techniques. [14] also
used a two-layered approach and A* as the global planner.
However, to avoid numerical instability, an analytical two
point boundary value problem (TPBVP) solver was used to
generate jerk-limited time-optimal trajectory.
This paper describes our design philosophy of a complete
solution for autonomous MAVs in GPS-denied environ-
ment: Robustness, Modularity, Extensibility and Usability.
Robustness means we leverage the state-of-the-art methods in
navigation, planning and control on our system. Modularity
in terms of both hardware and software makes our system
maintainable and improvable for each part. Extensibility
means more capabilities can be easily implemented and
adapted on the existing system. Usability is reﬂected on
program interface, user interface etc.

The system is highly modular with a robust ﬂight con-
troller, a UAVOS middleware based on Robot Operating

III. MULTIPLE SENSOR FUSION
A. Sensor Fusion Structure
To get a consistent state estimation for the autonomous
ﬂight, we build a two-stage multiple sensor fusion solution.
The orientation and position are estimated separately with
two kalman ﬁlters. The attitude ﬁlter takes angular rate from
gyroscope, acceleration from accelerometer, heading from
laser SLAM to estimate a robust attitude. At the same time,
the position ﬁlter utilizes position measurement from laser
SLAM, acceleration, attitude estimation and height measured
from TeraRanger One to provide a smooth position output.
The multiple sensor fusion structure is shown in Fig. 4. We
use north-east-down (NED) coordinate system in our sensor
fusion algorithms.

B. Laser based SLAM
For structured 2.5D environment, 2D laser scanner can
provide an accurate positioning information for MAVs. In
this paper, we evaluate two state-of-the-art laser SLAM
algorithms as the front-end of our state estimation module.
They are Hector SLAM [8] and Cartographer SLAM [9].

Hector SLAM calculates the pose by aligning incoming
laser scan with the map maintained from all previous scans.
Gauss-Newton optimization is utilized to ﬁnd an optimal
transformation. Inspired by the idea of image pyramids, a
multi-resolution map representation is applied to improve the
numerical stability.
Google Cartographer SLAM is a representative modern
SLAM algorithm. Theoretically, it share the similar scan
to map matching method for localization as Hector SLAM.
Moreover, it includes a real-time loop closure algorithm to
further reduce the positioning error. The key idea is to build
multiple submaps, and align new scans to nearby submap to
generate constraints on a graph. The real-time loop closure
alignment is achieved by a branch-and-bound approach. In
comparison, Hector SLAM use a ﬁxed size map and can not
get a drift-free estimation especially in larger space without
the help of loop closure.

IV. CONTROL AND NAVIGATION
A. Cascaded Control and Path Planning
A cascaded control structure [17] is adopted for precise
and robust maneuvering. The controller structure is displayed
in Fig. 5. Outer loop controller, which adopts robust perfect
tracking (RPT) algorithm, transfers the user command into
acceleration reference. Whereas the LQR based inner loop
controller takes in acceleration reference and generates motor
control signal.
Fig. 5: The ﬂight controller structure
The MAV adopts a two-layered planning structure [14]
to achieve online path planning and obstacle avoidance.
First of all, a search-based global planner, speciﬁcally, A*
is used to ﬁnd the geometric collision-free path on the
updated map without considering any vehicle dynamics.
Then, dynamically-feasible trajectory is generated along the
collision-free path by solving a series of two point boundary
value problems. Fig. 6 illustrates the procedure of our path
planning. The local target will be switched if the trajectory
of current path enter the deceleration phase.
Fig. 6: The path planning structure
B. Task Tree based Mission Managment
We use a task tree-based framework [18] to manage
multiple tasks of different types. The tasks are organized
into a behavior tree and executed in a manner of depth-ﬁrst
traversal. Fig. 7 shows a typical tree structure. Each leaf
node task contains only one single action. In other words,
executing that leaf node task is equivalent to executing the
corresponding action. Some ﬂight actions can be take off,
ﬂy to a global coordinate, ﬂy to a local coordinate, drop a
payload, land and so on.
The mission management is further enhanced with an
reactive planning scheme triggered by events. An event is
Fig. 7: The mission planning structure
an occurrence of some asynchronous external activity that
requires an action to take care. As a result, current action
is terminated and a special action is inserted and executed
to handle the event. For example, when a visual target is
detected during a target searching mission, a ‘target found’
event will be triggered and a ‘navigate to found target’ action
takes over. If the event is not triggered, the default action will
be completed.
C. Visual Target Detection
Although the laser based localization is accurate enough
for waypoint ﬂy, a visual detection module is necessary
when the target position is unknown for some tasks, such
as precision landing on moving platforms. We develop a
visual detection module which supports both ﬁducial markers
and color visual targets. It is an important supplement for
our system. The relative position and orientation of target
are calculated in real-time. It is able to correct the target
waypoint and guide the MAV precisely. The ﬁducial marker
deployed in our system is Apriltag [19]. It is based on a
near-optimal lexicographic coding system. Four corners on
a marker can be detected with corresponding order under
different lighting conditions and view angles. Utilizing the
exact size of the marker, the camera pose and target position
in body frame are completely determined with OpenCV
solvePnP function.
For the color visual target detection, a color segmentation
on HSV channel is ﬁrst applied on the image. Thanks to our
ﬂexible mission management, we can restrict the searching
area to reduce the complexity of background image. There-
fore, a larger HSV threshold range is applied to increase the
detection robustness. In our implementation with OpenCV,
the HSV range for red color is from (165, 40, 50) to (179,
255, 255) and from (0, 40, 50) to (15, 255, 255). Contours are
detected and analyzed in the binary image after segmentation.
The detected contour which has largest area and satisﬁes the
minimal area requirement is regarded as the correct target.
The target center is extracted and target position can be
computed considering the drone’s current state and assuming

VI. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we have presented our autonomous MAV
solution for completing multiple complex missions in GPS-
denied environment. Comprehensive hierarchical structures
for both hardware and software make our system robust
enough to accomplish versatile tasks. We have developed
the robust state estimation based on 2D laser scanner. Also,
real-time path planning and trajectory generation is achieved
with constrained on-board computation power. Furthermore,
vision based detection and mission management signiﬁcantly
improve the system efﬁciency. A video footage 2 demonstrat-
ing the performance is available online. Currently, we are
working on camera based navigation solution for our system.
We will expand capabilities of the system on perception and
planning in arbitrary 3D environment.
