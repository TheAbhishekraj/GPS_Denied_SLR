A B S T R A C T
Vision-based Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) localization systems have been extensively investigated for 
Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-denied environments. However, existing retrieval-based approaches 
face limitations in dataset availability and persistent challenges including suboptimal real-time performance, 
memory inefficiency for onboard deployment, environmental sensitivity, and limited generalization capability, 
particularly in dynamic or temporally varying environments. To overcome these limitations, we present 
a large-scale Multi-Altitude Flight Segments dataset (MAFS) for variable altitude scenarios and propose a 
novel Semantic-Weighted Adaptive Particle Filter (SWA-PF) method. This approach integrates robust semantic 
features from both UAV-captured images and satellite imagery through three core innovations: a compact 4-
DoF particle representation with strictly linear memory complexity, a semantic weighting mechanism, and an 
optimized particle filtering architecture. The compact particle design achieves negligible per-particle memory 
overhead, enabling memory-efficient deployment on resource-constrained UAV onboard computing platforms. 
Evaluated using our dataset, the proposed method achieves approximately 10× end-to-end computational 
efficiency gain over classical feature-based methods when embedded in the same particle filter localization 
framework, maintains global positioning errors below 10 meters with respect to onboard GNSS-based pose 
measurements, and enables rapid 4 degree of freedom (4-DoF) pose estimation within seconds using accessible 
low-resolution satellite maps. Code and dataset will be available at https://github.com/YuanJiayuuu/SWA-PF.
1. Introduction
UAVs are widely deployed across various domains [1]. While the 
Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) is the primary localization 
solution [2], its vulnerability in complex environments [3] has driven 
significant interest in autonomous cross-view UAV-to-satellite localiza-
tion [4]. However, practical large-scale deployments demand contin-
uous flights at variable altitudes with strict onboard computational 
constraints, necessitating robust, real-time 4-DoF pose estimation.
Current absolute localization methods, including template match-
ing [5,6], feature-based approaches [7,8], visual odometry [9,10], and 
deep learning [11–13], typically frame localization as image-to-map 
matching. They often rely on paired data, assume fixed altitudes, 
and restrict estimation to 2-DoF or 3-DoF. In realistic unconstrained 
flights, altitude changes and barometer drift inevitably introduce scale 
variations, while onboard compasses suffer from electromagnetic in-
terference. Consequently, full 4-DoF estimation (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑧, 𝜃) is opera-
tionally essential to simultaneously resolve both scale and rotational 
ambiguities.

To address these continuous 4-DoF tracking requirements, parti-

non-Gaussian, multi-modal uncertainties without the historical graph 
overhead of optimization techniques or the scene-specific pre-training 
of neural implicit models. Yet, conventional PF implementations are 
computationally expensive and sensitive to appearance variations [14], 
often demanding memory that scales poorly with the geographic search 
area. Thus, a critical gap persists: existing methods struggle to simul-
taneously achieve continuous 4-DoF pose estimation, real-time perfor-
mance, and strictly linear memory efficiency for resource-constrained 
onboard deployment.
Furthermore, the rigorous evaluation of such 4-DoF algorithms is 
fundamentally hindered by the limitations of existing UAV localiza-
tion datasets [13,15–17]. Most current benchmarks utilize one-to-one 
paired data, which presents three key limitations: (1) sparse, discontin-
uous trajectory sampling [17] that prevents the evaluation of temporal 
filtering; (2) fixed-altitude acquisition that restricts scale variation

The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
1. We propose SWA-PF, a real-time semantic-aware framework that 
formulates localization as a 4-DoF estimation problem, overcom-
ing the restricted DoF assumptions of existing approaches.
2. We construct MAFS, a large-scale multi-altitude continuous-
trajectory benchmark addressing fixed-altitude and sparse-
sampling limitations.
3. We provide SemanticMAFS with pixel-level annotations to en-
able semantic-aware probabilistic localization research.
4. We release the datasets and source code to promote reproducibil-
ity and facilitate future research.
5. We show SWA-PF achieves strong robustness against low-
resolution satellite imagery, enabling practical deployment with 
limited bandwidth and storage.
2. Related works
This section briefly reviews cross-view localization datasets, ab-
solute UAV localization algorithms, and semantic-based localization 
methods, providing context for the design of our MAFS dataset and 
SWA-PF framework.
2.1. Comparison of cross-view localization datasets
Existing cross-view localization datasets have transitioned from 
ground-to-satellite matching under static viewpoints to incorporat-
ing aerial perspectives. CVUSA [15] pairs panoramic ground-level 
images with satellite imagery across large geographic regions, en-
abling large-scale one-to-one matching. To explicitly model UAV view-
points, University-1652 [17] introduces tri-view matching by combin-
ing ground, UAV, and satellite imagery from 1652 university buildings. 
SUES-200 [16] investigates altitude variation by capturing UAV im-
ages at four discrete flight altitudes. UL14 [13], an extension of 
DenseUAV [18], introduces dense spatial sampling and multi-temporal 
satellite imagery, formulating UAV localization as a one-to-many
matching task.
Despite this progress, most existing benchmarks fundamentally rely 
on fragmented sampling strategies or rigid fixed-altitude assumptions. 
Recent formal benchmarks further expand cross-view evaluation di-
mensions: The SatLoc dataset [19] provides synchronized multi-source 
UAV-satellite data for GNSS-denied scenarios, but its altitude range is 
limited and lacks continuous variable-altitude trajectory annotations. 
ViLD [20] supports self-supervised cross-view localization with high-
altitude UAV imagery, yet it does not provide frame-level IMU and pose 
metadata for filtering-based sequential evaluation. This inherently re-
stricts their ability to support continuous, variable-altitude localization 
evaluation under realistic flight conditions, highlighting the necessity 
of a temporally continuous, multi-altitude benchmark like MAFS.
2.2. Absolute visual localization algorithms for UAVs
Absolute UAV localization methods face distinct trade-offs between 
robustness, computational cost, and generalization. Template match-
ing methods [5,6] estimate pose via direct pixel-level comparison but

2.3. Semantic-based scene localization methods
To overcome the fragility of appearance-level matching, recent stud-
ies have leveraged high-level semantic scene understanding (e.g., se-
mantic graph matching, histograms, bag-of-words, and Gaussian mix-
tures) [24–27]. The latest R2PLoc framework [28] further designs a 
hierarchical semantic aggregation module to fuse multi-scale semantic 
features for region-to-point UAV geo-localization, achieving improved 
robustness against scale and viewpoint shifts. However, most exist-
ing semantic methods formulate localization as a discrete database 
matching task.
In contrast, our proposed semantic-aware probabilistic localization 
explicitly models 4-DoF pose estimation as a continuous Bayesian fil-
tering process. Instead of nearest-neighbor retrieval, SWA-PF utilizes 
a Semantic-Weighted Distance Map (SWDM) to directly evaluate the 
observation likelihood of continuous particle states. This inherently 
preserves multi-modal hypotheses, integrates temporal motion priors, 
and naturally accommodates continuous variations in altitude and yaw 
without the overhead of maintaining an exhaustive template database.
3. Dataset
To evaluate the proposed semantic-weighted localization frame-
work, we construct the Multi-Altitude Flight Segments (MAFS) dataset 
with synchronized UAV imagery, satellite maps, and trajectory annota-
tions, along with its pixel-level semantic extension dataset (Semantic-
MAFS).
3.1. MAFS dataset
MAFS is a large-scale benchmark for continuous UAV localiza-
tion. As summarized in Table 1, existing datasets adopt fragmented or 
sparsely sampled trajectories, whereas MAFS features long temporally 
continuous flight sequences to support sequential and filtering-based 
evaluation.
MAFS covers 14 university campuses and a 2 km × 2 km urban 
region in Hangzhou, China, collected by a DJI Mavic 3E UAV. As shown 
in Fig. 2, it includes fixed-altitude and variable-altitude flight modes 
covering an altitude range of 100–500 m.
The dataset has three core advantages: (1) It supports multi-altitude 
evaluation with both fixed and continuous variable-altitude trajecto-
ries, filling the altitude variation gap in prior benchmarks. (2) It pro-
vides tightly synchronized 4K/30fps video and IMU data with frame-
level pose metadata (latitude, longitude, altitude, yaw, velocity) for 
temporal-continuous localization research. (3) It covers diverse urban 
scenes with a total trajectory length over 40.5 km, each paired with a 
geo-referenced Google Maps satellite image (zoom level 19).
Survey-grade RTK/PPK ground truth is not included. The UAV in-
tegrates a dual-frequency GNSS module (nominal ±0.1 m RTK, ±0.5 m 
standalone accuracy under open sky). All data are collected in open 
campus environments with minimal signal obstruction, so onboard 
GNSS measurements are adopted as the error evaluation reference, a 
widely accepted proxy for GNSS-denied scenarios.
3.2. SemanticMAFS dataset
SemanticMAFS extends MAFS with pixel-level semantic annotations 
for both views to support semantic cross-view localization. Frames are 
uniformly sampled and manually annotated via Eiseg [29]: satellite 
images have 4 categories (building, ground, road, vegetation), and UAV 
images follow the VDD protocol with 7 categories (roof, wall, ground, 
road, vegetation, water, vehicle). Representative annotated samples are 
presented in Fig. 3, and average per-image annotation time exceeds 
60 min due to high scene complexity.
Per-class pixel distribution is summarized in Table 2. Vegetation 
dominates both views (53.81% UAV, 58.37% satellite), while stable 
structural classes (roofs, roads) provide reliable alignment cues. Inter-
annotator agreement exceeds 99% pixel overlap, confirming high anno-
tation consistency. The dataset partitioning for reproducible evaluation 
is detailed in Table 3, and joint training with VDD mitigates the limited 
scale of manual annotations.
3.3. Dataset availability
MAFS, SemanticMAFS, source code, pretrained models, and ex-
periment scripts will be publicly released under an academic non-
commercial license (v1.0 tag for reproducibility), with long-term main-
tenance following semantic versioning.

we design two core components: a multi-altitude semantic segmenta-

4.1. Semantic segmentation
We perform semantic segmentation on both satellite and UAV im-
ages. For satellite imagery, a VGG-pretrained U-Net is trained on three 
densely annotated Google Maps images, which suffices to capture the 
semantic structures required for localization given consistent visual 
styles.
UAV top-view images are more challenging due to altitude variation 
and partial building visibility. We adopt a 7-class scheme following the 
VDD protocol [30] and select SegFormer-B0 as the backbone to balance 
accuracy and onboard efficiency.
As shown in Fig. 5, despite real-world noise, segmentation quality 
suffices for downstream localization. Stochastic data augmentation is 
applied during training to improve generalization.

4.2.3. Particle representation and memory efficiency
A key advantage of SWA-PF is its compact particle representation: 
each particle stores only a 4-DoF state vector, forming a lightweight 
𝑁× 4 tensor with strictly linear (𝑁) memory complexity. This design 
yields negligible per-particle overhead, far lower than feature-matching 
methods that maintain high-dimensional descriptors. It enables high-
density PF on SWaP-constrained onboard platforms without memory 
exhaustion. As verified in experiments, GPU memory is dominated by 
semantic map tensors rather than Semantic observation of particles, 
ensuring strong scalability for large-area localization.
4.2.4. Parameter setting and tuning
Motion noise is set to 𝜖= 15 (Eq. (3)), exceeding typical IMU error 
to emulate challenging conditions. Semantic weights 𝛼𝐿 mitigate class 
imbalance in the measurement model, rebalancing class contributions 
and improving sensitivity to geometrically meaningful regions. The nor-
malization constant 𝛾= 10 balances convergence speed and prevents 
premature particle collapse.
Notably, no trajectory-specific tuning is performed: a single unified 
hyperparameter set is used across all trajectories, datasets, and ablation 
studies to ensure fair comparison and validate framework robustness.
4.3. Performance optimization
Discretized Yaw Optimization. Image rotation is a major PF compu-
tational bottleneck. We discretize yaw into 100 uniform bins (3.6◦ per 
bin) and pre-rotate UAV images to these angles. As demonstrated in Fig. 
9, particles then index the nearest pre-computed rotation, reducing per-
particle rotation overhead from (𝑁) to a constant (100) per iteration 
with negligible quantization error.
Matrix Batch Optimization. Further acceleration is achieved via 
optimized matrix stacking, eliminating explicit loops in weight cal-
culation. With GPU parallelization, 20,000 particles are evaluated in 
4.9 s.
Density-Based State Estimation. Direct averaging of posterior par-
ticles causes bias from outliers. We use DBSCAN [33] to remove low-
density outliers and identify dominant clusters, then compute cluster 
centroids as the final estimate once posterior covariance falls below a 
threshold, yielding robust estimation under noisy conditions.

ventional PF pipeline includes particle initialization, motion predic-

SWA-PF introduces four core improvements: (1) semantic-aware 
measurement model based on SWDM; (2) center distance field weight-

5.1. Comparison of particle filter-based localization algorithms
To comprehensively evaluate the proposed SWA-PF, we established 
a rigorous experimental setup encompassing diverse flight scenarios, 
multiple baseline comparisons, and a systematic set of evaluation met-
rics.
5.1.1. Datasets and baselines
SWA-PF is evaluated on three MAFS trajectories: two fixed-altitude 
(200 m) scenarios (MAFS-03, MAFS-10) and one variable-altitude 
(150–500 m) scenario (MAFS-14). We use 5000 particles for MAFS-
03/10 and 40,000 for MAFS-14 to accommodate the larger search 
space. Particles are uniformly sampled based on the dataset protocol: 
𝑥∈[0, 𝑊𝑚𝑎𝑝], 𝑦∈[0, 𝐻𝑚𝑎𝑝], 𝑧∈[50, 500] m.
We compare SWA-PF with traditional feature-based and template-
matching methods (MI, HIST, SIFT, AKAZE, BRISK, ORB) and deep 
learning-based baselines (OS-FPI, DenseUAV, SSPT). All traditional 
methods are integrated into the same particle filter framework as 
SWA-PF to ensure fairness.
The motion noise is set to 𝜖= 15 to emulate challenging conditions, 
and the normalization constant is fixed at 𝛾= 10. To balance statistical 
reliability and computational cost, all particle filter-based methods 
share identical random seeds across 20 independent runs for the same 
run index, ensuring identical particle initialization and stochastic noise 
realization. The adequacy of this sample size (𝑛= 20) is mathematically 
supported by post-hoc statistical power analysis. Because statistical 
power is fundamentally driven by the magnitude of the effect size, 
the exceptionally large performance gaps observed in our experiments 
(with Cliff’s 𝛿 consistently exceeding the threshold for a ‘‘Large’’ effect, 
|𝛿| ≥0.474) ensure that 𝑛= 20 yields a statistical power (1 −𝛽) well 
above the conventional 0.80 threshold. This confirms that the eval-
uation is adequately powered to detect true performance differences 
without a significant risk of Type II errors.

5.1.2. Evaluation metrics
We systematically assess performance along three dimensions – 
computational efficiency, localization accuracy, and statistical
reliability – as summarized in Table 4. Computational efficiency met-
rics are Fitting Time (s), the duration from particle initialization to 
convergence (covering semantic segmentation, state prediction, weight 
computation, resampling, and final state estimation), and Finish Time 
(s), the total cumulative time needed to complete full trajectory local-
ization from the initial frame. Localization accuracy is measured by 
RMSE (p) (root mean square error of global position estimates), Median 
(p) (median error, robust to extreme outliers), and Recall@10 (%) 
(percentage of frames with position error < 10 m). For statistical eval-
uation (vs. SWA-PF), we report means and 95% confidence intervals 
(CI) over 20 independent runs, using the format 𝑎±𝑏. Because absolute 
positioning errors are zero-bounded and severely right-skewed with 
occasional extreme outliers, we employ pairwise Wilcoxon signed-rank 
tests with Holm-Bonferroni correction (𝛼= 0.05) instead of parametric 
alternatives. The 𝑝-value tests the null hypothesis of no median RMSE 
difference, while Cliff’s 𝛿 quantifies effect size (negligible |𝛿| < 0.147, 
small 0.147 ≤|𝛿| < 0.33, medium 0.33 ≤|𝛿| < 0.474, large |𝛿| ≥0.474).

5.1.6. GPU memory usage and scalability
GPU memory usage is profiled to evaluate scalability. During ini-
tialization, the method consumes approximately 3004.21 MB of GPU 
memory, increasing to a peak of 3499.58 MB during particle iterations. 
This increase is dominated by tensor representations of satellite and 
UAV semantic maps rather than particle storage. Particle states are 
stored as a compact 𝑛× 4 tensor, resulting in negligible per-particle 
memory overhead. Quantitatively, assuming standard 32-bit floating-
point precision, each particle requires merely 16 bytes of memory. 
Even for the most complex variable-altitude scenario (MAFS-14) uti-

40,000 particles, the total state memory footprint is 
strictly bounded to approximately 0.61 MB. Extrapolating to larger-
scale deployments, maintaining 100,000 particles would consume only 
about 1.53 MB. This is orders of magnitude lower than the memory 
required to cache dense visual descriptors or high-dimensional network 
embeddings in retrieval-based methods. Consequently, the scalability 
of SWA-PF is primarily constrained by satellite map resolution and 
coverage, rather than by the number of particles.
In terms of runtime scaling, the per-iteration computational com-
plexity grows approximately linearly with the number of particles 
(𝑁), as each particle requires semantic cropping and weight compu-
tation. Increasing the satellite map size beyond the current 2 km × 2 
km setting primarily enlarges the spatial search domain, which may 
require a higher particle count to maintain spatial coverage density. 
However, the per-particle state representation remains a compact n ×4 
tensor, resulting in negligible memory growth per particle. Therefore, 
runtime increases are mainly driven by particle density requirements 
rather than by map size itself, while GPU memory usage is dominated 
by the semantic map tensor resolution.

5.2.2. Performance under low resolution and temporal map variations
A practical advantage of the proposed framework is its ability to 
operate reliably with low-resolution satellite imagery. In real-world 
deployment scenarios, high-resolution maps are not always available 
due to bandwidth limitations, storage constraints, or outdated satellite 
coverage.
To evaluate robustness under degraded map quality, we reduce 
the satellite image resolution by half and re-run the localization ex-
periments. Despite the doubled pixel-to-meter ratio, the resulting lo-
calization error exhibits only marginal variation compared with the 
original-resolution setting, as illustrated in Fig. 16. This behavior indi-
cates that the semantic-weighted measurement model relies primarily 
on structurally stable semantic distributions rather than fine-grained 
texture details.
Unlike appearance-driven or dense feature-matching methods,
which often degrade significantly under resolution reduction, SWA-
PF maintains stable convergence behavior and comparable positioning 
accuracy. This robustness provides explicit operational advantages for 
real-world UAV deployment. First, it drastically reduces onboard solid-
state storage requirements, allowing resource-constrained edge devices 
to cache reference maps for vastly larger geographic areas. Second, in 
emergency response or remote exploration missions where high-speed 
communication infrastructure is compromised or unavailable, SWA-PF 
facilitates on-the-fly map updates via low-bandwidth telemetry links, 
as downloading gigabytes of high-resolution imagery in such environ-
ments is physically infeasible. This robustness substantially enhances 
practical deployability, allowing localization over large geographic 
regions using highly accessible, lightweight satellite maps.

We analyze the weight distribution for each semantic category. 
Localization methods based on a single semantic fail consistently. When

5.5. Summary of experiments
We conducted extensive experiments on the MAFS dataset under 
fixed-altitude and variable-altitude conditions. The proposed SWA-PF 
achieved approximately a tenfold speedup over classical feature-based 
methods within the same particle filter framework. The global position-
ing RMSE remained below 25 p, and the recall for errors under 10 m 
exceeded 95% on the MAFS-10 trajectory. Full 4-DoF pose estimates, 
including altitude and yaw, were obtained within seconds using only a 
low-resolution satellite map. SWA-PF also demonstrated strong robust-
ness to map resolution degradation and altitude variations. Statistical 
analysis confirmed significant improvements over all baselines (𝑝<
0.05) with large effect sizes (|𝛿| ≥0.474). These results show that SWA-
PF effectively balances localization accuracy, computational efficiency, 
and memory usage for onboard UAV deployment.
6. Conclusion
This work tackled key limitations of retrieval-based UAV localiza-
tion by introducing the multi-altitude MAFS dataset with pixel-level

semantic annotations (SemanticMAFS) and proposing the Semantic-
Weighted Adaptive Particle Filter (SWA-PF). SWA-PF combines a com-
pact 4-DoF particle representation of strictly linear memory complexity, 
a semantic weighting mechanism that leverages structurally stable 
scene layouts, and an optimized adaptive filter. These designs yield a 
tenfold end-to-end speedup over conventional feature extraction within 
the same particle filter framework, keep the global positioning error 
below 10 m relative to onboard GNSS, and deliver 4-DoF pose estimates 
within seconds. Crucially, SWA-PF shows exceptional robustness to 
low-resolution satellite imagery: unlike appearance-driven methods, it 
relies on high-level structural distributions rather than fine textures, 
sustaining accuracy even when map resolution is halved. This enables 
large-scale UAV localization with lightweight, storage-efficient maps, 
eliminating the need for high-resolution data in bandwidth-limited or 
mission-critical settings.
7. Limitations
Despite these advances, SWA-PF exhibits several limitations. First, 
severe class imbalance – particularly when vegetation exceeds 50% 
– flattens the posterior distribution and slows convergence. Second, 
extreme domain shifts such as heavy snow or highly unstructured 
rural terrains violate the urban structural priors, causing matching 
failures. Third, the current implementation depends on GPU accelera-
tion; CPU-only execution of SegFormer inference and matrix operations 
is approximately 15× slower, challenging real-time deployment on 
resource-constrained platforms.
8. Future work
Building on these findings, future research will proceed along three 
main directions. We will develop domain-adaptive semantic weighting 
schemes to extend robustness to rural and unstructured environments, 
and design uncertainty-aware mechanisms that mitigate segmentation 
noise. Furthermore, we aim to reduce reliance on continuous IMU input 
by integrating lightweight global re-localization modules, enabling the 
system to recover from tracking loss and operate reliably in intermittent 
scenarios.
