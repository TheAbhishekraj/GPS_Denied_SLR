A B S T R A C T
Scene matching plays an important role for UAV positioning under GPS-denied conditions. However, traditional
feature matching methods struggle with scale, viewpoint, and illumination heterogeneity between UAV and
satellite images, often relying on fixed thresholds that cannot adaptively suppress outliers. To address these
issues, this paper introduces a scene matching method that integrates triplet relationships with a control-
theoretic optimization strategy. Specifically, SuperPoint is first used for feature extraction, followed by triplet
relationships and a greedy selection strategy based on matching scores and spatial distribution to improve
initial model robustness. For the inlier optimization stage, this paper is the first to embed a sliding mode
controller in the iterative refinement process, enabling dynamic residual feedback and adaptive outlier
suppression. Additionally, to gain an insight of the working mechanism of sliding mode control strategy on
feature matching, this paper provides a theoretical mechanism analysis, a Lyapunov-based convergence proof,
and a parameter sensitivity analysis to clarify the physical interpretability and tunability of the SMC(sliding
mode control) module. Extensive experiments on the University-1652 dataset, DenseUAV dataset, and a self-
collected UAV dataset demonstrate that the proposed method significantly improves spatial representativeness,
outlier removal capability, and matching stability. Compared to the RANSAC and TRESAC series of methods,
the proposed method achieves an average improvement of approximately 4.51%–9.31% in F-score, reaching up
to 96.24% in some complex scenarios, while maintaining relatively stable computational efficiency. The results
verify the high matching accuracy and robustness of the proposed method under GPS-denied environments.
1. Introduction
The most widely adopted UAV navigation method currently com-
bines inertial navigation system (INS) with the global positioning
system (GPS) [1]. This integrated navigation strategy achieves high-
precision positioning in most environments, ensuring stable UAV oper-
ations across various missions. However, in GPS-denied environments
— such as urban canyons [2], dense forests [3], and underground or
indoor scenarios [4] — where GPS signals are blocked or severely
disrupted, traditional GPS-based navigation methods often fail, directly
compromising the safety and continuity of UAV missions.
To address this challenge, visual localization methods have emerged
as a viable alternative. Visual localization methods can be broadly
categorized into two types based on their reliance on prior environ-
mental knowledge [5]: one comprises mapless autonomous mapping

Among visual localization methods that require prior knowledge,
scene matching is currently regarded as the most prominent tech-
nique [10]. The workflow of scene matching in GPS-denied envi-
ronments is illustrated in Fig. 1. Specifically, its core mission lies
in robust feature extraction and matching between aerial and ref-
erence images. The precision of feature matching directly impacts
the accuracy of position estimation. Commonly used feature detec-
tion and description algorithms include SIFT(Scale Invariant Feature
Transform), SURF(Speeded Up Robust Features), and ORB(Oriented
FAST and Rotated BRIEF), all of which exhibit good scale and rotation
invariance and are widely employed in traditional image matching mis-
sions. Nevertheless, applying these methods to UAV aerial and satellite
image matching encounters numerous challenges. First, the differing
imaging modalities of UAV aerial imagery and satellite imagery result
in substantial heterogeneity between the two different image types,
including variations in resolution and viewpoint, which can introduce
numerous outliers in the feature extraction results. Second, traditional
feature matching methods often neglect spatial structural information
within images, failing to ensure a reasonable spatial distribution of ini-
tial matches, which adversely affects the robustness and generalization
capability of model estimation.
To address the above issues, this paper proposes a novel UAV scene
matching method that integrates triplet relationship and control theory
concepts, introducing mechanisms from modern control theory into
the image matching process to improve the efficiency and stability
of outlier filtering. The primary contributions and innovations of this
paper are summarized as follows:
(1) To address the limitation that traditional feature matching meth-
ods in GPS-denied environments rely solely on Top-K score selection
for the initial inlier subset, which often results in excessive clustering,
limited spatial spread, and inadequate geometric representation, this
paper proposes a greedy subset selection strategy based on matching
score and spatial distribution. This strategy first selects high-scoring
points and then introduces a spatial distribution heuristic that itera-
tively maximizes the minimum Euclidean distance between candidate
and selected points, thereby greatly enhancing the spatial diversity
and representativeness of the initial inlier set. As a result, this method
significantly improves the geometric consistency and overall robustness
of UAV aerial image matching against heterogeneous satellite images.
(2) To address the limitation of traditional residual-sorting-based
inlier update methods, which usually adopt fixed step sizes and static
thresholds and therefore lack global feedback to adjust for dynamic
residual variations, making them prone to local optima or model oscil-
lations in complex scenes, this paper introduces, for the first time, the
concept of sliding mode control (SMC) from modern control theory into
the inlier optimization process for image matching. A novel population-
based inlier iterative optimization strategy is proposed, combining error
feedback, adaptive perturbation, and residual sorting. By designing
a sliding surface function that incorporates both the error term and
its derivative, and by implementing a continuous reaching law, the
method dynamically regulates the convergence of the inlier set. This
significantly improves the stability of geometric model fitting conver-
gence and the accuracy of outlier removal in UAV scene matching
involving high outlier ratios.
In summary, this paper is the first to incorporate sliding mode
control into the iterative optimization of feature matching algorithms,
overcoming the limitations of traditional image matching optimization
methods that rely solely on geometric structures or local score updates.
The optimization method proposes a novel inlier selection mechanism
from a feedback control perspective, providing a new technical solution
for high-precision autonomous UAV visual localization in GPS-denied
environments.
2. Related work
As the demand for UAV navigation increases in GPS-denied environ-
ments, scene matching has emerged as a critical alternative solution.
Feature extraction and matching, as pivotal techniques in scene match-
ing, predominantly determine localization performance in terms of
both accuracy and robustness. In recent years, considerable research
efforts have focused on image feature extraction, feature matching,
and robust geometric model estimation, resulting in notable advance-
ments. Nonetheless, when faced with challenges such as viewpoint
variations, scale changes, and high outlier ratios between UAV and
satellite images, current methods, such as RANSAC remain inadequate
in spatial locality during subset selection, and convergence stability
during optimization. This section classifies the related work into three
categories: feature extraction methods, feature matching methods, and
geometric model optimization and outlier filtering.

and ORB [13], have been widely adopted due to their strong scale

methods achieve superior performance in scenarios with minor view-
point or lighting variations. However, in cross-scale, large viewpoint
difference, and heterogeneous UAV–satellite matching scenarios, these
methods remain constrained by training data distribution and lack
sufficient adaptability to real multi-source environments [22]. Thus, re-
lying solely on feature extraction is insufficient to guarantee matching
reliability; the absence of geometric outlier rejection strategies often
leads to numerous outliers.
2.2. Feature matching methods
Historically, most feature matching methods have employed fixed-
threshold nearest neighbor distance ratio algorithms (e.g., K-nearest
neighbors and Lowe’s ratio test) [23]. However, these methods heavily
depend on appropriate threshold selection and lack effective guidance
for threshold tuning and geometric consistency filtering under varying
conditions. To enhance robustness, previous algorithms have intro-
duced geometric constraints, such as pairwise compatibility based on
edge distance consistency (e.g., GMS [24], NM-Net [25]) or triplet
consistency scoring methods [26]. San Jiang et al. developed local
geometric constraints in adjacency structures via Delaunay triangula-
tion [27]. Qin Ye et al. [28] proposed a two-stage feature matching
approach: the first stage employed an adaptive and strict seed point
selection algorithm, while the second stage recovered correct matches
discarded in the initial phase. Although this method achieves sat-
isfactory matching results, its two-stage design inevitably increases
computational cost due to the need to both reject outliers and recover
correct correspondences.
In deep learning-based feature matching, Sarlin et al. introduced the
SuperGlue algorithm following their earlier SuperPoint proposal [29].
Zhiwen Liu et al. incorporated hierarchical and block strategies into
SuperGlue, integrating UAV prior data to enhance feature matching
accuracy and efficiency [30]. Han Gao et al. proposed the LightGlue
network, which extracts features from neighboring UAV images and
leverages their sequential relationships to perform feature matching
within overlapping regions. Despite achieving strong matching results
on public datasets, these deep learning networks exhibit suboptimal
performance when matching UAV aerial and satellite images with
significant viewpoint and scale variations, primarily due to the ab-
sence of effective outliers filtering modules and suitable multi-view
training datasets. Consequently, outliers issues persist. Furthermore,
graph-optimization methods (such as the Hierarchical Motion Consis-
tency Constraint [31]) remove outliers by reinforcing spatial or motion
consistency, but their computational complexity is high, limiting real-
time UAV deployment. In contrast, the greedy subset selection strategy
based on matching score and spatial distribution utilized in this paper
maintains high matching scores while maximizing spatial distances
between selected subsets and candidates. This method achieves su-
perior coverage and diversity, thereby significantly improving subset
representativeness and the robustness of subsequent model fitting.
2.3. Geometric model optimization and outlier removal
In geometric fitting, RANSAC (Random Sample Consensus) [32] and
its variants (e.g., PROSAC [33], LO-RANSAC [34]) remain the most
widely used robust estimators for geometric model estimation. These
methods maximize inlier sets through random sampling. However, due
to excessive iterations, fixed update rules, and the absence of feedback
mechanisms, these RANSAC-based estimators are prone to chattering
or stagnation when outliers are abundant. Certain optimizations, such
as Graph Cut RANSAC [35], can enhance robustness but depend on
global optimization, which increase computational cost. Hanlin Guo
et al. introduced TRESAC (Triplet Relationship Guided Sampling Con-
sensus) [36], a robust model estimator to leverage triplet relationship
for effective initialization and improve sampling process. By utiliz-
ing triplet relationship, TRESAC improves outlier filtering capability.
Nonetheless, by scoring potential inliers using a Top-K strategy without
feedback, it suffers from spatial clustering: high-score points often con-
centrate locally or within dense outlier regions, resulting in insufficient
initial subset coverage and misguiding model fitting.
To address these shortcomings, recent research has explored feed-
back mechanisms inspired by control theory. For instance, PSA (PID-
inspired Search Algorithm) has been applied to optimize search pro-
cesses, demonstrating that feedback regulation can alleviate random
sampling behavior. However, it still lacks model residual-based in-
lier updating and integrated optimization [37]. Accordingly, this pa-
per integrates residual ranking mechanisms with sliding mode control
theory to establish a dynamic ‘‘error feedback–disturbance–update’’
loop, which endows model iterations with convergence and chattering
damping capabilities, thereby markedly enhancing fitting accuracy and
stability.
2.4. Summary
In summary, although current scene matching algorithms perform
well in specific scenarios, existing feature matching methods exhibit
limited robustness when applied to heterogeneous images and can only
partially suppress outliers. Among triplet relationship-based geometric
model fitting methods, the Top-K strategy often results in poor spatial
coverage, which can lead to outlier clustering and misguide the model
fitting process. Furthermore, geometric model fitting generally lacks
adaptive feedback mechanisms, often resulting in model chattering and
convergence failures. To address these challenges, this paper proposes
a matching framework that integrates triplet relationship and control
feedback optimization, aiming to enhance the efficiency and precision
of UAV visual localization in GPS-denied and other complex scenarios.
This framework provides a reliable technical pathway for achieving
stable UAV localization under GPS-denied conditions.

3. Methodology
The primary workflow of the proposed UAV scene matching method
for GPS-denied environments is illustrated in Fig. 2. Specifically, the
algorithm first extracts image feature points using the SuperPoint al-
gorithm, then applies Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce
data dimensionality, and subsequently constructs a candidate matching
point set guided by triplet relationship. To ensure the rationality of geo-

from the candidate matches. Finally, by leveraging sliding mode con-

is designed to dynamically suppress and remove outliers, effectively
mitigating the chattering and unstable convergence that often occur
during geometric model fitting in traditional optimization methods.

To address this issue, this paper introduces sliding mode control
theory from modern control theory for the first time to enhance the
stability and robustness of the optimization process. Sliding Mode
Control (SMC) is a class of nonlinear control methods characterized by
significant robustness. Its core idea is to construct a sliding surface and
design a switching control law so that the system state reaches and
remains in the vicinity of a predefined sliding manifold within a finite
time, thereby possessing an innate rejection capability against model
uncertainties and external disturbances [39]. Owing to its insensitivity
to parameter variations and noise interference, SMC has been widely
applied in complex chemical process control, nonlinear dynamical
systems, and engineering systems with strong uncertainties [40]. In
recent years, researchers have further integrated fuzzy logic, adap-
tive mechanisms, and other strategies into the SMC framework to
enhance system stability and convergence performance under unknown
or strongly perturbed conditions [41]. For instance, in scenarios such
as complex reaction kinetic modeling, large-scale industrial processes,
and underactuated system control, robust control strategies based on
sliding modes have proven effective in coping with system uncertainties
and noise disturbances. The sliding mode control principle is illustrated
in Fig. 4. Specifically, the principle of a sliding mode controller com-
prises several key stages: first, defining a reference input according to
system requirements, which serves as the desired trajectory; second,
designing a sliding surface that characterizes the desired dynamic
behavior of the state trajectory; third, using the sliding surface and
system state to devise a sliding mode reaching law that drives the
system state to reach and maintain the sliding surface; fourth, applying
the control input to the controlled object via actuators; and finally,
continuously measuring the actual system state through sensors and
feeding it back to the sliding mode controller, thereby forming a closed-
loop adjustment that dynamically suppresses state errors and facilitates
adaptive convergence. Compared to traditional fixed-parameter control
strategies, sliding mode controllers are highly robust against parameter
perturbations and external disturbances, allowing for precise control
and convergence even when system dynamics vary significantly or
uncertainties exist. Accordingly, this paper proposes an optimization
strategy inspired by sliding mode control, functioning as a geometric
model inlier iterative update framework endowed with self-feedback
and self-regulation capabilities, embedded within the inlier update
procedure to enhance the stability and robustness of geometric model
estimation in UAV visual matching.
In image feature matching optimization, outliers are essentially
points that deviate excessively from the target model, and the concepts
of bias adjustment and target solution alignment in control theory
align well with this task. In this paper, the system error is defined
as the deviation between the current candidate inlier subset and the
historically optimal inlier structure, with the inlier subset regarded as
the controlled object. The sliding mode controller dynamically adjusts
the inlier subset structure, adapts the model update strategy based on
error feedback trends, and achieves a stable and rapid model fitting
process, thereby enabling robust outlier filtering in feature matching.
Meanwhile, this paper proposes a ‘‘residual sorting assisted update
mechanism’’ that, during each iteration, computes the residuals of the
current best model, selects the top K inliers as a ‘‘trusted region’’,
and combines it with a termination criterion to guide the search out
of local optima, ensuring stable convergence while enhancing global
exploration capability. It should be noted that the chattering prob-
lem discussed in classical continuous-time sliding mode control typi-
cally originates from the implementation limitations of high-frequency
switching control laws in physical actuation systems. However, in the
inlier optimization problem for image matching studied in this paper,
the SMC mechanism does not directly act on a physical system or
actuator; its control output is used to guide the continuous updat-
ing and probability normalization process of the matching mask. To
suppress potential discrete oscillation behavior, a continuous approx-
imation based on the hyperbolic tangent function is adopted in the
control law design, combined with Sigmoid mapping to achieve smooth
updates, thereby avoiding the high-frequency switching introduced by
the traditional sign function. This design is equivalent to introducing a
boundary layer near the sliding surface, effectively reducing numerical

first openly available dataset for UAV self-localization tasks. DenseUAV
provides densely sampled UAV imagery of low-altitude urban environ-

5.8.4. Fitness function design and balancing parameter 𝜆
To analyze the influence of the balancing parameter 𝜆on the
method’s performance, a parameter sensitivity study is conducted on
50 image pairs selected from each of the three UAV scene datasets
for different 𝜆values. As shown in Fig. 20, when 𝜆= 0, the fitness
function consists solely of the geometric residual term. In this case,
the optimization process relies mainly on local geometric consistency,
making it susceptible to interference from occasional low residual but
structurally unreliable matches. This leads to insufficient stability of
the inlier set, resulting in an overall lower F score. As 𝜆increases,
the matching structure score gradually participates in the optimization.
When 𝜆lies in the interval [0.3, 0.5], the geometric residual constraint
and the triplet structural consistency complement each other effec-
tively: outliers are suppressed while good coverage of true matches is
maintained, allowing the proposed method to achieve stable and rela-
tively high F scores across all three datasets. When 𝜆increases further
(𝜆 > 0.5), the structural score dominates the fitness function, while the
geometric constraint is relatively weakened. This tends to retain some
incorrect matches that are structurally consistent but geometrically
inconsistent, especially in scenes with uneven feature distribution or
repetitive textures, leading to a decline in matching precision and
overall F-score.
Based on the above analysis, 𝜆= 0.5 is uniformly adopted as the
balancing parameter in all experiments to achieve a favorable trade off
between geometric consistency and structural constraint.
6. Discussion on applicability and limitations
The proposed method is primarily designed to address performance
degradation in UAV scene matching under GPS-denied environments,
particularly in scenarios involving large viewpoint changes, scale incon-
sistencies, and high outlier ratios. By integrating triplet relationships
constraints with optimization strategy based on sliding mode controller,
the method enhances model fitting robustness when a sufficient number
of geometrically consistent initial correspondences are available. As
with most feature matching frameworks based on geometry consis-
tency, the performance of the proposed method inherently depends on

the quality and quantity of initial correspondences. When the feature
extraction stage yields very sparse or highly degraded matches, or
when the geometric distribution of correspondences becomes severely
degenerate, the subsequent residual driven optimization may offer
limited improvement. Similar limitations may also arise in texture-poor
regions or scenes dominated by repetitive patterns, where initial match
ambiguity is difficult to resolve purely through geometric refinement.
Among these general limitations, extreme appearance variations
such as pronounced illumination changes (e.g., day-to-night transitions)
or seasonal differences (e.g., summer-to-winter) represent particularly
challenging cases. Under such conditions, significant changes in local
texture, contrast, and global intensity distribution can substantially
reduce feature repeatability, primarily affecting the feature extraction
and initial matching stages rather than the geometric optimization
process itself. Nevertheless, when a minimal set of reliable initial
correspondences can still be preserved, the proposed method remains
effective in suppressing appearance-induced outliers and stabilizing the
convergence of the geometric model. Future work will explore the inte-
gration of illumination and season invariant feature representations, as
well as cross-temporal learning-based matching frameworks, to further
extend the applicability of the proposed method to more challenging
real-world GPS-denied scenarios.
7. Conclusion
This paper introduces a UAV scene matching method for GPS-denied
environments by integrating triplet relationships with a sliding mode
control (SMC)-based iterative optimization strategy. SuperPoint is used
for robust feature extraction, followed by triplet relationships and a
greedy selection strategy that enhances the spatial representativeness
of the initial subset. For inlier refinement, this paper is the first to intro-
duce SMC into feature matching, designing a residual feedback-based
dynamic update mechanism that stabilizes convergence and suppresses
outliers.
In addition to the algorithm design, this paper conducts a theoretical
mechanism analysis, Lyapunov-based convergence proof, and param-
eter sensitivity evaluation, clarifying the physical interpretability and
tunability of the SMC module. Comparative experiments and ablation
studies on three datasets verify that the proposed method achieves
higher matching precision, stronger outlier removal, and better con-
vergence stability than mainstream approaches such as RANSAC and
TRESAC++. Despite these advances, the proposed method may face
limitations such as sparse feature points and diminished neighbor-
hood consistency in large-scale dynamic scenes, extremely low-texture
regions, or environments affected by multi-source noise interference,
which consequently affect matching robustness and real-time perfor-
mance. Future research will focus on extending the scalability of this
method within multi-sensor fusion, adaptive thresholding, and end-to-
end learning frameworks, facilitating its practical implementation and
engineering application in complex UAV navigation missions.
