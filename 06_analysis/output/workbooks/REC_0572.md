Abstract—
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have gained a lot of
attention due to their applications in various sectors. Most of
fully autonomous UAVs navigation system is dependent on
GNSS for localization. But the security threats that emanates
from GPS system; spoofing and jamming makes the reliance
on GNSS problematic. In this work, we proposed a fully au-
tonomous UAV navigation system without GNSS and wireless
communication. The application area of interest in this work is
military battlefield. The proposed navigation method combines
information gathered by Intelligence Preparation for Battle-
field (IPB) and Intelligence Surveillance Reconnaissance(ISR)
group and onboard sensors. Then it applies AI-enabled multi
sensor fusion, motion planning and decision making to accom-
plish the navigation successfully. The integrated way of navi-
gation used to assess the conceptual possibility of autonomous
navigation without the use of external navigation system and
reliable operation of UAV at GPS and wireless signal denied
environment. In addition, to ensure reliable operation of the
navigation system, devices health monitoring system through
the use of distributed monitoring agent and AI algorithms are
proposed. The conceptual architecture is designed as six layer
architecture with cross-layer interaction feature.
Index Terms—Autonomous navigation, multi-sensor fusion, AI-
enabled motion planning and control, distributed monitoring.
I. INTRODUCTION
UAVs have gained increased attention from the research
community and industry due to their potential in various
sectors. Their use have been demonstrated in various applica-
tions such as in agriculture, surveillance, photography, target
tracking, logistics, disaster management, and remote sensing.
To accomplish any mission, a UAV must be aware of its
states such as current location, navigation speed, environmen-
tal conditions and flight direction and location of its target.
Navigation capability is among the key features for enabling
autonomous UAVs. Navigation is a continuous process where
the UAVs make a plan on how to safely reach the target
location and execute the mission by relying on the various state
information including location. The required autonomy level
of a navigation method varies depending on the application
type and the mission environment. In short-range missions
with possibility for line-of-site operations, the navigation can
be manually controlled by the remote pilot at the ground
station. In semi-autonomous navigation of the UAVs, there is a
need for the navigation system to be assisted from the ground
stations, GNSS system and sensors. In most of the previously
proposed fully autonomous navigation method, GNSS plays a
crucial role in UAVs localization. GNSS is used for planning
a predefined flight route and it is also involved in correcting
or offsetting the deviation on the onboard IMU sensors based
relative position tracking. Though GNSS dependent navigation
is a most common practice, it is vulnerable to spoofing
and jamming attacks as well as it is unreliable in some
environments such as indoor, urban with high rise buildings
and battlefields. To address this issue, the research community
followed three different approaches. The first approach focuses
on detection and mitigation/prevention of GPS spoofing and
jamming attacks [1]–[4]. The second one focuses on assisting
the loss of GPS signal with other localization method and
the third is developing a navigation method without the use
of GNSS system [17], [18]. This work focuses on the third
approach where the navigation system is made to be able to
operate autonomously without GNSS. Autonomous navigation
encompasses the capability of UAVs to perform environmental
perception, localization, collision avoidance, motion planning
and motion control without GNSS.
In this work, we propose the conceptual architecture of an
autonomous navigation system and presented its components
design. The proposed navigation system relies on data col-
lected by the onboard sensors and multi-sensor fusion for the
perception of the environment. The autonomous navigation
system is envisioned as AI-enabled system where AI models
will be implemented for multi-sensor sensor fusion, motion
planning and control. To ensure the reliable operation of
onboard sensors, interfaces and computing platforms, devices
health monitoring system that continuously evaluate the health
and correct operation of the devices are proposed as part of the

II. RELATED WORK
Aviation industry navigation and localization system relies
on GPS. It became common cause for security threats in the
industry. The research community has been active on preven-
tion of GPS spoofing attacks. Most of the works concentrate on
detecting the attacks while there are few efforts on responses
on the detected attacks. Another alternative is to abandon
the use of GNSS for localization and rely on some other
approaches in order to avoid security threats associated with
the GPS. This work falls on the latter category. By integrating
information collected prior to the UAV mission, the use of
various sensors, multi-sensor fusion, AI-based motion plan-
ning and control, UAVs can navigate autonomously without
the need to rely on GNSS. To prevent security vulnerabilities
brought by the GPS system, vision-aid, regional localization,
and ML/DL prediction techniques are frequently applied.
Scholars at different place and time have proposed, a variety
of methods to detect and localize GPS spoofing attackers
[1]–[4]. They are still using on the existing navigation and
communication method; hence hackers strive for other type of
attack on it. Some researchers are worked on machine learning
and deep learning approaches to detect GPS spoofing signals
[5]–[8]. These methods would detect attacks then could not
prevent it. Predicting UAVs attitude based on their behaviors
during flight by [9] and [10]. They [16] also proposed a metrics
to identify GPS attacks. Most of researchers still working on
detection of attacks rather than preventing them.
Different scholars have utilized a variety of ways to track
the movement of UAVs when GPS banned maneuvering flying.
Among those techniques of controlling vision aid methods
have been proposed by scholars [13]–[15], [17]. However, they
used wireless communication method to control UAVs through
visual aid devices; which can be compromised. Radar tech-
nology also used as alternative means of controlling method
[19]. Relative localization method also used in a GPS denied
navigation system [20]. New localization method also used
wireless communication for controlling UAVs; hackers are still
working on this channel. Image processing and deep learning
prediction algorithms are widely used to help GPS navigation
system [21].
Scholars [23] have developed a relative navigation strategy
using wireless range information on the ground in advance
to address the local positioning demands of UAVs in GPS-
deficient environments. Others have proposed an integrated
vehicular system incorporating unmanned ground vehicles
(UGVs) for autonomous exploration, mapping, and navigation
in GPS-denied situations [24], [25]; however, they have no
means to differentiate from the others.
III. AUTONOMOUS NAVIGATION SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
The navigation system architecture is made up of five
layers which interacts each other for navigating autonomously
without external assistance. The layers are:
• Devices Health Monitoring Layer: this is responsible for
monitoring the correct operations of various hardware
components (sensors, interfaces, computing platforms)
and software modules. This is important to ensure the
reliable, secure and safe operation of the UAV. The
monitoring is done through distributed monitoring agents
throughout the whole system. This can be implemented
as a middleware.
• Intelligence Group Layer: it is the integration of IPB and
air ISR information. The layer is responsible for gath-
ering information about the battle field terrain, weather
condition, the type of weapons used by rivalries, specific
location of their target, pros and cons of the terrain,
distance in air, and possible obstacles in air.
• Sensing Layer: the sensing layer consists of various on-
board sensors such as IMUs, LiDARs, camera, and radars
for environmental perception and UAV state estimation.
Acquisition of sensor data and pre-processing of the
sensor data to remove noises and conversion to the needed
format are also performed at this layer.
• Multi-sensor Fusion Layer: it extracts effective informa-
tion from diverse and complex data collected by multiple
sensors. Its purpose is to establish the correlation between
sensory information of local environment and UAV states
as well as providing information for map reconstruction
for safe path planning.
• Motion Planning and Decision Layer: is responsible for
safe path and trajectory planning as well as performing
continuous actuation on UAV motion control. This is
necessary to navigate, accomplish the mission and return
to base safely.
• AI Layer: is an enabler for the fully autonomous feature
of the navigation system by applying AI algorithms in
sensor fusion, devices health monitoring, motion planning
and UAV control operations of the system.

IV. SUBSYSTEMS DESIGN
A. Devices Health Monitoring
The devices health monitoring system has distributed
software-based monitoring agents and reconfiguration con-
troller. The monitoring agents continuously observe and col-
lect states of hardware devices, software modules execution
process, and sensors data trust level. They also monitors any
security related incidents and failures. They report any inci-
dents that require some level of actuation to the reconfiguration
controller. The sensors trust values are computed using data-
driven model. The performance data of radar, LiDAR and
camera on various weather conditions are collected and the
collected data is employed for developing a machine learning
model that determine the sensors detection accuracy with
the environmental conditions. Information from the weather
sensors are critical for trust computation as perception sensor
may function improperly under certain weather conditions,
e.g., LiDAR may have problems during snowy winters. The
controller component evaluates the collected data and act
accordingly whether to reconfigure states, shut-down some
modules or activate safe-mode that enforce UAV to return to
its ground station. The implementation of the devices health
monitoring system should support automatic reconfiguration
as well as fault and security incidence containment and re-
covery. It should be capable of recognizing, configuring and
establishing communication and control over the subsystems
automatically. It should also able to isolate subsystems that are
either failed or experienced unauthorized intrusions. If there
are failures, the system should switch to properly functioning
alternative modes, for example dynamically adjusting virtual-
ization parameters or use of redundant sensors.
B. Intelligence Group
The integrated intelligence groups gather information about
the battle field terrain, weather condition, the type of weapons
used by rivalries, specific location of their target, pros and
cons of the terrain, distance in air, possible obstacles in air
and expected and usual space condition of the region. IPB
is the organized intelligence groups which collect battlefield
information prior, during and after combat to enable forces
to prepare itself for defending or/and counter-attack. Air ISR
is another intelligence group typically responsible for gaining
control of the air, carrying out strategic and tactical bombing
missions, and providing support to land and naval forces often
in the form of aerial reconnaissance and close air support.
Basically air force performs is tasks on selected strategic,
logistic and military bases; which cannot be changed their
location minutes. Both IPB and air ISR are represented in the
intelligence information prior to flight as part of every nation’s
military intelligence system. In the proposed navigation sys-
tem, the information gathered by the IPB and ISR as shown in
Figure 3 are served as one of the inputs for motion planning
and decision making.

C. Sensing
The required sensors for navigation in GPS-denied environ-
ments can be classified based their function as localization,
altitude, attitude, mapping and object detection. IMU sensors
that integrates gyroscope and accelerometer have been widely
used for localization, attitude and velocity measurement in
UAVs. Vision sensors such as cameras (monocular, stereo,
thermal) and LiDARs are often used for velocity measurement,
object detection, mapping and attitude estimation. Radars are
the other type of sensors that can detect and track objects.
Radars gained more attention recently because it is more ro-
bust in harsh environments compared to cameras and LiDARs.
It is also attractive due to its ability in unobtrusive and privacy-
respecting detection.
D. Environment Perception
The combination of information from various sensors pro-
vides a means for achieving high accuracy environmental
perception compared to a single or few sensors. Accurate
environmental perception (object detection, classification and
tracking) is one of the key factors for safe navigation of UAVs.
But it is very challenging because of dynamic changes in
the environment and encounters of multi-class targets in the
perceived environment. The limitations of the environmental
sensors on weather conditions (rain, darkness, fog, snow) is
another difficulty for accurate perception of the environment.
The use of different types of sensors, redundancy and making
sure the correct operation of the sensors help to minimize
the impact of sensors limitations. For example, radars are less
affected by weather conditions. In addition, the application of
AI algorithms for object detection and classification improves
the perception accuracy considerably.
The proposed sensor fusion design is shown in Figure
4. Each sensor data is pre-processed to remove noises and
convert it in the required format. Appropriate algorithms are
applied on the pre-processed data to perform object detection,
classification and tracking. To achieve reliable environment
perceptions, several algorithms have to be developed and
applied. The algorithms are needed for recognition, classi-
fication and tracking of static and mobile objects/targets in
the UAV field of view despite noisy background environment.
Localization and tracking of the detected object are performed
by applying intelligent algorithms for positioning and predict-
ing its movement and next positions. List of objects on the
reference map is generated through coordinate transformation
of the individual sensors based detection and by associating
the coordinate transformation overlaying it on the pre-defined
map of the mission. The outputs of the sensor fusion is
the detected and classified object list, their positions and
velocity(longitudinal and later) with respect to the UAV.
E. Motion Planning and Control
A trajectory planning algorithm that make use of the sensor
fusion output is necessary. The algorithm should be capable
of predicting the behavior of UAV over a finite time interval
and optimizing the UAV’s trajectory based on the predicted
behaviour. After the UAV is up and flying based on local
perception, the next step is planning and executing trajec-
tories in partially known environments, so that it is possi-
ble to perform waypoint navigation and obstacle avoidance
efficiently. The location of the UAV and the objects in its
surrounding environment can be determined using the fused
information from different localization (IMU) and environment
sensors (LiDARs, radars, stereo cameras), By integrating this
information with the pre-planned path of the UAV, reference
map and prior information about the mission environment
together, the location can be updated incrementally, which
consequently defines the path. Applying AI-based planning
algorithm facilitates the navigation in a complex environment
as it can learn and update the features intelligently.

state of UAV and the path. By defining the relationship

dynamically changing environments. The use of heterogeneous
sensors with redundancy, the pre-processing of sensors data,
