Abstract
This paper presents a robust approach for achieving accurate indoor positioning and au-
tonomous navigation of quadcopters through the fusion of multiple radars and an inertial
measurement unit (IMU) named hybrid-filtered Radar–Inertial Odometry (Hybrid-RIO).
The Hybrid-RIO system integrates four-directional Frequency-Modulated Continuous-
Wave (FMCW) millimeter-wave (mmWave) radars simultaneously with a high-performance
IMU to continuously estimate the quadcopters’ position, velocity, and orientation even in
low-light and indoor environments. The autonomous flight commands from the system
further enable indoor navigation without requiring human intervention. Experimental
results reveal notable advancements in both the accuracy and consistency of position-
ing. The integration of the proposed Hybrid-RIO approach holds promise in a wide
spectrum of domains, including cave exploration, tunnel rescue operations, and indoor
navigation solutions.

1. Introduction
Accurate and consistent positioning is of paramount importance in various contem-
porary domains, such as autonomous driving [1], robotics navigation [2], and emergency
services [3]. While the advent of the Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) has substan-
tially improved outdoor positioning, indoor positioning still remains a formidable challenge
due to signal obstructions and interference, particularly within confined, dark spaces [4].
To address this challenge, several positioning solutions have been proposed (see Table 1),
including Inertial Odometry (IO) [5], Visual–Inertial Odometry (VIO) [6], LiDAR–Inertial
Odometry (LIO) [7], and Radar–Inertial Odometry (RIO) [8].

Inertial Odometry (IO), or an Inertial Navigation System (INS), is a self-contained
positioning method that estimates motion by integrating accelerometer and gyroscope mea-
surements over time [10]. This strapdown integration offers continuous estimates of position
and orientation at high update rates, enabling operation in diverse environments [11]. How-
ever, biases and noise in the sensors can accumulate over time, causing unbounded drift
in the estimated trajectory without correction [12]. Recent approaches eliminate this drift
using compensation techniques such as Kalman filtering or periodic resets like zero-velocity
updates [13], though these increase system complexity. As a result, pure IO provides accu-
rate short-term pose estimation but its long-term accuracy is limited unless supported by
additional sensors or recalibration [14].
In VIO systems, visual features captured by the camera are utilized to constrain
IMU-based motion estimates, effectively correcting drift [15]. The tightly coupled fusion
of visual and inertial measurements results in more consistent and accurate trajectory esti-
mates over time [16]. VIO performs optimally in well-lit, feature-rich environments where
the camera can track distinct points. However, its performance degrades in low-texture or
poorly illuminated scenes, and during rapid motion causing motion blur, as the camera’s
measurements become unreliable and cannot fully correct inertial drift [17]. While thermal or

LIO is widely regarded as a gold standard for ego-motion estimation due to its pre-
cise ranging capabilities [19]. LIO combines LiDAR and IMU data, generating dense 3D
maps and accurate position estimations through scan-matching and IMU-aided motion
prediction [20]. Its robustness against lighting changes allows accurate mapping even in
darkness [21]. However, the LiDAR sensors’ weight, power consumption, sensitivity to
adverse weather, and uncertainty in geometrically sparse or distant environments remain
significant limitations [22].
Traditional Radar–Inertial Odometry (RIO) systems primarily utilize radar types such
as 2D automotive radars [23] and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) [24]. Radar sensors can
provide reliable performance under adverse conditions such as fog, dust, low illumination,
and partial occlusions like smoke or foliage [25]. However, some suffer from coarse spatial
resolution, radar cross-section (RCS) fluctuations, multi-path reflections, mutual interference,
and sparse detections [21,26–29]. These factors complicate feature association and scan
matching, resulting in a lower localization accuracy [30].
Based on the above discussion and our prior work [31], and to address the limita-
tions summarized in Table 1, we propose a multi-Radar–Inertial Odometry framework
(Hybrid-RIO), as shown in Figure 1a, which is specifically designed to operate in per-
ceptually degraded and infrastructure-denied scenarios where conventional vision- or
LiDAR-based sensing pipelines tend to fail. The proposed framework integrates multiple
Frequency-Modulated Continuous-Wave (FMCW) millimeter-wave (mmWave) radars,
each oriented in distinct directions, to enhance environmental perception and motion
estimation. Additionally, a downward-facing mmWave radar functions as an altimeter,
offering direct height measurements to constrain vertical drift. FMCW mmWave radars
enable the capture of detailed 4D data, including range, azimuth, elevation, and Doppler
velocity, providing enhanced spatial resolution [32]. By utilizing wide bandwidths and
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) antenna arrays, these radars achieve high angular
resolution while maintaining a small size and low cost [33]. The multi-radar array exploits
spatial diversity to suppress multi-path interference and mitigate radar cross-section (RCS)
fluctuations, improving point-cloud reliability [34]. Finally, incorporating Doppler velocity
measurements fused with inertial measurement unit (IMU) data effectively corrects inertial
drift, improving six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) ego-motion estimation and localization ac-
curacy in perceptually degraded, GPS-denied environments [35]. This enhanced robustness
enables the proposed system to be potentially deployed across a broad range of chal-
lenging application domains, including search-and-rescue missions, industrial inspection,
underground exploration, and even planetary environments.
In this work, our main contributions are the following:
•
We develop an Error-State Extended Kalman Filter (ES-EKF) framework for
synchronized multi-FMCW mmWave Radar–Inertial Odometry, incorporating a
downward-facing mmWave radar to enhance terrain estimation accuracy for a quad-

The Hybrid-RIO state estimate is fed directly into our previously developed robust
sliding-mode flight controller (SMC) [31,36]. A dedicated flight-state EKF running
within the open-source PX4 autopilot architecture [37] further refines the estimate,
resulting in greater stability and flight performance and enabling fully autonomous
UAV navigation via scripted missions.
•
We experimentally validate Hybrid-RIO in challenging indoor settings featuring low
illumination (see Figure 1b) and quantify its positioning and autonomous naviga-

This paper is structured as shown in Figure 2, where the above advancements of
Hybrid-RIO are integrated into a complete framework. First, the raw radar point-cloud
data is obtained and synchronized. Each point includes the object’s X, Y, Z position,
Doppler velocity, and intensity information. Meanwhile, IMU data is also collected and
filtered. All raw data is then passed into the Hybrid-RIO module, which consists of the
state definition, state propagation, radar measurement update, state update, state injection,
and error-state reset. The processed output is further refined by a flight-state EKF to smooth
the estimates. The output of the Hybrid-RIO—the local pose estimation—is then fed into a
previously developed full-DoF disturbance observer-aided nonlinear sliding-mode surface
flight controller. Given a user-defined or system-defined desired pose, the UAV can then
autonomously navigate in a dark, GPS-denied indoor environment. The performance of
Hybrid-RIO is evaluated by comparing it with UAV positions captured by a stereo IR depth
camera which is fixed to the ceiling of the testing field and serves as the ground truth.
The proposed Hybrid-RIO differs from conventional RIO due to its hybrid architecture,
which integrates heterogeneous radar sensing and layered state estimation. We argue that
radar data fusion with spatial diversity is the key factor enabling the system to overcome
the inherent limitations of traditional radar odometry, such as detection sparsity and sus-
ceptibility to interference. Multiple omnidirectional mmWave radars in the horizontal
plane expand sensing coverage and provide measurement redundancy, yielding more
robust planar ego-motion constraints under occlusion and sensor degradation. To over-
come the limited vertical observability of horizontal radars, a dedicated downward-facing
radar is incorporated to enable reliable altitude estimation. All radar measurements are
software-synchronized to form a consistent composite point cloud for tightly coupled
radar–IMU fusion.
At the estimation level, perception-level odometry is decoupled
from flight-critical control states and further refined by an onboard flight-state EKF, im-
proving robustness, state consistency, and autonomous flight reliability in challenging
indoor environments.

2. Mathematical Modeling
Since the UAV operates in dark indoor environments, conventional vision-based
methods are ineffective. In addition, the confined space and propeller-induced turbulent
airflow lead to aggressive maneuvers and structural vibrations, which can degrade the
stability of LiDAR-based odometry. To address these challenges, we introduce the pro-
posed omnidirectional mmWave radar ES-EKF-based Hybrid-RIO system. Even when
measurements from one direction lack reliable features, the outlier removal process and
multi-directional sensing allow the remaining radars to provide sufficient information for
local pose estimation.

2.3.2. FMCW mmWave Radar-Based Terrain Altitude Estimation
The downward-facing mmWave radar has the capability to detect various ground
textures by measuring the strength or intensity of the reflected radar signal. By analyzing
these radar returns with different levels of intensity, one can distinguish between different
types of terrain. In this work, we filter radar points based on a defined intensity threshold
to isolate ground returns. This altitude information assists UAVs in navigating safely and
efficiently, particularly when flying at low altitudes or in complex terrains.

To achieve swift odometry estimation during runtime on the portable Nvidia TX2, we
implement a filtering process for the point-cloud intensity with a specified threshold of
20dB. This approach is motivated by three key factors: Firstly, radar data can be influenced
by different noise types, including electronic interference, environmental disruptions,
and acquisition-related noise. By applying an intensity threshold, we can effectively
eliminate low-intensity points likely caused by noise, thus enhancing data quality. Secondly,
when radar waves encounter surfaces or objects, a portion of the energy is reflected back,
offering valuable insights into object presence and location in the scene. The 20 dB intensity
threshold ensures the selection of strong initial reflections, excluding points that might
result from radar penetration or material refraction. This refinement contributes to better
performance and a more accurate object representation. Thirdly, having fewer points due
to the filtering process reduces the computational load on the Nvidia TX2, leading to faster
processing times and improved overall system efficiency.
To gain a deeper insight into the distribution of point clouds, we monitor the changes
in the number of points over time. As depicted in Figure 8, the average count of inlier
points from each radar utilized for RIO hovers around 5. Even in scenarios where one or
more radars have a scarcity of points (no valid points in specific directions), the remaining
radars continue to contribute data, bolstering the RIO system’s robustness.
The RIO estimates undergo a refinement process through the utilization of an addi-
tional flight-control EKF (Extended Kalman Filter) embedded within the PX4 autopilot.
This refinement leverages the strengths of both the flight-control EKF and the navigation
ES-KF, as explained in previous sections, with each excelling in its respective area. The
flight-control EKF is known for its smoothing effect on state estimates, especially for lin-
earizable parts of system dynamics and measurement models. On the other hand, the raw
ES-KF estimate effectively handles the impact of strong nonlinearities in the system. By com-
bining the capabilities of both the EKF and ES-KF, a hybrid approach (Hybrid-RIO) emerges,
capitalizing on the advantages of both filters. This hybridization aims to achieve a more
balanced and smoother estimation across the entire state space, as we demonstrate next.

specific camera’s field of view. Consequently, it precisely calculates both the global position
and orientation of the object in a three-dimensional space. The result of this motion-capture
system is the object’s odometry information, which is subsequently published to an ROS

4. Conclusions
This study targets a specialized UAV operating scenario characterized by low-light,
high-vibration, and visually degraded GPS-denied indoor environments, where localiza-
tion pipelines such as VIO and LIO are known to be unreliable or even inoperable on
flying UAV platforms. To address these challenges, we propose a robust ESKF-based
Radar–Inertial Odometry framework that employs multi-directional FMCW millimeter-
wave radars. The work focuses on methodological enhancement achieved through a con-
trolled comparison of conventional RIO and the proposed Hybrid-RIO. Both approaches
share the same radar hardware, IMU, experimental platform, and control stack, enabling
a fair evaluation that isolates and clearly demonstrates the contribution of the proposed
hybrid formulation.
The proposed approach effectively combines high-frequency IMU data with synchro-
nized radar measurements. The estimation of radar ego velocity is accomplished through
a three-point RANSAC-LSQ method. To mitigate the influence of airflow on the barom-

eter, we incorporate an independent vertical FMCW radar for accurate terrain altitude
fusion. The resulting odometry data is then processed through a refined EKF to enhance
flight performance. The pose estimation is subsequently integrated into the PX4 autopilot,
an open-source platform.
The experiments are conducted using fully autonomous flights in PX4 OFFBOARD
mode. The Hybrid-RIO pipeline, together with autonomous command generation for
the test UAV, is executed within the Robot Operating System (ROS) on a lightweight
onboard companion computer. The proposed Hybrid-RIO enhances mission-level au-
tonomy by providing drift-bounded and perception-robust state estimates that can be
reliably consumed by the onboard flight-control and mission-execution stack. Specifically,
the Hybrid-RIO output is tightly integrated with the PX4 autopilot architecture and a distur-
bance observer-aided sliding-mode controller (SMC), enabling stable closed-loop control,
accurate waypoint navigation, and aggressive maneuvers to be executed reliably under
low illumination, strong vibration, and visually degraded conditions without reliance on
external localization infrastructure or vision-based sensing. Experimental tests involving a
motion-capture system yield a strong alignment between the Hybrid-RIO estimates and
the ground truth. Evaluation of the dataset indicates small RMSE position errors around
0.53% and MAE position errors at approximately 0.38%.
Future work will extend the proposed system beyond the current small-scale indoor
cage setup, which was selected to ensure flight safety, experimental repeatability, and the
availability of external ground truth. Since the infrared-based reference system used in this
study has a limited operational range, future experiments will focus on larger and more
cluttered indoor environments where improved motion-capture systems can be deployed
to provide more accurate ground truth. Under these settings, we will evaluate scalability
through long-range and long-duration autonomous flights using accuracy-based metrics,
further validate performance at higher flight speeds, and conduct systematic comparisons
against representative baselines such as VIO and LIO. Additionally, because some methods
exhibit higher latency and lower update rates, a fair comparison would require decoupling
all sensor-based odometry outputs from the flight-control loop and using an independent
localization source for control, thereby avoiding confounding closed-loop effects.
The present study focuses on typical cluttered and confined indoor environments
with predominantly static structural features and a limited proportion of outlier radar
returns, which are representative of many practical indoor scenarios with few dynamic
objects. However, more challenging environments—such as those dominated by highly
specular surfaces or certain glass—remain difficult for mmWave radar sensing due to
increased dynamic reflections and multi-path effects. For such scenarios, future work
will investigate the integration of complementary sensing modalities (e.g., vision or Li-
DAR) within a multi-sensor fusion framework, together with more aggressive dynamic
point-filtering strategies. In addition, future efforts will emphasize hardware improvements
and mission-level autonomy by integrating FMCW mmWave radars with larger fields of
view, higher-performance companion computers, and onboard obstacle-avoidance algo-
rithms. These enhancements will enable more robust autonomous missions in large-scale
GPS- and vision-denied indoor environments.
