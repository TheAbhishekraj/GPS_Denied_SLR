Satoshi Suzuki

3-15-1 Tokida, Ueda-shi, Nagano 386-8567, Japan

[Received December 12, 2017; accepted May 4, 2018]
In this study, a novel robust navigation system for a
drone in global positioning system (GPS) and GPS-
denied environments is proposed.
In general, the
drone uses position and velocity information from
GPS for guidance and control. However, GPS can-
not be used in several environments; for example,
GPS exhibits huge errors near buildings and trees,
indoor environments.
In such GPS-denied environ-
ments, a Laser Imaging Detection and Ranging (LI-
DAR) sensor-based navigation system has generally
been used.
However, the LIDAR sensor also has a
weakness, and it cannot be used in an open outdoor
environment where GPS can be used. Therefore, it is
advantageous to develop an integrated navigation sys-
tem that operates seamlessly in both GPS and GPS-
denied environments. In this study, an integrated nav-
igation system for the drone using GPS and LIDAR
was developed. The design of the navigation system is
based on the extended Kalman ﬁlter, and the effective-
ness of the developed system is veriﬁed by numerical
simulation and experiment.
Keywords:
autonomous drone, GPS-denied environ-
ments, integrated navigation, SLAM, Kalman ﬁlter
1. Introduction
In the last ﬁve years, the great success of the small
unmanned aerial vehicle, also known as a drone, has at-
tracted enormous attention all over the world [1]. A drone
can potentially be used for applications in the industrial
ﬁeld under various environments. For example, it could
be used for information gathering at the time of a disaster,
working at heights, inspection of large infrastructure like
bridges and dams, and security. To easily apply the drone
to such tasks, autonomous capacity in the drone is a desir-
able feature. For autonomous ﬂight, it is necessary to ob-
tain the position and velocity information of the drone. In
general, the drone uses Global Positioning System (GPS)
or Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) to measure
the position and velocity for autonomous ﬂight. However,
GPS cannot be used in several environments; for exam-
ple, GPS exhibits huge errors near buildings and trees,
and indoors. To achieve autonomous ﬂight of the drone in
such GPS-denied environments, several sensors are used
instead of GPS to measure position and velocity.
An optical motion capture system [a, b] is commonly
used for research on autonomous ﬂight in an indoor envi-
ronment. The motion capture system measures the pre-
cise position and attitude of the drone. Therefore, the
system is used for research on precise autonomous ﬂight
control of drones [2, 3]. However, the system needs large-
scale facilities, and it is only applied to a limited area.
On the other hand, a vision sensor is also used for au-
tonomous ﬂight in several studies [4, 5]. The monocular
and stereo vision systems are useful because they can be
applied in various environments. However, they are eas-
ily affected by a change in the light environment; their
robustness is, therefore, inadequate. The Laser Imaging
Detection and Ranging (LIDAR) sensor is mostly used
for research on autonomous ﬂight in a GPS-denied envi-
ronment [6–8]. LIDAR-based Simultaneous Localization
and Mapping (SLAM) is a mature technology in the ﬁeld
of robotics, and it obtains accurate positions. However,
the LIDAR sensor also cannot be used in several envi-
ronments, such as an environment where insufﬁcient laser
reﬂections are obtained.
As mentioned above, every sensor has its own weak-
ness, and there are boundaries between the environments
where each sensor could be used. Therefore, it is desir-
able to develop an integrated navigation system that op-
erates seamlessly in various environments. In this paper,
an integrated navigation system based on GPS, the LI-
DAR sensor, and the extended Kalman ﬁlter is proposed.
The integrated navigation system can be applied to both
GPS and GPS-denied environments. Conventional study
of integrated navigation system is shown in [9]. In [9], an
unscented Kalman ﬁlter was used for integrated naviga-
tion to avoid the computation of the Jacobian. However,
in our case, only GPS and LIDAR are fused, and the com-
putation of the Jacobian is not so complex. Therefore, the
extended Kalman ﬁlter is more suitable for computation-
ally constrained drones than an unscented Kalman ﬁlter
because the computational cost of the extended Kalman
ﬁlter is not expensive. This is the main contribution of
this work.

4. Ofﬂine Experiment
A ofﬂine experiment was carried out to verify the pro-
posed integrated navigation system, and to adjust the pa-
rameters. First, sensor data were collected in ﬂight by
using the drone shown in Fig. 2. The main speciﬁcation
of the drone is listed in Table 1. The drone has six ro-
tors, and it is also known as a multi-rotor helicopter. The
drone has a large payload capability compared with its
dry weight. The sensor and computer system mounted on
the drone are shown in Fig. 3. The system consists of
GPS, LIDAR, IMU, and the embedded computer. Flight
data were collected in several environments as shown in
Figs. 4–6.
Next, the ofﬂine experiment of the integrated naviga-
tion was carried out by using the collected data. The nav-
igation system was implemented using MATLAB code.
The parameters adjusted by the experiment are listed in
Fig. 3. Sensor and computer system.
Table 2, and the results are shown in Figs. 7 and 8.
Figure 7 shows the horizontal position, and Fig. 8
shows the horizontal velocity. In each ﬁgure, the dashed
line represents data from GPS, the dotted line represents
data from LIDAR SLAM, and the solid line represents
the estimates of the integrated navigation. Here, it can be
seen that the LIDAR SLAM has been reset several times
during ﬂight. From the ﬁgures, it is shown, especially
for velocity data, that the integrated navigation could sup-
press the large sensor noise of GPS. In addition, at ap-
proximately 370 s in Fig. 7(a), GPS data exhibits a large
drift because the robot enters the indoor environment at
that time. In contrast, the position of the LIDAR system at
approximately 370 s is quite stable because a large num-
ber of laser reﬂections could be obtained in the indoor
environment. Thus, the LIDAR system could output the
accurate position. The integrated navigation compensated
the estimated position and velocity by using accurate po-
sitions from the LIDAR system. The results veriﬁed the
efﬁciency of the integrated navigation system.
5. Online Experiment
5.1. Experiment Without Control
The integrated navigation system was implemented on
an embedded computer and a navigation experiment in
ﬂight was carried out. In this experiment, the drone shown
in Fig. 2 was used. The drone was manually operated and
the navigation system was veriﬁed. The result is shown in
Fig. 9. The ﬁgure shows the ﬂight trajectory obtained by
GPS and the integrated navigation system. In the ﬁgure,
the dashed line represents the trajectory from GPS, and

navigation system. From the ﬁgure, it is clear that the tra-
jectory of the integrated navigation is smoother than the
trajectory of GPS. In particular, when the drone enters the
narrow space between buildings, there are large errors in
the trajectory of GPS. On the other hand, the integrated
navigation system could still estimate a precise and con-
tinuous trajectory. The result shows the efﬁciency of the
designed integrated navigation system was shown.

6. Conclusion
In this paper, an integrated navigation system for an
autonomous drone in both GPS and GPS-denied environ-
ments was introduced. The navigation system consists of
GPS and a LIDAR sensor, and its design is based on the
extended Kalman ﬁlter. The results of a numerical sim-
ulation and experiment demonstrate the efﬁciency of the
proposed navigation system.
In future studies, the navigation system will be ex-
tended to utilize a vision sensor, barometer, and ultrasonic
sensor to achieve more robust navigation in various envi-
ronments.
