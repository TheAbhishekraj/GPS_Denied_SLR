Group Coordination and Path Replan Tactics in GPS Denied
Environments
M Suresh1 and Debasish Ghose2
Abstract— Success of a cooperative mission entirely depends
on precise navigation information of each UAV. Though present
day UAVs are equipped with GPS, the UAVs have to rely only
on navigation information obtained from inertial navigation
system while operating in GPS denied environments. This
research work, addresses the problem of establishing a GPS
protected wireless MAV network (WIMAN) using three classes
of navigation system, one equipped only with GPS, an other
equipped only with high accuracy inertial navigation system
and the third equipped with relatively low accuracy inertial
navigation system, to enable missions in GPS denied environ-
ments. NP hardness in establishing GPS protected WIMAN
architecture is discussed and a heuristic algorithm is proposed.
Group coordination and tactical replan procedures, that enable
WIMAN to maintain its conﬁguration, reconﬁguration to adapt
to various phases of cooperative threat reconnaissance mission,
is demonstrated through simulation results.
Keywords: MAVs, cooperative control, group coordi-
nation, group tactical replan, GPS denined environment,
distributed cooperative localization
I. INTRODUCTION
In recent years, high altitude long endurance UAVs,
carrying multi-spectral mission sensors, are developed and
operationalized for strategic inter-continental surveillance
missions [1]-[3]. However, Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs)
were also developed for deployment in low altitude tactical
missions. These MAVs are deployed at soldier level to collect
intelligence information on smaller regions of interest. De-
ploying a large number of micro class of UAVs, and enabling
cooperation among them, will cover a wider area and provide
time critical intelligence on the enemy movements. Due
to its low weight, size, and cost, with high expendability
factor, deploying MAVs in a swarm for military missions
has gained enormous importance among UAV deployment
policy decision makers. MAVs equipped with MEMS based
Inertial Navigation System (INS) measure linear and angular
accelerations and provide relatively low accurate continuous
navigation solution when compared with GPS system, which
provides relatively high accurate navigation solution at a low
frequency of update.
Many cooperative and coordination algorithms that are
available in the literature are widely based on the assumption
that both INS and GPS system outputs are available at all
conditions. This assumption becomes invalid in a situation

Many papers in the literature discuss the use of onboard
multi-sensor data fusion for both indoor and outdoor lo-

Techniques based on optical ﬂow sensor [4]-[19], camera

Mobile sensor network based localization [24], multi-
objective path planning algorithm under localization con-

localization where each sensor updates its location based on
measurement from its neighboring nodes. The extension of
the algorithms to mobile agents and large scale systems in
the presence of noisy measurements are presented in [33]-
[35].
A standard for measuring autonomous mission operations
is discussed in [36] and Figure 1 shows various UAV
autonomous capability levels. The ﬁrst ﬁve autonomy levels
deﬁne the standards for single UAV and are classiﬁed as solo
autonomy. The next six autonomy levels deﬁne the standards
for group of UAVs and are classiﬁed as group autonomy.
These autonomy levels address group coordination (Level 5)
in which a group of UAVs achieve complex mission through
efﬁcient cooperation and collobaoration, Group tactical re-

of autonomy levels is also available in [37].

II. CONTRIBUTIONS
A cooperative intelligence, surveillance and reconnais-
sance mission involving hetergenous mix of multiple MAVs
equipped only with GPS, only with low accuracy and only
with high accuracy INS, is considered. The MAVs equipped
only with GPS are called GPS enabled MAV or GEM. In
situations where position of a MAV, equipped with INS, is
updated by GEMs, the MAV is termed as GPS protected
MAV (GPM). Also, mission scenario are assumed to be
consisting of several operational phases in indoor environ-
ments and also in GPS denined environments. This scenario
is very practical and it will be encountered in both military
and civilian applications on cooperative missions involving
MAVs with higher levels of group autonomy. This scenario
motivates us to make the following contributions
(a) To design a wireless MAV network (WIMAN) with
GEMs and GPMs that are capable of executing a
mission in indoor and GPS denied environments.
(b) To demonstrate group coordination procedures (Level
5) to maintain a WIMAN conﬁguration.
(c) To demonstrate group tactical path replan procedures
(Level 6) to achieve different WIMAN conﬁgurations
to adapt to various mission phases and MAV failures.
(d) A distributed localization algorithm, discussed in [32],
is applied to dynamically compute the true position of
MAV equipped with INS based on measurements from
its neighbours.

A. Micro Aerial Vehicles
The micro-aerial vehicles considered in this work are
assumed to be powered by electric propulsion with ﬂight

B. Internode Distance Measurement based on RSS
RSS is deﬁned as the voltage measured by the received
signal strength indicator circuit of the receiver based on RF
signal and it serves as an indication of connectivity between
two devices. Patwari et al. [38] discussed the characteristics
of various measurement techniques and showed that RSS is
most valuable in high density sensor networks as its measure-
ments are relatively inexpensive and simple to implement in
hardware.

V. NP HARDNESS IN CONSTRUCTING GPS PROTECTED
WIMAN ARCHITECTURE
A. Problem Statement
Theoretically, the study of the position-location
problem is the study of the inverse of the measurement
map [40]. That is, for a given distance assignment d, we
try to determine the structure of the set of all positions that
are mapped into d by the measurement map. The fact that
a minimal number of m + 1 MAVs equipped with GPS
is required to localize an arbitrary number M of MAVs
equipped with INS that lie in the convex hull of these m+1
MAVs equipped with GPS, leads to a triangle structure in a
plane. Each vertices of a triangle is positioned with GPS
equipped MAVs and MAVs required to be localized lies
within the triangle. The network can be formed by cascading
triangles, whose vertices are MAVs equipped with INS or
GPS.
B. NP Hardness
Theorem: Constructing a GPS protected WIMAN archi-
tecture is NP-complete.
Proof: In a position-location problem, we search for triangles
(if they exist), and then construct and paste them to locate
positions. After each triangle has been positioned, it is
possible to locate another position up to a reﬂection with
respect to a common edge. Each additional point admits
two possible reﬂections and it may be reﬂected with respect
to its neighbors. The problem of reﬂections can be shown
to be NP-complete [40]. Consider the problem of ﬁnding a
partition of a given set of numbers {a1, a2, . . . , an} into two
disjoint sets of equal sums; it is known [41] that this problem
is NP-complete. Consider the problem of assembling a
structure (Figure 2) such that the distance between A0 and
An should be zero. The problem of ﬁnding a reﬂection of
the chain of rectangles to satisfy the distance constraints

VI. ALGORITHM TO GENERATE GPS PROTECTED
WIMAN ARCHITECTURE
Given that problem of constructing GPS protected
WIMAN architecture is NP-complete, one has to resort
to heuristic algorithms (Algorithm 1 and 2) to solve the
problem. The input required for the algorithm are the set
containing MAVs equipped with GPS and INS, total number
of MAVs equipped with GPS and INS and the maximum
communication range among each MAV.
First, the algorithm determines whether at least three
MAVs equipped with GPS is available to establish an ar-
chitecture. If the minimum condition is satisﬁed then the
algorithm proceeds to the next step of determining the
triangle number. The nth triangle number is the number of
MAVs composing a triangle with n MAVs on a side, and
it is equal to the sum of the n natural numbers from 1 to
n. After determining the triangle number, the next step is to
generate grids of a triangle such that the vertices forms the
outermost grid (that is, all other grids lies within the line
joining the vertices). Each of the grids are separated by the
maximum communication range RCmax.
From the grids, the outermost vertices are extracted and
MAVs equipped with GPS are positioned. Similarly, the
left most, right most and center grids are extracted and
the MAVs equipped with high and low accuracy INS are
positioned. Depends on the mission requirement, if the
number of MAVs equipped with GPS is more than 3, they are
positioned in the left most, right most and center grids. This
kind of conﬁguration is useful to create GPS protected sub
groups to achieve tactical manuvers during the mission. After
positioning the MAVs in each of the grid, the vertices of
graph are sorted from outermost top grid and corresponding
edges are constructed to generate a GPS protected WIMAN
architecture.
In this architecture, the MAVs equipped with INS are to
be localized from the information obtained from the MAVs
equipped with GPS. It is essential to generate a triangulation
set for each of MAV equipped with INS and the procedure
is described in Algorithm 2. The ﬁrst step is to determine at
least three closest neighbors for each vertices. Then, whether
the vertices lie within the convex hull of three selected
neighbours is determined. If the convex hull test is passed
then the three neighbours are considered as the triangulation
set for the vertices. This procedure is repeated for all the
MAVs equipped with INS.

B. Mission Modes
The mission control system of each MAV commands
its associated ﬂight control system to execute a speciﬁc
maneuver in the form of mode command. The various modes
of operation are as follows:
Stationing mode: In this mode, each of the MAV ﬂies
from its current position to the designated station with the
mission sensors in the inactive state.
Restore mode: In this mode, each of the MAV ﬂies
from its current station to a position it occupied before the
stationing mode, with mission sensors in the inactive state.
Recce mode: In this mode, each of the MAVs executes
a coordinated maneuver with the mission sensors in active
state.
Loiter mode: In this mode, each of the MAVs loiters
around the predeﬁned point with mission sensors in inactive
state.
