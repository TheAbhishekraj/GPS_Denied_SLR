Abstract
Highly accurate positioning is a crucial prerequisite of multi Unmanned Aerial Vehicle
close-formation ﬂight for target tracking, formation keeping, and collision avoidance. Although
the position of a UAV can be obtained through the Global Positioning System (GPS), it is difﬁcult
for a UAV to obtain highly accurate positioning data in a GPS-denied environment (e.g., a GPS
jamming area, suburb, urban canyon, or mountain area); this may cause it to miss a tracking target
or collide with another UAV. In particular, UAV close-formation control in GPS-denied environ-
ments faces difﬁculties owing to the low-accuracy position, close distance between vehicles, and
nonholonomic dynamics of a UAV. In this paper, on the one hand, we develop an innovative
UAV formation localization method to address the formation localization issues in GPS-denied
environments; on the other hand, we design a novel reinforcement learning based algorithm to
achieve the high-efﬁciency and robust performance of the controller. First, a novel Lidar-based
localization algorithm is developed to measure the localization of each aircraft in the formation
ﬂight. In our solution, each UAV is equipped with Lidar as the position measurement sensor instead
of the GPS module. The k-means algorithm is implemented to calculate the center point position of
UAV. A novel formation position vector matching method is proposed to match center points with
UAVs in the formation and estimate their position information. Second, a reinforcement learning
based UAV formation control algorithm is developed by selecting the optimal policy to control
UAV swarm to start and keep ﬂying in a close formation of a speciﬁc geometry. Third, the inno-
vative collision risk evaluation module is proposed to address the collision-free issues in the forma-
tion group. Finally, a novel experience replay method is also provided in this paper to enhance the
learning efﬁciency. Experimental results validate the accuracy, effectiveness, and robustness of the
proposed scheme.

1. Introduction
With the development of science and technology, there is an
increasing demand on the intelligence and clustering of
UAVs.1–3 The performance of a single UAV is restricted.
For example, the survey area and inspection range are
restricted when performing ground detection tasks.4–6 When
performing transportation tasks, the capacity and transporta-
tion range will be limited.7–10 In addition, if a UAV fails in per-
forming a task, the whole task is jeopardized. Multiple UAVs
in close formation have attracted much attention in the
research community11–13 because they can better explore
search regions and dimensions. Close-formation ﬂight involves
ﬂying in a formation geometry with a lateral spacing that does
not exceed twice the wingspan. For a complex, dynamic ﬂight
environment, it is desirable for multiple UAVs in close forma-
tion to be capable of high-accuracy position measurement and
disturbance rejection. High-accuracy localization and a stable
controller are the most cited challenges for UAV close-
formation tracking control in GPS-denied environments.2,14
The problem of UAVs in close formation has attracted
much attention in the research community. Various formation
strategies relevant to UAV ﬂocking have been developed,
including the leader–follower strategy, distributed formation
control, behavior-based method, and virtual formation struc-
ture. Each solution has its strengths and weaknesses. For
example, the leader–follower strategy has a simple system
structure which can be conveniently implemented, but it is
overly dependent on the performance of the leader UAV,
and has insufﬁcient communication between the leader and
followers. Yang et al.15 proposed an adaptive output feedback
controller to address the leader-following consensus problem,
where the action of a single UAV can reach consensus in for-
mation ﬂight. However, this method does not consider the
inﬂuences of backlash and hysteresis on the control model.
The scientist Ali et al.16 proposed an efﬁcient adaptive control
method addressing the transmission delay problem in the lea-
der–follower formation. In another solution,17 the virtual lea-
der technology is also utilized to provide a robust and effective
formation controller. Yu et al.18 proposed a neural adaptive
control algorithm to accelerate convergence and eliminate
tracking errors in distributed formation structure. Wang et al.19
provided a switching methodology based controller to address
the consensus problem in swarms. Liu et al.20 optimized a non-
linear mapping method and prescribed a controller to realize
collision avoidance in the distributed formation structure.
The primary challenge of the behavior-based algorithm is uti-
lizing the behavior state of each aircraft in an optimal routine.
The virtual formation structure achieves highly adaptive per-
formance and has sufﬁcient robustness because each vehicle
can be transferred to a virtual leader or follower state. How-
ever, the virtual structure requires a high communication
and computation capacity, which may not be easily imple-
mented on a UAV in real-time applications.
We carefully designed a novel ﬂight control system for
UAV close formations in GPS-denied environments, whose
framework is shown in Fig. 1. Each UAV is equipped with
Lidar for coordinated localization. UAVs in formation are
regarded as dynamic objects, and each UAV can measure
the relative positions of surrounding UAVs by processing
Lidar point cloud data. A Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient
(DDPG)-based control algorithm is proposed to achieve sta-
bility and robust performance in formation ﬂight. The pro-
posed strategy can measure the relative localization of the
drones in a formation without using the GPS, import coordi-
nated relative positions into the control module to guide vehi-
cles in formation ﬂying in a GPS-denied environment, and
conduct close formation tracking missions with collision
avoidance. To the best of our knowledge, we are the ﬁrst to

2.2. Reinforcement learning based UAV formation control
solutions
A high-precision, stable UAV control method is crucial to
close-formation ﬂight. Traditional control technology can
ensure a certain accuracy of control. However, in close forma-
tion in GPS-denied environments, it faces many challenges due
to the stability, robustness and precision requirements of the
controller. A discrepancy exists between the simpliﬁed UAV
model and the real dynamic plant, which may decrease the per-
formance of close-formation ﬂight. An RL-based method36–39
can realize the optimal control policy and output optimal con-
trol instructions by learning the real world, and RL technology
has been employed to simulate a dynamic UAV model and
improve control accuracy.40–43 Based on the deep reinforce-
ment learning technology,44 Pham et al.45–46 studied the auton-
omous navigation process of UAVs, and applied it to the
autonomous target area coverage problem. The improved
learning-based adaptive formation control method47 decreases
the probability of UAVs’ collisions in complex environment.
Hu et al.48 proposed a novel cooperative control method based
on the reinforcement learning theory, which makes the UAV
swarms be adaptive to the actuators’ fault in the formation
ﬂight. The relevant experience learning algorithm was devel-
oped to speed up the training phase of the controller.49 An

agent can be used by sharing policy to improve the control
accuracy. The most cited challenge of the learning-based solu-

Inspired by the above methods, we investigate a learning-

and efﬁcient for real-world applications. In this study, we

3. Relative localization strategy
For the close-formation mission of VTOL UAVs in GPS-
denied environments, a key issue is to obtain the relative loca-
tion of each aircraft. Solutions such as Lidar, visual measure-
ment, ultrasonic sensors, and motion capture systems can
measure the relative distances of devices without a GPS mod-
ule. We developed a novel Lidar-based localization method for
three reasons.
(1) Reliability: vehicles in close formations move at high
speeds; this requires stable, high-resolution measure-
ment results for efﬁcient guidance information. Lidar
can obtain the highest localization accuracy and run sta-
bly while an aircraft moves at a high velocity with signif-
icant vibrations.
(2) Measurement range: a UAV needs to sense other objects
to avoid collisions. A single visual camera, ultrasonic
sensor, and UWB module have a limited ranging area,
which does not provide the optimal solution in VTOL
close-formation tasks. Lidar has a large measurement
range, and can provide surrounding localization for an
aircraft in a high-speed formation.
(3) A power-limited UAV onboard computer: this can well
process Lidar data in real time.
Hence we propose a Lidar-based localization algorithm for
UAV close formation, by which a UAV can measure the rela-
tive positions of its neighbors by processing point cloud data
without GPS sensors. The collected data include the linear dis-
tance of a detected object, and the angles of the object relative
to the Lidar on the horizontal plane and in the vertical direc-
tion. It should be noted that a follower UAV in this paper is
equipped with Lidar to measure the position of its neighbor.
We use the k-means algorithm to process and classify the orig-
inal point cloud data to calculate the center point position of
neighbor UAVs around followers. The point clouds handled
by k-means can only know the position data where the UAVs
exist, and cannot associate this with the number information
of UAVs in formation. To solve this problem, we design a for-
mation position vector matching algorithm to obtain the cor-
responding number of position data.
3.1. Formation point cloud process
The point cloud data obtained by UAV-equipped Lidar con-
tain points of the UAV and those around it. The point cloud
of the UAV should be ﬁltered out. According to the installa-
tion position of Lidar on the vehicle and its fuselage size, we
set a threshold D1 and ﬁlter out points with a distance less
than that. We use a UAV with length 1.5 m and wingspan
3 m, with Lidar installed at a height of 0.15 m. According to
the Lidar measurement principle and research experience, we
set D1 ¼ 2:5 m.

the point cloud data from each UAV collected by Lidar are
divided into several point clouds. Since vehicles need to main-

between point cloud clusters, according to which the k-
means algorithm is implemented to obtain the coordinate rel-
ative position information. K-means is an unsupervised clus-

3.2. Formation position vector matching algorithm
According to the above k-means-based point cloud process
method, we can only get the center coordinates of other UAVs
around followers, but we cannot get the corresponding UAV
formation number of these center coordinates. To address this
problem, we designed a new Formation Position Vector
Matching (FPVM) algorithm to associate the center position
with the number of UAVs in a formation. The relative position
of each UAV in formation can be known through the preset
formation map. Due to errors during ﬂight, the actual position
of a drone may deviate from its predetermined position. It is
supposed that the actual position of a drone is within a circle
with the preset position as the center and position deviation R
as the radius. From this, the pre-formation relation position
vector of a UAV can be calculated. Its direction is from the
position of follower to another UAV. Lidar can measure the
relative localization between UAVs. We deﬁne the relative
localization vector measured by Lidar as the Lidar position
vector. Its direction is from the position of the follower to
the position of another UAV detected by Lidar. In formation
ﬂight, a vehicle can obtain n-1 pre-formation position vectors
and k Lidar position vectors, where n is the number of vehicles
in the formation, and k is the number of vehicles detected by
Lidar-equipped followers.
By comparing the lengths and angles of the vectors, the best
matching pair of vectors can be selected, and the UAV number
corresponding to the position vector of Lidar can be known.

4.1. Actor-critic network
The deep deterministic policy gradient is a policy-based deep
reinforcement learning algorithm that works with continuous
state and action spaces. Policy-based reinforcement learning
methods aim to directly search the optimal policy p, which
provides a feasible framework for continuous control. DDPG
utilizes the actor-critic paradigm, adopts an experience play-
back mechanism and a separate target network, reduces the
correlation between data, and increases stability and robust-
ness. To approximate a deterministic policy (Actor) and Q
function (Critic), the actor-critic structure, which is composed
of an actor network and critic network, is applied in the
DDPG algorithm.
The actor network module observes the state of the envi-
ronment, uses the neural network to obtain the selection prob-
ability of action, and completes the interaction process
between agent and environment. The rewards calculated from
the interaction are utilized to maintain and update the action
selection policy of agent. The critic network evaluates the value
of the environmental state and action. To be speciﬁc, the critic
network estimates the value of the actor network module by
observing the state and action, and its output can guide the
action selection policy of the actor network. It can be seen that
there are two networks for the actor-critic algorithm, and the
actor and critic networks need to construct their own neural
networks. The artiﬁcial neural network of the actor network
module realizes the refection from the observation state to
the action selection policy of the agent, and its training process
must consider the error of the critic network module. The arti-
ﬁcial neural network of the critic network obtains scores
through the observation of the environmental state and action,
and maps them to the corresponding score. The soft update
method is applied to update the parameters of target networks,
and can be expressed as

4.2. Collision risk evaluation map
We present a novel collision risk evaluation map for UAV for-
mations to avoid collisions with obstacles and other moving
UAVs during ﬂight. The UAV domain2,9,10 represents the
ﬂight area where collisions should be avoided. The UAV

5. Formation ﬂight experiments
We discuss the experimental conﬁguration and results. The
proposed reinforcement learning control algorithm was imple-
mented in Python using TensorFlow.44 We trained the pro-
posed formation control model in virtual formation ﬂight
environments based on a self-developed ﬂight simulator, con-
sisting of an environment module, multi-type UAV model,
physical engine, sensor modules, rendering interface, and inter-
face API. To make the model better adapt to the real environ-
ment, we executed the training process in a real-world
formation ﬂight to ﬁne-tune the network parameters. We uti-
lized the parameters obtained in a virtual environment, and
implemented the model on an Nvidia Jetson Tx2 onboard
ﬂight control computer. In each episode of real-world training,
UAVs conducted close-formation ﬂight tasks to ﬁnish training
episodes. When a vehicle deviated from the desired formation
trajectory, the safety drone operator took over the ﬂight-
control system and ended the episode. The state data recorded
by the ﬂight log were used to update parameters and optimize
the control policy. After an amount of ﬁne-tuning training in
real environments, the proposed model could achieve the
desired effectiveness and robustness.

The ﬂight space was 10 km long, 8 km wide, and 150 m high.

UAV formation started in a random position and executed

6. Conclusions
In this study, we proposed a cooperative relative localization
methodology for close-formation ﬂight in GPS-denied envi-
ronments, where each vehicle in formation could measure the
relative localization of its neighbors by processing Lidar point
cloud data instead of using a GPS sensor. The novel relative
localization algorithm is developed to measure the position
of UAV in the formation. To estimate the center points of
UAVs, a k-means-based point cloud method was developed.
An innovative position vector matching algorithm is proposed
to obtain the complete relative localization information in for-
mation. The estimated inter-UAV distances and relative veloc-
ity were fed into the designed reinforcement learning based
formation control module to generate optimal actions to con-
trol vehicles to start and keep ﬂying in formation in a speciﬁc
geometry. To avoid collisions in formation ﬂight, a novel col-
lision risk evaluation map was developed. A dynamic priori-
tized experience replay algorithm was proposed to improve
training efﬁciency. A series of experiments, including simula-
tion tests, hardware-in-the-loop experiments and comparison
experiments with the latest methods were conducted to verify
the theoretical elements, and real-world ﬂight experiments fur-
ther showed positive results. Our method overcame the difﬁ-
culty
of
precisely
localizing
UAVs
in
formation
ﬂight
without global positioning data, and provided robust and
effective performance of formation control in complex ﬂight
situations. The method offered a highly efﬁcient and stable
solution for close UAVs in formation without GPS in practical
engineering tasks. The combination of the proposed system
with a visual navigation system will be investigated in our
future work.
