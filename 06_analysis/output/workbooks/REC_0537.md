Abstract — Accurate position estimation during navigation 
is essential for missions conducted using Unmanned Aerial 
Systems (UASs), particularly in fully autonomous operations or 
missions performed in Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) 
conditions. In outdoor environments, conventional positioning 
solutions primarily rely on Global Navigation Satellite Systems 
(GNSS). However, GNSS-based approaches are not suitable for 
indoor navigation scenarios or GPS-denied environments, 
where satellite signals are unavailable or severely degraded. As 
a result, alternative positioning and navigation technologies are 
required to ensure reliable and accurate UAS operation in such 
conditions. Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) are becoming 
essential components of UAS navigation architectures. By 
integrating low-cost Inertial Measurement Units (IMU), INS 
provide reliable navigation and attitude estimation capabilities, 
particularly 
in 
GNSS-denied 
environments. 
However, 
uncompensated bias and drift errors lead to the accumulation of 
position and velocity errors, resulting in significant navigation 
inaccuracies. This work investigates the impact of IMU bias and 
drift errors on UAS navigation and attitude estimation and 
introduces an estimation technique based on the Extended 
Kalman Filter (EKF) to jointly estimate and compensate these 
error sources. The proposed EKF-based approach estimates 
both bias and drift terms affecting the UAS position and 
attitude, thereby mitigating the accumulation of velocity and 
position errors over time. The effectiveness of the proposed 
method is validated through simulation tests conducted in 
MATLAB® and Simulink® environments, reproducing realistic 
operational scenarios. Simulation results demonstrate that the 
proposed approach significantly enhances UAS navigation 
performance by improving estimation accuracy and reducing 
overall navigation errors. 
Keywords—Indoor Navigation, UAS, IMU, INS, Indoor 
Positioning, Attitude Estimation, EKF. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
During missions conducted using Unmanned Aerial 
Vehicles (UAVs), localization refers to the determination of a 
vehicle’s position and attitude in two or three-dimensional 
space. The use of small and micro UAVs has significantly 
increased in recent years, driven by higher levels of autonomy 
and the rapid development of low-cost electronic components, 
such as microcontrollers and onboard sensors [1] [2]. 
In particular, multirotor platforms (e.g., quadcopters, 
hexacopters, etc.) offer vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) 
capabilities, omnidirectional motion, and the ability to hover 
at a fixed position. These characteristics make them well 
suited for reconnaissance and monitoring operations in hostile 
or hazardous environments, where human intervention is 
impractical or unsafe. Nowadays, the most widely adopted 
navigation architectures for UASs rely on the integration of 
Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) and Inertial 
Navigation Systems (INS), which include an Inertial 
Measurement Unit (IMU) composed of magnetometers, 
gyroscopes, and accelerometers. While GNSS provides 
absolute position information by processing signals from 
satellite constellations, it becomes unreliable or unavailable in 
indoor environments due to signal attenuation and loss [3]. 
MARG (Magnetic, Angular Rate and Gravity) sensors are 
typically based on Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems 
(MEMS) 
technology 
and 
integrate 
gyroscopes, 
accelerometers, and magnetometers. These sensors enable the 
estimation of the orientation of a rigid body with respect to a 
fixed reference frame. Unlike GNSS-based positioning 
systems, MARG sensors can provide attitude information in 
indoor environments, since they do not rely on signals from 
external sources. Moreover, the widespread adoption of these 
sensors in many applications is mainly due to their low weight, 
low cost, low power consumption, and relatively high real-
time performance [4]. 
MEMS-based IMUs, are commonly used to estimate 
position by double integration of acceleration measurements 
[5]. However, IMU sensor readings inherently contain errors 
such as random noise and sensor biases, which degrade 
navigation performance. In addition, numerical integration 
introduces further inaccuracies due to time discretization, 
which exacerbates the accumulation of drift over time [6] [7]. 
Over the years, several approaches have been developed 
to address the problem of attitude and position estimation, and 
various sensor fusion algorithms have been implemented and 
tested for onboard applications. Among the available 
estimation techniques, the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is 
commonly employed for nonlinear state estimation problems, 
enabling the fusion of inertial and external sensor 
measurements to improve the accuracy and robustness of 
navigation solutions.

framework for UAS operating in indoor and GNSS-denied

III. SIMULATIONS AND RESULTS 
Tests were conducted in a controlled indoor environment 
designed to simulate UAV navigation conditions in the 
absence of GNSS signals. During the data acquisition 
campaign, a set of predefined motion profiles was performed, 
including take-off and landing sequences, as well as 
controlled roll, pitch, and yaw maneuvers. These trajectories 
were designed to sufficiently excite the system dynamics 
across all degrees of freedom. 
The IMU sensor was rigidly mounted on a rotary platform, 
allowing repeatable and well-defined angular excitations. 
This configuration enabled the assessment of the system’s 
response to both vertical accelerations and rotational 
dynamics. Sensor data were acquired at a frequency of 10 Hz. 
Prior to the experiments, an initial calibration procedure 
was performed to compensate for sensor biases and scale 
factor errors. In particular, magnetometer calibration was 
carried out to correct for hard-iron and soft-iron distortions, 
enabling reliable heading estimation. The calibrated 
measurements were used to initialize the state vector of the 
EKF. A static alignment phase was conducted at the 
beginning of each experiment, during which the system was 
assumed to be at rest. This phase allowed for the estimation 
of initial roll and pitch angles from accelerometer 
measurements, whereas the yaw angle was initialized using 
magnetometer data. Gyroscope biases were estimated during 
this stationary interval and included in the initial state. The 
data acquired during each measurement session were 
transmitted via USB cable to a laptop and stored for 
subsequent post-processing, during which the proposed 
methodology was applied. 
The initial state covariance matrix was defined to reflect the 
uncertainty associated with the initial conditions, with higher 
variance assigned to position and velocity states and lower 
variance to attitude components after alignment. Process 
noise was modeled as zero-mean white Gaussian noise, 
accounting for accelerometer and gyroscope measurement 
noise as well as bias instability. Measurement noise 
covariance was defined based on sensor specifications and 
empirical tuning. This setup provided a consistent and 
repeatable framework for evaluating the performance of the 
EKF-based state estimation algorithm under controlled 
conditions. In particular, both the process noise covariance

IV. CONCLUSIONS AND FURTHER WORK 
This paper presents an attitude and position estimation 
framework for indoor UAV navigation systems operating in 
GNSS-denied environments. In particular, the study 
investigates the impact of IMU bias and drift errors on UAV 
navigation and attitude estimation and proposes an estimation 
approach based on the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) to 
jointly estimate and compensate for these error sources. 
Experimental tests were conducted in a controlled 
environment, where both static and dynamic sessions were 
performed. Static tests were used for IMU characterization 
and EKF calibration, while dynamic experiments reproduced 
translational and rotational motion conditions representative 
of typical UAV maneuvers. The application of the EKF 
significantly improved the quality of the estimated navigation 
states by reducing the effects of sensor noise, drift, and bias 
accumulation. In particular, the proposed filtering approach 
achieved a position error reduction of 68.2% along the y-axis 
and 89.3% along the x-axis with respect to the raw 
measurements, while simultaneously improving attitude 
estimation through the mitigation of drift errors in the 
estimated Euler angles. 
This preliminary work represents part of the development 
of an indoor navigation system for UAV applications in 
GNSS-denied environments. Future developments will focus 
on the integration of the proposed EKF-based estimation 
framework with a Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) indoor 
navigation system previously developed by the authors [14], 
[15].
