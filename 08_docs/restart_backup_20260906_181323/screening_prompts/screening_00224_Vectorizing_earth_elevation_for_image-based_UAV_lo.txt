TITLE: Vectorizing earth elevation for image-based UAV localization in GNSS-denied environments
AUTHORS: Ahmedelbadawi H.; Żugaj M.; González-Moreno M.T.; Moreno Cuenca L.
YEAR: 2025.0
ABSTRACT: This paper presents a vision-based framework for unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) localization in GNSS-denied environments, integrating multi-modal terrain data with stereo vision to achieve robust and accurate pose estimation. Unlike conventional RGB-based methods that are sensitive to lighting, seasonal changes, and environmental variability, the proposed approach incorporates elevation-derived information, including digital surface models (DSM), slope, aspect, and vector ruggedness measure (VRM). These datasets are vectorized into numerical representations, enabling invariant feature matching against georeferenced reference maps even in visually degraded or textureless scenes. The proposed framework integrates a downward-facing stereo camera to accurately capture depth information and ensure scale consistency. Distinctive keypoints are extracted from UAV-acquired RGB imagery using the Scale-Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT), while features derived from the DSM are vectorized into slope, aspect, and VRM descriptors to enhance terrain characterization. A k-d tree structure is employed to accelerate nearest-neighbor searches, and feature correspondences are efficiently established using the Fast Library for Approximate Nearest Neighbors (FLANN) matching. To account for variations in scale and orientation, a homography matrix is estimated, followed by a geo-to-pixel transformation that ensures precise georegistration of UAV imagery within the reference map frame. Experimental results demonstrate a reduction in localization drift by more than 30% compared with conventional RGB-only SIFT-based approaches. The achieved localization accuracy ranges from approximately 40 meters to as low as 0.6 meters. As the framework relies entirely on classical computer vision techniques without deep learning, it maintains computational efficiency and is suitable for real-time deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms. © 2025 SPIE.
