Model-Based Cooperative
Navigation for a Group of
Flying Robots
ALI FAGHIHINIA
M.A. AMIRI ATASHGAH
University of Tehran, Tehran, Iran
S.M. MEHDI DEHGHAN
Malek-Ashtar University of Technology, Tehran, Iran
In this article, a model-based cooperative navigation system for a
group of quadrotors, while no absolute position data is accessible, is
proposed. The growth of position errors in a GNSS-denied condition
is such that the navigation methods are not reliable. In the proposed
method, instead of the conventional inertial navigation equations, a
different dynamic equation based on drag forces is used. Together with
the relative position measurements between agents, the acceleration
measurements on the plane of the rotors of at least one of the agents
are also used in the observation process. For evaluation of the pro-
posed method, an algorithm-in-loop 6DOF simulation environment is
developed. The results illustrate that the growth rate of navigation
errors is signiﬁcantly reduced; 97% in the horizontal plane and 40%
in the vertical one. In short, integrating the proposed model-based and
cooperative navigation methods is more effective than using each of
them separately.

I.
INTRODUCTION
With the advent of a new generation of mobile robots
(MR), an idea called “cooperative robotics” has recently
emerged to ease the achievement of complex missions. A
group of ﬂying robots (FRs) is more robust than a single
one, in which more complex missions can be performed [1].
Most of the prevailing navigation approaches are devoted
to the optimal fusion of the measurements of propriocep-
tive and exteroceptive instruments for measuring vehicle
motion and surrounding environment signals. To resolve
accumulated errors of inertial measurement unit (IMU),
position ﬁxing methods (i.e., GNSS) are fused with the
dead reckoning (DR) ones. However, GNSS signals may
be unavailable for a variety of reasons.
In this regard, Kurazume et al. proposed “cooperative
positioning” in 1994 [2]. Later, a method called “collec-
tive localization” without using any external source was
presented [3]. Using linear observability analysis, it was
found that the system is not observable unless at least one
of the robots accesses the absolute position. Rekleitis et al.
[4] investigated the effects of different factors like relative
range and bearing measurements and the number of agents
on the performance of CN. In this regard, Rekleitis and
Roumeliotis [5] and [6] followed analytical solutions for the
odometry errors. For the ﬁrst time, Martinelli and Siegwart
[7] presented a nonlinear observability analysis method
based on Lie algebra for two MRs. Later, a closed-form
solution for steady-state position error covariance in [8] was
presented.
CN was ﬁrst introduced by Sharma and Taylor in which,
by nonlinear observability analysis, they veriﬁed that the
IMU biases are observable [9]. Next, they presented a
distributed CN by measuring only the relative bearing an-
gle between the agents [10]. Moreover, a consistent CN
for multiple autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) was
proposed in [11]. Afterward, Gao and Chitre [12] presented
a CN for two AUVs using range-only measurements. To
analyze the nonlinear observability of the bearing-only CN,
a graph-based method was proposed in [13]. At the same
time, a centralized cooperative localization method, based
onrelativemeasurementsbetweentheagentsandlandmarks
with a known position, was presented in a GNSS-denied
condition [14]; however, Goel et al. [15] used a UWB
moduleineachagentasarelativesensor.Later,Chakraborty
et al. [16] demonstrated that each group can localize itself
using only two known landmarks and relative bearing-only
observations. Russell et al. [17] solved the problem of
localizing a GPS-denied UAV (agent B) with the assistance
of a GPS-enabled UAV (agent A), cooperatively.
In references that investigate model-based inertial navi-
gation, the two terms “model aided” and “model-based” are
well known. In “model aided” methods a precise dynamic
model alongside the INS is used. Koifman and Bar-Itzhack
[18] are well-known pioneers in this ﬁeld. In their work, a
complete model of the dynamics of a ﬁxed-wing drone is
presented as an alternative to the GPS, in which the error
of the coefﬁcients and parameters in the model during the

References that use the “model-based” term employ a
model such as an algebraic constraint or a differential con-

and [26].
According to the reviews, the CN of ﬂying robots in
which no agent has access to absolute position information
still demands more effort. In this regard, an analytical
solution was derived by Faghihinia et al. [27]. The author
demonstrated that the position error covariance does not
depend on ﬂight trajectory and noise characteristics of the
relative position measurements, while it depends on the
number of the agents in the group and the noise covariance
of the accelerometers. To enhance the CN in [27], in this
article, a drag force-based (DFB) approach is proposed.
The rest of this article is organized as follows: In Sec-
tion II-A, the equations related to a single agent model-
based navigation are derived. Section II-B describes coop-
erative navigation and related derivations, and Section II-C
demonstrates the model-based cooperative navigation al-
gorithm. Next, the results of the simulations, developed in
MATLAB, and the evaluation of the proposed method are
presented in Section III. Finally, Section IV concludes this
article.

A.
Single-Agent Model-Based Navigation

an EKF to perform the CN algorithm. It is assumed that

C.
MB-CN Structure
In the following, the introduced MB method (see Fig. 2)
is integrated with the CN algorithm. The proposed method,
instead of the conventional IMU-based DR, is based on
(5)–(14), while each agent separately propagates its model.
Therefore, the μ of each agent is augmented to the state
vector of the whole system. Moreover, in addition to rel-
ative position observations between the agents, the body
acceleration measurements, ax and ay of one of the agents,
is also used as an observation in the update step. Fig. 3
presentstherelatedblockdiagramofanN-agentMB-CN.In
the proposed method, the propagations are accomplished in
each agent distinctly. Likewise, the update step is performed
on a single central EKF, which can be implemented on a
ground station or the processor of one agent. Each quadrotor
can be equipped with IMU and UWB device for measuring
and communicating the relative position data, including
relative distance and bearing angle between the agents.
The update step has two separate sections, each of which
performs the update at a different rate. In the ﬁrst part,
the update is performed at a high rate using acceleration
observations of only one agent of the group. In the second
section, the update is performed at a lower rate using relative
position observations generated by UWB transceivers. Only
in one agent of the group, the measured accelerations ax
and ay, is added to the output vector. Earlier than the update
step starts, all data are aggregated into a uniﬁed structure.
After the update step, the estimated data including the
states and covariances are distributed among the agents.
In CN, each relative observation between agents forms a
cross-correlation between them. When the agents observe
each other, all elements of the covariance matrix, including
the cross-correlations and covariances of each agent, gains
a nonzero value. Hence, it is necessary that the ax and
ay measured in one of the agents, is augmented to the
observation vector of the whole system.

loop. In the following, ﬁrst, the CN method based on the new
dynamic equation (DFB: drag-force-based) is evaluated.
Next, the MB-CN performance evaluations are performed
via the simulation environment. Finally, the efﬁciency of the
proposed method is assessed through performance indices.
A.
Evaluation of DFB-CN Method
To assess the effect of the DFB dynamic equations on
the CN, two series of simulations are performed. In the
ﬁrst one, the CN is based on the new equations (DFB).
Then, the evaluation process of the CN, based on con-
ventional IMU, is conducted. In both simulations, only
relative observations are used in the update step. Since
the simulation conditions are the same, the performance
of both methods can be examined and compared with each
other. In the following, at ﬁrst, only the DR navigation,
based on these two dynamic equations is simulated without
any update. In this simulation, the DR navigation code
executes 200 times where every ﬂight lasts 100 s. In all
simulations, the quadrotor is simulated in a stationary state
on the ground. Here, the parameter μ is known exactly just
before the simulation starts. In this regard, Fig. 4 shows
the 2-D position error in the horizontal plane in the 100 s
versus the simulation run number. As depicted in Fig. 4,
the dispersion of the position error regarding the IMU case
is greater than the one in the DFB instance. One reason
is that in the IMU case, the noise of all accelerometers
and gyros is propagated, while in DFB equations, only the
noise of one accelerometer and three gyros are propagated.
It should be noted that the dispersion of the altitude errors
are the same in both cases; since the errors initiate from the
vertical measurements az. Fig. 5 depicts the estimated 2-D
positions in the horizontal plane for both cases. This ﬁgure
also indicates that based on the DFB dynamic equations,
the CN experiences higher performance. Again, It should
be noted that only relative observations are employed here
and acceleration observations are not used for updates. One
of the reasons for the performance improvement is that the
estimation algorithm which uses DFB equations involves a
higher capacity to estimate vx and vy and consequently the
roll and pitch angles as well. One of the remarkable results
shown in Fig. 6 is the improvement of the μ estimation
errors.

IV.
CONCLUSION
In this article, an MB method was employed to enhance
the performance of the CN of a group of quadrotors. This
method is only for multirotor type robots because the dy-
namic model used in the proposed method is based on DFB;
due to rotors drag, which is a linear function of vx and vy.
Therefore, it is possible to directly observe body velocities
in the same directions by the accelerometers data ax and
ay. By observing the velocities, the roll and pitch angles
can also be updated well. By transferring the velocities
in the NED frame and integrating over time, the position
on the horizontal plane can be obtained with appropriate
accuracy. However, although there is no external source
of absolute position data, the position errors and their co-
variance increase over time at a much slower rate relative
to the CN. It was also shown in the simulations that the
parameter μ in the drag force model of each quadrotor can
bewellestimatedwithoutaccelerationobservationsviaonly
relative observations. The results illustrate that RMSE of
MBC-EKF is reduced 97% in the 2-D (horizontal) position
and 40% in the vertical one, relative to the DFB-C-EKF (CN
based on DFB) algorithm; while it is 42% in the horizontal
plane and 73% on the vertical one, relative to MB-EKF.
The followings are some activities that can be conducted as
further research:
1) sensitivity analysis of the proposed method for var-
ious parameters such as drag coefﬁcients, mass and
inertia tensor changes, and also measurement limits
(on the range and bearing measurements);
2) using of experimental and test data to validate the
proposed method;
3) development of a model-based CN for a nonhomoge-
nous group;
4) development and implementation of a model-based
SLAM.
