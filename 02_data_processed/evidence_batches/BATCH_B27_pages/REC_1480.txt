Pose Estimation for an Autonomous Vehicle using
Monocular Vision
Nikunj Kothari, Misha Gupta, Leena Vachhani and Hemendra Arya
Abstract—Vision-based pose estimation is particularly important
for small sized mobile robots and aerial vehicles that have limited
payload constraints. Accurate localization is of importance for any
autonomous vehicle, especially in indoor and GPS-denied (Global
Positioning System) environment. The aim of this work is to provide
a solution for the pose estimation of an autonomous vehicle using
monocular vision alone so that even a small-size vehicle can perform
pose estimation without the help of additional sensors. Unlike Visual
Odometry which operates on a sequence of images to estimate
robot’s motion, the proposed method estimates the robot’s pose using
individual images by constantly tracking four ﬁxed feature points
in a rectangular pattern, whose positions are known a priori. Also,
unlike Monocular Visual Odometry which suffers from scale ambiguity
problem, the proposed algorithm can estimate the pose of the vehicle
and can be applied to both planar robots (3-DoF) as well as aerial
vehicles (6-DoF).
The proposed method has been validated by implementing on
a Raspberry Pi2 model B and a RPi camera. Simulation results of
controlling a differential drive robot using the proposed pose estimation
method are also presented.
Keywords—Autonomous, Localization, Pose, Estimation
I.
INTRODUCTION
Autonomous vehicles with small form factor, quick response
and the ability to operate remotely in a difﬁcult and challenging
environment have wide applications. To accomplish an autonomous
task, the vehicle should have the capability to determine its pose
using sensors mounted on the vehicle. Accurate pose estimation of
the autonomous vehicle still remains a signiﬁcant challenge, espe-
cially in Global Positioning System (GPS) denied environments.
Small size vehicles like Micro Aerial Vehicle (MAV) have
major constraint of limited payload carrying capacity. Therefore,
a wise selection of sensor(s) and processing unit is important.
Equally important is an on-line and real-time estimation of the
pose of the vehicle.
An acoustic-based solution for pose estimation [1] has been
developed, that which requires multiple geometrically-conditioned
observations for applying lateration techniques using localiza-
tion. A robust and accurate position-orientation pose estimation
method [2], requiring computationally expensive acoustic array
signal processing techniques. Vision-based positioning arises as a
complementary navigation subsystem and plays a supplementary
role in the GPS-denied environment. Also, it is able to free the
system from relying on the external positioning devices as well as
provide the MAV a natural sensing method for object detection
and tracking. Monocular Camera, being a light weight and an
inexpensive sensor providing rich information has been used for
completely autonomous ﬂights [3] for indoor and GPS-denied
environments. However, the vision algorithms are executed off-
board on a ground station that establishes wireless communication
to send the control commands to the vehicle. Since wireless
communication is not always reliable and time-consuming, it is
essential for computing all tasks of the control framework on board.
Two separate sensing solutions are presented in [4] towards an
integrated helicopter system capable of indoor ﬂight using both
laser rangeﬁnder and a stereo camera system on board. The visual
framework [5] has been optimized for an embedded solution to
satisfy the constraint of limited on board processing power. A
vision-based framework [6] estimates the ﬁve degrees-of-freedom
pose using a camera mounted on a quadrotor helicopter. [7]
proposes a vision-based state estimation approach which combines
the advantages of monocular vision with that of stereo vision. [8]
identiﬁes window as an object of interest and estimate MAV’s
pose with respect to it. They developed safe path-planning method
using the information provided by the GPS and the on board
inertial and stereo vision sensors. Instead of using stereo vision to
estimate the pose, we are able to estimate the pose using monocular
vision. Real-time localization for the autonomous navigation of
a MAV [9] has been achieved on a mobile processor using an
on board computing unit(1.6 GHz Atom processor) and multiple
sensors (laser, camera, and IMU). A scanning laser range sensor
retroﬁtted with mirrors is used as a primary source of information
for position and yaw estimation. This is followed by simultaneous
localization and mapping algorithm(SLAM). A landmark-based
monocular localization technique [10] uses the time-consuming
Scale Invariant Feature Transform (SIFT) for absolute pose estima-
tion. Hybrid pose estimation approach [11] uses coloured markers,
mobile accelerometers, and multi-sensor data fusion techniques.
Two different fusion algorithms for the pose estimation have been
proposed based on stereo vision and monocular vision. Monocular
vision [12] has been used to extract edges and compared with
known 3D model of the environment followed by particle ﬁlter
for localization. The autopilot uses an optic ﬂow-based vision
system [13] for pose estimation based on autonomous localization
and scene mapping. It requires implementation with 3 Nested
Kalman Filters (3NKF) for robust estimation. Real-time visual-
inertial navigation using an iterated EKF has been shown in [14]
with a complexity which grows with the number of features. The
more efﬁcient approaches in [15] considered pairwise images for
visual odometry and fused the output with inertial measurements
in an EKF. Altimeter is also used to measure unknown scale. The
IMU measurements [16], [17] have been coupled with EKF SLAM
framework. However, the computational cost of EKF SLAM is
O(N 2) for N features. The need is to estimate the pose of the
vehicle using a light weight sensor requiring minimal processing.
The proposed vision-based technique for pose estimation is an
iterative approach that can be applied to both ground and aerial
robots. Navigation of a mobile robot to a desired ﬁnal position
is shown using simulations in the paper. Experimental result of
estimating the pose or a camera are also included in the paper.
[p.2]
Simulations and experiments are performed in an environment
where corner points of a square window are used as key features
to localize a differential drive robot. Although the paper presents
the algorithm based on rectangular features, the proposed technique
can be extended to any shape with more than three distinct features.
This approach can perform autonomous ﬂight navigation in
indoor environments, solely using a monocular camera as sensor
while all computation is done on the on board Raspberry Pi. In the
simulation of a differential drive robot, pose estimation error in
robot’s position in x and z directions are 3.7 × 10−4 relative units
and 7.3 × 10−5 relative units respectively. The estimation error in
robot’s heading is 0.043◦. In the experimental validation of this
approach in 3D, position error of less than 0.02m is achieved.
The proposed algorithm takes on average less than 0.1 sec for
estimating the camera position and orientation on a Raspberry Pi
Model B computer. Computationally inexpensive, low processing
time requirement and good precision are the major advantages of
the proposed technique that supports on board execution.
Our work starts by studying the effect of changes in camera
pose to transformations in the image, followed by the methodology
to estimate the camera pose in Section II. Explanation of image
correcting algorithm and the underlying maths behind them is
presented in Section III. Validation of the algorithm using simula-
tion and experimental results is done in Section IV-C. Section V
concluded the paper by highlighting the advantages of the proposed
algorithm, along with directions for future work.
II.
PROPOSED POSE ESTIMATION TECHNIQUE
We estimate the pose of the vehicle using the projection of four
feature points on the image plane. The feature points are the corners
of a door, window or any rectangular object. Fig 11 describes the
convention used for window image throughout the paper. (xj, yj)
are the pixel values of the four window corner points. ζj represent
the internal angle formed by the window shape at the jth vertex.
The x and y axis are along the direction shown in Fig 11, and
z axis is orthogonal to the plane of the paper, as arrived by the
right-hand rule. For a rectangular window, j = 1, 2, 3, 4.
When the camera is rotated with respect to the window, the
captured image of a square window is not a square. However, it is
a simple quadrilateral. The proposed method iteratively transforms
the captured image such that the four edge points of the quadrilat-
eral again forms a square. The method draws its inspiration from
studying the independent effects of camera translation and rotation
on the image of a square window.
•
Effect of camera translation on the captured image:
The translation of the camera along the X and Y axis
changes the centroid location of the window image in
the image plane, while translation along the Z direction
changes the scale of the window image. Translation does
not distort the image. In particular, the shape of the object
is preserved and the internal angles of the shape obtained
by joining the adjacent feature points on the image are
unchanged. Now, we have ζ1 = ζ2 = ζ3 = ζ4 = 90◦for
translation along any axis.
•
Effect of non-zero camera roll on the captured image:
Camera Roll, similar to camera translation, does not
distort the image, however, it rotates the window image
with respect to the centre of the image plane by an angle
equal to the camera roll angle. All the internal angles are
unchanged, thus ζ1 = ζ2 = ζ3 = ζ4 = 90◦.
•
Effect of non-zero camera yaw on the captured image:
The non-zero yaw angle of camera distorts the image and
changes its internal angles. If there is a positive yaw, then
ζ1 and ζ4 are less than 90◦, while ζ2 and ζ3 are more than
90◦.
•
Effect of non-zero camera pitch on the captured
image: Unlike the effects of non-zero translation and roll,
pitching the camera distorts the image and changes the
internal angles. If there is a positive pitch in the camera,
then ζ1 and ζ2 are less than 90◦, while ζ3 and ζ4 are
more than 90◦
It is fairly easy to compute the pose variables when only one
variable is non-zero. However, the estimation of pose is challenging
when multiple non-zero variables have coupled effect on the shape
of window image. We next describe our proposed technique to
estimate the pose in real scenarios when the vehicle can have
multiple non-zero position and orientation values. We assume that
the camera is rigidly mounted on the vehicle. Hence, estimating
the pose of the camera estimates the pose of the vehicle.
A. Estimation of camera pose
The proposed pose estimation iteratively corrects the image of
the window that would appear on the camera, if the camera had
no rotation with respect to the image source. The objective is to
obtain the ﬁnal image of the window as square after the iterative
corrections in pitch and yaw angle. The centre of the square in
ﬁnal window image gives the translation along X and Y axis.
The area of the ﬁnal window image serves as an estimator of
the translation of the camera along Z axis. With the knowledge
of the focal length of the camera and the global coordinates of
the corners of the square window, estimation of position with a
very high accuracy is obtained using the proposed technique. We
next present the estimation of each variable describing the pose of
vehicle.

The pose of a planar robot is described by three variables:
position (x, y) and orientation θ. The unicycle robot is controlled
by linear velocity (ν) and angular velocity (ω). The dynamics of

(xf, yf).

V.
CONCLUSION
An accurate pose information is always beneﬁcial for control-
ling an autonomous vehicle. This paper proposes a novel technique
for estimating the pose of an autonomous vehicle using monocular
vision. The iterative technique presented in this paper is aimed to
be used in an on board processor in small size vehicles with limited
payload constraint. The objective of the proposed technique is to
estimate the pose of the vehicle using a computationally inexpen-
sive and requiring low processing time method with high accuracy.
The simulation and experimental results validate these objectives.
