Distance-based Autonomous Docking for UAV-MP in GPS-denied
Environment
Cheng Cheng 1,2, Xiuxian Li1,2,3, Li Li1,2,3, Lihua Xie1,4
1. College of Electronics and Information Engineering, Tongji University, Shanghai 201804, China
2. Shanghai Institute of Intelligent Science and Technology, Shanghai 201804, China
3. Shanghai Research Institute for Intelligent Autonomous Systems, Shanghai 201804, China
4. School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Nanyang Technological University, 639798, Singapore
Abstract:
In this paper, we study the docking problem between a UAV and a mobile platform (MP) by estimating
the relative position through distance information and its own displacement, and design the approaching trajectory for
eliminating the need of any global positioning systems or other infrastructures. The main challenges of this problem are
to estimate the relative position between the UAV and the MP and design controllers for the navigation trajectory control.
Since the MP is not equipped with odometer and its motion changes from time to time, the UAV is iteratively navigated
using the estimated relative positions to make it inﬁnitely close to the MP, so that the MP ﬁnally enters the ﬁeld of view of
the camera and accomplishes the autonomous docking. A simulation experiment is carried out and the theoretical results
are veriﬁed.
Key Words: UAV, MP, Autonomous Docking, GPS-denied Environment
1
INTRODUCTION
In the past few years, multi-rotor unmanned air vehicle
(UAV) technology has been developed to a large extent and
has led to a large number of applications, in areas such as
agriculture, transport and exploration [1–4]. How to navi-
gate a UAV to land on a MP is always a popular research
topic. The ﬁrst step in completing the landing task is, of
course, navigation, i.e., controlling the UAV to ﬂy over the
MP (the MP comes within the UAV’s ﬁeld-of-view (FOV)
of camera). Depending on the positioning methods, there
are two main types of autonomous docking: absolute posi-
tioning (GPS, Beidou, MCS) and self-search navigation (V-
SLAM) [5–8]. The former is based on the signal strength
of the base setup which determines its accuracy in absolute
coordinate systems and will lead to deviations or failures in
navigation when the signal is limited. On the other hand,
the latter requires a large amount of search until the MP
appears within the FOV of the UAV, often requiring signif-
icant computing power and time. Therefore, it is necessary
to propose autonomous navigation algorithms for UAV the
navigation algorithm is only for UAV, which do not depend
on external infrastructures, and do not need a mass of com-
puting resources and too much search time. In turn, the
method of using relative positioning in navigation has be-
come a research direction.

Problem Statement
This section introduces the dynamic models and relative
positioning of UAV and MP. Because this paper consid-
ers a GPS-denied environment, they cannot position them-
selves in any global coordinate system. The UAV can per-
form self-positioning and navigation in its own local in-
ertial coordinate system through the integrated gyroscope,
accelerometer and camera measurements.
However, the
MP is not equipped with any positioning sensors, so the
displacement of its random motion is unknown.
Assuming that the UAV can maintain its altitude, the UAV’s
model is considered as
pU(k) = pU(k −1) + Tu(k),
(1)
where pU(k) ∈R3 is the UAV’s position at time instant
k ≥0, T represents the sampling period and u(k) is the
control input at time instant k ≥0. Similarly, the MP dy-
namics is considered as
pM(k) = pM(k −1) + Tv(k),
(2)
where pM(k) ∈R3 is the MP position at time instant k ≥
0, v(k) ∈R3 is the average speed of MP within a sampling
period T at time instant k ≥0, and the z-coordinate is
kept as the height of the MP. Assume that MP will change
its direction and velocity magnitude once per cycle, i.e.,
making irregular random all-directional displacements.
Remark 1. Note that the dynamics model of MP in (2)
has been used for the Omnidirectional Mobile Robot (MY
wheel, MY wheel-II, Swedish wheel and Omni-wheel) [19].
In the approaching phase, the MP is simply treated as a
mass point due to long-range distance and relatively slow
movements compared with the faster UAV.
The docking problem is solved when the MP is not in the
FOV of the UAV and cannot complete the landing if we can
design a control law to achieve the following:

(3)
where ¯d > 0 is a constant, representing the ﬂight altitude
and maximal FOV of the UAV.

above problems, but this work is different from others. For
example, this paper differs from references [5-7] in that it
does not rely on infrastructures. In addition, for the dock-

4
Simulations
This section presents the simulation validation of the au-
tonomous docking method proposed in this paper. Through
the measurement data collected by the range sensor, geo-
metric calculation is performed, and then the relative po-
sitioning is obtained by the centre-of-mass method. Based
on the obtained relative positioning, an optimal progressive
navigation trajectory is designed until the MP enters FOV
of the UAV.
In the simulation, the initial point of random motion (the
speed of MP is less than 2m/s) of MP with random
size and random direction changing every 1s is located at
(−35, 40, 0.2), where 0.2 is the platform height of MP. The
UAV is moving at a maximum speed of 5m/s, starting at
the position (0, 0, 4), where the height is 4m, α = 0.95.
First, based on the accuracy of realistic UWB sensors, a
zero-mean random noise with uniform distribution on the
interval (−0.1m, 0.1m) is added to the distance measure-
ment. In addition, a zero-mean noise with uniform dis-
tribution on the interval (−0.01m, 0.01m) is added to the
odometer of displacement measurement.

5
CONCLUSIONS
This paper introduces a nonlinear UAV-MP autonomous
docking scheme based on distance and displacement mea-
surements. The distance is measured by the circumferential
displacement to solve the relative positioning. Finally, the
relative positioning is estimated by the centroid method,
and the navigation approach trajectory is designed. The
feasibility of the scheme is veriﬁed by numerical examples.
In addition, we plan to verify the result by real experiments
in future.
