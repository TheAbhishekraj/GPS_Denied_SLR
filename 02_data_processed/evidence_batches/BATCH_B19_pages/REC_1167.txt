Abstract
To enable autonomous route planning for UAV swarms in dynamic air–terrestrial coop-
erative navigation scenarios within GNSS-denied environments, this paper proposes a
lightweight framework based on the Artificial Potential Field (APF) method. In the con-
sidered architecture, UAVs act as mobile transit navigation nodes that relay positioning
information from sparse ground anchors to terrestrial users. For TOA-based cooperative
positioning, the instantaneous geometric configuration of the UAV swarm significantly
affects the overall system accuracy. Therefore, the impact of UAV positions on the end-to-
end navigation performance is rigorously analyzed, yielding a comprehensive Dilution
of Precision (DOP) matrix for the entire air–terrestrial system. By applying the Schur
complement, the global performance metric is decomposed, resulting in a scalar evalua-
tion function that directly reflects the geometric quality of the configuration. In practical
scenarios involving dynamic and heterogeneous users, real-time trajectory adaptation of
the UAV swarm is essential to continuously optimize user positioning accuracy. To this
end, an APF-based autonomous joint route planning approach is developed. The poten-
tial field is constructed directly from the derived geometric evaluation model, where its
negative gradient generates virtual forces that autonomously guide the UAV swarm. This
elegantly bridges high-level navigation performance optimization with low-level motion
control of the swarm. The simulation results show a 76.1% improvement in the average
comprehensive GDOP for users compared to the baseline of hovering UAVs, validating the
effectiveness and real-time capability of the proposed lightweight framework.
Keywords: GNSS-denied environments; air–terrestrial cooperation navigation; artificial
potential field; route optimization; lightweight computing
1. Introduction
The rapid advancement of near-ground and low-altitude applications has profoundly
impacted economic and military developments. However, in many operational scenarios,
the absence of precise position and timing information undermines mission-critical sup-
port. Navigation, positioning, and timing (PNT) services have thus become indispensable
infrastructure for modern tasks.
Existing navigation solutions for near-ground and low-altitude environments face vari-
ous limitations, lacking a universally effective approach. Global Navigation Satellite Systems
(GNSSs) provide worldwide coverage but are susceptible to interference, jamming, and signal

blockage [1]. Inertial navigation systems (INSs) are resilient to external disruptions but suffer
from error accumulation over time [2], requiring auxiliary absolute measurements for correc-
tion. Matching-based methods, such as visual navigation, rely on prior fingerprint databases
and are sensitive to environmental inaccuracies or changes [3,4], leading to significant perfor-
mance degradation. In contrast, ground-based radio navigation systems, anchored to fixed
terrestrial stations, offer high positional accuracy and stable timing through synchronization
or high-precision clocks, delivering reliable PNT services to users [5].
Despite these advantages, ground-based systems have inherent drawbacks: limited
coverage due to terrain constraints and obstacles, which further diminish service qual-
ity. Integrating Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) as auxiliary positioning nodes holds
promising potential. UAVs operate at elevated altitudes with diverse platforms, high
operational ceilings, and flexible networking capabilities, allowing for on-demand deploy-
ment [6]. By forming air–terrestrial cooperative navigation systems, UAVs complement
ground-based setups, significantly alleviating coverage limitations and enhancing overall
performance [7]. For UAV spatiotemporal referencing in such systems, ground stations
often provide stronger aerial coverage than terrestrial, enabling UAVs to self-localize us-
ing ground anchors [8]. Once positioned with sufficient accuracy, UAVs can rebroadcast
navigation signals to serve ground users or low-altitude peers, establishing a synergistic
air–ground positioning network [9–13]. This architecture has broad application potentials
in tasks such as Internet of Things (IoT) integration and intelligent vehicle fleets, etc.
Among radio measurement techniques suitable for air–terrestrial cooperation, Time
of Arrival (TOA)-based methods are the most prevalent, alongside velocity and angle
measurements [14]. A primary challenge in these approaches is the geometric distribution
of base stations relative to receivers, which amplifies measurement errors into positioning
inaccuracies. Effective control of this geometry is crucial for users, necessitating thorough
analysis, evaluation, and optimization. However, three key issues persist: the heterogeneity
and dynamism of aerial nodes and users, the complexity of network topologies, and the
computational demands from expanding network scales.
In precision analysis and geometric evaluation for air–terrestrial cooperative navigation,
mainstream approaches primarily rely on global modeling techniques. Methods based on the
Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) and Cramér–Rao Lower Bounds (CRLB) provide rigorous,
network-wide performance bounds and configuration insights [15,16]. Similarly, Dilution of
Precision (DOP) analysis decomposes positioning accuracy into geometric factors and User
Equivalent Range Error (UERE), offering an intuitive metric for evaluating instantaneous con-
figurations [9,17,18]. While effective for static or small-scale scenarios, these global approaches
often result in high-dimensional matrices when node counts and observations increase to
ensure low-altitude coverage and connectivity. The inversion or eigenvalue computation of
such large matrices incurs substantial computational overhead, hindering real-time evaluation
in dynamic environments with heterogeneous, mobile nodes. Moreover, the multi-hop error
propagation inherent in air–ground layered structures further complicates direct applica-
tion of conventional single-layer models [19]. Therefore, there is a need for a precision and
light evaluation approach that preserves geometric interpretability while enabling efficient,
scalable computation—particularly through matrix decomposition techniques that reduce
dimensionality without sacrificing system-level fidelity.
Route planning for UAV swarms in air–terrestrial cooperative networks faces sig-
nificant scalability and dynamism challenges. Heterogeneous and mobile user groups
require continuous trajectory adaptation to maintain favorable geometries, rendering static
hovering inadequate. The joint optimization of multiple UAVs inflates the search space and
computational complexity due to tight coupling in user accuracy determination. Existing
methods span a wide spectrum, including sampling-based planners, convex optimization,

bio-inspired metaheuristics, and learning-based approaches. Sampling methods like RRT*
and PRM* excel in high-dimensional exploration but often produce non-smooth paths and
struggle with real-time replanning in dynamic environments [17,18]. Convex techniques,
such as ADMM-based configuration optimization and model predictive control (MPC),
provide rigorous handling of constraints but incur high per-step computation, limiting
onboard feasibility [15,16]. Metaheuristic algorithms, including NSGA-III variants and
particle swarm optimization (PSO), offer strong global exploration and multi-objective
capability [15,20], yet their population-based iterations remain computationally intensive
and typically offline-oriented [11,21,22]. Learning-based methods, such as reinforcement
learning (RL) and federated RL, adapt well to uncertainties but demand extensive training
data and lack provable optimality guarantees [11]. In contrast, gradient-driven reactive
methods, particularly those based on Artificial Potential Fields (APFs), provide inherent
advantages for this context: extremely low per-step complexity, and seamless integration of
real-time feedback from system metrics like GDOP. These properties make gradient-based
reactive planning particularly suitable for lightweight, autonomous trajectory adaptation
in resource-constrained UAV platforms under dynamic heterogeneous user demands.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a lightweight framework based on the
Artificial Potential Field (APF) method for autonomous UAV swarm route planning in dy-
namic air–terrestrial cooperative navigation scenarios within GNSS-denied environments.
The main contributions are as follows:
(1)
The influence of UAV positions on the overall system accuracy is analyzed, forming a
comprehensive DOP matrix. The Schur complement decomposes the global metric,
yielding a geometry-configuration-based evaluation model that supports efficient
gradient computation.
(2)
An APF-based method enables autonomous joint route planning for the UAV swarm.
The derived geometric evaluation model constructs the potential field, with its gra-
dients serving as virtual forces to guide the swarm, effectively bridging high-level
navigation performance optimization with low-level motion control.
(3)
Simulations demonstrate the framework’s effectiveness in achieving real-time geometry
optimization and user accuracy improvement under dynamic heterogeneous demands.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 presents the precision
analysis of the air–terrestrial cooperative navigation system, including the system model,
positioning equations, and the lightweight Schur complement-based decomposition for user
geometric evaluation. Section 3 details the proposed APF-based UAV swarm route planning
framework, covering the algorithm principle, construction of the Artificial Potential Field,
and derivation of virtual forces with finite-difference gradient approximation. Section 4
evaluates the framework through extensive simulations in static and dynamic scenarios,
demonstrating convergence, real-time adaptability, and significant improvements in user
positioning performance. Finally, Section 5 summarizes the conclusions of this paper. An
appendix provides the analytical gradient derivation of the system precision matrix for
deeper insight into UAV position sensitivity.
2. Precision Analysis
This section presents a detailed analysis of the positioning precision in the air–
terrestrial cooperative navigation. It first describes the architecture and measurement
model, and then derives the linearized observation equations and the full precision covari-
ance matrix. Finally, a lightweight decomposition approach based on the Schur complement
is introduced to efficiently evaluate the user geometric precision while reducing computa-
tional complexity for real-time applications.

2.1. Air–Terrestrial Navigation Scenarios
The air–terrestrial cooperative navigation scenarios consists of three types of nodes:
ground anchors with precisely known positions, aerial UAVs acting as dynamic transit nodes,
and ground users requiring positioning service. The fundamental measurement employed
throughout the system is pseudorange observation, achieved via unidirectional broadcasting.
The operational sequence is as follows: Ground anchors, whose positions are fixed
and accurately surveyed in advance, continuously broadcast pseudorange signals. These
signals embed the anchor’s known position coordinates and timing reference information.
UAVs in the airspace receive signals from multiple ground anchors simultaneously. The
UAV then broadcasts its own pseudorange signals, together with its received anchors’
pseudorange signals, to ground users and potentially to other UAVs in the vicinity. Ground
users demodulate these broadcasts and apply SPP to determine all of the positions and
clock offsets relative to the system time [2].
Notably, UAVs are not limited to serving only ground users; they can also receive
pseudorange broadcasts from neighboring UAVs. This UAV-to-UAV unidirectional ranging
enables mutual observation among aerial nodes, which can further enhance swarm-level
geometric diversity, improve robustness against anchor signal blockage, and support
cooperative refinement of UAV positions in cases of partial anchor visibility.

of the composite potential field using the geometric evaluation metric from Section 2 as
the attractive component and operational constraints as repulsive terms. The derivation
of the resulting virtual forces, including a lightweight finite-difference approximation for
gradients, is then presented to enable real-time motion control.

3.1. Principle of Algorithm
The Artificial Potential Field (APF) method, originally proposed by Khatib in 1986 [23],
is a classical reactive approach for real-time path planning and motion control of robotic
agents. By analogy with physical potential fields, the method models the environment
and task objectives through virtual attractive and repulsive forces. The resultant force,
computed as the negative gradient of the total artificial potential, directly guides the agent
toward the goal while avoiding obstacles or undesirable configurations. Over the past
decades, APF and its numerous variants have been widely applied to multi-agent coordi-
nation, UAV trajectory planning, and swarm robotics, owing to its conceptual simplicity,
extremely low computational cost per control cycle, and natural suitability for distributed
implementation [24,25].
Despite its advantages, the classical APF suffers from well-known limitations, most
notably the susceptibility to local minima, oscillations in narrow passages, and goal non-
reachability under certain configurations [26]. In recent years, various improvements have
been proposed to mitigate these issues, including harmonic potential fields, virtual force
hybrid methods, and adaptive repulsive force functions [25,27].
For the air–terrestrial cooperative positioning problem considered in this paper, the
classical APF remains highly attractive for the following reasons: First, the primary objective
is to continuously optimize the geometric configuration of the UAV swarm in response to
dynamic and heterogeneous user demands, rather than to guarantee convergence to a globally
optimal static formation. Second, the onboard computational resources of UAVs are strictly
limited, making lightweight, reactive methods far more practical than global optimization
techniques such as evolutionary algorithms or convex solvers. Third, the geometric evaluation
metric derived in Section 2.2 is naturally differentiable and can be directly used to construct
the potential field, enabling a seamless bridge between high-level positioning performance
and low-level motion commands. These characteristics make APF particularly well-suited as
a real-time, trajectory adaptation framework for the UAV swarm in this context.
Consequently, rather than proposing a new variant to address the local minima issue
of APF itself, this work focuses on the specific application of the classical APF to the
UAV swarm route planning problem in air–ground collaborative radio navigation systems.
The emphasis is placed on how to properly define the potential field using the proposed
geometry evaluation model so as to achieve effective real-time optimization of user-end
positioning accuracy under dynamic conditions.

4. Simulation
This section evaluates the effectiveness and performance of the proposed framework
through comprehensive simulations. It first examines a static scenario to verify convergence
and baseline geometric improvement, and then extends to dynamic scenarios with piece-
wise linear user motion to demonstrate real-time adaptability and robustness. Comparative
analyses against non-adaptive baselines highlight the algorithm’s superior user accuracy,
inter-user balance, and temporal stability under varying conditions.

toward geometries that minimize the user comprehensive GDOP (GU). This serves as a

complement-based metric GU successfully guides the swarm downhill in the potential
field, while repulsive potentials enforce operational constraints.

the inherent limitations of classical Artificial Potential Field methods, including local minima,
oscillations in narrow passages, and target unreachability. In our specific application—real-
time geometric configuration optimization for UAV swarms serving dynamic ground users
in GNSS-denied regional navigation—these defects manifest less severely than in traditional

5. Conclusions
This paper proposed a lightweight, APF-based framework for autonomous UAV swarm
route planning in dynamic air–terrestrial cooperative navigation scenarios operating in GNSS-
denied environments. By integrating a Schur-complement-based geometric evaluation metric
directly into the attractive potential, the method enables the UAV swarm to continuously
optimize its configuration in real time so as to minimize user positioning errors while satisfying
operational constraints such as minimum altitude and inter-UAV separation.
https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15061207
[p.18]

The core innovation lies in decomposing the full system precision covariance ma-
trix via the Schur complement, yielding a scalar, differentiable user-equivalent GDOP
function that faithfully captures end-to-end geometric quality. This metric is then used
to construct the attractive potential, with virtual forces derived via a computationally
efficient finite-difference approximation, ensuring onboard real-time feasibility even on
resource-constrained UAV platforms.
Extensive simulations validated the framework across static and dynamic scenarios.
In the static case, the algorithm drove the UAV swarm from poor initial geometries to
near-optimal configurations, reducing the average user GDOP from approximately 10 to
below 4 within 150 s and maintaining stable convergence. In the dynamic scenario in-
volving piecewise constant-velocity user motion over 600 s, the swarm exhibited rapid
adaptive navigation at each velocity transition point. Compared to the baseline of fixed
UAV positions, the proposed method achieved substantial performance gains: the mean
user comprehensive GDOP was reduced by 76.1% on average. Moreover, the algorithm
significantly enhanced inter-user balance and temporal stability, demonstrating its effec-
tiveness in handling heterogeneous and mobile user demands.
These results confirm that the framework successfully bridges high-level navigation
performance optimization with low-level motion control, offering a practical solution for
real-time UAV swarm coordination in GNSS-denied air–terrestrial positioning networks.
