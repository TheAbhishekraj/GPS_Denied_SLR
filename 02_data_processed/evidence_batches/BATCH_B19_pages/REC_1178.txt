Aerial-Ground Robots Collaborative 3D Mapping in GNSS-Denied
Environments
Yufeng Yue1∗, Chunyang Zhao2, Yuanzhe Wang2, Yi Yang1, Danwei Wang2, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—Collaborative heterogeneous robots are expected to
perform comprehensive perception, mapping and coordination
in search and rescue scenarios. The challenge of collaboration
between heterogeneous robots lies in their huge differences
in perception, mobility and processing capabilities. In this
paper, a novel collaborative UAV-UGV mapping framework is
proposed in GNSS-denied and unknown environments. The key
novelty of this work is the proposing of a uniﬁed framework to
formulate the UAV-UGV collaborative mapping problem with
a continuous-discrete model, as well as its realization in real
robotic systems. In order to project continuous space into
discrete space, a novel information gain trigger scheme is pro-
posed. The continuous space allows each robot to perform high
frequency local map estimation, while discrete space describes
the problem of multi-resolution hybrid map fusion. Considering
the nature of data heterogeneity, a ﬂexible probabilistic fusion
algorithm is proposed that addresses the multi-resolution hybrid
map fusion problem, where the local maps generated by UAV
and UGV are fused based on Bayesian rule. The proposed
UAV-UGV hybrid system is validated in various challenging
scenarios, demonstrating its accuracy and utility in practical
tasks.
I. INTRODUCTION
Compared to homogeneous robots that have identical
functions, diverse capabilities of heterogeneous robots are
brought together to achieve enhanced performance in search
and rescue missions [1], [2]. Many current systems require
partial environment information to be known as a prior,
which is utilized to perform missions such as task planning
[3], relative localization [4], target docking [5]. However,
search and rescue applications require the robots to op-
erate in situations without prior environmental models. In
such cases, collaborative perception and mapping are critical
capabilities required by multi-robot systems to understand
the environment. Therefore, the premise of achieving online
operation is that heterogeneous robots need to understand
the scene, share their observations between UAV and UGV,
and generate a globally consistent map in real time. This
article provides collaborative hybrid UAV-UGV mapping as
a systematic solution for heterogeneous robots perception and
mapping.

Employing heterogeneous robots can complement their
strengths, constructing a comprehensive and accurate de-
scription of the environment. In order to operate in Global
Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) denied and unknown
environments, each robot needs to estimate its pose and
local map in high frequency [6]. On this basis, UAV and
UGV share their local maps and generate a global fused
map [7]. Most approaches generate the global environmen-
tal representation off-line in a centralized setting [7]–[10].
However, it is more robust for each robot to maintain and
update a global map distributively, which supports online
path planning and navigation from a holistic view. Consider-
ing the limited computational power on-board, especially for
the UAV, higher-level objects such as collaborative mapping
can be performed periodically in discrete space. In order to
balance the contradiction between map fusion and limited
computing power, the ﬁrst challenge is to carefully design
a hierarchical framework that jointly considers the tasks
of local mapping and global map fusion, which should be
computational efﬁcient and adaptable to environment.
Compared with homogeneous robots, the installation of
heterogeneous sensors on UAV and UGV brings another layer
of challenge. In view of high payload and power supply,
UGV is equipped with a high-precision 3D Lidar to capture
360-degree environmental information. However, UGV can
easily fall into a blind spot or be blocked by obstacles,
leading to failure of mapping missions. In contrast, UAV
has high ﬂexibility and maneuverability, but only has limited
payload. This inherent feature makes the UAV can only
be equipped with light low-resolution sensors. Due to the
huge difference in sensing resolution, detection range, and
ﬁeld of view (FoV), map fusion between hybrid robots is a
challenging problem. Up to date, most existing approaches
on UAV-UGV mapping [11]–[13] assume that UAV and
UGV are equipped with same type of sensors and only need
to fuse homogeneous data. Considering the nature of data
heterogeneity, the second challenge is to propose a ﬂexible
probabilistic fusion algorithm that integrates and combines
the multi-resolution local maps from hybrid robots.
Above limitations hinge on the individual capabilities of
the robots, either in distributed information sharing strat-
egy, or multi-resolution hybrid map fusion. This interwoven
nature of the problem motivates our research towards a
systematic collaborative UAV-UGV mapping approach. The
main contributions are listed as follows:
• A collaborative hybrid UAV-UGV mapping framework
is proposed based on continuous-discrete model. The
continuous space achieves real time local map genera-
tion, while the discrete cycle performs sporadically map

II. RELATED WORK
In this section, an extensive survey of existing approaches
related to heterogeneous robots perception and mapping is
provided.
A. Homogeneous robots mapping
Reference [14] summarizes different solutions for multiple
UGVs mapping. In challenging 3D scenarios, submap based
map fusion algorithm is proposed in [15]. Then, the authors
in [16] propose map updating algorithm based on Octree
structure. In the unstructured environment, a probabilistic
fusion strategy is proposed for heterogeneous map matching
in [17]. In day and night environment, [18] has proposed
a collaborative mapping framework based on multi-modal
environment perception. To provide the view of eye in the
sky, there are also approaches that utilizes multiple UAVs for
environmental mapping. An introduction of multiple UAVs
environmental mapping is reviewed in [19]. Reference [20]
develops a system consisting of multiple drones that can
perform autonomous navigation and 3D mapping in GNSS-
denied environments. Reference [21] proposes a framework
to implement vision-based multi-UAV collaborative mapping
in simulation. In [10], multi-UAV cooperative SLAM is
proposed, but the map fusion is handled ofﬂine in the central
station.
These approaches made signiﬁcant progress in collabora-
tive mapping for multiple UAVs or UGVs. However, they
only focus on the cooperation between same type of robot
platforms, and the advantages of UAV and UGV have not
been combined. In addition, existing references have not
considered the problem of designing a systematic framework
of generating local maps and global map with continuous-
discrete model.
B. Heterogeneous Robots Mapping
The work of UAV-UGV teaming is pioneered by [22],
where maps generated by aerial robots are used to design
navigation controllers and plan missions for the UGV. A
detailed evaluation of various relative localization algorithms
for UAV-UGV can be found in [9]. To leverage the ﬂexible
perception ability of UAV, reference [11] provides bird eye
view for UGV. A cooperative surveillance system is proposed
that allows the UAV to land on the UGV [23].
In order to achieve detailed environmental mapping,
ground and aerial robots are utilized to perform collaborative
mapping in an earthquake-damaged building [24]. Similarly,
UAV is located in the coordinate frame of UGV and a global
map is generated in [8]. In digital construction, the point
cloud from UAV and UGV are registered and stitched using
2D image features [7]. To realize precision farming, a aerial-
ground collaborative 3D mapping framework is proposed in
[12]. More recently, UAV-UGV exploration [25] is proposed
that aims to solve the area coverage and navigation problem.
To make a short summary, aforementioned collaborative
mapping algorithms for UAV-UGV team perform ofﬂine data
processing and 3D reconstruction. Furthermore, they have not
considered the fusion between multi-resolution maps.
III. SYSTEM FRAMEWORK
A. System Architecture
The objective of this work is to develop a collaborative
hybrid UAV-UGV mapping framework that enables hybrid
robots to generate a consistent global map. The overview of
the system architecture consists of two levels: single robot
continuous mapping level and UAV-UGV discrete map fusion
level. Since the hybrid systems operate in a distributed conﬁg-
uration, the organization of this paper follows a hierarchical
structure from the single robot level to the collaborative level.

V. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
Real world experiments in GNSS-denied and unstruc-
tured scenarios are investigated, including NTU Rainforest
(Sec. V-B), Two-Level Building (Sec. V-C).

erating UAV and UGV in two different scenarios in Nanyang
Technological University. All algorithms are executed on the
onboard platform for single robot SLAM and collaborative
mapping. For each robot, the map fusion is triggered inde-
pendently, each robot updates and maintains its global map.

NTU campus, heterogeneous robots seek to map the
environment.

VI. CONCLUSIONS
This paper has presented an approach for collaborative
UAV-UGV mapping in GNSS-denied environment. This pa-
per has proposed a uniﬁed framework to formulate UAV-
UGV collaborative mapping problem with a continuous-
discrete model, as well as its realization in real robotic
systems. The continuous space allows each robot to perform
high frequency local map estimation, while the discrete
space describes multi-resolution hybrid map fusion based
on information gain trigger scheme. The results have shown
that the proposed algorithm is able to perform collaborative
mapping in GNSS-denied and unstructured environment. In
summary, the proposed collaboration system provides a new
perspective of perception and collaboration between hetero-
geneous robots, which is complementary to the individual
robot perception and mapping.
