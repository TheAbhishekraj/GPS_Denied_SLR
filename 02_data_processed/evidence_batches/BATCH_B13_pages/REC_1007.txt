Abstract
The use of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) is rapidly increasing, making accurate localization essential for safe and reli­
able navigation. However, GPS signals can become unreliable or entirely unavailable in urban canyons, mountainous terrain, 
or environments with intentional interference. While many vision-based navigation methods rely on optical imagery, the 
potential of UAV-borne Synthetic Aperture Radar (UAVSAR) which performs reliably in all-weather and day–night condi­
tions has received relatively little attention.This study presents a new three-stage geo-localization framework specifically 
designed for UAVSAR imagery: (1) coarse region retrieval by matching each UAVSAR image against low-resolution satellite 
tiles, (2) fine image alignment within the selected region using high-resolution reference imagery, and(3) final localization 
based on affine transformation estimation with compensation for the side-looking geometry of SAR. The feature extrac­
tion and matching process is driven by a Convolutional Multi-Scale Network with a ResNet-50 backbone, trained to learn 
robust, invariant features from image pairs captured under varying illumination, viewpoint, and sensor modality. Keypoints 
are extracted from intermediate feature maps, and mismatches are removed using adaptive distance filtering and RANSAC-
based geometric verification. Experiments on a UAVSAR dataset covering more than 46,000 km² and 12 diverse flight paths 
demonstrate that the proposed method achieves RMSE values between 1.83 and 2.86 m (average 2.23 m) and yields a high 
Recall@1 in the coarse-retrieval stage.Compared with existing cross-modality matching approaches, the proposed framework 
consistently achieves higher retrieval accuracy and demonstrates more reliable operational performance.Training and testing 
across geographically distinct regions further confirm the strong generalization capability of the method.Overall, the results 
show that SAR-based visual navigation is a practical and robust alternative for GPS-limited or GPS-denied environments, 
offering a structured multi-stage pipeline and a cross-modality matching strategy that clearly distinguish it from prior work.

Furthermore, advancements in digital imaging systems and

Modern UAV visual localization methods rely on image 
registration, categorized into traditional (Castaldo 2015; Lin 
2013; Senlet 2011; Bansal 2011) and convolutional neural 
network (CNN)-based approaches (Khurshid 2024; Nas­
sar 2018). Traditional techniques use handcrafted features 
(e.g., blobs, corners) to match images at pixel or subpixel 
levels (Rublee 2011; Szeliski 2010), but their performance 
degrades under varying conditions, especially between SAR 
and optical imagery. In contrast, deep learning methods 
learn robust, invariant features from large datasets, enhanc­
ing matching accuracy. Thus, recent research trends show a 
shift from handcrafted to learning-based scene matching (Yi 
2016; Noh 2017). Image-based geo-localization primarily 
relies on image matching, which is a key process for esti­
mating UAV position and orientation by correlating aerial 
imagery with georeferenced satellite data. Depending on the 
sensing modality, matching can be performed between opti­
cal–optical image pairs or across different modalities such 
as SAR–optical.For optical–optical matching, deep learning 
has substantially advanced feature correspondence and geo­
metric consistency under wide viewpoint variations. Repre­
sentative approaches include HMSM-Net (He et al. 2022)
and Deep3D (Ada-MVS) (Liu et al. 2023), together with 
registration frameworks such as CNN-based Deep Regis­
tration (Wang et al. 2018), Region-based CNN Registration 
(Zeng et al. 2020), and Deep Residual Network Registration 
(Zhao et al. 2021). In SAR–optical matching, significant 
radiometric and geometric disparities between modalities 
make the task more complex. Recent research focuses on 
cross-modality representation learning through models such 
as Deep Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) (Zhang et al. 
2019), Deep Dense Feature Network (DDFN) (Zhang et al. 
2020), MAP-Net (Cui et al. 2021), CIRSM-Net (Wang et 
al. 2025), OS-CycleGAN (Liaghat et al. 2025), and RMSO-
ConvNeXt (Yang et al. 2025).
Overall, these developments demonstrate that deep learning 
based feature matching, both intra-modality and cross-modal­
ity, provides a robust foundation for UAV geo-localization in 
GPS-denied or visually degraded environments.
Recent research has increasingly focused on leveraging 
deep learning with satellite imagery to enhance UAV pose 
estimation across various and challenging environments. 
Hou et al. 2020, proposed a UAV localization method that 
extracts deep learning-based features from satellite imag­
ery to improve pose estimation accuracy in GNSS-denied 
environments. Their approach integrates a top-view opti­
cal camera on UAVs and utilizes CNNs for image feature 
extraction, combined with a bundle adjustment-based local 
optimization to minimize localization errors. The study 
emphasizes the challenges posed by inconsistent viewing 
angles and environmental variations, which complicate 
accurate UAV geo-localization.
Building on this, Cui et al. 2023 developed a cross-view 
geo-localization approach between UAV and satellite imag­
ery using a Vision Transformer architecture. Their method 
effectively resolves inconsistencies in view angles through a 
single-stage training strategy and introduces a novel piece­
wise soft-margin triplet loss function, improving image 
retrieval accuracy. Zhuang et al. 2022 proposed a trans­
former-based network incorporating a Semantic Guidance 
Module (SGM) that aligns semantic regions between UAV 
and satellite images, enhancing contextual understand­
ing and achieving an 8% increase in localization accuracy 
compared to CNN-based approaches. Similarly, Dai et al. 
2021 introduced a transformer-based Feature Segmentation 
and Region Alignment (FSRA) framework to address chal­
lenges such as position shifts and scale uncertainty in cross-
view localization. FSRA segments and aligns image regions 
automatically through the transformer’s heat distribution, 
improving feature robustness and contextual consistency 
without extra supervision.
Sui et al. 2022 presented a novel two-stage, detector-
free matching method designed for low-altitude UAV 
visual geo-localization. By incorporating a perspective 
transformation module and utilizing the DSNT transform, 
their method effectively mitigates illumination and view­
point differences. Using a combination of coarse matching 
based on minimum Euclidean distance and fine matching 
through DSNT, it achieves an average localization preci­
sion of 2.24 m. This method demonstrates strong robustness 
and computational efficiency, particularly when deployed 
on Jetson Xavier NX hardware. Wang 2024 introduced a

However, despite these advancements, pose estima­
tion for UAV autonomous navigation still faces numer­
ous challenges and limitations (Wang 2024). A key issue 
lies in the large disparity of scale and resolution between 
high-detail UAV imagery and the broader, lower-resolution 
satellite imagery, which complicates direct image match­

al. 2022). Complex urban environments also increase dif­

rapid progress in vision-based UAV navigation, a significant 
research gap remains. Most prior studies have focused on 
optical imagery or simulated scenarios, with limited atten­
tion to real UAVSAR data. Existing methods that employ 
synthetic or satellite-derived SAR images often overlook 
the unique challenges of real airborne UAVSAR imagery, 
including side-looking geometry and substantial radio­
metric and structural differences between SAR and optical 
data. Furthermore, the lack of evaluation on large-scale and 
diverse datasets raises concerns about the generalizability 
and robustness of these approaches.
To address these limitations, this study presents a novel 
deep learning-based framework for autonomous UAV local­
ization using real airborne UAVSAR imagery matched with 
high-resolution optical reference images. The proposed sys­
tem introduces a cross-modality image-matching strategy 
built on a multi-scale convolutional network (CMN-Net) 
with a ResNet-50 backbone. This architecture is trained 
to extract robust, modality-invariant features and enables 
accurate alignment of heterogeneous imagery across differ­
ent viewpoints and temporal gaps.
The key contributions of this work are as follows:
(1)	 A unified geolocation framework specifically tailored 
for UAVSAR data, enabling fully autonomous naviga­
tion without reliance on GPS;
(2)	 A robust feature extraction and matching pipeline for 
cross-modality image registration using deep semantic 
representations;
(3)	 Extensive validation across 12 flight paths over diverse 
terrain types, confirming both accuracy and adaptability 
of the proposed method.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that uti­
lizes real UAVSAR imagery acquired from airborne plat­
forms for fully autonomous UAV geo-localization. This 
work bridges a critical research gap and opens a new path­
way for SAR-based navigation solutions applicable to GPS-
denied scenarios.
Methodology
In this study, a deep learning-based framework is proposed 
for UAVSAR localization. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the overall 
methodology consists of three key stages: (1) determining 
the initial target area, (2) accurate image matching focused 
on selected approximate tiles, and (3) SAR image registra­
tion with the georeferenced reference image to geolocalize 
the UAV. Initially, SAR imagery is coarsely matched with 
Google Earth satellite tiles at a low zoom level to identify 
the target area. Once the target area is defined, the SAR 
image within the selected region is matched with a higher-
resolution reference image containing a greater number of 
key points. In the subsequent stage, with a sufficient num­
ber of matching points (a minimum of three points), an 
affine transformation matrix is computed to align the SAR 
image with the reference image. This enables registration 
of the SAR image with the reference image to achieve 
georeferencing.
The registration stage, which constitutes the core of this 
research, specifically addresses the challenge of robust 
feature matching in heterogeneous images. To this end, 
we propose a CNN architecture designed to mitigate the 
effects of radiometric and geometric discrepancies and 
extract invariant feature representations. The network is 
tailored for accurate keypoint detection and description by 
leveraging high-level semantic information from deeper 
feature maps, which are more resilient to variations in 
radiation and imaging geometry. Moreover, the model is 
trained using image pairs with significant differences in 
illumination and viewpoint, enabling it to learn features 
that remain consistent across diverse visual and sensor 
conditions. Further technical details are provided in the 
following sections.

Training
The training phase of the system relies on pairs of images 
depicting the same scene but captured under different con­
ditions, such as varying viewpoints, lighting, or sensor 
modalities (e.g., UAVSAR and optical images from Google 
Earth). The training process leverages these image pairs to 
teach the network how to identify and describe features that 
are invariant to these changes. Specifically, the network is 
trained to maximize the similarity of descriptors at corre­
sponding locations in the paired images while minimizing 
the similarity of descriptors at non-corresponding locations. 
This encourages the network to learn robust and discrimi­
native features that can be reliably matched across images 
with significant radiometric and geometric differences. For 
each pair of corresponding image points, random image 
regions measuring 256 × 256 pixels, centered around these 
points, were selected and input into the network for training. 
The network was trained using the Adam optimizer with an 
initial learning rate of 1 × 10⁻³, a batch size of 8, and for 20 
epochs. A contrastive loss function was employed to enforce 
descriptor similarity between positive and negative pairs. 
The learning rate was reduced by a factor of 0.1 every 10 
epochs to ensure stable convergence.
Network model
The overall matching architecture is based on the Convo­
lutional Multi-Scale Network (CMN-Net) (Chaozhen et al. 
2021), enhanced by employing ResNet-50 as the feature 
extraction module. Originally designed for classification 
tasks, ResNet-50 consists of multiple residual blocks that 
preserve detailed information across layers. The initial 
layers of ResNet-50, with their smaller receptive fields, 
effectively capture basic features such as edges and cor­
ners, providing high localization precision. As the network 
progresses, it abstracts features further, integrating more 
global information that is robust to variations across differ­
ent images. However, this increased abstraction can lead to 
reduced localization precision. To strike a balance between 
abstraction and precise localization, the output from the 
second convolution layer in the third stage (Conv3_2) was 
chosen as the feature map. This output represents a com­
bination of multiple convolution operations and residual 
processes applied to the input image, with its dimensions 
corresponding to the number of convolution filters in that 
layer.

To evaluate the accuracy and performance of the proposed

Eliminating mismatched points
Given the pronounced differences between heterogeneous 
images and the large number of extracted candidate feature 
points, mismatches are common and can significantly affect 
the accuracy of image matching. To effectively address this, 
a combined approach is employed, integrating a dynamic 
adaptive distance constraint with Random Sample Con­
sensus (RANSAC) for robust mismatch elimination. First, 
an adaptive distance threshold is determined based on the 
average distance differences between matched pairs. This 
threshold serves to filter out pairs that do not meet the rela­
tive distance criterion, preserving only the closer and more 
likely correct matches. Subsequently, RANSAC is applied 
to perform a geometric verification using an affine trans­
formation model, ensuring only geometrically consistent 
matches remain. This two-tiered method significantly 
enhances match quality by reducing erroneous points and 
preserving reliable correspondences for accurate alignment.
Refined image matching within targeted 
approximate tiles
After the selection of initial tiles, the process focuses on

Assessment of estimated UAV flight path accuracy
In this study, it is assumed that when the UAV is in GPS-
denied conditions, its position is determined using an 
image-matching-based method. The autopilot system or 
onboard navigation system then checks whether the UAV 
is aligned with the pre-defined flight path. If a deviation is 
detected, the system adjusts the course to bring the UAV 
back to the correct path. Essentially, the flight path consists 
of a series of predefined coordinates that the UAV must fol­
low. After each new position is estimated via the proposed 
method, the system calculates how the UAV should move 
toward the next coordinate. If GPS disruption persists, the 
UAV continues to capture new images and matches them 
with reference images. This allows continuous updates of 
the UAV’s position, ensuring the flight path is followed.

Net. Since the primary objective of this study is accurate UAV 
trajectory recovery in GPS-denied conditions the comparison 
focuses on a practical scenario using FlightLine 4 which con­

characteristics. For each method the matching accuracy the

The comparison demonstrates that conventional cross-

accuracy (72.4%) and low Recall@1 (59.4%). Deep learn­
