A Unified Error-State Kalman Filtering Framework for Multi-Sensor
Navigation of Fixed-Wing UAVs in GNSS-Denied Scenarios
Guillermo E. V. Hernandez1, Rafael C. Fernandes1, Artem Sorokin1, Maheedhar Korimi1, and Felipe O. Silva2
Abstract— Reliable navigation in Global Navigation Satellite
System (GNSS)-denied environments remains a critical chal-
lenge for fixed-wing Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). In
such conditions, Inertial Navigation Systems (INSs) must be
aided by heterogeneous sensors to mitigate drift and ensure
long-term accuracy. This paper presents the development,
implementation, and experimental validation of a unified Error-
State Extended Kalman Filter (ESEKF) designed for fixed-
wing UAV navigation under GNSS-denied and GNSS-degraded
scenarios. The proposed framework integrates an INS with
magnetometer, barometer, pitot tube with pseudovanes, Visual-
Inertial Odometry (VIO), and Computer Vision (CV) Convolu-
tional Neural Network (CNN)-based position estimates within a
unified estimation architecture. A comprehensive mathematical
description of the ESEKF is provided, with special attention
given to the modeling of sensor systematic errors, which are
explicitly included in the augmented error-state vector. The
estimator performance is evaluated through a flight exper-
iment using a custom fixed-wing UAV platform, where the
contribution of each aiding sensor is assessed via systematic
sensor deactivation under GNSS outage conditions. Experi-
mental results demonstrate the robustness and modularity of
the proposed ESEKF architecture, highlighting its ability to
maintain navigation accuracy despite the loss of individual
aiding sensors (except barometer and CV). The presented
framework consolidates previously fragmented sensor fusion
approaches into a single, self-consistent navigation solution,
providing both a practical implementation reference and a
foundation for future extensions and tuning strategies.
I. INTRODUCTION
Reliable navigation is a fundamental requirement for
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), particularly fixed-wing
platforms operating in complex and contested environments.
In nominal conditions, Global Navigation Satellite Systems
(GNSSs) provide accurate and drift-free estimates of position
and velocity, enabling precise guidance and control. How-
ever, GNSS signals are vulnerable to blockage, multipath,
interference, and intentional jamming, making GNSS-denied

UAV systems [1]–[3]. This challenge is exacerbated for small
UAVs, where constraints on size, weight, power, and cost
limit sensor redundancy and quality [4]–[6].
In GNSS-denied environments, Inertial Navigation Sys-
tems (INSs) form the backbone of the navigation solution.
Strapdown INS architectures provide high-rate estimates of
attitude, velocity, and position through dead reckoning, but
their errors grow unbounded due to sensor biases and noise
digital integration [7]. To mitigate this drift, INSs must be
aided by external sensors whose measurements are fused
through probabilistic state estimation frameworks, most com-
monly Kalman Filter (KF)-based approaches [8], [9].
Among the available aiding sensors, magnetometers play
a central role in constraining heading errors, particularly
for low-cost inertial systems. However, magnetometer mea-
surements are highly sensitive to hard-iron and soft-iron
effects, scale factor errors, and sensor misalignment, which,
if unaccounted for, can significantly degrade navigation per-
formance [10], [11]. Over the past decades, a wide range of
calibration strategies have been proposed, including attitude-
independent formulations and two-step analytical methods
[12], [13]. More recently, extended formulations capable of
operating with distributed, non-simultaneous, and redundant
measurements have been introduced to improve robustness
in practical applications [14], [15]. Accurate geomagnetic
field modeling, typically based on global references such as
the World Magnetic Model (WMM), is also essential for
consistent magnetometer integration [16].
Altitude estimation represents another critical aspect of
GNSS-denied navigation. Barometric sensors provide high-
resolution relative altitude measurements but are affected by
temperature, weather variations, and sensor-specific system-
atic errors [17], [18]. Several baro-aided INS architectures
have been proposed to mitigate vertical drift, ranging from
fixed gains servo-mechanism-based feedback loops [19], to
Loosely Coupled (LC) and/or Tightly Coupled (TC) KF-
based integrations that explicitly estimate barometric biases
and scale factor errors [20], [21]. Incorporating temperature-
dependent models has been shown to significantly improve
long-term altitude consistency in real-world operations [22].
Vision-based navigation has emerged as a powerful al-
ternative for GNSS-denied operation, with Visual-Inertial
Odometry (VIO) systems providing drift-bounded velocity
and pose estimates over extended periods. Foundational

For fixed-wing UAVs, airspeed-related measurements offer
an additional source of information that can be exploited for
navigation [5]. Pitot tubes combined with flow-angle sensors,
i.e. vanes, enable the estimation of airspeed and aerodynamic
angles, which can be leveraged to constrain velocity states
and estimate wind components [27]. Such measurements are
particularly valuable in environments where both GNSS and
vision-based sensors are unavailable or unreliable, comple-
menting traditional inertial and barometric aiding strategies.
Despite the availability of multiple aiding sensors, the per-
formance of integrated navigation systems critically depends
on proper stochastic modeling and parameter tuning. Inac-
curate noise characterization can lead to filter inconsistency
or suboptimal performance, motivating extensive research on
sensor error modeling using, e.g., Allan variance analysis
[9], [28], [29]. More recently, optimization-based and data-
driven tuning strategies, including genetic algorithms and
machine learning techniques, have been explored to automate
parameter selection and improve robustness across operating
conditions [20], [21], [30], [31].
In this context, the Error-State Extended Kalman Fil-
ter (ESEKF) has become a de facto standard for multi-
sensor navigation systems due to its favorable numerical
properties, modular structure, and clear separation between
nominal state propagation and error dynamics [1]–[3], [32]–
[34]. However, existing literature often presents fragmented
descriptions of specific sensor integrations or isolated sub-
systems, making it difficult to reproduce, extend, or system-
atically evaluate complete navigation architectures.
This paper addresses this gap by presenting a compre-
hensive development, testing, and validation of a unified
ESEKF-based navigation system for fixed-wing UAVs op-
erating in GNSS-denied environments. The proposed system
integrates an INS with magnetometer, barometer, pitot tube
with pseudovanes, monocular VIO that provide real scale ve-
locity estimations, and Computer Vision (CV) Convolutional
Neural Network (CNN)-based position estimates obtained
by matching UAV images against satellite imagery within a
unified estimation framework. While several of these sensor
integrations have been previously investigated in isolation,
this work consolidates their mathematical models, error-state
formulations, and practical implementation aspects into a
single coherent architecture.
The main contributions of this paper are twofold. First,
we provide a unified and self-consistent description of the
ESEKF architecture, including reference frames, nominal
INS propagation, linearized error dynamics, and measure-
ment models for all integrated sensors. Second, we present a
rigorous experimental evaluation using a custom fixed-wing
UAV platform, which assesses the contribution of each aiding
sensor through systematic sensor deactivation tests under
GNSS outage conditions.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows:
Section II gives an overview of the system, as well as intro-
duces the reference frames and main modeling assumptions.
Section III reviews the nominal INS propagation model,
while Section IV presents the error-state formulation and
linearized dynamics of the ESEKF. Section V details the
measurement models of the integrated sensors, including
magnetometer, barometer, airspeed, vision-based, and GNSS
measurements, as well as the outlier rejection mechanism and
error state injection. Section VI describes the experimental
setup and validation methodology, as well as discusses the
results. Finally, Section VII concludes the paper and outlines
directions for future work. An appendix is also provided to
detail the derivation of the magnetometer error model.
II. SYSTEM OVERVIEW AND PROBLEM FORMULATION
This section presents a high-level overview of the pro-
posed navigation system and formally defines the problem
addressed in this work. The adopted reference frames, mod-
eling assumptions, and state representations are introduced
to provide a consistent basis for the developments in the
subsequent sections.
A. System Overview
Figure 1 illustrates the overall architecture of the proposed
navigation system, whose core is a unified ESEKF, responsi-
ble for fusing inertial and auxiliary sensor measurements to
estimate the navigation states of a fixed-wing UAV operating
in GNSS-denied or GNSS-degraded environments.
The INS, composed of a tri-axial accelerometer and rate
gyro, provides high-rate measurements that are used to prop-
agate the nominal navigation states. These states are period-
ically corrected using measurements from multiple aiding

B. Reference Frames
Multiple reference frames are employed to describe the
vehicle dynamics, sensor measurements, and environmental
quantities. The following are used throughout this work:
• Inertial frame (i): An Earth-Centered Inertial (ECI)
frame, assumed to be non-accelerating and non-rotating,
used as a theoretical reference for defining inertial
motion.
• Earth

(e):
An

to represent geodetic quantities such as latitude, longi-
tude, and altitude.
• Navigation frame (n): A local-level North-East-Down
(NED) frame, with its origin attached to the vehicle’s
Center of Mass (CoM). This frame is used to express
navigation states such as attitude, velocity, and wind.
• Body frame (b): A body-fixed Forward-Right-Down
(FRD) frame, rigidly attached to the UAV’s CoM and
aligned with its principal axes. Inertial sensor measure-
ments are expressed in this frame.
• Magnetometer frame (m): A sensor-fixed frame associ-
ated with the magnetometer triad, generally misaligned
w.r.t. the body frame due to installation imperfections
and/or soft-iron magnetism.
• Camera frame (c): A sensor-fixed frame associated with
the vision system, used to express VIO measurements.
This frame may be misaligned w.r.t. the body frame.
Rotations between frames are represented using Direction
Cosine Matrices (DCMs), while small attitude errors are
modeled using three-dimensional error vectors.
C. Modeling Assumptions
The proposed navigation framework relies on the follow-
ing assumptions:
• The vehicle is a fixed-wing UAV operating in the
atmosphere, with dynamics compatible with standard
strapdown inertial navigation formulations.
• Wind is modeled as a slowly varying vector expressed
in the navigation frame and is explicitly included in the
state vector.
• Attitude

• Certain aiding sensors exhibit systematic errors, such
as biases, scale factors, and misalignment parameters,
which are explicitly estimated by augmenting the error
state vector with corresponding scalar Gauss-Markov
(GM) or Random-Walk (RW) processes.
These assumptions are consistent with standard practices
in inertial navigation and error-state filtering and allow a
unified treatment of heterogeneous sensor measurements
within the ESEKF framework.

G. Innovation Filtering and Error Injection
All measurement updates employed in this work are
protected against outliers through scalar innovation filtering
[2]. After computing the measurement innovation δz and
its covariance, each scalar component is individually tested
against a predefined threshold. Innovations whose normal-
ized magnitude exceeds this threshold are rejected, while
the remaining components are retained for the update. This
strategy allows partial acceptance of multi-dimensional mea-
surements and prevents corrupted observations from degrad-
ing the filter consistency, while preserving valid information
from unaffected channels.
Following a successful measurement update, the estimated
error-state vector is injected into the nominal navigation
states. For a generic scalar state x, vector state x, and rotation
matrix C, the injection mechanism can be written as ˆx+ =
ˆx−−δˆx, ˆx+ = ˆx−−δˆx, and ˆC+ =

tities. This formulation is consistently applied to attitude,
velocity, position, sensor biases, scale factors, misalignment
parameters, and wind velocity states. After injection, the
corresponding error states are reset to zero, ensuring that
the error-state formulation remains locally valid and that the
nominal states fully absorb the correction.

VII. CONCLUSION
This paper presented the development and experimental
validation of a unified ESEKF framework for multi-sensor
navigation of fixed-wing UAVs operating in GNSS-denied
and GNSS-degraded environments. The proposed architec-
ture consolidated INS propagation and heterogeneous aid-
ing sensors, including magnetometer, barometer, pitot tube
with pseudovanes, VIO velocity estimates, CV CNN-based
position updates, and GNSS measurements, into a single
self-consistent estimation structure with explicit modeling of
sensor systematic errors through an augmented error-state
vector.
A real flight experiment with intentional GNSS outages
demonstrated that the proposed unified ESEKF maintains
bounded navigation errors. Systematic sensor deactivation
tests further highlighted the modularity of the framework
and quantified the role of each aiding sensor, showing that
barometric aiding is essential for vertical stability, while CV
position updates play a key role in horizontal channel and
heading performance.
The results also indicated that magnetometer aiding may
be redundant under the tested conditions, suggesting limited
observability of the corresponding augmented calibration
states, which motivates further investigation. Future work
will focus on a formal observability analysis of the complete
filter structure and on the systematic evaluation of TC
measurement models for all auxiliary sensors to improve ro-
bustness and consistency in extended GNSS-denied missions.
