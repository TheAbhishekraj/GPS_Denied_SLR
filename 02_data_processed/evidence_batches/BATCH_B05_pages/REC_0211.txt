SIA-KalmanNet: A Structurally Integrated Attention
Kalman Filter for Multi-UAV Localization in
Partially GNSS-Denied Environments
Xiuli Xin , Hongyu Zhou , Xiaoxue Feng , Feng Pan , Jiacheng Wang , and Zhenxu Li
Abstract—Cooperative localization is an essential task in
multiple uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) networks, especially
in environments with partial global navigation satellite system
(GNSS) denial. By eﬀectively integrating low-precision inertial
navigation system (INS)-derived priors, inter-UAV relative dis-
tances, and sparse anchor-based measurements, the extended
Kalman ﬁlter (EKF) can be employed for UAV localization.
However, localization accuracy suﬀers under nonlinear dynam-
ics and inaccurate knowledge of process and measurement
noise statistics. To address these challenges, this article pro-
poses a structurally integrated attention Kalman ﬁlter called
SIA-KalmanNet for accurate and robust state estimation. By
combining state-space models with attention-based neural net-
works in the Kalman ﬂow, the estimator preserves the data
eﬃciency and interpretability of traditional model-driven meth-
ods while implicitly learning second-order covariance matrices
from data. Speciﬁcally, two networks are devised to learn the
Kalman gain: the self-attention residual masked network (SA-
RMN) and the self-cross attention residual masked network
(SCA-RMN). Among them, SA-RMN is a lightweight network for
end-to-end state estimation. SCA-RMN introduces masked cross-
attention to adaptively fuse forward state features and backward
historical correction information to obtain a more precise state
estimate. Furthermore, a practical training strategy based on
sliding window and causal masking is developed to ensure eﬃ-
cient training and real-time inference. The experimental results
show that the proposed algorithm achieves superior localization
performance compared to the traditional EKF and KalmanNets.
Moreover, it exhibits strong robustness against INS drift and
non-line-of-sight (NLOS)-induced distance errors, even in harsh
environments where only a limited number of UAVs can access
GNSS signals.
Index Terms—Cooperative localization, deep learning, Kalman
ﬁlter, multisource information fusion, Transformer.

The Bayesian ﬁlters have been widely employed in coop-
erative localization for multi-UAV networks. These methods
aim to extract a hidden state from indirect and uncertain
measurements, with the primary objective of minimizing
state estimation errors while counteracting uncertainties and
perturbations [11]. To mention a few, Qu and Zhang [12]
developed a cooperative localization algorithm based on inter-
UAV relative distances, which employs an adaptive Kalman
ﬁlter to select the optimal reference point for minimizing
the horizontal dilution of precision (HDOP), thus enhanc-
ing accuracy in GNSS-denied environments. The method
in [13] introduced a robust decentralized cooperative local-
ization (RDCL) framework, which enhances the extended
Kalman ﬁlter (EKF) by adaptively fusing measurement data
contaminated by outliers, thus bolstering both accuracy and
robustness in multirobot systems facing measurement anoma-
lies. Song et al. [14] proposed a cooperative localization
method that combines manifold gradient ﬁltering with geomet-
ric dilution of precision (GDOP)-based rate regulation, thereby
improving adaptability in challenging environments.
These ﬁltering-based approaches are supported by solid
theoretical foundations. However, they depend on precise
dynamic models and idealized assumptions, making them
vulnerable to model mismatch and sensor failures [15]. In
multi-UAV cooperative networks with partial GNSS signal
loss, system dynamics are often highly nonlinear and uncer-
tain, challenging the applicability of model-based ﬁlters [16],
particularly when considering the long-term correlated and
cross-node error behaviors inherent to distributed navigation
systems [17]. Furthermore, inertial navigation systems (INSs)
accumulate drift overextended ﬂights, and wireless localiza-
tion techniques such as time of arrival (TOA) and time
diﬀerence of arrival (TDOA) perform poorly in obstructed
or non-line-of-sight (NLOS) environments [18], revealing
the limitations in robustness. To enhance the accuracy and

Consequently, conventional Bayesian ﬁlters struggle to deliver

In recent years, deep learning-based methods have gradually
achieved empirical success in real-world applications [23].
These methods eliminate the reliance on precise mathematical
descriptions of system dynamics and the characteristics of
noise distribution through end-to-end data-driven neural net-
works, thereby mitigating performance degradation caused by
model assumption deviations and inaccurate noise statistics in
traditional ﬁlter-based methods [24]. Considerable deep learn-

For instance, Li et al. [25] proposed a self-supervised monoc-
ular vision framework for multirobot relative localization that
uses training data from ultrawideband (UWB) to enable the
neural network to predict 3-D relative positions, thus sup-
porting scalable localization in GNSS-denied environments.
In [26], a multi-UAV cooperative tracking algorithm based on
deep neural networks (DNNs) was introduced, which actively
selects the most informative regions in images to enhance the
detection accuracy of small-scale and distant targets, achieving
eﬃcient and continuous aerial visual tracking. Additionally, Lu
et al. [27] presented a communication-constrained multirobot
intelligent collaborative exploration (CMICE) framework that
reduces bandwidth usage via a nonessential noncommunica-
tion (NENC) mechanism, thereby improving the eﬃciency and
adaptability of cooperative exploration under communication
constraints. However, these end-to-end approaches are often
viewed as “models with limited interpretability,” as they
rely on statistical correlations rather than explicit physical
models, leading to limited interpretability and generalization
and increased sensitivity to dynamic or noise variations [24].
The limitations of both ﬁltering-based and deep learning-
based methods have motivated the development of hybrid
approaches. By combining the interpretability of ﬁltering
with the model-free nature of deep learning, these methods
achieve superior performance. The most representative method
is KalmanNet [22], [28], [29], [30], [31], which uses DNNs
to learn the Kalman gain for state estimation in partially
nonlinear dynamic systems. For instance, [22] introduced
a KalmanNet algorithm that integrates gated recurrent unit
(GRU) [32] with a Kalman ﬁlter, enhancing adaptability
to model mismatch and nonlinearity. The Split-KalmanNet
algorithm [30] employed a split-structure recurrent neural
network (RNN) to adaptively learn both prior state estimates
and an innovation covariance matrix, thereby compensating for
model mismatches and signiﬁcantly improving state estimation
accuracy. In [31], the RKFNet method integrated a robust
Kalman ﬁlter with deep learning, using a neural network and
unsupervised scheduling sampling (USS) to enhance estima-
tion stability and accuracy under heavy-tailed noise.
It is worth noting that most KalmanNet methods com-
bine RNNs with Kalman ﬁltering to enhance temporal data
processing. However, vanishing gradients [33] and opaque
hidden states limit their ability to capture complex long-term
interactions in multi-UAV systems [34]. Beyond algorithmic
limitations, multi-UAV interactions in partially GNSS-denied
environments are further shaped by system-level constraints,
including heterogeneous capabilities [35], [36], intermit-
tent communication [37], and limited resources [38]. These
constraints give rise to asynchronous, context-dependent inter-
actions that classical RNN-based KalmanNet architectures
struggle to capture.
In contrast, the Transformer architecture [39] is more suit-
able for modeling the complex interactions challenging for the
aforementioned architectures. By leveraging self-attention and
cross-attention mechanisms [40], this architecture eﬀectively
captures long-range temporal correlations, thereby strength-
ening sequential information modeling and facilitating robust
interactive feature extraction [41]. Moreover, although most
deep learning-assisted ﬁltering methods typically rely on
supervised learning, the limited availability of ground-truth
data in mission-critical environments can be mitigated through
pretraining [42] and online adaptation [43], suggesting that
such hybrid architectures remain broadly applicable. For
instance, FormerReckoning [44] combined physics-informed
priors with a Transformer to enhance inertial localization,
though its performance remains constrained by IMU data
limitations. A-KIT [45] integrated a Transformer with the
EKF to adaptively learn process noise in real time, improving
fusion accuracy but exhibiting limited generalization to unseen
conditions. KalmanFormer [46] introduced a Transformer-
based model that learns the Kalman gain directly from data
without prior noise parameters, but its end-to-end regression
design lacks second-order covariance propagation constraints,
resulting in a higher level of abstraction. Therefore, exploring
the eﬀective integration of Kalman ﬁltering and Transformer-
based attention mechanisms is a promising research direction
for accurate and robust multi-UAV localization in partially
GNSS-denied environments.

C. Preliminaries: EKF
The EKF is a state estimator widely used in multisensor
fusion applications. Based on the aforementioned state-space
model, the ﬁlter can be divided into prediction and update
phases. As shown in Fig. 2, during the prediction phase,
the system obtains prior information by fusing attitude and
velocity information provided by the INS. During the update
phase, the system corrects position using high-precision sensor
information (e.g., GNSS or interaircraft ranging). In practice,
such sensors are low frequency but high precision, constituting
a widely used loosely coupled localization scheme that com-
plements the high-frequency yet drift-prone INS prior [24].

and update.

V. CONCLUSION
This article proposes the SIA-KalmanNet algorithm to
address the cooperative localization problem of dynamic multi-
UAV networks under partial GNSS signal loss conditions. The
method combines the learning capabilities of DNNs with the
low complexity and analytical capabilities of ﬁlters, making
it particularly suitable for state estimation with inaccurate
knowledge of process and measurement noise statistics under
nonlinear dynamics. Followed by the information ﬂow of the
EKF, two structures are designed to learn Kalman gain: a
lightweight SA-RMN for eﬃcient estimation and a highly
interpretable SCA-RMN for performance enhancement. A
dedicated multi-UAV cooperative localization dataset and tai-
lored training strategies are also developed to support practical
implementation. Experimental results demonstrate that SIA-
KalmanNet consistently outperforms existing methods in both
accuracy and robustness across partial GNSS loss scenarios.
In future work, we will further investigate the theoretical
foundations of the SIA-KalmanNet, with particular emphasis
on stability analysis of the hybrid architecture. Moreover, real-
world UAV ﬂight experiments are planned to validate the
proposed framework and to evaluate its eﬀectiveness under
practical IoT deployment conditions.
