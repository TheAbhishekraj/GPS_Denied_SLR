Abstract
In this paper, we present a distributed framework for the lidar-based relative state esti-
mator which achieves highly accurate, real-time trajectory estimation of multiple Unmanned Aerial
Vehicles (UAVs) in GPS-denied environments. The system builds atop a factor graph, and only on-
board sensors and computing power are utilized. Beneﬁting from the keyframe strategy, each UAV
performs relative state estimation individually and broadcasts very partial information without
exchanging raw data. The complete system runs in real-time and is evaluated with three experiments
in different environments. Experimental results show that the proposed distributed approach offers
comparable performance with a centralized method in terms of accuracy and real-time perfor-
mance. The ﬂight test demonstrates that the proposed relative state estimation framework is able
to be used for aggressive ﬂights over 5 m/s.

1. Introduction
The deployment of the collaboration of UAVs has the poten-
tial to enable surveillance, search, information gathering, and
more efﬁcient coverage and monitoring of vast areas.1–6 Both
of these works rely on the availability of ground truth mea-
surements. But external sensors, such as GPS,7,8 motion cap-
ture system,9,10 Ultra-Wideband (UWB),11,12 is unreliable in
dynamic, partially-known environments. Therefore, it is neces-
sary to develop a state estimation system that only relies on
onboard sensing and computation, which is able to provide
both state estimation and relative state estimation of a multi-
UAV system. This paper provides a detailed description of a
distributed approach for lidar-based relative state estimation
which can be adopted by the multi-UAV systems in GPS-
denied environments.
Robust state estimation is one of the key requirements for
safe ﬂight. For real-world ﬂight, it is common to encounter

1.1. Related work
Multi-robot Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)
has gained recent attention for the increased viability and
accessibility of multi-UAV systems. In recent years, it is shown
that researches on multi-UAV systems are more likely to focus
on planning and control,17–20 and the relative state estimation
is always leaped by adopting external positioning systems.
Related work of relative state estimation investigates different
sensing modalities, including vision,21 UWB,11 and lidar.22
Several works have adopted the vision-based methods for
relative localization. In Ref.23, a pattern detection-based visual
relative localization approach is proposed. In the system, each
UAV is equipped with a circle marker for detection purpose,
which can be quite redundant. In Ref.24, an ultraviolet light-
based onboard relative localization method is presented, this
method relies on additional UltraViolet Direction and Rang-
ing (UVDAR) sensor and active marker for tracking purpose.
However, due to the limitation of sensor Field-of-View (FOV),
this method can only be applied to directed leader–follower-for
mation control. In Ref.25, an inter-robot loop closures based
collaborative localization method is running on a centralized
ground station. The authors of Ref. 21 extend the method in
Ref.25 to the distributed system with an outlier rejection mech-
anism. Nonetheless, all the systems may encounter potential
failure or drift when the points of interest are out of the
FOV of the cameras, which limits the application in leader–fol-
lower formation.
UWB based ranging measurements can assist in relative
state estimation. In Ref. 11, an onboard UWB ranging and
communication network is adopted to sense the inter-UAV
distance and exchange information for relative localization
estimation in 2D spaces. In Ref.26, a state estimator for both
single and multi-robot systems is presented. However, this esti-
mator requires a UWB anchor, which limits the application
scenario. The method proposed in Ref.27 represents a good
solution for decentralized relative state estimation of the aerial
swarm. However, solely relying on the vision for localization
and mapping can be challenging as cameras are directional
and sensitive to illumination changes and environmental
conditions.
3D lidar is a viable sensing choice to map complex unstruc-
tured environments, which provides a 360 horizontal FOV
and high sampling rate, and does not rely on external light
sources. The authors of Ref.28 present a strategy for collabora-
tive mapping of an earthquake-damaged building via ground
and aerial robots. In Ref.29, an integrated autonomous 3D
mapping system for multi-robot exploration is proposed.
However, the map-merging strategies proposed in Ref.28 and
Ref.29 are both performed ofﬂine. In Ref. 30 and Ref. 31,
the authors present an online localization and mapping system
for multiple ground robots equipped with 3D lidars, which is
available open-source and easy to compare. In Ref.
22, a
lidar-based multi-robot SLAM system is presented, which
developed in the context of the DARPA Subterranean
Challenge. Nonetheless, both methods proposed in Ref. 30,
Ref. 31, and Ref. 22 are centralized systems, all of them rely
on a central server with potentially big computational capac-
ity. The method proposed in Ref.32 represents a good solution
for lidar-based decentralized relative state estimation for
ground robots. To the best of our knowledge, most of the
works related to lidar-based relative state estimation technol-
ogy are centralized system or demonstrated with ground
robots. This is the ﬁrst time a distributed lidar-based relative
state estimation framework is demonstrated on a multi-UAV
system during a real-world aggressive ﬂight.
1.2. Contribution
A lidar-based on-board real-time relative state estimation sys-
tem is proposed in this paper, which can be adopted by multi-
UAV systems. The main contributions are as follows:
(1) A distributed relative state estimation framework is
developed to build atop a factor graph, that is suitable
for the multi-UAV systems. Only onboard sensors and
computing power, including lidars, inertial measurement
units, as well as Intel NUC, are utilized. No external off-
board sensors or computing power are required;
(2) A keyframe-based lidar-inertial odometry and inter-
UAV loop closures detection method is proposed with
an outlier rejection mechanism. Each UAV performs rel-
ative state estimation individually and broadcasts very
partial information, including keyframes and odometry
information, without exchanging raw data;
(3) The proposed framework is validated with an extensive
experimental evaluation including indoor test, outdoor
test, and aggressive ﬂight test in the real world.
2. System overview
Fig. 1 shows the architecture of our lidar-based relative state
estimation framework. The goal of each UAV is to estimate
its own trajectory and relative pose of the observed UAV.
The modules of each UAV can be categorized into 6 main
parts: on-board sensor, lidar-inertial odometry, keyframe
selection, distributed place recognition, relative pose-graph
optimization, map management unit. The on-board sensor
consists of 3D lidar and IMU, which is a popular choice as
a sensing payload since they provide point clouds, accelera-
tion, and angular velocity to enable UAV state estimation in

3. Relative state estimation framework
In this section, a lidar-based relative state estimation frame-
work is presented in a distributed fashion. The functionalities
of the individual modules shown in Fig. 1 are described in the
following.
3.1. Lidar-inertial odometry
The lidar-inertial odometry is in charge of using the raw sensor
data to obtain pose measurements of a single UAV. Inspired
by Ref.15, a keyframe strategy is proposed to reduce memory
consumption. Compare to traditional two-stage odometry esti-
mation, we use IMU prediction instead of scan-to-scan to
reduce the computational cost, without reducing localization
precision. As done in other lidar odometry pipelines,15 a
scan-to-map matching step is utilized to produce an accurate
pose estimate.

To improve the real-time performance of the lidar odometry,
we adopt the concept of keyframe selection.15 The keyframe
selection criteria can be represented as: If the change in
UAV pose between the current frame and the latest keyframe
is beyond a certain threshold, the current frame is treated as a
new keyframe. The keyframe strategy provides a trade-off
between map density and memory consumption that makes
the global map maintained in the map management unit suit-
able for scan-to-map matching.

3.5. Map management
Each UAVi (i ¼ 1; 2;:::; n) in the system is in charge of main-

4. Experiments
We now describe three experiments to analyze the relative state
estimation framework stated in Section 3. The performance of
the proposed method in different scenarios is compared with
SegMap30,31 which successfully integrates different state-of-

with regards to closing loops, computational complexity, and
communication bandwidth requirements. During experiments,
the interconnection topology of the multi-UAV system is
strong connectedness (every UAV is reachable from other
UAVs) based on the multi-master network of Robot Operat-
ing System (ROS). In the ﬁrst experiment, an indoor test is
introduced to evaluate the accuracy of the proposed method
in 2 UAVs scenario by it comparing with ground truth data
obtained from the OptiTrack motion capture system. Consid-
ering that the limited working area (2:5 m  7 m) of the
motion capture system prevents the real ﬂight experiments of
multi-UAV, a full functional experiment with manual move-

For the multi-UAV system, each UAV uses an identical hard-

ﬂight controller, a Velodyne VLP-16 lidar, a MicroStrain

8550U CPU, 32 GB RAM). The raw data from the IMU at

5. Conclusions
This paper presents a distributed framework for the lidar-
based relative state estimator for performing real-time trajec-
tory estimation of the multi-UAV system in GPS-denied envi-
ronments.
The
proposed
method
combines
lidar-inertial
odometry and loop closures together and provides a highly
accurate relative state estimation result based on a factor
graph. Beneﬁting from the keyframe strategy, each UAV per-
forms relative state estimation individually and broadcasts
very partial information, including keyframes and odometry
information, without exchanging raw data. The proposed
framework is validated with three experiments to demonstrate
the feasibility and effectiveness. The experiment results show
that the relative estimation results reaching a precision of
centimeter-level, and the proposed distributed method outper-
forms the centralized method SegMap, which is not limited by
GPS and can be prevalently adopted for aerial swarm aggres-
sive ﬂight over 5 m/s.
Though the proposed method is demonstrated during a
real-world simultaneous ﬂight test with three UAVs, the 3D
lidar with 360 FOV limits the take-off weight (5 kg) and
wheelbase (650 mm) of our aerial platform, which makes the
proposed aerial platform difﬁcult to through a narrow ﬂight
corridor. In future work, we would like to extend the proposed
method to lightweight solid-state lidar with small FOV and
testing
the
proposed
approach
on
different
platform
modalities.
