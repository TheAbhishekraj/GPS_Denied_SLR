ABSTRACT Uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) are increasingly used for environmental monitoring and
riverine studies; however, their dependence on Global Positioning System (GPS) localization is often
compromised in forested areas, under bridges, or above highly dynamic water surfaces. Conventional vision-
based navigation methods struggle with sparse textures and transient disturbances, leading to unstable
positioning and drift. This paper presents a robust real-time visual–inertial navigation framework that
integrates neural network–based optical flow estimation (NeuFlow v2), the Dynamic Divided Method
(DDM), and inertial measurement unit (IMU) data through a Kalman Filter (KF). The DDM adaptively
segments the optical-flow field according to water-surface dynamics and local texture stability, suppressing
transient artifacts such as ripples and reflections, while the KF stabilizes state estimation by compensating for
outliers and short-term measurement loss. Field experiments at Kangqiao, Green River, and Wu River sites
demonstrate that DDM-enhanced visual odometry (VO) significantly improves robustness in low-texture and
dynamic conditions. The fused framework combining NeuFlow v2, DDM, and KF achieves high trajectory
similarity with GPS benchmarks, reduced drift, and enhanced spatial consistency. These findings validate a
practical and effective solution for accurate UAV localization and autonomous environmental monitoring in
GPS-degraded riverine environments.

I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become indispens-
able tools across diverse applications, including environ-
mental monitoring [1], disaster response [2], infrastructure
inspection [3], and precision agriculture [4]. Their ability to
capture high-resolution data in remote or challenging ter-
rains makes them particularly valuable for monitoring aquatic
ecosystems such as rivers [5], lakes, and wetlands. These
missions demand precise localization and robust autonomous
navigation to assess water quality, track pollution sources,
and evaluate climate change impacts. However, achieving
reliable navigation in complex environments remains a sig-
nificant challenge.
The associate editor coordinating the review of this manuscript and
approving it for publication was Xiwang Dong.
One of the primary obstacles for UAVs in real-world sce-
narios is their dependence on Global Positioning System
(GPS) for localization. While GPS performs reliably in open
areas, its signals are often obstructed or unavailable in dense
forests, under bridges, or within narrow riverine corridors.
Rivers pose unique challenges due to their winding paths,
dynamic water surfaces, and surrounding vegetation, which
can lead to signal loss, causing UAVs to drift, lose con-
trol, or fail missions. These limitations necessitate alternative
localization methods capable of ensuring robust navigation in
GPS-denied environments.
Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) has emerged as a promis-
ing approach, combining camera imagery with inertial mea-
surement unit (IMU) data to estimate position and velocity.
VIO has shown significant potential in GPS-denied settings,
such as urban canyons and forested areas [6], by leveraging
visual features like edges, textures, and corners [7], [8], [9]

Despite its strengths, VIO faces challenges in dynamic and
low-texture environments like calm rivers. Rapidly changing
water surfaces, variable lighting, and dynamic objects—
such as birds or fish—reduce the availability of stable
visual features. Motion blur during high-speed flight further
degrades image quality, compromising localization accuracy.
For example, VINS-Mono [10], a leading monocular VIO

Recent advancements in learning-based optical flow esti-

RAFT [11], which incorporate attention mechanisms and

applications such as river surface velocity analysis [12].
However, RAFT’s computational demands limit its suitabil-

1–2 frames per second on devices like the Jetson Orin Nano.
In contrast, NeuFlow v2 [13] achieves over 20 frames per
second at 512 × 384 resolution, offering a 10–70× speedup
over RAFT [11] while maintaining comparable accuracy,
making it ideal for embedded UAV systems.
This study proposes a real-time UAV navigation system
tailored for dynamic riverine environments. The system inte-
grates NeuFlow v2 [13] for efficient optical flow estimation,
IMU data for robust motion tracking, and the Dynamic
Divided Method (DDM) to adaptively process optical flow
under varying environmental conditions. A Kalman Filter
(KF) [14] facilitates multi-sensor fusion, stabilizing outputs
by filtering noise and mitigating errors from dynamic objects.
Optimized for real-time performance on embedded hardware,
the system ensures reliable operation in scenarios where
GPS-based navigation fails. The main contributions of this
work are:
• A Novel VIO Framework for GPS-Denied Riverine
Environments: The system combines NeuFlow v2 opti-
cal flow estimation with IMU data fusion, enabling
robust and efficient localization where traditional GPS
or vision-based methods falter.
• Efficient Real-Time Optical Flow Computation: Neu-
Flow v2 delivers high-speed, accurate optical flow
estimation on resource-constrained UAV platforms,
ensuring practical deployment in real-world scenarios.
• Kalman Filter-Based Multi-Sensor Fusion: The KF inte-
grates optical flow and IMU data, compensating for
outliers caused by dynamic objects and enhancing nav-
igation stability and accuracy in challenging riverine
conditions.
• Comprehensive Field Validation: The system was tested
at Kangqiao, Green River, and Wu River, across diverse
trajectories including straight paths, rectangular routes,
and curved river-following flights. Results demonstrate
superior navigation robustness and accuracy compared
to conventional methods, affirming its feasibility for
autonomous environmental monitoring.
II. RELATED WORKS
The following section reviews key UAV navigation technolo-
gies, focusing on GPS-based and GPS-denied approaches.
As illustrated in Fig. 1, navigation methods are categorized
into GPS-based and GPS-denied techniques. While GPS is
effective in open environments, its limitations in areas with
poor signal reception, such as forests or water bodies, have
spurred the development of alternatives like VIO [15], opti-
cal flow estimation [16], Inertial Navigation Systems (INS)
[17], and Ultra-Wideband (UWB) [18]. These methods col-
lectively address the challenges of reliable UAV navigation
in complex, GPS-denied environments, with VIO and opti-
cal flow-based approaches showing particular promise for
dynamic settings like rivers.
A. GPS-BASED UAV NAVIGATION
GPS remains the most widely adopted technology for UAV
positioning and navigation. By receiving signals from mul-
tiple satellites, GPS systems provide absolute position and
velocity estimates for UAVs. The main advantage of this
method is its simplicity, accuracy, and ease of implemen-
tation, especially in open environments where UAVs can
rely on GPS signals for precise positioning and velocity
estimation. However, when UAVs enter areas such as dense
forests, caves, or water surfaces, traditional GPS-based nav-
igation methods become ineffective, and UAVs must rely on
other technologies to achieve autonomous localization and
navigation.
In addition to conventional GPS positioning, Real-Time
Kinematic (RTK) [19] is a high-precision GPS-based posi-
tioning technique. RTK improves accuracy by using base
stations and mobile measuring units to provide centimeter-
level positioning, making it an indispensable technology for
applications requiring high accuracy, such as agriculture,
surveying, and precise navigation. Despite these advan-
tages, GPS and RTK remain vulnerable to signal blockages,
multipath effects, and disturbances such as solar magnetic
storms [20], which can lead to significant errors or even total
signal loss.
B. LOCALIZATION METHODS IN GPS-DENIED
ENVIRONMENTS
When GPS signals are unavailable or unreliable, UAVs
must rely on alternative localization strategies. Prominent
approaches include VIO, optical flow estimation, INS,
and UWB positioning, each with unique advantages and
constraints.
1) VIO
VIO combines camera imagery with IMU measurements
to provide real-time position estimation. It enables UAVs
to maintain stable localization in environments with sparse
visual features or dynamic conditions. VO, as a core com-
ponent of VIO, estimates UAV motion by analyzing feature
point displacement between consecutive frames [21]. Com-

2) OPTICAL FLOW ESTIMATION
Optical flow estimation analyzes pixel displacement between
consecutive images to estimate object motion. Traditional
methods, such as Horn–Schunck and Lucas–Kanade [24], are
computationally efficient and effective for small motion but
degrade in environments with sparse textures, dynamic back-
grounds, or abrupt illumination changes. Recent advances
in deep learning, including FlowNet [25], PWC-Net [26],
RAFT [11], and FlowFormer [27], significantly improve
robustness and accuracy by leveraging convolutional and
attention-based architectures.

4) UWB
UWB is a short-range, high-precision localization technology
that measures distance between devices using high-frequency
signals [29]. By deploying multiple anchors, UAVs can
achieve centimeter-level indoor localization through triangu-
lation. UWB is robust to lighting and weather conditions,
but its reliance on fixed infrastructure and limited opera-
tional range restricts its applicability in large-scale outdoor
environments.
Each UAV navigation method presents distinct strengths
and limitations. GPS-based navigation provides high accu-
racy in open environments but degrades severely in signal-
deprived areas. VIO and VO offer robust alternatives in
GPS-denied environments, leveraging visual and inertial data
for stable state estimation but remain sensitive to low-texture
scenes, lighting changes, and dynamic objects. Optical flow
estimation complements VO by providing relative motion
tracking in homogeneous or water-surface environments but
is also sensitive to visual ambiguity. INS delivers reliable
short-term estimates but suffers cumulative drift, while UWB
excels in confined indoor spaces with centimeter-level accu-
racy but is limited by infrastructure requirements.

flow estimation, (2) the DDM for adaptive flow segmentation
and robust feature extraction under varying river conditions,
and (3) KF for multi-sensor fusion, which integrates IMU
measurements with optical flow data to enhance stability

For optical flow estimation, this study employs NeuFlow
v2, a neural network-based framework originally proposed
in [13]. NeuFlow v2 was designed to provide a balance
between computational efficiency and estimation accuracy,
making it particularly suitable for UAV navigation tasks
where onboard resources are constrained. Traditional optical
flow approaches such as Lucas–Kanade or Horn–Schunck
[24] are computationally efficient but degrade significantly
in low-texture or dynamic environments. In contrast, deep
learning–based models such as RAFT [11] achieve state-
of-the-art accuracy, but their computational demands render
them impractical for real-time UAV deployment on edge
devices. NeuFlow v2 [13] occupies a middle ground: it
inherits the robustness of learning-based frameworks while
incorporating architectural optimizations that make it deploy-
able in real time on embedded hardware.
The network architecture of NeuFlow v2 [13] is shown
in Fig. 2. It follows a lightweight encoder–decoder design
optimized for embedded deployment. The encoder extracts
hierarchical, multi-scale feature maps from the input images,
ensuring that both coarse global motion and fine local dis-
placements can be captured. The decoder then progressively
refines these features through a series of convolutional and
upsampling layers to produce dense optical flow predictions.
Compared with RAFT [11], which uses large recurrent mod-
ules and extensive correlation volumes, NeuFlow v2 [13]
significantly reduces memory access and floating-point oper-
ations while preserving competitive accuracy. These design
choices make it an attractive candidate for UAV naviga-
tion in GPS-denied riverine environments, where maintaining
real-time updates is essential for flight stability.

system, NeuFlow v2 [13] was benchmarked on a Jetson

Beyond runtime evaluation, we further examined how
frame-rate reductions affect UAV navigation accuracy and
long-term trajectory stability. Specifically, a static-scene
experiment with controlled wind disturbances was conducted
to emulate real-world perturbations.
As illustrated in Fig. 3, when the optical-flow update
rate dropped to low FPS levels, the VIO pipeline exhib-
ited pronounced cumulative drift and positional bias, even
though the environment remained static. This degradation
arises because a lower flow update frequency increases the
temporal interval between measurements, thereby amplifying
integration error and weakening the feedback stability of the
Kalman filter loop. Consequently, the delayed correction of
optical-flow residuals allows unmodeled disturbances—such
as wind gusts or sensor bias—to accumulate over time.
This observation underscores the necessity of maintain-
ing sufficiently high optical-flow update rates to ensure
stable closed-loop state propagation. In the proposed sys-
tem, NeuFlow v2’s runtime characteristics (as summarized
in TABLE 1) are jointly leveraged with the DDM and
KF-based fusion to mitigate outlier effects induced by
dynamic objects, while maintaining high-frequency correc-
tion capability. Together, these components stabilize the
navigation output by preserving both spatial coherence and

To balance localization accuracy and computational effi-
ciency, this study adopts an input resolution of 512 × 384 for
optical-flow estimation on the Jetson Orin Nano prior to
integrating the DDM module. This configuration achieves an
average inference time of approximately 64 ms per frame,
corresponding to an effective update frequency of around
15 frames per second (fps). The selected resolution provides
an optimal trade-off between processing speed and spatial
fidelity—fast enough to sustain real-time navigation while
retaining sufficient texture detail for reliable optical-flow
extraction. This setting serves as the baseline configura-
tion for all subsequent on-board experiments and system
deployment.
To further enhance input representativeness without
increasing computational load, an input selection and
reassembly strategy was developed to preserve spatial diver-
sity during optical-flow estimation. As shown in Fig. 4, the
original 1920 × 1080 image is evenly divided into four
quadrants—upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, and lower-
right. From the center of each quadrant, a 128 × 96 pixel
patch is extracted to capture regional variations in brightness,
texture, and motion. These four patches are then spatially
rearranged according to their original positions to form a new
composite frame with a resolution of 512 × 384, which is
subsequently fed into the NeuFlow v2 model for optical-flow
computation.
This composite-patching design maintains both the com-
putational efficiency of a reduced-resolution input and the
scene-awareness of a full-frame image. By retaining spatial
diversity across quadrants, the system minimizes local feature
bias and enhances the robustness of flow estimation under
heterogeneous visual conditions, such as asymmetric lighting
or texture imbalance across the river surface.
This design aims to enhance the global representativeness
and robustness of the estimated optical flow. In riverine

environments, using input images concentrated in a single
region can easily lead to biased motion estimation—local
disturbances caused by water vegetation, aquatic animals,
or variations in underwater terrain may be mistakenly inter-

all four quadrants and reassembling them into a composite
frame, the method preserves spatial diversity and balances
illumination and texture variations across the scene. This
approach reduces the influence of local noise and enables
extraction of globally consistent motion cues essential for
stable navigation.
While the reassembly strategy broadens spatial coverage,
additional mechanisms are required to handle dynamic dis-
turbances commonly encountered in riverine environments.
To this end, the Dynamic Divided Method (DDM) is intro-
duced. The DDM adaptively segments the optical-flow field
according to water-surface dynamics and local texture stabil-
ity, emphasizing motion-consistent regions while suppressing
transient artifacts caused by ripples, reflections, or vegeta-
tion motion. Unlike conventional flow-smoothing techniques
that apply uniform filtering, the proposed approach performs
localized spatiotemporal weighting, allowing it to preserve
coherent motion in stable regions and enhance robustness
under fluctuating environmental conditions.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
This section validates the effectiveness and feasibility of
the proposed visual–inertial fusion method through compre-
hensive simulation and field evaluations. The experimental
protocol is designed to assess both accuracy and robust-
ness under diverse riverine conditions. The section first
outlines the simulation environments and flight scenarios,
followed by a comparative analysis of trajectory similarity
between visual–inertial odometry (VIO) results with and
without the proposed DDM, using GPS data as the reference
benchmark.
A comprehensive performance evaluation was conducted
using real-world riverbank scenes as the simulation envi-
ronment. Three representative locations were selected for
on-site data acquisition, ensuring diversity in water flow,
background complexity, and illumination. All simulations
and algorithmic tests were executed on authentic video and
sensor recordings, rather than synthetic datasets, to better
approximate real operational conditions. The experimental
framework encompasses the rationale for site selection, the
design of representative flight missions, the data acquisition
and post-processing pipeline, and the criteria for system per-
formance assessment.
Each test site provided field-collected multimodal data—
comprising video imagery, inertial measurements, and GPS
trajectories—serving as the basis for evaluating the fusion
algorithm. Representative flight missions, including straight
out-and-back, rectangular, curved river-following, and turn-
ing trajectories, were designed to systematically challenge
localization performance across varying degrees of motion
dynamics and environmental complexity. These trajectories
were replayed in post-processing using the recorded datasets,
enabling controlled yet realistic analysis of the proposed
system’s consistency and adaptability.

V. CONCLUSION
This study presented a hydro-adaptive visual–inertial fusion
framework designed to enable autonomous UAV naviga-
tion in GNSS-denied and visually degraded environments.
Unlike conventional methods that treat environmental motion
as noise, the proposed approach leverages dynamic sur-
face cues to enhance optical-flow stability and localiza-
tion robustness. Through comprehensive simulations and
real-world flight experiments across multiple river sites, the
system demonstrated reliable performance under diverse mis-
sion geometries—including straight, rectangular, and curved
trajectories—while maintaining accurate trajectory estima-
tion without continuous GNSS feedback.
At the core of the framework, the Dynamic Divided
Method
(DDM)
performs
adaptive
segmentation
of
optical-flow fields based on local texture stability and motion
coherence, transforming fluctuating water dynamics into
structured features that support consistent visual odometry.
The Kalman Filter–based fusion further stabilizes these visual
measurements by compensating for transient sensor noise and
measurement loss, ensuring geometric consistency even in
low-texture or reflection-dominated scenes.
Beyond addressing GNSS-denied navigation, the frame-
work exhibits robustness in perception-limited environments,
where traditional vision-based systems often fail due to
sparse textures or featureless surfaces. Although this study
focuses on riverine conditions, the proposed principles are
broadly applicable to coastal and marine domains, where flow
patterns are more stable but spatial scale and illumination
variability pose distinct challenges.
Overall, this work establishes a generalizable paradigm of
environment-aware autonomy, transforming dynamic envi-
ronmental disturbances from obstacles into usable perception
