Abstract
Indoor localization for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) remains challenging in GNSS-
denied environments due to the difficulty of position calibration of multiple ultra-wideband
(UWB) anchors and the asynchronous fusion of heterogeneous sensors. This paper pro-
poses a multi-sensor fusion localization framework that integrates multi-anchor one-shot
calibration with factor graph optimization (FGO). First, Landmark Multidimensional Scal-
ing (LMDS) is used to reconstruct the relative geometry of the anchors and the onboard tag
from ranging measurements. Then, rigid Procrustes alignment is performed using a small
number of anchors with known coordinates in the East–North–Up (ENU) frame to recover
the transformation to the ENU frame, thereby enabling efficient position calibration of
multiple UWB anchors and UAV pose initialization. Subsequently, a tightly coupled factor
graph is constructed by incorporating inertial measurement unit (IMU) pre-integration,
UWB ranging, laser rangefinder height measurements, and visual–inertial odometry (VIO)
pose constraints. The resulting nonlinear optimization problem is solved using incremen-
tal smoothing, which improves robustness against non-line-of-sight (NLOS) errors and
long-term drift. Experimental results on anchor calibration, public datasets, and real-world
indoor UAV flights demonstrate that the proposed method improves the accuracy and
robustness of indoor UAV localization. In particular, on the real-world rectangle trajectory,
FGO-TC reduces the RMSE by approximately 38.8% compared with FGO-LC.

1. Introduction
In recent years, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been increasingly used in
applications such as indoor inspection, warehouse automation, and emergency search
and rescue, creating a growing demand for accurate and reliable autonomous localization
and navigation [1,2]. However, in indoor environments where Global Navigation Satellite
System (GNSS) signals are severely degraded or unavailable, UAVs cannot rely on satellite-
based navigation and must instead estimate their states using onboard sensors [3]. Common
sensors for indoor localization include inertial measurement units (IMUs), ultra-wideband
(UWB) devices, vision sensors, and altimeters. These sensors are highly complementary:
IMUs provide high-frequency motion information but suffer from drift, vision sensors offer
high accuracy but are sensitive to illumination changes, occlusions, and texture degradation,
while UWB provides absolute range constraints that help improve global consistency [4].
Despite these advantages, the rapid calibration of UWB anchor networks and the unified
fusion of asynchronous measurements from heterogeneous sensors remain key challenges
for indoor multi-sensor localization systems.
Existing indoor localization methods can generally be categorized into three groups:
radio-based localization, vision/light detection and ranging (LiDAR)-based localization,
and inertial/odometry-based localization. Radio-based methods typically rely on wireless
infrastructure such as WiFi, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), and ultra-wideband (UWB), and
estimate position from measurements such as received signal strength indicator (RSSI),
time of arrival/time difference of arrival (TOA/TDOA), or angle of arrival (AOA) [5]. By
contrast, vision/LiDAR-based and inertial/odometry-based methods recover platform
pose through simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) and inertial dead reckoning,
respectively [6]. Compared with single-sensor approaches, multi-sensor fusion can better
exploit the complementary characteristics of heterogeneous sensors, including IMUs, UWB,
vision, LiDAR, and altimeters, and therefore generally provides higher accuracy and robust-
ness. In particular, indoor UAV localization methods that integrate multiple UWB anchors,
IMU, VIO, and altimeters have attracted increasing attention in recent years. Before UWB
observations can be incorporated into a fusion framework, however, anchor geometry
calibration and initial UAV pose estimation are usually required so that all measurements
can be processed in a unified coordinate frame. Existing studies can be broadly divided into
two directions. The first focuses on the calibration and initialization of multi-anchor sys-
tems, where anchor layouts are recovered through manual measurement, trajectory-based
self-calibration, or methods combining MDS with nonlinear optimization [7]. Although no-
table progress has been made, existing methods in multi-anchor scenarios still often rely on
incremental or offline calibration, making efficient one-shot calibration difficult. The second
focuses on multi-sensor fusion localization built on such initialization, where IMU, UWB,
visual/LiDAR odometry, and altimeter observations are jointly modeled within filtering-
or graph optimization-based frameworks [8]. However, current fusion frameworks remain
limited in the unified modeling and robust optimization of asynchronous measurements
from heterogeneous sensors.
Before performing multi-sensor fusion localization, calibration of multi-anchor UWB
networks and estimation of the UAV’s initial pose are essential prerequisites. As indoor
environments become larger and higher positioning accuracy is required, practical systems
typically deploy many UWB anchors whose coordinates must be expressed in a unified
ENU world frame FW to enable joint fusion with IMU, vision, and altimeter measure-

ments. Existing approaches often rely on manual surveying, sequential calibration, or
complex platform motions combined with iterative nonlinear least-squares optimization.
These procedures are cumbersome, sensitive to trajectory design, and may introduce sys-
tematic errors in multi-anchor scenarios [4]. To address these issues, a variety of anchor
calibration and initialization methods have been proposed. Delama et al. [4] presented a
multi-step anchor initialization method based on UAV motion excitation and geometry
dilution of precision (GDOP)-guided trajectory design. Batstone et al. [9] studied real-
time anchor position estimation from sequential UWB TOA measurements using robust
solvers. Jung et al. [10] proposed a two-stage method that first computes coarse anchor
positions and then refines them through nonlinear optimization. Jia et al. [11] introduced a
long–short sliding-window strategy for unknown-anchor scenarios, combining VIO with
UWB measurements to estimate anchor positions and their uncertainty within a tightly
coupled framework. Shi et al. [12] proposed an anchor self-localization method based on
UWB and low-cost IMU measurements, jointly estimating anchor positions and tag pose
in an error-state Kalman filter (ESKF) framework. Corbalán et al. [13] developed a self-
localization system for large-scale multi-hop UWB networks using only relative ranging
and lightweight MDS, which is a geometric reconstruction method that recovers node coor-
dinates from pairwise distance information. Liu et al. [14] further proposed a factor graph
optimization framework that jointly estimates UWB anchor positions and robot poses from
UWB and LiDAR observations. Although these methods have made substantial progress,
most still depend on complex motion excitation, incremental procedures, or additional
sensing support. As a result, rapid deployment and unified initialization in multi-anchor
UWB scenarios remain challenging.
To overcome the limitations of single-sensor localization, many studies have inves-
tigated filtering-based multi-sensor fusion methods. Approaches such as the Extended
Kalman Filter (EKF), Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF), and ESKF recursively fuse obser-
vations from IMUs, UWB sensors, vision sensors, and altimeters within a state-space
framework. These methods are attractive because of their relatively simple implementation,
high computational efficiency, and natural ability to handle asynchronous measurements
through prediction and update steps. However, filtering-based methods rely on single-step
linearization and local Gaussian assumptions, which limit their ability to exploit historical
observations and model strongly nonlinear state coupling. In the presence of large noise
or outliers, such as UWB NLOS errors, the estimator may become inconsistent or even
diverge. Moreover, as the number of sensors and state variables increases, noise modeling,
covariance tuning, and stability analysis become more difficult. Representative studies
illustrate both the effectiveness and limitations of this line of research. Liu et al. [15] pro-
posed an INS/UWB localization method based on Sage–Husa fuzzy adaptive filtering
to suppress time-varying noise and abnormal observations. Zhao et al. [16] developed a
tightly coupled UWB/IMU framework based on an ESKF, with initialization performed by
weighted nonlinear least squares. Liu et al. [17] combined ESKF, variational Bayesian infer-
ence, and UKF to improve IMU/UWB localization in underground coal mines. Ji et al. [18]
introduced an improved Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF) with adaptive and attenuation
factors to mitigate divergence, while Yang et al. [19] proposed a low-cost UAV localization
system based on an Adaptive Square-Root CKF. Jiang et al. [20] developed a tightly coupled
visual SLAM/UWB method for identifying UWB NLOS errors, and Oursland et al. [21]
improved heading estimation using an Invariant Extended Kalman Filter (InEKF). Over-
all, filtering-based methods have achieved promising results, but their unified modeling
capability and robustness remain limited in complex indoor environments.
More recently, factor graph optimization-based multi-sensor fusion methods have
attracted considerable attention in UAV and robotic localization. In these methods, system

states such as position, attitude, velocity, and sensor biases are represented as graph nodes,
while measurements from IMUs, UWB sensors, LiDAR, and vision sensors are modeled
as factors, so that joint state estimation can be obtained by minimizing the overall sum
of squared residuals. Compared with filtering-based methods, factor graph optimization
can exploit historical observations and redundant multi-source constraints more effec-
tively within a unified probabilistic framework, and therefore generally provides higher
accuracy and stronger robustness. Existing studies have demonstrated the potential of
this paradigm in a variety of scenarios. For example, Li et al. [22] integrated stereo VIO,
single-base-station UWB ranging, and AOA measurements within a factor graph frame-
work for six-degree-of-freedom localization of unmanned ground vehicles in occluded
environments. Liu et al. [23] proposed the GLIO system, which tightly couples GNSS,
LiDAR, and IMU measurements in a factor graph framework, although its suppression
of NLOS and multipath effects remains insufficient in severely degraded GNSS environ-
ments. Zhang et al. [24] developed the continuous-time gnssFGO framework, which uses
Gaussian-process trajectory representations to jointly fuse GNSS, IMU, wheel odometry,
and LiDAR odometry. Wang et al. [25] constructed a factor graph-based method for cooper-
ative localization and mapping using UWB and IMU measurements in multi-agent systems.
Zheng et al. [26] proposed a UWB-assisted multi-robot LiDAR cooperative localization
and mapping system for GNSS-denied environments and further improved robustness
through an NLOS identification strategy. Song et al. [27] introduced IMU pre-integration
factors together with UWB ranging factors into a factor graph to realize tightly coupled
UWB/INS integrated navigation. Meng et al. [28] further incorporated a convolutional
neural network (CNN)-driven stochastic model to adaptively suppress NLOS effects and
noise, significantly improving indoor UAV localization performance. Although substantial
progress has been made in factor graph-based multi-sensor fusion, most existing studies
focus primarily on the fusion of IMU with vision or UWB, while relatively few systemat-
ically integrate IMU, UWB, laser rangefinder, and VIO within a unified factor graph. In
addition, UWB anchor calibration and initial pose estimation are still commonly treated
as independent preprocessing steps, and a complete integrated solution for multi-anchor
UWB systems remains largely absent.
To address the above challenges, this paper proposes a unified framework for position
calibration of multiple UWB anchors and multi-sensor fusion localization on an indoor UAV
platform equipped with an IMU, UWB, laser rangefinder, and a camera. First, under FW,
a small number of UWB anchors with known coordinates are used as reference anchors.
LMDS is a computationally efficient variant of classical MDS, in which a small subset
of nodes is selected as landmarks to capture the global geometric structure, while the
remaining nodes are embedded according to their distances to these landmarks. In this
work, LMDS is employed to recover the relative geometric configuration of all anchors
and the onboard tag in the MDS coordinate frame FM from the ranging measurements
between N anchors and one onboard tag. Subsequently, rigid Procrustes alignment is
performed to estimate the optimal transformation FM to FW, thereby enabling one-shot
position calibration of multiple UWB anchors. In this paper, one-shot calibration refers to
estimating and aligning the positions of multiple UWB anchors within a single initialization
stage, without requiring sequential anchor-by-anchor calibration or repeated calibration
maneuvers. Compared with conventional incremental or sequential calibration strategies,
it improves deployment efficiency and reduces setup complexity, which is particularly
beneficial for indoor UAV applications requiring rapid setup. Furthermore, by combining
the extrinsic calibration between the tag and the UAV body with the gravity-direction
observation provided by the stationary IMU, the initial position as well as the roll and
pitch angles of the UAV in FW can be obtained. After initialization, a factor graph is

factors, including IMU pre-integration factors, UWB ranging factors, laser rangefinder

iSAM2 incremental smoothing algorithm implemented in the GTSAM library. The iSAM2
algorithm is an incremental smoothing method that updates the factor graph solution
efficiently as new measurements arrive, thereby avoiding repeated batch optimization
of the full graph. This formulation provides a unified and practically oriented multi-
sensor graph optimization framework for indoor UAV localization, where IMU, UWB, laser
rangefinder, and VIO measurements are jointly optimized in a common ENU-referenced
factor graph to improve estimation accuracy and robustness. The main contributions of
this paper are summarized as follows:
1.
One-shot anchor calibration and initialization:A one-shot method for position calibra-

extrinsic parameters with stationary IMU measurements, the initial UAV position and
attitude, including roll and pitch, can be jointly estimated.

Tightly coupled factor graph fusion: A practically oriented factor graph optimization

in the GTSAM library, thereby enabling accurate and robust multi-sensor fusion

calibration experiments as well as localization evaluations on an open-source dataset
and a real-world dataset, are conducted to validate the proposed method. The re-
sults demonstrate that the proposed framework is effective in position calibration of
multiple UWB anchors, initial pose estimation, and multi-sensor fusion localization.

work on multi-anchor calibration and multi-sensor fusion localization. Section 3 consists
of two parts. The first introduces the system model, coordinate frame definitions, and the
proposed method for position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and UAV initialization

2. Related Work
A first line of research concerns anchor calibration, which is required before UWB
measurements can be fused with other sensing modalities in a unified reference frame.
Existing studies have mainly addressed this problem through sequential surveying, motion-
excitation-based self-calibration, or optimization with additional sensing support. For
https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18091407
[p.6]
Remote Sens. 2026, 18, 1407
6 of 27
instance, Delama et al. [4], Batstone et al. [9], and Jung et al. [10] all relied on sequential
estimation or carefully designed motions to recover anchor geometry. These methods
improve calibration accuracy under controlled conditions, but they also increase deploy-
ment complexity and make the final performance more sensitive to trajectory design. By
comparison, Jia et al. [11] and Shi et al. [12] reduced manual intervention by introducing
VIO or IMU constraints, whereas Corbalán et al. [13] and Liu et al. [14] further extended
the problem to MDS-based and factor-graph-based formulations. Such methods improve
automation and reduce the burden of pure manual calibration; however, many of them
still depend on incremental procedures, repeated motion excitation, or additional sensing
support. In this context, rapid one-shot deployment and unified initialization remain insuf-
ficiently explored for practical multi-anchor UWB systems. The present work addresses this
issue through a deployment-oriented calibration strategy that supports one-shot anchor
calibration and UAV initialization with reduced calibration effort and favorable scalability.
A second line of research concerns multi-sensor fusion for indoor localization, espe-
cially through filtering-based and graph-based estimators, as well as robustness-oriented
extensions for degraded measurements. Filtering-based methods, such as EKF-, UKF-,
and ESKF-based frameworks, are attractive because they naturally handle asynchronous
measurements and remain computationally efficient [15–19]. This makes them suitable
for efficient recursive estimation, but their reliance on local linearization and recursive
updates limits their ability to exploit long-term historical information and strongly cou-
pled multi-sensor constraints. By contrast, factor graph optimization provides a more
flexible framework for jointly modeling redundant observations, and has shown effec-
tiveness in UWB/IMU fusion, cooperative localization, and broader GNSS/LiDAR/IMU
systems [22–25,27]. These studies demonstrate that graph-based optimization can improve
global consistency and estimation accuracy, especially when multiple sensing constraints
need to be exploited over time. In parallel, another body of work has focused on robustness,
where UWB localization is improved through NLOS identification, adaptive filtering, or
stochastic modeling [15,26,28]. Such reliability-aware methods can improve robustness
under adverse sensing conditions, but they usually focus on measurement-level reliability
handling within a given estimator rather than on linking calibration, initialization, and
fusion into a single workflow. Against this background, a unified indoor UAV localiza-
tion pipeline that consistently connects multi-anchor UWB calibration, ENU-referenced
initialization, and IMU/UWB/laser rangefinder/VIO fusion is still lacking. The present
work focuses on this architectural aspect by integrating calibrated anchor geometry, ENU
initialization, IMU pre-integration, UWB ranging, laser rangefinder height constraints, and
VIO pose observations within a common graph optimization framework.
3. Methods
This study develops a multi-source heterogeneous sensor fusion localization system
for an indoor quadrotor UAV, integrating an IMU, a UWB tag, a stereo camera, and a laser
rangefinder altimeter, as illustrated in Figure 1. The proposed system consists of two tightly
coupled modules. The first performs one-shot position calibration of multiple UWB anchors
and UAV initial pose estimation based on LMDS and rigid Procrustes alignment. During
the stationary phase, a distance matrix is constructed from the ranging measurements
between the onboard UWB tag and the N anchors. LMDS is then applied to recover the
relative geometric configuration of all anchors and the tag in FM. Using the anchors
with known ENU coordinates, Procrustes alignment estimates the rigid transformation
from FM to FW, thereby obtaining the ENU coordinates of all anchors and the initial
position of the onboard tag in a single step. Meanwhile, the gravity-direction observation
provided by the stationary IMU is used to estimate the initial roll and pitch angles of the
https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18091407
[p.7]
Remote Sens. 2026, 18, 1407
7 of 27
UAV, yielding an initial pose with clear physical meaning. The second module performs
factor graph-based multi-sensor fusion localization. Given the calibrated ENU coordinates
of the UWB anchors and the initialized UAV pose, the UAV state, including position,
attitude, velocity, and IMU biases, is represented as graph nodes. Measurements from IMU
pre-integration, UWB ranging, laser rangefinder height sensing, and VIO relative poses
are modeled as different types of factors. Efficient optimization is then performed using
the iSAM2 incremental smoothing algorithm in the GTSAM library, producing a smooth
and geometrically consistent UAV trajectory in FW. Through this framework, the system
achieves rapid position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and pose initialization, while
efficiently fusing IMU, UWB, laser rangefinder, and VIO measurements within a unified
model, thereby improving the accuracy and robustness of indoor UAV localization.

3.1. One-Shot Calibration of Multiple Anchor Nodes
This subsection first defines the coordinate frames used in the system, then presents
the LMDS-based method for position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and coordinate
alignment, and finally describes the estimation of the initial UAV position and attitude
based on the calibrated anchor geometry. The proposed calibration strategy offers several
practical advantages, including one-shot deployment, reduced calibration effort, limited
motion requirements, and favorable scalability as the number of anchors increases.
3.1.1. Coordinate Frame Definition
The following five coordinate frames are used in this work:
•
World frame FW (ENU frame): A predefined global reference frame whose x-, y-, and
z-axes point to the east, north, and upward directions, respectively.
•
MDS frame FM: A data-driven frame constructed by LMDS from the ranging mea-
surements between the UWB anchors and the onboard tag. It preserves Euclidean
distance relationships but has no direct physical meaning in terms of origin or
axis orientation.
•
Body frame FB: A frame rigidly attached to the UAV body, with its origin located at
the IMU. The UAV attitude is described by roll ϕ, pitch θ, and yaw ψ.
•
UWB tag frame FT: A frame rigidly attached to the onboard UWB tag. The extrinsic
transformation between FB and FT, TBT = (RBT, tBT), is assumed to be known from
offline calibration.
•
laser rangefinder frame FH: A frame rigidly attached to the laser rangefinder, with its
origin at the optical center and its z-axis aligned with the ranging direction toward
the ground. The extrinsic transformation between FB and FH, TBH = (RBH, tBH), is
assumed to be known from offline calibration.

3.2. Factor Graph-Based Multi-Sensor Fusion Localization Method
After position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and UAV pose initialization, a
factor graph-based multi-sensor fusion localization framework is constructed in FW. This
component is intended as a unified framework for indoor UAV localization, where IMU,
UWB, laser rangefinder, and VIO measurements are consistently fused within a common
graph optimization architecture. The iSAM2 algorithm in the GTSAM library is adopted
as the backend solver for incremental nonlinear optimization. In the factor graph, state
nodes represent the system states to be estimated, whereas measurement factors introduce
probabilistic constraints on the corresponding states or on adjacent states. As shown
in Figure 2, the green circular nodes denote state variables at different time steps. The
yellow rectangular nodes represent IMU pre-integration factors that connect adjacent
states and describe the system motion evolution. The blue rectangular nodes denote UWB
ranging factors, which provide absolute distance constraints. The orange rectangular nodes
correspond to laser rangefinder height factors that constrain the altitude component of
the UAV state. The purple rectangular nodes denote VIO pose factors, which introduce
pose observations from the VIO system. The proposed framework adopts an incremental
smoothing strategy based on iSAM2. The historical constraintsshown on the left side of
Figure 2 represent past states and measurement factors that have already been incorporated
into the graph and are continuously maintained by iSAM2. These constraints are preserved
through the sparse factor graph structure and its associated Bayes tree representation,
and they continue to influence the estimation of current and future states. The iSAM2
incremental update shown on the right side indicates that, when new state nodes and
measurement factors are added, iSAM2 does not rebuild and solve the entire graph from
scratch, as in batch optimization. Instead, it updates the current Bayes tree and performs
relinearization, variable reordering, and local elimination only on the affected subgraph,
thereby incrementally updating the state estimate. Compared with conventional batch
nonlinear least-squares methods, iSAM2 significantly reduces the computational cost of
repeated optimization while maintaining estimation accuracy. This makes it particularly
suitable for asynchronous multi-sensor fusion scenarios such as the one considered in
this work.

3.2.2. Factor Graph Formulation
After defining the system state and the corresponding measurement models, the
localization problem is formulated within a factor graph optimization framework. In this
framework, state nodes represent the unknown UAV states, while measurement factors

factors, and UWB ranging factors, and the overall objective function is constructed as
the weighted sum of all residual terms. Under the unified FW, these heterogeneous
observations are jointly optimized to obtain a geometrically consistent estimate of the

is shown in Figure 3. For clarity, the key performance parameters of the sensors used in

China). The UWB module performed bidirectional ranging between the onboard tag and

4.2. Dataset Experiments
The proposed factor graph-based multi-sensor fusion localization method was further
evaluated using the public MILUV dataset [8]. This dataset was designed for indoor
autonomous localization and provides synchronized measurements from multiple sensing
modalities, including UWB ranging, stereo camera, IMU, laser rangefinder-based height
measurements, and ground-truth poses obtained from a motion capture system. Owing
to its standardized sensor configuration, time synchronization mechanism, and reference
ground truth, the dataset provides a fair and consistent benchmark for evaluating different
localization methods and is therefore well suited for validating the proposed framework.
To systematically assess the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method,
two representative trajectory sequences, namely, circular2D and circular3D, were selected
for evaluation and comparison with several baseline methods. The circular2D sequence
mainly represents circular motion within an approximately horizontal plane, whereas the
circular3D sequence involves more pronounced 3D motion and attitude variation, thus
placing stricter requirements on localization accuracy and robustness under complex ma-
neuvers. Three representative methods were compared, including the pure VIO method
VINS-Fusion, the tightly coupled extended Kalman filter method (EKF-TC), and the pro-
posed tightly coupled factor graph optimization method (FGO-TC). The primary evaluation
metric was the APE, and the corresponding RMSE, mean, median, standard deviation (Std),
minimum (Min), and maximum (Max) were reported to comprehensively characterize the
localization performance of different methods.
Figures 5a and 6a show the estimated trajectories of the three methods for the circu-
lar2D and circular3D sequences, respectively. In the circular2D sequence, all three methods
are able to recover the overall circular trajectory reasonably well. However, the proposed
method exhibits better trajectory closure and local smoothness, and its estimated trajectory
follows the ground truth more closely. In the circular3D sequence, the proposed method
reconstructs the spatial structure of the 3D trajectory more accurately than the baseline
methods. In particular, smaller trajectory deviations are observed in regions with alti-
tude variation and turning maneuvers, indicating that the proposed framework provides
stronger constraints in complex 3D motion scenarios.
Figures 5b and 6b present the cumulative distribution function (CDF) curves of the
localization errors for the two trajectories. A curve closer to the upper-left corner indicates
smaller localization errors for most samples. For the circular2D sequence, the CDF curves
of the proposed method and VINS-Fusion are very close, indicating comparable overall
performance. Nevertheless, the proposed method shows better stability in the low- and
medium-error ranges, further confirming its smaller error fluctuations. For the circular3D
sequence, the error CDF of the proposed method lies consistently to the left of those of the
other two methods, showing that it achieves smaller APE values for most time steps. This
observation is consistent with the results in Table 4, where the proposed method achieves
the best RMSE, Mean, and Median values.

4.3. Real-World Experiments
To further validate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method in real
indoor environments, real-world experiments were conducted on the self-developed UAV
platform. Multiple trajectories, including circular, rectangular, diamond-shaped, and
triangular paths, were designed to evaluate localization performance under different
motion patterns and geometric variations. Owing to space limitations, this subsection
focuses on two representative trajectories, namely, circle and rectangle. The circle trajectory
was used to evaluate localization performance under continuous and smooth motion,
whereas the rectangle trajectory involved more pronounced straight-line motion, sharp
turns, and altitude variation, thus imposing stricter requirements on state estimation during
complex maneuvers and around trajectory corners. In the experiments, the proposed
method FGO-TC was compared with the EKF, Particle Filter (PF), pure VIO, and a loosely
coupled factor graph optimization method (FGO-LC). The primary evaluation metric was
the APE, and the corresponding RMSE, Mean, Median, Std, Min, and Max were reported
to comprehensively assess localization performance.

deviations remain. By contrast, EKF and PF exhibit more obvious local jitter and deviation

graph framework can provide more accurate localization results for real-world trajectories
with significant geometric changes and asynchronous multi-sensor observations.

5. Discussion
The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed framework provides a prac-
tical solution for indoor UAV localization by consistently linking multi-anchor UWB cal-
ibration, UAV initialization, and tightly coupled multi-sensor fusion within a unified
ENU-referenced pipeline. In addition to the real-world anchor calibration experiment, the
comparative simulation experiments further show that the LMDS-based module maintains
competitive localization accuracy while offering clear advantages in computational scalabil-
ity as the network size increases. In particular, when the number of landmarks is selected
appropriately, LMDS achieves localization performance comparable to that of MDS-MAP
and SMACOF in the subsequent graph optimization stage, while remaining well suited
for one-shot deployment with reduced calibration effort and favorable scalability. The
localization experiments further show that the proposed FGO-TC framework benefits from
the complementary roles of different sensing modalities: UWB ranging provides absolute
geometric constraints, IMU pre-integration ensures motion continuity, the laser rangefinder
improves vertical observability, and VIO contributes local geometric consistency. The abla-
tion study confirms that the final performance gain is achieved through the coordinated
contribution of initialization quality, tightly coupled graph optimization, and multiple com-
plementary sensing factors. At the same time, several limitations should be noted. First, the
effectiveness of the laser rangefinder factor depends on the approximately planar-ground
assumption adopted in this work. Second, the current framework does not include an
explicit confidence-aware mechanism for handling degraded VIO observations, and its
robustness to visual drift mainly relies on complementary multi-sensor constraints. Third,
the calibration component is primarily deployment-oriented, and its advantage is reflected
more in initialization convenience and scalability than in universally superior calibration
accuracy. These limitations suggest that future work should investigate more general height
modeling, explicit reliability-aware visual constraint management, and broader validation
in larger and more diverse indoor environments.
6. Conclusions
This paper addressed two key challenges in indoor UAV localization under GNSS-
denied environments, namely, position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and multi-
sensor fusion localization. To this end, a unified method combining LMDS, rigid Procrustes
alignment, and factor graph optimization was proposed. First, a one-shot method for
position calibration of multiple UWB anchors and UAV initialization was developed. By
using a small number of reference anchors with known ENU coordinates together with
the ranging measurements between the onboard tag and multiple anchors, the relative
anchor geometry was reconstructed and aligned to FW, thereby obtaining the coordinates
of all anchors in a single step. By further combining the tag-to-body extrinsic calibration
with the gravity observation from a stationary IMU, the initial UAV position as well as
the roll and pitch angles were jointly estimated. Therefore, this LMDS-based formulation
is more suitable for practical one-shot deployment, as it reduces calibration effort while
retaining good scalability for larger anchor networks. On this basis, a tightly coupled
factor graph framework was constructed by jointly fusing IMU pre-integration, UWB
ranging, laser rangefinder height constraints, and VIO pose observations. The resulting
nonlinear optimization problem was solved using the iSAM2 incremental smoothing
algorithm in GTSAM, enabling continuous and globally consistent pose estimation in
FW. Compared with conventional filtering-based methods and loosely coupled fusion
strategies, the proposed method makes better use of historical observations and redundant
multi-sensor constraints, and therefore achieves improved accuracy and robustness in the
presence of UWB NLOS errors and complex maneuvers. Experimental results showed
https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18091407
[p.26]
Remote Sens. 2026, 18, 1407
26 of 27
that the proposed one-shot anchor position calibration method can effectively recover the
global geometry of the anchor network while maintaining a good balance among accuracy,
computational cost, and scalability. In addition, experiments on both a public dataset
and real indoor UAV flights demonstrated that the proposed tightly coupled factor graph
method consistently outperformed the comparison methods on 2D circular, 3D circular,
and rectangular trajectories. Overall, this work provides an effective solution for rapid
deployment of UWB anchor networks and tightly coupled multi-sensor localization for
indoor UAVs.
