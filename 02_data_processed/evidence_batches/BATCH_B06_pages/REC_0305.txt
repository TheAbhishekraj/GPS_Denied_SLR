A method based on CNN-BiLSTM for UAV
navigation error compensation in GNSS denied
environment
Yi-han Shao, Bin Han*, Ying Luo, Member, IEEE
Network and communication Research Institute, College of Information Engineering
SouthWest University of Science and Technology
Mianyang, Sichuan Province, China
shaoyihan1120@163.com,hanbin@swust.edu.cn,yluo@swust.edu.cn
Abstract—When
the
Global
Navigation
Satellite
System
(GNSS) signal is unavailable, the performance of GNSS/Inertial
Navigation System (INS) integrated navigation system will be
seriously degraded. In order to compensate the performance of
GNSS/INS integrated navigation system, an error compensation
method based on Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) combined
with Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network
is proposed. In the proposed method, the Inertial Measurement
Unit (IMU) dynamics measurement of Unmanned Aerial Vehicle
(UAV) is used as the input, the GNSS high-precision navigation
information is used as the label. CNN-BiLSTM establishes the
relationship between IMU dynamics measurement and GNSS
high-precision navigation information through the training of a
large number of input and label data, since it can achieve more
accurate prediction of position increment and velocity increment.
At last, the experimental results show that when the GNSS signal
is unavailable, CNN-BiLSTM obtains the lower average RMSE
and MAE of prediction error and the higher prediction fitting
degree compared with CNN-LSTM and LSTM.
Index Terms—GNSS/INS integrated navigation system, IMU
dynamics measurement, CNN-BiLSTM, GNSS denial
I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned Air Vehicle (UAV) swarm technology is based
on biological swarm behavior, it can accomplish a variety
of complex tasks through mutual perception, interaction, in-
formation transmission and collaboration between individual
UAVs[1-2]. However, this technology is dependent on high-
precision absolute positioning. Recently, the integrated nav-
igation technology is widely used in absolute positioning
due to high-precision[3-4]. When UAV swarm perform tasks,
each UAV is usually equipped with GNSS/INS integrated
navigation system, that is, the combination of Global Naviga-
tion Satellite System (GNSS) and Inertial Navigation System
(INS). In this navigation system, INS calculates the position
and velocity of UAV through the dynamics measurement such
as gyroscope data and accelerometer data provided by the
Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). However, the dynamics
measurement error of IMU accumulates with time, so that
INS cannot provide accurate position for a long time. GNSS
can provide accurate position and velocity information to
compensate the INS navigation error, but its refresh rate is
only 10-20Hz. GNSS/INS integrated navigation system has
the advantages of both for absolute positioning[5]. Therefore,
UAV can obtain high-precision navigation information.
When UAV executes ground survey missions, due to the
complexity of the terrain in the unknown area and the
extremely weak of GNSS signal, GNSS signal is easy to
failure. In GNSS denied environment, the position and velocity
calculated by INS cannot be corrected by GNSS navigation
information. Therefore, the position and velocity error of
UAV will increase. Finally, it leads to the problem that the
navigation information is not available.
To address the above-mentioned problem of the increasing
error of position and velocity in GNSS denied environment,
many methods have been recently proposed. The first method
is to add additional sensors to compensate the INS naviga-
tion error[6], e.g: laser scanners[7], cameras[8], etc. These
sensors can effectively enhance the navigation precision, but
they will increase the cost and complexity of the navigation
system. The second method is to learn the error model of
IMU. The learned model is utilized to reduce the noise of
IMU dynamics measurement and improve the accuracy of the
position and velocity calculated by INS. For example, authors
use Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) to construct the
output model of the gyroscope of IMU[9]. The model obtains
the error characteristics from the previous gyroscope data
and compensates the gyroscope error. Although this method
improves the positioning accuracy in a short time, it cannot
adapt to the long-time GNSS denied environment. The third
method is the error compensation algorithm based on Machine
Learning (ML) [10-14]. This method is used to simulate
the error propagation characteristics of a single INS and
designs and updates the connection between IMU dynamics
measurement and GNSS navigation information by training
machine learning models with GNSS signal. For example,
the literature [10] proposes a GNSS/INS integrated navigation
method based on Recurrent Neural Network (RNN). This
method uses the memory function of RNN to estimate the error
of inertial navigation system when GNSS signal is unavailable,
so as to obtain continuous and reliable navigation information.
In addition, the literature [11] also proposes a deep learning
network architecture- (GI-NN) to assist INS. GI-NN combines

the input of model is multi-sensor dynamics measurement, e.g:
gyroscope, accelerometer and magnetometer, the measurement
data is sent to CNN firstly. CNN can quickly and accurately
extract data features and increase the overall training rate of
the model.

II. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
In this section, a compensation method for integrated navi-
gation system during GNSS denial is proposed. IMU dynamics
measurement error model is introduced and the reasons for
fusing CNN and BiLSTM are explained.
A. The proposed method
The method is demcomposed into two steps, which are the
training stage and the prediction stage, as shown in Fig.1.
When GNSS signal is available, the system is working in
the training stage, as shown in Fig.1. Specifically, wx, wy,
wz, ax, ay, az and mx, my, mz offered by IMU dynamics
measurement indicate the angular velocity, acceleration and
the distribution of magnetic field. INS uses IMU dynamics
measurement to calculate position PINS and velocity VINS.
The position PGNSS and velocity VGNSS offered by GNSS
are input into Error Compensation System to correct error
of PINS and VINS. Finally, the output of Error Compensa-
tion System POUT and VOUT are as the navigation infor-
mation of integrated navigation system. At the same time,
the CNN–BiLSTM model is trained to find the relationship
between wx, wy, wz, ax, ay, az, mx, my, mz and PGNSS,
VGNSS.
When the GNSS signal is unavailable, the system is working
in the prediction stage, as shown in Fig.1. CNN–BiLSTM
predicts the pseudo-signals Ppred and Vpred of GNSS based on
wx, wy, wz, ax, ay, az and mx, my, mz. The Ppred and Vpred
are input into Error Compensation System to correct position
PINS and velocity VINS obtained by INS. The output of the
integrated navigation system POUT and VOUT are obtained,
which can be utilized for navigation during GNSS denial.

B. The IMU Error Output Model
The article [9] indicates the error output model of gyroscope
and accelerometer. The output error model of gyroscope and
accelerometer can be expressed as follows:
˜wt = (I + Sg + ∆Cg) ∗wt + bg + ε,
(1)
˜at = (I + Sa + ∆Ca) ∗at + ba + ξ,
(2)
where ˜wt and at are the output values of the gyroscope and
accelerometer, respectively. wt and at are the real values
of the angular velocity and acceleration in the body frame,
respectively. Sg and Sa are the scale factor error matrix
of the gyroscope and accelerometer, respectively; ∆Cg and
∆Ca are the installation error matrix of the gyroscope and
accelerometer, respectively. In addition, bg and ba are the bias
vectors, which change with time. They are important error
parts of gyroscope and accelerometer, ε and ξ are random
drifts, which are usually regarded as zero mean Gaussian white
noise.
In the proposed method, the ouput error model of magne-
tometer is also need to be considered[15]. It is expressed as
follows:
˜mt = CsfCnCm(CsiB + Bh) + bm + ψ,
(3)
where ˜mt represents the magnetormeters output vetor, Csf
represents the scaling error matrix, Cn and Cm represent
misalignment error matrix and non-orthogonal error matrix,
respectively. Csi represents the soft coefcient matrix, Bh
represents the hard magnetic error vector, B represents the
the local earth magnetic feld vector in body coordinate system,
and b represents the bias error vector. ψ represents the system
noise vector.
C. The Proposed Deep Learing Model
From the above IMU output error model, we can get such
a conclusion: During the flight of UAV, IMU dynamics mea-
surement error is mainly determined by the bias ε and ξ drift
over time. At present, Long Short-Team Memory(LSTM) can
process time series data, but it cannot process long time series
data well. It requires additional training layers to improve the
prediction effect.
BiLSTM can perform bidirection learning on long-term
IMU historical dynamics measurement at the same time. It can

III. THE CNN-BILSTM MODEL
In this section, the structure of CNN-BiLSTM is proposed.
Then the calculation processes of CNN and BiLSTM models
are introduced in detail, respectively.
A. CNN-BiLSTM
IMU dynamics measurement are used as the input of CNN-
BiLSTM in training stage. They include angular velocity wx,
wy, wz acceleration ax, ay, az and magnetic intensity mx, my,
mz. When GNSS signal is available, position increment and
velocity increment are selected as the label of model training,
including Pdlat, Pdlon, V dn and V de, as shown in the Fig.2.
Data preprocessing includes data selection, length cutting and
normalization.

The model structure mainly includes input layer, CNN layer

layer, Dropout layer, full connection layer and output layer.
B. CNN
CNN can be effectively applied to time series prediction.
Due to its characteristics of local perception and weight
sharing, it can greatly reduce the number of parameters and
extract features efficiently. CNN is mainly composed of three
parts (Fig.4), including convolutional layer, pooling layer and
fully connected layer. IMU dynamics measurement contain
three kinds of sensor data. In order to construct the hidden
relationship between multiple measurement data and extract
the global features of IMU dynamics measurement, CNN has
multiple convolution kernels and two convolution layers with
convolution kernel sizes of 20 and 10 respectively. However,
the convolution operation of multiple convolution kernels in
each layer will produce high-dimensional features of IMU
dynamics measurement, so the pooling layer is used to reduce
the feature dimension.

C. BiLSTM
BiLSTM was first proposed by Schuster and Paliwal in
1997 for speech recognition tasks [16]. It is composed of
forward LSTM and backward LSTM. BiLSTM can use the
information of both forward and backward contexts to better
capture the bidirectional semantic dependencies. The basic unit
of BiLSTM is still LSTM storage unit. The structure of LSTM
storage unit is shown in Fig.5. LSTM storage unit includes
forget gate, input gate and output gate, which are represented
by ft, it, ot , respectively. Where σ is sigmoid activation

IV. SIMULATION AND EXPERIMENTAL ANALYSIS
In order to evaluate the performance of CNN-BiLSTM,
CNN-BiLSTM is compared with CNN-LSTM and LSTM.
Each model compares the prediction error of position incre-
ment and velocity increment. Pdlat and Pdlon are position
increment in latitude and longitude, respectively. To make the
results clear, the units of latitude and longitude are converted
into meters. V dn and V de represent velocity increment in
north and east directions, respectively. Then, the prediction
fitting degree of each model is compared under different GNSS
precision.
A. Experimental Conditions
All deep learning models are implemented with Tensorflow
framework and Keras interface. Keras is an advanced neural
network API written in Python, which supports fast experi-
ments.
Since the input of the experiment is one-dimensional time
series, the convolution kernel of CNN is adjusted to one-
dimensional. The size of Conv1’s convolution kernel is 20
, and the step size is 1. The activation function of CNN is
ReLU. The mode of pooling is Max-Pooling. The area sizes of
Pooling1 and Pooling2 are 4 and 2, respectively. The Dropout
layer is to reduce the occurrence of overfitting, the forgetting
rate is set to 0.01. Three layers of BiLSTM are added in the
model. Each layer calls 128, 64 and 32 neurons for calculation.
The full connection layer is used for regression output. Finally,
Adamax algorithm is used to optimize the model, Huber is
selected as the loss function.

V. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
This paper presents a new error compensation method for
GNSS/INS navigation system. It can provide a reliable posi-
tioning solution for UAV in GNSS denied environment. This
method uses CNN-BiLSTM to achieve fast feature extraction
and long-term sequence memory learning, and predicts posi-
tion increment and velocity increment of UAV to compensate
the INS navigation error. At the same time, CNN-BiLSTM
is compared with CNN-LSTM and LSTM. The experimental
results show that when GNSS signal is unavailable, the average
RMSE of the prediction error of CNN-BiLSTM is lower
than 22.25 % of CNN-LSTM and 31.65 % of LSTM. At
the same time, the average MAE of the prediction error of
CNN-BiLSTM is lower than 26.75 % of CNN-LSTM and
33.82 % of LSTM. No matter what GNSS precision is used,
the prediction fitting degree of CNN-BiLSTM is higher than
CNN-LSTM and LSTM. Further research will explore the
implementation of Integrated Navigation System in GNSS
denied environment and consider more complex flight paths
to improve the robustness of this method.
