Abstract—Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs), when used in a 
formation setting, can be more advantageous. The concerted 
operation of UAV formations has many potential applications, 
such as cooperative reconnaissance, formation combat and 
search and rescue in mountainous regions. Nevertheless, modern 
navigation systems for UAVs are not able to guarantee the 
precision of pose estimation when GPS is unavailable in complex 
environment. The IMU drifts of navigation systems can cause 
poor calculation accuracy of position, velocity and attitude of all 
UAVs in the group within dozens of seconds. To solve this 
problem, this paper puts forward a new cooperative navigation 
method based on adaptive Cubature Kalman Filter which shares 
the relative observations between the UAVs and fuses these data 
with direct measurements from IMU to obtain better navigation 
performance. The simulation results demonstrate the validity of 
the proposed method. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
With the development of the technology of Unmanned 
Aerial Vehicle (UAV), it has been possible to keep human 
beings away from various complex and dangerous situations. 
UAVs have been widely used in both military and civil field 
for reconnaissance, hazardous search and rescue missions, 
aerial photography, air-data acquisition, etc. Nowadays many 
applications of UAVs require that a group of UAVs work in 
collaboration in order to perform a certain task, since multi-
UAV formation can effectively improve the reliability and 
coverage compared with a single one. 
Precise navigation for UAV is a necessary condition for 
success of almost all the tasks mentioned above. When a group 
of mobile vehicles is required to fulfil a task, if same external 
landmarks are used by different vehicles for self-navigation, or 
inter-vehicle observations are made, the navigation method for 
each vehicle is not independent. In these cases, the navigation 
problem is not resolved by dealing with the pose estimation 
problems of each vehicle independently, but fusing complex 
information from different vehicles of the group, which means 
cooperative navigation [1]. The research about cooperative 
navigation system aims to improve the accuracy of pose 
estimation. In the previous work, the main research directions 
on this topic fall into three categories: 1) analysis of multiple 
vehicles formation models and navigation algorithms; 2) study 
of error model and compensation method of cooperative 
network; 3) research on formation configuration [2].

At present international and domestic experts have made a 
lot of research in the field of cooperative navigation and 
achieved many significant results, especially on the area of 
autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) [3,4,5] and mobile 
robots [6,7,8]. Amedeo Vetrella [9] proposed a cooperative 
UAV navigation method that combined differential Global 
Positioning System (GPS) and visual tracking information 
with a sensor fusion algorithm based on EKF. Johnathan 
Votion [10] devoted himself to develop navigation and 
reconnaissance capabilities for cooperative multi-vehicle 
system in uncertain environment. Giancarmine Fasano [11] 
adopted distributed multi-antenna GNSS, vision and MEMS 
sensors to improve the navigation performance of UAVs in 
outdoor environments. 
Typical navigation systems for UAVs consist of an Inertial 
Measurement Unit (IMU) and GPS. Unfortunately, GPS 
signals may be unavailable in complex conditions due to GPS 
jamming, lack of line of sight, hardware failure, etc. Since the 
process of obtaining pose estimates by simply integrating the 
measurements from IMU will lead to significant deviations 
over time, UAVs cannot only rely on IMU for navigation. 
In order to solve the navigation problems for UAVs in GPS 
denied areas, Rajnikant Sharma [12] proposed a cooperative 
methodology based on the principle of exchanging IMU 
information to constrain the pose estimation drift in the 
absence of GPS. Anusna Chakraborty [13] compared and 
evaluated the accuracy of navigation algorithms for different 
relative measurements. Studying on cooperative navigation 
between UAVs and mobile robots, Victor O. Sivaneri [14] 
aimed at optimal motion planning of mobile robots to decrease 
the navigation error of UAVs. Panagiotis Agis [15] developed 
a new solution namely PHIMTA, which is a hybrid INS/ 
pedestrian dead reckoning algorithm, and then combined it 
with the most advanced navigation algorithms. Research 
results of Lvtianyang Zhang [16] indicated that a UAV which 
is equipped with GPS can assist the other GPS-denied one in 
navigation. Moreover, James Russell [17] did the similar 
research in 3-dimensional space. 
The integration of measurements from proprioceptive 
sensors and exteroceptive sensors is generally achieved by 
Kalman filter, or some variants such as extended Kalman filter 
(EKF). On the basis of Rajnikant Sharma’s research work, a 
novel cooperative navigation method based on adaptive 
Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF) is proposed in this paper, which

navigation method proposed in the paper, some simulations 
are carried out here. Suppose that a formation composed of 5 
independent UAVs flying in a GPS denied area, and they move

angle β equals to zero). Therefore the problem of cooperative 
navigation can be simplified to two-dimensional. The initial 
state of each UAV is randomly decided and the motion 
trajectory satisfies the conditions about (9). It is assumed that 
the biases of all the gyroscopes of each UAV are constant. The 
process and measurement noise are both white Gaussian noise,

V. CONCLUSION 
In order to solve the navigation problem for multi-UAV 
formation in GPS denied areas, research about a new 
cooperative navigation method based on adaptive CKF has 
been carried out in this paper, which adds more kinds of 
external measurements into the typical navigation system to 
enhance estimation accuracy of INS error. The simulation 
results indicate that the proposed method is better than 
Sharma’s method with smaller estimation errors.
