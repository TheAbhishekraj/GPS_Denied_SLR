Quantum-Enhanced Multi-Sensor Fusion for
UAV Navigation in GPS-Denied Environments
Ahlam Moussa, Ilham Nait Him, Kacem Chawqi, and Yassine Salih-Alj
School of Science and Engineering
Al Akhawayn University in Ifrane
Ifrane, Morocco
(Ah.Moussa, I.NaitHim, K.Chawqi, Y.SalihAlj)@aui.ma
Abstract—Reliable navigation in GPS-denied or degraded
environments remains a core challenge for Unmanned Aerial
Vehicles (UAVs), as inertial-vision systems accumulate drift over
time. This paper proposes a feasibility study of a quantum-
enhanced multi-sensor fusion framework that integrates cold-
atom accelerometer-inspired aiding and NV-center-based heading
stabilization with conventional IMU, camera, and LiDAR mea-
surements inside a unified Extended Kalman Filter (EKF). The
proposed architecture combines high-rate classical sensing with
low-rate, bias-stable quantum corrections to suppress long-term
drift during GNSS outages. Using real IMU data from the EuRoC
MAV benchmark, both a standard IMU+GPS/VO-style EKF and
a quantum-enhanced EKF under injected GPS-denial intervals
are evaluated. The results show substantially reduced velocity and
position drift, extended holdover time, and improved error distri-
butions for the quantum-aided filter. Deployment considerations,
including power, cost, integration complexity, and scalability, are
discussed to assess the real-world feasibility of quantum-aided
fusion as a promising pathway toward resilient all-weather UAV
autonomy across highly demanding environments.
Index Terms—Quantum sensing, UAV Navigation, Sensor Fu-
sion, GPS-Denied Environments, Extended Kalman Filter.
I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have become indispens-
able tools for precision agriculture [1], environmental monitor-
ing [2], forest-fire detection [3], and public-safety surveillance
[4]. Their effectiveness, however, depends on accurate naviga-
tion in complex terrains and degraded environments, where
Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) such as GPS
often suffer from multi-path, signal blockage, or interference
[5].
In the absence of reliable GPS signals, most systems rely
on the fusion of inertial measurement units (IMUs) with
exteroceptive sensors such as cameras and LiDAR. While
Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) and Simultaneous Localiza-
tion and Mapping (SLAM) frameworks, such as VINS-Mono
and ORB-SLAM3, provide low-drift pose estimation through
robust initialization and filtering [6], [7], their accuracy de-
grades over time due to cumulative drift and lack of absolute
referencing. The performance further declines under low vis-
ibility conditions caused by dust, fog, or poor lighting [8].
Recent efforts introduce alternative localization aids such
as Single-Photon LiDAR [9] and geomagnetic-field-based
navigation [10], which exploit spatial magnetic anomalies to
reduce odometric drift. However, these methods still face
limitations in maintaining long-term accuracy during extended
GNSS outages.
A promising advancement lies in quantum-aided inertial
sensing [11]. Cold-atom interferometers provide long-term
bias stability in acceleration and rotation measurements, while
diamond nitrogen-vacancy (NV) magnetometers offer high-
sensitivity room-temperature vector magnetic readings [12],
[13]. Rapid progress in compact, low-power quantum sensors
makes their integration into small UAVs potentially feasi-
ble. Studies demonstrate that coupling quantum and classical
sensors could significantly extend navigation holdover and
mitigate cumulative drifts [14].
Building on these advances, this paper investigates the
feasibility of a quantum-enhanced Extended Kalman Filter
(EKF) framework that tightly fuses a classical VIO/LiDAR-
IMU baseline with cold-atom accelerometry and NV-diamond
magnetometry to achieve long-term drift suppression and
absolute heading stabilization under GPS-denied conditions.
The proposed EKF synchronizes high-rate inertial and vi-
sual updates with low-rate quantum corrections, employing
adaptive covariance gating, bias tracking, and dynamic sensor
confidence weighting to maintain robust state estimation even
during degraded visibility or magnetic disturbances. Further-
more, power-aware duty-cycling and selective activation of
quantum sensors optimize Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP)
performance, enabling continuous real-time navigation on
lightweight UAV platforms without sacrificing endurance or
precision.
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Sec.
II describes the overall system architecture, the classical
sensing components, and the state-estimation equations. Sec.
III presents the proposed hybrid quantum-classical fusion
framework and its filtering structure. Sec. IV outlines the
dataset, the simulation setup, and the evaluation methodology.
Sec. V reports quantitative results obtained under GPS-denied
conditions. Sec. VI discusses deployment considerations to-
gether with cost and scalability aspects. Sec. VII concludes
the paper.

III. PROPOSED QUANTUM-CLASSICAL HYBRID
NAVIGATION MODEL
A. Quantum-Classical Fusion Framework
The hybrid model integrates quantum sensors (cold-atom
accelerometer, NV-diamond magnetometer) with the classical
sensing suite through an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF). Quan-
tum measurements provide absolute drift-free references that
constrain the biases of the classical IMU and improve long-
term navigation accuracy. The fusion framework adaptively
weighs sensor contributions based on confidence levels and
operational conditions, producing a final navigation estimate
that leverages the strengths of both classical and quantum
sensing.
The block diagram in Fig. 1 illustrates the combined system,
showing the classical and quantum units feeding into the
EKF, which outputs the fused navigation solution to the
visualization/mission interface.

A. Dataset and Protocol
The V1 01 easy EuRoC MAV sequence [23] provides high-
rate IMU data (about 200 Hz) with inherent sensor noise
and precise ground truth (GT), used only for evaluation and
generation of auxiliary observations. Pseudo-GPS/VO updates
are generated by adding Gaussian noise to GT, supplied
regularly outside the denial window D and sparsely inside it,
mimicking degraded VO. VO/VIO effects are approximated
through noisy GT-based position updates rather than a full
visual SLAM pipeline. Both filters use identical initialization:
a conventional EKF fusing the real EuRoC IMU measurements
with pseudo-GPS/VO, and a quantum-enhanced EKF receiving
low-rate bias-stable aiding (accelerometric nudges and heading
corrections) with reduced process noise representative of cold-
atom and NV-diamond sensors.
B. Evaluation Metrics
Performance is measured with respect to ground truth (GT)
using the following metrics:
1) Position error ep(t).
2) Velocity error ev(t).
3) Holdover time required for the position error to exceed
the thresholds {1, 5, 10, 20} m within D.
4) Cumulative distribution function (CDF) of ep(t) over D.
5) Drift-rate histogram obtained from local linear slopes of
ep(t) during GPS denial.

C. Implementation Details
All processing is performed in MATLAB. IMU measure-

noise levels representative of reported quantum sensor perfor-
mance, keeping the pipeline lightweight while illustrating the

The normalized velocity error shown in Fig. 2 demonstrates
the immediate divergence of the conventional EKF once GPS
becomes unavailable. Its error increases almost monotonically
and rapidly approaches saturation. In contrast, the quantum-
enhanced EKF grows more gradually and remains significantly
lower over the entire denial interval. This sustained gap illus-
trates how bias-stable quantum-inspired corrections constrain
drift growth and stabilize the velocity channel when classical
inertial propagation becomes unreliable.

VI. DEPLOYMENT CONSIDERATIONS AND COST
ANALYSIS
A. Power, Size, and Measurement Fidelity
Optomechanical and atom-based quantum sensors introduce
notable SWaP penalties compared to classical MEMS de-
vices. Although hybrid optomechanical sensors improve mea-
surement robustness and bandwidth, their optical assemblies
require tight environmental control and laser stabilization,
making integration into compact UAVs difficult [24]. Their
higher power draw, typically 2-15 W during active operation,
also reduces flight endurance, highlighting the need for energy-
aware scheduling and compact optical packaging.
Dynamic duty-cycling and shared timing control help lower
average power consumption while preserving long-term bias
stability. Hardware-filter co-design (e.g., activating the cold-
atom accelerometer only during prolonged GNSS outages)
offers practical trade-offs between holdover performance and
endurance.
B. Cost and Scalability
Cold-atom accelerometers and NV-diamond magnetometers
remain more expensive than MEMS or optical sensors due
to precision optical and vacuum subsystems. Prior work on
low-cost UAVs shows that improved fusion algorithms can
offset hardware limits [25], [26]. Extending this idea suggests
modular quantum-classical designs, using miniaturized NV-
diamond or compact cold-atom units only when mission
precision justified cost, creating a scalable architecture that
matches sensing performance to operational requirements.
This approach also helps avoid unnecessary SWaP burdens
on platforms that do not require full quantum precision.

At the mission level, the hybrid system improves reliability

VII. CONCLUSION
The proposed quantum-enhanced fusion framework inves-
tigated the potential of improving UAV navigation in GPS-
denied environments by combining cold-atom accelerometers
and NV-diamond magnetometers with IMU, vision, and Li-
DAR sensing in a unified EKF. Simulations showed a 19x
increase in holdover duration and lower positional drift than
conventional methods, highlighting the potential of quantum-
aided navigation where GNSS is unreliable, while acknowl-
edging that the quantum sensors in this study were modeled
using reported performance parameters and Gaussian noise
modeling rather than integrated hardware. Key challenges
remained in sensor cost, power demand, and scalability, though
chip-scale atomic and photonic advances continue to reduce
these barriers. Power needs, typically a few watts, were low-
ered through duty-cycling and shared timing, while modular
architectures enabled more compact low-SWaP designs. Future
work will add quantum gyroscopes, validate performance on
real UAV flights, and explore adaptive filters with learning-
based covariance tuning. Together with continued advances,
these developments position quantum-enhanced sensing as a
promising path to resilient autonomous navigation in GPS-
degraded environments.
