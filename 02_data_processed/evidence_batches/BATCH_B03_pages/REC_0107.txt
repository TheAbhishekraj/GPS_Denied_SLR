Abstract—Nowadays, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) 
have been exploited in numerous civil and military aspects such 
as disaster management and border monitoring. Although most 
of these UAVs are equipped with Global Navigation Satellite 
System (GNSS) / Inertial Navigation System (INS) reliable 
integrated system for localization proposes, they are susceptible 
to lose their GNSS signals under some environmental and 
artificial challenging circumstances such as, flying in urban 
areas, jamming, and spoofing. In such complicated scenarios, 
the navigation solution will degrade rapidly due to the 
accumulated INS drift errors. Although various aiding sensors 
such as cameras, and radar are employed to mitigate the INS 
drift errors, their estimated positioning accuracy is still affected 
by some factors. This paper presents a nested optimal filter 
approach (G-H filter, Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)) for UAVs 
in GNSS denied environment, to enhance the estimated 
positioning accuracy by integrating the Radar Odometry 
(RO)/INS/MAG to sustain a reliable navigation solution during 
GNSS outage. First, the UAV relative velocity is calculated 
based on image processing approach from the radar range 
doppler map, then the G-H filter is deployed to improve the 
accuracy of the vehicle estimated forward velocities based on the 
vehicle dynamic characteristics to reduce the effect of 
unrealistic velocity estimate from the radar. These improved 
velocities are then fused with the Inertial Measurement Unit 
(IMU), magnetometer, and barometer via EKF to mitigate the 
positioning RMS errors during the GNSS signals outages. The 
experimental results demonstrate the proposed system’s ability 
to notably enhance the 3D positioning accuracy during the 
GNSS signal outage by more than 99% when compared to a 
stand-alone IMU dead reckoning solution. 
Keywords—Navigation, EKF, G-H filter, Radar odometry. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Over the recent years, UAVs have been exploited 
significantly in many applications, such as firefighting, 
rescue, downtown traffic management, first aid delivery, and 
reconnaissance. The merits of deploying such UAVs in these 
aspects can help in minimizing human exposure to hazards 
and risky situations. The majority of the existing UAVs 
depend upon their onboard GNSS/INS integrated navigation 
system to localize themselves. Although this system 
configuration has the superiority for providing an accurate 
navigation solution, it is still prone to lose its satellite signals 
due to signals blockage, attenuation, multipath effects, 
jamming, spoofing, and flying in natural canyons. In such 
severe scenarios, the navigation solution is acquired only by 
the INS prior to retrieving the GNSS signals. Therefore, the 
estimated navigation solution by the INS will deteriorate 
during a short period of time especially if it is low cost. The 
fusion of other aiding sensors is important for reducing the 
INS standalone drift errors. Different GNSS denied 
environments navigation systems have been proposed by 
many researchers in an attempt to enhance the estimated 
positioning accuracy. 
Visual sensors-based approaches have been proposed as an 
alternative navigation system for UAVs during the GNSS 
signal outages[1], [2]. The consequents of the evolvement of 
the onboard camera’s technology, are to have low power 
consumption, small size, and lightweight. In addition, it can 
provide valuable imagery measurements such as, color 
intensity and features, which contribute to enhancing the 
navigation solution accuracy. Despite such great benefits of 
cameras, their measurements are substantially affected by the 
brightness, lighting conditions, and featureless areas. 
Monocular and stereo Visual Odometry (VO) based 
approaches have been presented in many works to estimate 
the vehicle motion [3], [4]. While in [5] they used a 
smartphone camera (attached on the dashboard) and On-
Board Diagnostics II (OBD-II) to estimate the change in 
heading and car velocity respectively, then they integrated 
this information with IMU through a loosely coupled EKF to 
bound the IMU errors during GNSS unavailability. The core 
limitation of the monocular VO is the scale ambiguity [6], 
which can lead to degrading the accuracy of the navigation 
solution dramatically, while the main drawbacks of the stereo 
VO configuration are the need for sufficient base-line and the 
sensitivity to the lever arm and boresight calibration 
parameters variations. The monocular VO has been fused 
with INS for resolving the scale ambiguity issue and 
improving the positioning accuracy in [7]–[10].

Due to the advancement in manufacturing microwave 
technology, a new generation of miniaturized Frequency 
Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) radars have been 
developed, which makes them more promising to be adapted 
for UAV navigation. Many researches had been proposed 
lately to accommodate such promising technology in 
navigation as presented in [11]–[17]. Another RO based on a 
Synthetic-Aperture Radar (SAR) aided inertial navigation 
system is proposed in [18]. 
This paper presents a nested optimal filter approach G-H 
filter [19], and Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) [20] for UAVS 
in GNSS denied environment, to enhance the estimated 
positioning accuracy by integrating the Radar Odometry 
(RO)/INS/MAG to sustain a reliable navigation solution 
during GNSS outage. The main contribution of the proposed 
nested optimal filter system is that it significantly enhances 
the 3D positioning accuracy during a long period of GNSS 
signal outages, by correcting the estimated forward vehicle 
velocity from the RO using the G-H filter, based on the UAV 
dynamic model constraint (acceleration). This paper is 
organized as follows: Section II describes the system 
overview of the proposed algorithm and system structure. 
The experimental results are presented and discussed in 
Section III. Finally, the conclusions are given in Section IV. 
II. SYSTEM STRUCTURE  AND PROPOSED METHODOLOGY 
A. Hardware setup  
The quadcopter utilized during the experiment is a 3DR 
SOLO quadcopter, with onboard Pixhawk-2 autopilot based 
on MEMS IMU (MPU6000), and U-Blox GNSS. The FMCW 
radar is mounted on the Quadcopter belly and connected to 
Single Board Computer (UDOO X86) for data management 
and data storing. The full hardware setup is shown in Fig.1. 
 
Fig. 1. UAV and radar hardware setup. 
B. Target Detection from Radar Range Doppler Map 
The target detection follows [2], it is performed by 
convolving a Gaussian filter with measured range-Doppler 
map image from the radar, to depress the clutters, which may 
affect the RO UAV estimated relative velocity. Then a local 
maxima-based approach is employed with the global 
threshold to separate the cluttered noisy background from the 
desired features foreground as in Fig.2.  
The strongest 15 candidates are treated as detected 
targets.  The radial velocities for these detected targets are 
obtained directly from the horizontal axis of the range-
Doppler map after converting the pixel location to velocity. 
The forward velocities components for each target are then 
acquired by projecting these radial velocities by the radar 
tilting angle. The resultant forward velocity is then computed 
by averaging these components together for each epoch. 
 
Fig. 2. Target detection process from the cluttered RDM. 
 
The epoch shown in Fig.2 is an ideal case where all the 
clutters are removed, but that’s not usually the real scenario. 
Mainly the estimated velocity from the detected features 
which is based on simple averaging technique suffers from 
outliers. These outliers affect the total estimated UAV relative 
velocity. To improve the estimated velocity before fusion with 
INS a G-H filter is utilized which is constrained with the 
dynamic capabilities of the UAV.

IV. CONCLUSION 
 
Due to the massive spread of low-cost MEMS-based IMU 
in navigation systems nowadays; the navigation systems can 
maintain reliable performance during GNSS signal outages 
for a limited time period. This limitation raises the need for a 
different aiding system to bound the low-cost MEMS-based 
IMU drift during the unavailability of the GNSS system. 
 
This paper proposes an integrated navigation system based 
on radar odometry/magnetometer/IMU through EKF, to aid in 
bounding IMU drift during this GNSS outage period. To 
reliably integrate the information from the radar with 
magnetometer and IMU, a G-H filter is utilized with vehicle 
dynamic constraint (based on the limitation of the quadcopter 
acceleration) to adapt the filter parameters.  
 
The 
experimental 
results 
revealed 
the 
massive 
enhancement in the navigational system solution of the 
proposed method when compared to the IMU/magnetometer 
dead reckoning solution. The proposed system 2D RMS errors 
reached 1.5 m during 240 seconds of a complete GNSS system 
outage, while the performance of the IMU dead 
reckoning/mag integration reached thousands of meters for 
2D RMS error for the same GNSS outage period. These 
results prove the effectiveness of the proposed nested filter 
scheme (G-H and EKF) to integrate radar information with 
magnetometer and IMU to reach a reliable navigation system 
solution.
