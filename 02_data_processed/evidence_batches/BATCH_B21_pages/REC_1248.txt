A B S T R A C T
In GPS-denied environments, errors in angular velocity, acceleration, and zero-bias drift degrade inertial navi-
gation system (INS) accuracy over time. Limited high-resolution geomagnetic maps and magnetic disturbances 
further reduce navigation reliability. This paper introduces the gradient perception approach (GPA), a mapless 
framework that fuses geomagnetic vector gradient perception with inertial data. Using a multi-vector parallel 
guidance model and model predictive control (MPC) with state constraints, GPA generates stable heading 
corrections without requiring prior high-resolution geomagnetic maps or precise initial INS parameters, relying 
only on the discrete magnetic signature at the target. Dynamic fusion weights address model uncertainties, 
allowing graceful degradation to state-propagated tracking in areas with unobservable gradients. Evaluations 
with SuperMAG-driven magnetic storm simulations, short-term drone tests, and long-term flight logs show the 
GPA outperforms uncompensated predictive baselines. Monte Carlo simulations under uniform disturbances 
demonstrate terminal accuracy improvements of 78.19% and 95.89% over LTI-MPC and LTV-MPC, respectively, 
while empirical flight tests confirm its drift-suppression capability, mainly contingent on the observability of 
local geomagnetic gradients.
1. Introduction
Passive navigation methods, such as inertial, geomagnetic, and 
celestial navigation, are emerging as reliable alternatives for long-
distance navigation in Global Positioning System (GPS) denied environ-
ments [1]. This paradigm shift has garnered significant attention from 
the research community. Geomagnetic navigation systems can support 
long-endurance missions while offering enhanced security and stealth 
capabilities [2]. However, the accuracy and stability of traditional 
map-less geomagnetic navigation systems are significantly compro-
mised in regions characterized by unobservable geomagnetic gradients, 
geomagnetic anomalies, or magnetic storm disturbances [3,4]. Con-
versely, while Inertial Navigation Systems (INS) provide high precision 
over short durations and remain immune to external environmental 
variations [5], their errors accumulate over time, and their perfor-
mance depends heavily on the accuracy of initial parameter alignment. 
Consequently, researchers leverage the complementary strengths of 
geomagnetic and inertial navigation in integrated systems.

To enhance the performance of combined navigation systems, var-
ious matching strategies and filtering techniques have been devel-
oped. For example, improved correlation matching algorithms based on
contour constraints have been utilized to increase navigation accuracy. 
Similarly, Ding and Cheng [6] proposed a two-stage strategy consisting 
of coarse matching followed by contour-based fine matching to ensure 
robustness. Furthermore, to mitigate INS errors in long-range scenarios, 
while Luo et al. [7] introduced a geomagnetic navigation method based 
on an adaptive sequence hybrid filter, Chen et al. [8] proposed an im-
proved iterated closest contour point (ICCP) algorithm with a gradient 
extraction neural network model, and Tian et al. [9] formulated an 
iterated local search-improved particle swarm optimization to realize 
geomagnetic matching for mobile robots. Additionally, Quan et al. [3] 
introduces an inertial constraint based on the Hidden Markov Model 
(HMM) to improve the ICCP algorithm to resolve the degradation prob-
lem under conditions including multiple contours within the matching 
area. However, a fundamental limitation common to these approaches 
is their heavy reliance on accurate a priori geomagnetic maps. The 
difficulty of acquiring such high-resolution map data for practical

To reduce reliance on prior maps, Zhang et al. [10] pioneered the 
use of model predictive control (MPC) in long-distance geomagnetic 
navigation by utilizing real-time geomagnetic data. However, this ap-
proach depends solely on the predictive model, making navigation 
accuracy highly sensitive to the precision of heading calculations and 
rendering the system vulnerable to disturbances such as magnetic 
storms. Parallel to these control-theoretic advancements, significant 
progress has been made in managing system uncertainties through so-
phisticated strategies, including relaxed MPC for Takagi–Sugeno (T–S) 
fuzzy systems [11], adaptive dynamic programming (ADP) for nonlin-
ear time-varying systems [12], and saturated-threshold event-triggered 
adaptive control [13]. Recent active-perception localization methods 
show that navigation performance can benefit from actively adjust-
ing sensing geometry or perception direction according to observ-
ability and environmental uncertainty. Representative examples in-
clude uncertainty-aware motorized light detection and ranging (LiDAR) 
odometry [14], active environment-aware unmanned aerial vehicle 
(UAV) LiDAR-inertial scanning [15], and adaptive whole-body active 
rotating control for LiDAR-inertial odometry [16]. These studies mo-
tivate the view that localization robustness depends on the coupling 
among perception, control, and filtering. Learning-aided filtering has 
also been explored for INS error mitigation, especially with MEMS-
grade sensors. Safvat and Keighobadi [17] use deep-learning-aided 
filters to model nonlinear error dynamics, while learning-aided IEKF 
and visual-inertial fusion methods provide references for adaptive noise 
tuning, missing-IMU-data handling, and rate-mismatched sensor fusion 
[18,19].
Traditional map-matching algorithms, such as ICCP [3] and MAG-
COM [20], are often constrained by the challenge of acquiring contin-
uous, high-resolution prior geomagnetic maps. This dependency limits 
their applicability in unknown or unmapped environments. To address 
this limitation, mapless geomagnetic navigation [21] and Simultaneous 
Localization and Mapping (SLAM) [22] offer promising alternatives by 
operating without prior global maps. For instance, recent studies have 
explored interactive marginalized particle filters (PF) [23] and mag-
netic field SLAM approaches [22] to jointly estimate the magnetic map 
and the vehicle states. However, PF often suffers from particle degener-
acy during long-endurance missions, and SLAM frameworks implicitly 
rely on partial loop closures to eliminate accumulated errors, which 
is highly impractical for long-range, straight-line navigation scenarios. 
To eliminate this global dependency, mapless geomagnetic navigation 
has been proposed [24], which utilizes real-time local magnetic field 
gradients to determine heading and position dynamically.
Despite its potential, mapless navigation faces clear physical and 
algorithmic constraints. The primary difficulty is gradient vanishing 
in regions with subtle magnetic variations, which can induce heading 
ambiguity and amplify sensor noise or dynamic external disturbances 
[12]. Specifically, state-of-the-art deep learning models, such as Gradi-
ent Extraction Neural Networks [8] and Deep Q-Networks [25], exhibit 
remarkable localization capabilities but depend heavily on massive, 
high-quality training datasets that are typically unavailable in novel 
GPS-denied environments. Moreover, data-driven models frequently 
struggle to generalize against unforeseen environmental disturbances, 
such as sudden magnetic storms. Although adaptive and robust filters 
[7] can handle bounded sensor noise, they face divergence challenges 
when subjected to highly non-linear magnetic field distortions. To 
address the lack of lightweight, adaptive mechanisms in existing ap-
proaches, there is a critical need for mapless, computationally effi-
cient navigation approach capable of robust online compensation for 
geomagnetic anomalies.
To address these challenges, this paper proposes a mapless
INS/geomagnetic navigation system based on a gradient perception 
approach (GPA). Rather than relying on a continuous high-resolution 
prior map along the trajectory, the system uses real-time onboard mag-
netic measurements and the destination magnetic-field vector as ter-
minal information. The method integrates MPC with an ESKF through 
three mechanisms: (i) an online gradient perception model that con-
verts local declination-inclination variations into heading guidance; (ii) 
a gradient-driven dynamic compensation term that reduces the influ-
ence of time-varying magnetic disturbances in the predictive model; 
and (iii) an ESKF-based fusion architecture that constrains long-term 
inertial drift. The compensation mechanism is analytically bounded 
through the pseudo-inverse condition number and is evaluated through 
controlled simulations, a short-term drone test, and a long-distance 
commercial-flight data.
1. A mapless gradient perception approach (GPA) is proposed for 
INS/geomagnetic fusion. By integrating a multi-vector geomag-
netic guidance model into an MPC-ESKF architecture, GPA pro-
vides an application-oriented online compensation framework 
for heading estimation without a continuous high-resolution 
prior route map. Evaluations using a short-term drone test and 
a long-distance commercial-flight log indicate improved naviga-
tion accuracy and stability relative to the considered LTI- and

2. A dynamic compensation mechanism is developed to reduce the 
effect of geomagnetic anomalies and simulated magnetic storm 
disturbances. By estimating time-varying magnetic distortions 
online and applying a condition-number safeguard, the map

observable-gradient regions. Historical SuperMAG dataset super-
imposed on the World Magnetic Model are used to assess this 
mechanism under controlled storm-like scenarios.
The remainder of this article is organized as follows: Section 2 
introduces the fundamental theory of geomagnetic navigation and the 
MPC algorithm for navigation systems. Section 3 presents a combined 
geomagnetic and inertial navigation approach based on the GPA al-
gorithm. Section 4 analyzes and evaluates the method’s performance 
through various interference simulations and experiments using real 
data. Finally, Section 5 concludes the study.
2. Fundamental theory
This section establishes the theoretical foundation of the proposed 
GPA. First, the geomagnetic field model is introduced to provide the 
positioning basis. Next, the mapless navigation principle using parallel 
approach guidance is detailed. Finally, the MPC algorithm is formu-
lated, defining the state equations, operational constraints, and the 
objective function for robust heading control.

3. Methodology
This section presents the integrated architecture of GPA, which 
combines gradient-aware predictive control with an INS, as illustrated 
in Fig. 3. The INS first propagates attitude, velocity, and position at 
the inertial sampling rate. When a magnetometer sample is available 
at the navigation epoch, the measured declination and inclination 
update the local gradient matrix. The compensated MPC then solves the 
heading-control QP over the prediction horizon, and the first optimized 
command is applied. Finally, the geomagnetic position estimate and 
INS estimate are fused through the ESKF correction step.

3.1. Geomagnetic gradient perception approach
This section details the dynamic compensation mechanism of the 
GPA algorithm. Traditional geomagnetic navigation MPC methods typ-

3.2. Combined navigation systems state equation
When there is a slight alteration in the spatial distribution of the 
local magnetic gradient, the precision of the heading angle computation 
diminishes [33,34]. The proposed GPA algorithm uses a short-term 
interval to combine the results of the inertial navigation error equation 
with the position error from geomagnetic navigation using a Kalman 
filter. This makes the geomagnetic navigation system more accurate 
and stable. The state equation for the combined geomagnetic and 
inertial navigation system is calculated as follows:

sensors do not have sufficient time to accumulate significant drift. 
While the active predictive tracking of GPA introduces slight control-
induced deviations compared to the uncompensated short-term INS, 
it systematically outperforms all other mapless predictive baselines. 
This confirms that the longer-distance flight and storm-disturbance 
simulations presented in the subsequent sections remain essential to 
validating the cumulative drift suppression capabilities of the GPA 
framework over extended operational periods.

In the experiments described in this section, the methods are eval-
uated using actual measured data. The measurement device used in 
the study is shown in Fig. 14. The system integrates a u-blox MAX 
M8Q for GPS location (used as ground truth), an MPU9250 (which 
provides 3-axis magnetometer and 3-axis accelerometer/gyro data), 
and a ZYNQ-7020 for data processing and heading calculation.
For evaluation, long-term flight data were collected from commer-
cial flight MU2259, traveling from Xi’an (34.43◦ N, 108.75◦ E) to

the magnetometer was calibrated to correct platform-specific hard-

5. Conclusion
This study introduces the GPA, a mapless navigation approach that 
integrates MPC with an ESKF for operations in GPS-denied environ-
ments. By employing a real-time gradient compensation mechanism, 
the approach limits cumulative INS drift and mitigates nonlinear mag-
netic disturbances without relying on continuous high-resolution prior 
maps. Evaluations based on historical magnetic storm simulations, 
short-term drone tests, and long-distance flight logs demonstrate that 
the GPA achieves improved terminal accuracy and trajectory stability 
compared to standard LTI and LTV-MPC baselines.
However, the current approach depends on the observability of local 
magnetic gradients and the availability of target magnetic parameters. 
Furthermore, its robustness against high-intensity magnetic storms was 
assessed using historical SuperMAG data rather than real-world flight 
measurements during such events. Future research will focus on devel-
oping multi-sensor fusion architectures to address gradient vanishing 
in low-variation regions and will prioritize physical flight testing under 
active space weather conditions.
