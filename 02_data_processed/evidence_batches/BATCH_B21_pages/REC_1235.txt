Autonomous Positioning System for UAV 
Scene Matching Under GNSS-Denied 
Environments 
Shenao Dua, Chenshuo Maa, Pengyang Wua, Anxi Yua,1 , Dexin Lia and Zhen Donga 
aNational University of Defense Technology, Changsha 410073, China 
Abstract. To address the significant decline in UAV navigation and positioning 
accuracy caused by severe error accumulation of inertial measurement units (IMUs) 
in GNSS-denied environments, which severely restricts operational capabilities, we 
propose an autonomous UAV positioning system based on image matching/inertial 
navigation fusion. First, a Multi-scale Oriented Gradient Fusion (MOGF) image 
matching algorithm is developed to achieve rapid and high-precision matching for 
UAV scene imagery. Subsequently, an autonomous positioning model integrating 
image matching with INS is established to accurately determine the spatial 
coordinates of the UAV. Finally, a prototype system for autonomous positioning via 
image matching is designed and implemented on the RK3588 domestic computing 
platform, with field flight tests conducted using the DJI M210 UAV platform. 
Experimental results demonstrate stable system performance, achieving a 
positioning accuracy of 16.4 meters and a real-time processing rate exceeding 8.3 
Hz. These outcomes satisfy the requirements for high-precision and real-time 
applications. 
Keywords. UAV, GPS-denied, scene matching, inertial navigation, high-precision 
positioning. 
1. Introduction 
Currently, the most mature approach for autonomous unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) 
positioning is integrated positioning technology based on the Global Positioning System 
(GPS) combined with an Inertial Navigation System (INS) [1]. However, in complex 
terrains, under conditions of malicious interference, or during wartime when GPS signals 
may be unavailable, satellite navigation systems can experience denial or failure. Under 
these GNSS-denied conditions, INS errors accumulate over time, resulting in degraded 
positioning accuracy and consequently posing significant operational risks to the aircraft 
[2]. 
Addressing the challenge of autonomous high-precision positioning for UAVs in 
GPS-denied environments, researchers globally have primarily proposed two categories 
of solutions: traditional matching algorithms based on handcrafted feature descriptors, 
and intelligent algorithms leveraging deep learning techniques. Edstedt [3] introduced a 
Convolutional Neural Network-based cascaded matching network, achieving sub-pixel-
level scene matching accuracy through a coarse-to-fine two-stage matching strategy.

Building on [3], Literature [4] incorporated a Transformer module, utilizing the self-
attention mechanism to enhance contextual feature fusion and matching robustness. 
Zhang et al. [5] employs template matching with ResNet-based feature extraction for fast, 
high-precision matching. Hughes et al. [6] proposes an end-to-end FCN model using 
deep learning. 
However, despite the significant precision advantages of DL approaches, their 
difficulties in model deployment pose challenges for practical embedded UAV 
applications. Consequently, lightweight handcrafted feature methods remain prevalent. 
Mei et al. [7] refined traditional feature extraction strategies by pruning features sensitive 
to illumination and seasonal variations and optimizing the matching framework, 
significantly enhancing stability and matching efficiency in complex environments. 
Furthermore, Yu et al. [8] proposed a pose estimation method based on multi-feature-
point scene matching. This method fuses scene matching results with INS data to directly 
resolve the UAV’s full three-dimensional attitude angles and position information, 
thereby achieving comprehensive state estimation. To achieve rapid and high-precision 
matching, Ye et al. [9] proposed the CFOG algorithm based on HOG operators, which 
significantly enhances matching speed and robustness. To address heterogeneous image 
matching challenges, Yao et al. [10] introduced a novel descriptor combining anisotropic 
weighted moments and absolute phase congruency gradient histograms. 
In practical deployment environments, autonomous UAV positioning algorithms 
must concurrently satisfy dual constraints of positioning accuracy and real-time 
performance, yet existing methodologies exhibit limitations in reconciling these 
competing requirements [11]. To address the high-precision positioning challenge for 
UAVs, we propose an autonomous positioning method. The method employs an efficient 
and robust matching algorithm that initiates with perspective projection model-based 
preprocessing of real-time aerial imagery captured by the UAV alongside segmentation 
of satellite reference maps. Subsequent image registration using Multi-scale Oriented 
Gradient Fusion (MOGF) feature operator yields an affine transformation matrix, 
enabling computation of the longitude and latitude coordinates corresponding to the real-
time image's center point, thereby inferring the UAV's absolute position. Implementation 
via a hardware system architected on the RK3588 embedded platform achieves 
lightweight algorithm deployment, delivering meter-level positioning accuracy while 
fulfilling real-time processing constraints. 
2. Design of the System 
As illustrated in Fig 1, the system derives the real-time imagery, base maps, and platform 
attitude data. Subsequent perspective transformation correction determines the ground 
coverage area captured by the current sensor. 
The algorithm extracts feature points from real-time imagery, delineates regions of 
interest for matching, and defines corresponding search areas in base maps. Descriptors 
characterizing feature properties are constructed for detected points. Feature descriptors 
from real-time images are matched against reference base map feature descriptors within 
designated search regions, with mismatches eliminated through robust outlier rejection 
algorithms. Finally, precise geographic coordinates of the UAV are computed through 
geometric analysis of validated correspondence pairs.

3. Autonomous Localization Method Based on Scene Matching with MOGF
3.1. System-Level Image Correction
To address significant scale and coverage disparities between real-time imagery and 
reference satellite images, the system incorporates two techniques: geometric distortion 
correction [12] and predictive region-of-interest segmentation.
As illustrated in Fig. 2, the perspective distortion model establishes correspondence 
between 3D coordinates in the East-North-Up (ENU) frame and 2D projection 
coordinates in the imaging plane. This bidirectional mapping is mathematically 
represented as a homography matrix [13], enabling precise perspective rectification.
Similarly, the geodetic coordinates of the image center can be derived from aircraft 
attitude and altitude parameters. Following calculation of the coverage area based on 
camera optical characteristics, the relevant section is cropped from the large-area base 
map.
3.2. System-Level Image Correction

information. Region of Interest determination is guided by these feature points, where 
texturally salient areas demonstrate higher ability to match. Subsequently, the algorithm 
enhances the MOGF framework by replacing the ROEWA operator with Sobel-based 
gradient computation. Integrating Sobel operators with Gaussian smoothing yields a 
MOGF operator for robust feature descriptor construction.

4. Scene Matching Autonomous Positioning System 
An RK3588-based airborne processing unit was integrated onboard a DJI Matrice 210 
UAV, handling real-time data processing and communicating navigation outputs to a 
ground control station via data telemetry [15]. Final positioning results are visualized on 
the GCS interface. The system architecture is shown in Fig. 3. 
The system's data processing workflow as Fig. 4 initiates with real-time image 
acquisition via an onboard camera and synchronous attitude data capture from the INS 
module; these dual data streams are processed by the RK3588 SoC implementing scene 
matching and platform localization algorithms; finally, solved states and raw GNSS/INS 
data are downlinked through telemetry modules to the ground control station for real-
time visualization on its monitoring interface.

During high-speed flight or under high-frequency vibration, UAV camera images 
are prone to the "jello effect," which causes blurring and distortion, as shown in Fig 5(a). 
To resolve this issue, we adopt the following two methods:
1.Mechanical Stabilization: By mounting a stabilized gimbal, vibrations from the 
airframe are effectively isolated, reducing their interference on the imaging process.
2.Use of a Global Shutter: A traditional rolling shutter employs row-by-row 
exposure, which produces distortion artifacts from inter-row misalignment during rapid 
motion. In contrast, a global shutter exposes the entire frame instantaneously, eliminating 
this type of distortion at its source.
After implementing this combined approach, the image quality was significantly 
improved, as shown in Fig 5(b).

System-level image correction is highly dependent on the accuracy and real-time 
performance of the UAV's attitude data—namely, its heading, pitch, and roll angles. On 
one hand, inaccurate attitude angles can cause significant spatial misalignment between 
the predicted reference image generated by the algorithm and the real-time image. On 
the other hand, a low data update frequency cannot capture the instantaneous changes in 
the aircraft's attitude, leading to matching latency. Both of these situations severely 
impact the success rate and accuracy of the matching process.
To meet these stringent requirements, we select the DETA90 high-performance 
inertial navigation module. This module not only possesses high-precision attitude 
determination capabilities but also features an IMU data output frequency of up to 400Hz, 
which is sufficient to ensure the precision and real-time availability of attitude 
information. Furthermore, its built-in anti-magnetic interference function provides a 
guarantee for heading stability in complex environments. The key performance 
parameters of this module are shown in Table 1.

5. Field Test Results 
Field flight tests were conducted to validate the efficacy and real-time performance of an 
autonomous UAV positioning system based on scene matching in GNSS-denied 
environments. 
The test site comprised diverse terrain features including buildings, playgrounds, 
and vegetation. A predefined flight trajectory of approximately 200 meters was 
established, with all localization algorithms operating in real-time onboard. System 
validation included aerial imagery shown as Fig. 6(a), reference maps Fig. 6(b), and key 
parameter configurations shown as Table 2.

Fig. 7(a) depicts the platform's localized trajectory (blue solid line) versus raw GPS 
data (red dashed line) rendered on the ground control interface. Congruent trajectory 
trends validate stable operation of the scene matching-based autonomous positioning 
system. 
Fig 7(b) shows the matching point pairs obtained by processing a single aerial image 
frame with the MOGF algorithm. From these matching pairs, the latitude and longitude 
coordinates of the aerial image's center point can be calculated, which are then used to 
derive the UAV's spatial coordinates. 
Fig 7(c) shows the localization accuracy of the algorithm, plotting the RMSE 
between the algorithm's calculated position and the GPS ground truth over 40 
consecutive matches. The results demonstrate a high degree of stability in localization 
accuracy, with the RMSE remaining below 20 meters for the vast majority of samples. 
The average RMSE across all samples is 16.4 meters, indicating that the system 
possesses high accuracy. 
Fig 7(d) illustrates the system's processing efficiency. Initially, the processing time 
is longer due to latency in the input data. However, the average time for the algorithm to 
process a single image frame is only 0.12 seconds, corresponding to a processing 
frequency higher than 8.3 Hz. This demonstrates the system's high real-time performance. 
Collectively, these experiments verify high reliability, accuracy, and real-time capability 
of the scene matching system, significantly enhancing platform survivability in GNSS-
denied environments. 
6. Summary 
To address the navigation degradation of INS/GNSS integrated systems under complex 
electromagnetic countermeasures or terrain where GNSS signals suffer from interference 
and occlusion, this study proposes an autonomous UAV positioning system based on 
scene matching. The system employs a three-layer "preprocessing-matching-solving" 
architecture for end-to-end localization, utilizing a multi-scale fused gradient method for 
rapid scene matching to satisfy real-time and high-precision requirements. Hardware 
implementation adopts a domestic RK3588 platform as the core processing unit. The 
system effectively suppresses high-frequency UAV jitter and achieves synchronized 
high-frame-rate IMU measurements with real-time image processing. Field tests 
conducted on DJI M210 platforms demonstrate positioning accuracy of 16.4m with a 
processing rate exceeding 8.3Hz, confirming the system's high reliability, precision, and 
real-time capability.
