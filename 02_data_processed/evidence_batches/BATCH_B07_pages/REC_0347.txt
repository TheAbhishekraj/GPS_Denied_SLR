A Novel Three-Stage Robust Adaptive Filtering
Algorithm for Visual-Inertial Odometry in
GNSS-Denied Environments
Zhe Yue , Chengkai Tang , Member, IEEE, and Yuting Gao
Abstract—Visual-inertial odometry (VIO) has been widely
applied in the autonomous navigation and positioning of
unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and unmanned ground
vehicles (UGVs) in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-
denied environments. However, existing VIO filtering algo-
rithms have the defects of low positioning accuracy and
weak robustness, especially in complex and changeable envi-
ronments. Therefore, a novel robust adaptive VIO algorithm
is proposed here. First, the H∞criterion is introduced
into the popular cubature multistate constraint Kalman filter
(CMSCKF), which improves the robustness of the VIO sys-
tem. Second, this article designs a characterization method
to judge the uncertainty degree of VIO based on the limited
memory exponential weighting theory. Finally, inspired by the idea of the Institute of Geodesy and Geophysics (IGG) III
model, we further put forward a three-stage robust adaptive H∞filtering algorithm and test the performance with the
numerical simulation and the publicly available real-world Karlsruhe Institute of Technology and Toyota Technological
Institute (KITTI) dataset. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed algorithm has better filtering accuracy
and robustness.

II. RELATED WORKS
The filter-based VIO algorithms are computationally more
efficient with comparable accuracy [20]. Presently, many dif-
ferent filtering frameworks are used in VIO. And multistate
constraint Kalman filter (MSCKF) [21], [22], which is used to
solve the dimension explosion problem of traditional extended
Kalman filter (EKF)-VIO, is widespread and has been taken
special attention. Traditional EKF-VIO adds feature points to
the state vector and estimates them with the IMU error state.
Consequently, the dimension of the state vector will become
very large when many feature points exist. And MSCKF is
known to operate the standard EKF with some essential mod-
ifications on the filter structure, which adds the camera poses
at different moments to the state vector and triangulates the
3-D position of the feature points according to the historical
camera pose state [23]. Then, it uses the constraint of feature
points on multiple historical camera states to construct an
observation model and updates the state vector. Finally, if the
number of camera states exceeds N, the oldest camera state
and the corresponding covariance will be eliminated. Since the
number of camera pose is much smaller than the number of
feature points, the dimension of the MSCKF state vector is
significantly reduced compared with EKF-VIO. The historical
camera pose states will be continuously removed, and only
a fixed number of camera poses will be maintained [24],
which limits the calculation quantity of MSCKF. Therefore,
we conclude that MSCKF is a filtering method and can balance
the calculation quantity and accuracy, which has also attracted
extensive attention for its superior performance.
Nevertheless,
MSCKF
still
needs
to
reconstruct
the
3-D position of the feature points. And it is complex and
unnecessary for the VIO system whose primary purpose is
to estimate the vehicle pose rather than construct a 3-D map.
Therefore, to avoid the estimation of the 3-D position of the
feature points and simplify the MSCKF measurement update
module, Hu and Chen [25] proposed a modified MSCKF based
on unscented Kalman filter (UKF). The experiments show that
their proposed algorithm achieved good results and reduced
the computational complexity of VIO. However, the weights
of the center sampling points of UKF may be negative due to
the high-dimensional nonlinear VIO system. As a result, this
will make the filter unstable or even terminated [26]. To solve
this problem, Nguyen et al. [27], [28] introduced a cubature
MSCKF (CMSCKF) algorithm using the cubature Kalman
filter instead of UKF. They proved that CMSCKF could
get better filtering accuracy and numerical stability. Thus,
we also choose CMSCKF as the VIO filter algorithm in this
article.
Unfortunately, CMSCKF must meet the accurate system
model and perfect knowledge of noise statistics. In addi-
tion, the noises should be white with Gaussian distribution.
However, when these conditions are not met, the filtering
accuracy of CMSCKF will be seriously affected. In practice,
the VIO system is constantly moving and changing. Influenced
by the uncertainty of itself and the external environment,
the statistical noises are always non-Gaussian with unknown
characteristics and even may change over time [29]. For
example, motion blur and illumination change will make
the measurement noise of VIO change. On the other hand,
there may be some uncertainty in the system model. For
example, the actual model of the system may not completely
match the established theoretical model, such as model sim-
plification, parameter mismatch, and reliability of component
measurement information [30]. However, these situations are
inevitable. Therefore, the VIO system based on CMSCKF still
needs further improvement.
H∞filter introduces the H∞criterion into the above
filtering problem, which can minimize the H∞norm from
the interference input to the filter error output [31], [32].
It is not necessary for the H∞filter to know the statistical
properties of system noises, but it requires the noises to be
energy-bounded [29]. At the same time, H∞minimizes the
effect of the worst possible disturbances on the estimation
results. Therefore, it is relatively more robust against the
uncertainty of the system model. By combining H∞filtering
and CMSCKF, we construct the HCMSCKF algorithm in
this article to achieve a good filtering effect. However, since
the proposed HCMSCKF belongs to the category of H∞
filter in essence, it has the problem of sacrificing a certain
filtering accuracy in exchange for robustness. As a result, the
estimation results are relatively conservative. That is to say,
CMSCKF has better filtering accuracy but poorer robustness,
while HCMSCKF has better robustness but lower filtering
accuracy. Therefore, Sato and Toda [33] proposed an adaptive

IV. PROPOSED VIO ALGORITHM
According to CMSCKF, when the process noise and mea-
surement noise of the system is Gaussian white noise with
accurate statistics and the system model is accurate, it can
achieve good filtering accuracy. However, when these con-
ditions are not met, the filtering accuracy of CMSCKF will
be significantly affected. Unfortunately, in most applications,
especially in VIO systems, the statistical noises are always
non-Gaussian with unknown characteristics and even may
change over time. Even the system model will have some
uncertainty. Therefore, this article introduces the idea of the
H∞criterion into CMSKCF, which is named HCMSCKF
here. Then, as mentioned above, aiming at the shortcomings of
HCMSCKF, we further propose the three-stage robust adaptive
H∞filtering algorithm whose scheme is shown in Fig. 1.

perform a general boundary between the maximum energy
gain generated by the interference and the estimation error
under the known interference tolerance level. That is to say,
given a robust parameter γ > 0, we can find an estimation

C. Degree of System Uncertainty
In this article, we define the system uncertainty as the
following conditions: the system noise is colored noise, the
statistical characteristics are not accurately known, the system
model is not accurate, and so on. By comparing CMSCKF
and HCMSCKF, it can be found that CMSCKF has the
characteristics of high filtering accuracy but poor robustness;
on the contrary, HCMSCKF has high robustness but sacrifices
some filtering accuracy in exchange for the improvement
of robustness. Practically, if the two filtering algorithms are
combined to realize complementary advantages, the overall
performance of the filtering algorithm can be improved. Then
the combined algorithm will both have good filtering accuracy
and high robustness.

D. Three-Stage Testing
Given the features of CMSCKF and HCMSCKF, we intro-
duce a three-stage robust adaptive VIO filtering algorithm
based on the discussion in Sections III-A–III-C and the idea
of the IGG III model [38], [39]. Besides, we divide the system
uncertainty into three stages: no uncertainty, small uncertainty,
and enormous uncertainty. And different stages correspond
to different solving strategies. When the system normally
works, which means there is no uncertainty, the CMSCKF
algorithm is used to estimate high-precision filtering results.
Otherwise, the proposed HCMSCKF algorithm is introduced
to improve the system’s robustness. On the one hand, if the
uncertainty is small, CMSCKF and HCMSCKF are used for
filtering simultaneously, and the weighting coefficient is used
to optimize the filtering results. On the other hand, if the
uncertainty is very large, only HCMSCKF is utilized for filter-
ing estimation. In this way, the advantages of CMSCKF and
HCMSCKF algorithms can be integrated so that the system
has both high-precision filtering performance and highly robust
adaptive ability. And the weight coefficients of the CMSCKF

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS
The VIO system has been widely applied in the autonomous
navigation and positioning of UAVs and UGVs in GNSS-
denied environments. To overcome the shortcomings of the
existing VIO navigation algorithms, we propose a novel robust
adaptive VIO algorithm based on the idea of H∞filter and

weighted optimization. First, to not reconstruct the environ-
ment and calculate the 3-D coordinates of feature points, this
article constructs a VIO constraint model based on epipolar
geometry and trifocal tensor geometry. Second, the HCM-
SCKF algorithm is proposed by introducing the H∞idea
into the popular CMSCKF algorithm, which improves the
robustness of the system. Then, aiming at the limitations and
deficiencies of the CMSCKF and HCMSCKF algorithms, and
in order to further enhance the VIO navigation performance,
this article designs a characterization algorithm based on
the limited memory exponential weighting method, which
can detect the degree of system uncertainty. Subsequently,
a three-stage robust adaptive H∞filtering algorithm is further
proposed, referring to the idea of IGG III. Therefore, according
to different detection results, the corresponding weights are
set so that different filtering algorithms can be adaptively and
reasonably used. Finally, the effectiveness of AHCMSCKF
is verified by numerical simulations and the KITTI dataset.
The experimental results show that AHCMSCKF combines
the advantages of CMSCKF and HCMSCKF and has better
filtering accuracy and robustness.
Besides, the idea of AHCMSCKF can be further extended to
other filtering algorithms and application fields. It is noted that
the AHCMSCKF has some shortcomings. For example, it is
more complex with a heavy computation load when compared
with the CMSCKF and HCMSCKF. Therefore, future work
is required to reduce the computational complexity of the
AHCMSCKF.
