Robust UAV–Satellite Imagery Alignment via
Cross-Domain Descriptor Learning and
Homography Optimization
Yang Xiao , Yunbo Wu, Chen Yang, Zhen Qin , Ziping Yu, and Huilong Zheng
Abstract—Localization methods that leverage satellite maps
and their associated geospatial information are critical for
uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) executing time-sensitive missions
in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-denied environ-
ments. UAV and satellite images exhibit substantial cross-domain
diﬀerences, including appearance variations caused by tempo-
ral changes and geometric discrepancies between perspective
imaging and orthorectiﬁcation. However, existing methods in
the UAV–satellite setting often lack eﬀective supervision for
local feature matching, which limits the cross-domain adapta-
tion capability of feature matching models. Existing datasets
also provide limited support for rigorous ﬁne-grained registra-
tion evaluation. To address these issues, we present Co-Lo, a
calibrated UAV–satellite dataset. During dataset construction,
dense matching is combined with sparse keypoints to obtain
reliable keypoint correspondences. An initial calibration proce-
dure is then used to derive accurate ground-truth homography
matrices for evaluation. Based on these supervisory signals, we
validate the eﬀectiveness of the proposed weakly supervised
cross-domain adaptation strategy. In addition, a dual evalu-
ation protocol is designed to decouple algorithm robustness
(across texture variations) from sequence stability (along ﬂight
trajectories). Compared with representative sparse-matching
and semi-dense-matching baselines, the adapted models show
improved robustness in cross-domain matching. In texture-sparse
scenes, they also yield relatively stable continuous localization
performance.

odometry and SLAM [6], [7], [8] oﬀer reliable solutions in
GNSS-denied indoor or low-altitude environments, achiev-
ing stable, long-distance visual localization in open-sky
scenarios remains challenging [9]. Consequently, visual local-
ization methods that establish correspondences between UAV
imagery and georeferenced maps have gained signiﬁcant
traction.
Reference maps typically consist of 3-D models [10],
digital elevation models (DEMs) [11], or satellite imagery
[12]. Methods leveraging 2-D satellite maps—which assume
a nadir (downward-looking) UAV perspective and a planar
ground surface—are particularly advantageous due to their
rapid deployment, streamlined workﬂows, and lower costs.
These factors are vital for time-critical missions. Accordingly,
our work focuses on this satellite-based localization paradigm.
To minimize the discrepancy between UAV and satellite
images, UAVs typically capture images from a nadir view.
In this pipeline, the UAV is initialized with precise coordi-
nates, and subsequent continuous visual localization (CVL) is
maintained by establishing pixel-to-coordinate mappings [13]
or estimating homography matrices (H) [14].
However, further progress in UAV–satellite image registra-
tion and alignment remains constrained by two fundamental
issues. First, existing learning-based local feature match-
ing methods are typically developed using training data
with explicit geometric correspondences, such as supervision
derived from camera poses, depth information, or multiview
reconstruction. Such supervision provides strong pixel-level
constraints for local feature learning. In UAV–satellite registra-
tion, however, explicit camera correspondences are generally
unavailable, which limits the availability of cross-domain
supervision for training local matching models. Second, exist-
ing UAV–satellite datasets often exhibit varying degrees of
geospatial misalignment among UAV images, GNSS ground
truth, and satellite images. This geospatial inconsistency not
only aﬀects the evaluation of ﬁne-grained registration accuracy
but may also obscure the actual performance gains brought by
cross-domain adaptation methods.
To address these issues, this work investigates how dense
correspondences produced by dense matchers can be con-
verted into trainable supervisory signals in the absence of
explicit camera correspondences and pixel-level annotations.

On this basis, we construct the Co-Lo dataset. In this

more rigorous evaluation of ﬁne-grained registration accuracy.

second component is sequential evaluation, which measures
the projected localization error along ﬂight trajectories and

follows.
1) We present Co-Lo, a calibrated UAV–satellite dataset

2) We introduce a weakly supervised cross-domain adap-

pseudo-labels for local descriptor learning on repeatable

3) We further show that pseudo-homography matrices can
serve as eﬀective supervision for deep Lucas–Kanade

II. RELATED WORK
Some approaches [16], [17], [18] rely on preconstructed 3-D
models and estimate camera poses via the perspective-n-point
(PnP) algorithm combined with feature matching. However,
these methods entail high computational costs and have limited
spatial scalability. Other works [11], [19] incorporate digital
surface models (DSMs) for PnP-based localization; yet, the
scarcity and high acquisition cost of high-precision elevation
data limit the generalizability of these solutions. Consequently,
our work focuses on 2-D satellite map-based methods.
We review 2-D satellite map-based approaches, includ-
ing image retrieval-based and image registration-based visual
localization methods, and analyze their primary limitations.
A. Visual Localization Methods
Retrieval-based cross-view geo-localization methods [20],
[21], [22], [23] employ global descriptors, such as NetVLAD
[24], to retrieve the satellite tile from a prebuilt database
that most closely matches the current aerial perspective. The
database typically partitions the target area into a discrete grid,
using tile centers as positional labels. While computationally
eﬃcient, these methods provide only coarse localization, with
precision constrained by the spatial resolution of the tiles.
Matching/registration-based reﬁnement methods [25], [26],
[27], [28] typically identify a candidate region via global
retrieval or odometry, followed by pixel-level alignment within
the candidate region using local feature matching (e.g., Super-
Point [29] + SuperGlue [30]). However, eﬀective supervision
for local feature matching remains limited in the UAV–satellite
setting. Descriptor adaptation is challenging because most
learning-based local feature methods rely on explicit cor-
respondences. As a result, existing studies usually validate
localization methods by directly using pretrained models in
texture-rich urban environments. This may lead to performance
degradation in low-texture scenes, such as farmlands, grass-
lands, and deserts. Although [31] adopts a self-training boosted
matching method, this strategy mainly optimizes descriptors
associated with existing keypoints and is diﬃcult to extend
to more general keypoints. Therefore, we introduce a weakly
supervised cross-domain adaptation strategy that learns repeat-
able keypoint descriptors from dense correspondences, rather
than relying on the model’s own correspondences.
DLK optimization has also been explored to alleviate the
limitations of explicit keypoint matching. Some works [14],
[32], [33] use the DLK framework to iteratively optimize
homography parameters by minimizing feature-based reprojec-
tion errors. However, due to limitations in available data, these
methods are typically trained only on prealigned image pairs.
Such a training scheme is diﬃcult to apply to UAV–satellite
image pairs with perspective variation. We further convert
dense correspondences into homography matrices to generate
supervisory signals for image pairs, thereby supporting the
cross-domain adaptation of the DLK model.
B. Visual Localization Datasets
Cross-view retrieval datasets, such as University-1652 [34]
and SUES-200 [35], are widely used to benchmark the per-
formance of global descriptors in aerial/ground-to-satellite
matching. However, these datasets typically consist of dis-
cretely sampled oblique-view imagery and are not well suited
to evaluating continuous localization over ﬂight trajectories.
Other datasets, such as ALTO [36] and VPAIR [37], provide
nadir UAV–satellite pairs with high visual overlap. While
continuous localization can be approximated in these datasets
through image retrieval within a candidate range, the localiza-
tion error is intrinsically bounded by the sampling density and
overlap ratio of the reference images. For instance, DenseUAV
[21] uses an 80% overlap between adjacent frames. While
this improves retrieval performance, it introduces substantial
spatial redundancy and focuses on retrieval rather than the
eﬃcacy of the matching algorithms themselves.
A critical limitation of current datasets, such as UAV-VisLoc
[38] and AerialVL [39], is the geospatial misalignment among
the UAV viewpoint center, satellite images, and onboard GNSS
records (as shown in Table I). These discrepancies often
necessitate the use of overly permissive success thresholds;
for example, AerialVL employs a 50 m radius to deﬁne a
“successful” localization. While acceptable for coarse geo-
registration, such thresholds are inadequate for ﬁne-grained
localization where rigorous error metrics are required. At
the same time, larger error tolerances may make it diﬃcult

To address these gaps, we introduce the Co-Lo dataset.
We evaluate model generalization using discretely sampled
UAV–satellite pairs and assess continuous localization per-
formance using real-world ﬂight sequences across large-scale
satellite maps. Furthermore, we shift the evaluation paradigm
from image retrieval success rates to matching-based met-

homography accuracy (MHA). Using the homography matri-

2–10 m.
III. CO-LO DATASET
In this section, we ﬁrst provide an overview of the proposed
dataset’s characteristics. We then describe the construction
of UAV–satellite image matching pairs under conditions of
signiﬁcant cross-domain discrepancy.

A. Dataset Characteristics
To facilitate practical application, our dataset focuses on
near-planar homography-based correspondence rather than
6-DoF pose recovery. Fig. 2 illustrates the core components
of the dataset.
The Co-Lo dataset is divided into two tasks: algorithmic
evaluation and sequential evaluation. For the former, we
collect UAV images from public repositories and synthetic
data generated through realistic imaging pipelines. These sam-
ples span diverse geomorphological landscapes and altitudes.
By selecting multitemporal and multiscale satellite reference
maps, we explicitly model challenges such as seasonal tran-
sitions, varying lighting conditions, and land changes. The
dataset comprises 1000 unique UAV images, split evenly
between texture-rich and texture-sparse environments, with
an 800/100/100 ratio for training, validation, and testing,
respectively. Each UAV image forms four distinct matching
pairs.
For sequential evaluation, we use continuous real-world
ﬂight trajectories to measure localization accuracy. In this
mode, satellite imagery is selected to be as close in time as
possible to the UAV ﬂight date to reﬂect operational realities.
B. Dataset Construction
UAV Data Sampling: The source data for algorithmic eval-
uation comprises VPAIR and UAV-VisLoc. These sources
provide diverse environments and scenarios. For sequential
sampling, we use real-world ﬂight trajectories from AerialVL
and DenseUAV, applying rigorous alignment corrections to
resolve their inherent geospatial oﬀsets.
Satellite Data Sampling: To accommodate varying UAV
ﬂight altitudes, we collected satellite imagery across zoom
levels 18–21. Furthermore, to evaluate matching robustness
under diﬀerent textural and seasonal conditions, we gathered
multitemporal imagery spanning from 2017 to 2025. This
temporal diversity introduces signiﬁcant variations in land
cover, vegetation, and lighting.
Data Processing and Calibration: To establish reliable corre-
spondences under extreme cross-domain appearance gaps, we
use RoMa [40]—a dense matcher that fuses DINOv2 seman-
tic features with ﬁnegrained local features. RoMa produces

V. BASELINE MODELS
To improve descriptor adaptability to cross-domain imagery
and address the challenges of texture-sparse environments, we
perform enhanced training on the Co-Lo dataset. This includes
an XFeat-based descriptor enhancement model and a DLK-
based iterative homography optimization model.
A. XFeat-Based Enhancement
We use the lightweight XFeat [41] as our feature extrac-
tion backbone. The model comprises three primary modules:
keypoint detection, descriptor extraction, and reliability map
estimation [as illustrated in Fig. 3(b) and (c)]. The eﬃciency
of the keypoint module is achieved through a teacher-student
knowledge distillation framework.
The original XFeat is trained on MegaDepth [43], where
reprojections are derived from camera poses and depth maps.
Constructing such reprojections is highly challenging in
UAV–satellite scenarios. In this article, the point correspon-
dences provided by Co-Lo (see Section III-B) are used to
optimize the descriptor and reliability head, so as to obtain
cross-domain adaptive feature representations (XFeat*). Dur-
ing this weakly supervised process, the keypoint detection
module [Fig. 3(b)] is kept frozen.

VI. EXPERIMENTS
We evaluate the adapted models and analyze their perfor-
mance. Section VI-A describes the implementation details
and experimental setup. In Section VI-B, we perform a
comparative analysis of XFeat* and DLK* against several
state-of-the-art (SOTA) baselines. Section VI-C evaluates
the eﬀectiveness of the ground-truth homography matri-
ces and cross-domain point pairs provided by the Co-Lo
dataset. Finally, Section VI-D discusses the limitations of our
approach.
A. Implementation Details
We used XFeat* as the feature extractor, paired with Light-
Glue (LG) [45] for feature matching. For DLK*, an initial
transformation p0 [32] was required. In texture-rich environ-
ments, H0 was estimated by SuperPoint [29] + SuperGlue
[30] (SP + SG) and then converted to p0. In texture-sparse
scenarios, where feature-based initialization is unreliable, p0
was initialized by applying random Gaussian noise to the four
corner coordinates of the ground-truth homography projection
on the satellite image (with standard deviation σ = 10 px).
For matching-based methods, all input image pairs were
resized to 800 × 600. For DLK-based methods, images
were resized to 640 × 640. For detector-based matchers,
the keypoint detection threshold was set to 0.005, with a
maximum of 4096 keypoints, and the matching conﬁdence

formed using OpenCV’s USAC MAGSAC with a 3-pixel
reprojection threshold, conﬁdence 0.9999, and a maximum of
10000 iterations.

The performance of the cross-domain adapted models
(XFeat* and DLK*) was evaluated on the Co-Lo test set
(comprising 400 UAV–satellite image pairs, equally split
between texture-rich and texture-sparse scenes) using the
metrics established in Section IV. We mainly focused on the
limitations of zero-shot methods and the gains brought by
cross-domain adaptation. Inference was conducted within the
image-matching-webui framework. We compared our methods
against several representative baselines: SIFT [46] (a tradi-
tional local feature method); SP + SG, ALIKED [47] + LG,
and XFeat + LG (detector-based methods); and LoFTR [48]
and ASpanFormer [49] (detector-free methods).

D. Limitations and Future Work
Although the weakly supervised cross-domain adaptation
strategy improves descriptor training by establishing keypoint
correspondences, several limitations remain.
Correspondence Noise: When using the sampled RoMa
correspondences as pseudo-labels, the average reprojection
error is approximately 4 px. In texture-sparse scenes, the
original XFeat detects fewer keypoints. We aim to obtain
more keypoints. However, biased pseudo-labels may introduce
incorrect supervision for keypoint locations.
In this work, we freeze the keypoint detection branch of
XFeat. Our goal is to treat the sampled RoMa correspondences
as pseudo-labels and train the descriptor in a teacher-student
manner to mimic RoMa’s correspondence behavior. Under this
setting, the correspondence error mainly manifests as noise in
descriptor supervision. XFeat descriptors typically aggregate
local cues within an 8 × 8 neighborhood. As long as the
reprojection error (4 px) stays within this range, the descriptor
supervision still covers the dominant discriminative region.
The impact on descriptor learning is typically limited.
In fact, bias propagation caused by pseudo-labels is a
common challenge in weakly supervised or self-supervised
approaches. In future work, we aim to establish more
cross-domain keypoint correspondences for stable matching.
Reducing the reprojection error will be crucial for enabling
joint training of the keypoint detector and descriptor.
Practical Applications: Although DLK is robust in many
texture-sparse environments, it may still fail in regions with
almost no distinguishable features, such as deserts and open
water, or in areas with severe terrain height variations. For
real-world deployment, we will explore a more resilient
localization framework by fusing visual cues with inertial
measurements (INS/IMU) to improve reliability in extremely
low-texture scenarios.
VII. CONCLUSION
We investigate UAV–satellite image registration and con-
tinuous localization in scenarios where no camera-pose-based
reprojection can be established between UAV and satellite
images, and where the scene does not strictly satisfy a homog-
raphy model. To address these issues, we construct Co-Lo, a
calibrated UAV–satellite dataset that supports both pairwise
registration evaluation and continuous localization evaluation.
Based on this dataset, we introduce a dual evaluation protocol
that separates algorithmic matching performance from sequen-
tial localization stability, enabling a more rigorous analysis of
cross-domain matching methods.
We further propose a weakly supervised cross-domain
adaptation strategy that transfers dense correspondences into
supervisory signals for sparse feature learning. Speciﬁcally,
dense matches are ﬁltered and converted into sparse keypoint
correspondences for descriptor adaptation, while pseudo-
homography matrices are used to supervise DLK optimization.
This design allows descriptor learning and homography reﬁne-
ment to beneﬁt from cross-domain correspondence cues
without requiring manual pixel-level annotations.
Experimental results show that the adapted models improve
matching quality. In particular, XFeat* outperforms its zero-
shot counterpart in both matching accuracy and homography
accuracy, and also improves continuous localization perfor-
mance. Additional experiments with SP* further show that
the proposed adaptation strategy is not limited to a single
sparse feature model. In texture-sparse scenes, DLK* provides
competitive image-level alignment performance, indicating
that learned feature-map alignment remains eﬀective when
reliable sparse correspondences are diﬃcult to obtain.

approximately 20 m. As a result, retrievalbased approaches
produce localization errors that are signiﬁcantly larger than
matching-based errors, which are approximately 4 −5 m.
Under the severe viewpoint diﬀerences caused by low-altitude
ﬂights, reducing the localization error without introduc-
ing digital orthophotos (DOPs) or DSMs requires further
investigation.
