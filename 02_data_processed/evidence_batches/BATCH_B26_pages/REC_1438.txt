3D Motion Planning for UAVs in GPS-Denied Unknown Forest
Environment
Fang Liao1, Shupeng Lai2, Yuchao Hu1, Jinqiang Cui1, Jian Liang Wang3, Rodney Teo1 and Feng Lin1
Abstract— In this paper, a decomposition hierarchic on-line
motion planning approach consisting of path planning and
trajectory generation is proposed for VTOL UAVs to ﬂy in a
GPS-denied unknown obstacle-rich environment such as forest
and urban canyon. A closed-loop 3D path planning based on A∗
search algorithm is used to generate collision-free path and a
3D on-line trajectory generation based on maneuver automaton
methods is used to generate a collision-free reference trajectory.
The simulation and experiment on a VTOL UAV demonstrate
the effectiveness of the proposed motion planning approach.
I. INTRODUCTION
Autonomous navigation of UAVs in complex environments
has been an active research topic for a decade. The motion
planning problem for UAVs is difﬁcult since these vehicles
have fast, complicated and uncertain dynamics [1] and strict
payload limitations [2], they are compounded by the issues
of real-time navigation in 3D space and involve continual
interaction with the environment.
The
goal
of
motion
planning
of
UAVs
is
to
generate
a
collision-free
trajectory
in
real-time
for
kinematics/dynamics-constrained vehicles to go through a
three-dimensional environment with obstacles. Currently,
there
does
not
exist
an
algorithm
that
provides
an
exact analytic solution to such a problem. Indeed, even
state-of-the-art
approximation
algorithms
operating
on
a three-dimensional subspace of this problem space are
difﬁcult to compute in real time. Many of the algorithms
designed to solve this problem rely on a decomposition
hierarchic method, ﬁrst solving a path planning problem,
applying smoothing constraints, forming a trajectory that
conforms to the path, and using a control loop to follow
this trajectory [3]. The advantage of this method is that
any of the traditional kinematics/dynamics-unconstrained
algorithms found in the ﬁeld of robotics may be used for
the path planning stage, and it is easy to integrate the
motion planning algorithm with existing low level dynamic
controller.
The algorithms for path planning are evaluated in terms
of optimality, completeness and computational complexity.
As it is difﬁcult to optimize all these elements in the

discretization and search algorthm [4], [5], [6], [7], [8],
heuristic approaches [9], [10], rapidly exploring random tree
[11], [12] and artiﬁcial potential ﬁeld approaches [13], [14].
Trajectory generation problems are often considered as
constrained optimization problems. It solves the trajectory
that is physically possible to be tracked by an UAV from
one node to another subjected to dynamic constraints. An
exact solution is generally not possible in three-dimensional
space. As a result, approximation solutions are usually used
to ﬁnd a feasible or sub-optimal trajectory subject to dy-
namic constraints. The existing methods can be classiﬁed as
smoothing methods [15], mathematical programming meth-
ods [16], [17], [18], [19], potential-based methods [20], [21]
and maneuver automaton methods [22], [23], [24], etc. The
general idea of maneuver automaton methods [22], [23], [24]
is to reduce the optimization or search problem from an
inﬁnite-dimensional space to a ﬁnite one. It helps to signif-
icantly reduce the computational complexity of a trajectory
optimization. The commercial trajectory generation software
Reﬂexxes is based on this type of methods.
Since the computational time of deterministic and com-
plete algorithms grows exponentially with the dimension of
the conﬁguration space, most of the existing algorithms do
not provide an adequate solution for on-line (real-time) UAV
motion planning in unknown natural environments. Majority
of the existing motion planning methods only provide sim-
ulation results based on idealized vehicles and operational
conditions and do not discuss practical implementation.
In this paper, a 3D motion planning method for VTOL
UAVs to ﬂy in a GPS-denied unknown obstacle-rich environ-
ment such as forest and urban canyon is proposed. A closed-
loop on-line path planning based on A∗search algorithm is
used to generate collision free path with the shortest distance
from any current position to a given destination in the local
map of the UAV. Then an on-line trajectory generation based
on maneuver automaton methods generates a collision-free
reference trajectory and output to the lower level of control
system to control the VTOL UAV. This proposed motion
planning method has some inherent, implicit robustness to
uncertainties due to its closed-loop implementation.
Since we utilize existing techniques for sensing, map
building, path planning and trajectory generation, our contri-
bution is not so much to these individual ﬁelds, but rather one
of combining these techniques in a novel way to facilitate 3D
navigation of a UAV in GPS-denied unknown environments.

II. 3D MOTION PLANNING OF UAV IN UNKNOWN
ENVIRONMENT
Motion planning of UAVs in unknown environment is
faced with a large amount of uncertainties such as environ-
ment uncertainty, measurement noise, sensor limitation and
wind disturbance, etc. Moreover, collision with obstacles is
catastrophic for UAVs. Hence, soundness is a very crucial
property for motion planning of UAVs. A sound motion
planner is one that always guarantees the vehicle will enter
the goal region and stop there without hitting any obstacle
despite uncertainty in sensing and control.
As the majority of UAVs currently in production already
have a low-level tracking control system and the decom-
position hierarchic motion planning method is the easiest
one to integrate with the existing control scheme. In this
paper, the decomposition hierarchic method is adopted to
implement motion planning of UAVs in unknown obstacle-
rich environment. This method consists of an on-line path
planning that generates a collision-free path and an on-line
trajectory generation that generates a collision-free trajectory.
Both of them work together to achieve the soundness of the
proposed motion planning. The diagram of motion planning
of a single UAV is shown in Figure 1.
Fig. 1: Diagram of motion planning of single UAV
A. Environment Representation
In this research, the environment is detected by LIDAR
sensors. The point clouds generated by the LIDAR sensors
are used to build the 3D occupancy grid map surrounding the
UAV. 3D occupancy grid map represents the 3D environment
by dividing the environment into evenly cubes and labelling
each cube as free (w/o obstacle) or occupied (with obstacle).
The resolution of the occupancy grid map is a trade-off
result after considering the accuracy of the environment
representation, the physical size of the UAV and on-line
implementation.
As the environment is unknown and the computational
time of motion planning algorithms grows with the size of
the environment, to implement on-line motion planning, a
local but not global occupancy grid map is built based on
the sensed data. In the local occupancy grid map, a ﬁxed
area surrounding the UAV is represented by its corresponding
occupancy grid map and the rest area is represented by free
spaces. The size of the ﬁxed area depends on the sensing
range of LIDAR and the ﬁxed area moves with the UAV.
Figure 2 shows the local occupancy grid map on which the
3D A∗algorithm works.
Fig. 2: Local occupancy grid map.
To make the UAV keep a safe distance with the obstacles,
each occupied cube in the local map is expanded, i.e., the
neighbors of the occupied cube are all set as ”occupied”.
B. 3D Path Planning
Path planning is referred to the generation of a feasible
ﬂight path from a start position to a destination without
consideration of dynamic feasibility.
As A∗algorithm is a simple, fast, complete and optimal
algorithm, we use A* to search the shortest path from the
start node to the destination node in the 3D local occupancy
grid map. While A∗traverses the map, it builds up a tree
of partial paths. The leaves of this tree (called the open set)
are stored in a priority queue that orders the leaf nodes by
a cost function. The cost function f(n) is described by
f(n) = g(n) + h(n)
(1)
where g(n) is the distance travelled from the initial node
to node n and its value is tracked by the algorithm, h(n)
is a heuristic estimate of the cost to reach the goal node
from node n. For the algorithm to ﬁnd the actual shortest
path, the heuristic function must be admissible, namely, it
never overestimates the actual cost to get to the destination
node. One candidate for h(n) is the Euclidean distance from
node n to the destination node as it is physically the smallest
possible distance between any two points. In the following,
the 3D A∗algorithm developed in this research is given:
Step 1: Initialization: Deﬁne the start node as node s. Let
g(s) = 0 and the cost function f(s) = g(s)+h(s) with h(s)
the Euclidean distance from the start node s to the destination

node t. Set the closed set consisting of all occupied nodes
(obstacles) in the occupancy grid map and node s. And set
node s as its parent node.
Step 2: Node Expansion: Find free neighbour nodes of
the node n (n = s at the start node) from its 26 neighbour
nodes. Save the cost and corresponding parent of these free
neighbour nodes. Keep these free neighbour nodes in an open
set in the order of cost value.
Step 3: Node Selection: If there is no node in the open set,
display ”No Path” and exit from this algorithm; otherwise,
identify the node n + 1 with minimum cost in the open set.
Delete this node from the open set and put it into the closed
set, and save its parent node.
Step 4: Stopping Criteria: If the node n + 1 is the
destination, go to step 5; otherwise, let n = n + 1 and go to
step 2.
Step 5: Short Cut: If there is no obstacle on the straight
line between a node i (start from the destination node t) and
its parent’s parent, replace its parent with its parent’s parent.
Otherwise, let i = i+1 and repeat Step 5 until the start node
is reached.
Step 6: Path Finding: From destination node and its
parent and its parent’s parent, and so on, by using backward
induction, the shortest path from start node s to destination
node t can be found.
It is noted that the path generated by the A∗algorithm
may not be the shortest path on the global map since the A∗
algorithm works on the local occupancy grid map. However,
the computational time will be reduced and the completeness
of the A∗algorithm will not be affected.
C. 3D Trajectory Generation
As the path generated by the on-line path planning does
not consider kinematic/dynamic feasibility, it is difﬁcult for
a UAV to follow it without stopping. As a result, on-line
trajectory generation is required to connect the high-level
path planning with the low-level motion control of the UAV
such as the low-level motion control can control the UAV
with kinematic/dynamic constraints ﬂying along the path
generated by the high-level path planning.
In this paper, Reﬂexxes Type IV Motion Library [25]
based on maneuver automaton methods is adopted to gen-
erate 3D trajectory for UAVs. This trajectory generator is
capable of computing an entire time-optimal trajectory from
an arbitrary initial state of motion to a given target within one
servo control cycle (typically one millisecond or less) subject
to velocity, acceleration and jerk limitations. Figure 3 shows
the inputs and outputs of the position-based Reﬂexxes tra-
jectory generator. As Reﬂexxes trajectory generator directly
uses its outputs as its inputs of current state of motion for
the following control cycle, it is considered as an open-loop
control.
Since only kinetic time-optimality is regarded in this
on-line trajectory generation algorithm, all DOFs can be
considered as linearly independent, that is, decoupled. For
multiple DOF systems, one more step is to synchronize all
Fig. 3: Inputs and outputs of the on-line trajectory generator [25].
DOFs to the synchronization time and calculate all trajectory
parameters based on the synchronization time.
The main reason to apply this algorithm to the research
is that it generates 3D time optimization trajectory with
consideration of kinetic constraints and can be implemented
in real time. However, its drawbacks are 1) only symmetrical
velocity, acceleration and jerk constraints are considered
in this algorithm and UAV dynamic constraints are not
addressed. 2) obstacle avoidance is not considered.
As VTOL UAVs but not ﬁxed-wing UAVs are used in this
research, the ﬁrst drawback is not so signiﬁcant. However,
the second drawback must be solved here.
Since Reﬂexxes trajectory generator is open-loop and does
not consider obstacle avoidance, the generated trajectory may
cause the UAV colliding with obstacles. Hence, a collision
check on the trajectory is necessary to ensure soundness. As
a result, an intermediate element between the path planning
and Reﬂexxes trajectory generator, called local target gen-
erator, is introduced to carry out the collision check of the
trajectory.
The aim of local target generator is to generate a local tar-
get for Reﬂexxes trajectory generator such that the trajectory
generated by Reﬂexxes trajectory generator from the current
position to the local target is collision-free.
Since the path generated by the 3D path planning algo-
rithm consists of a serial waypoints arranged in a pre-deﬁned
order, the local target generation algorithm selects the way
points one by one as the local target input to Reﬂexxes
trajectory generator. To switch the local target from one
waypoint to the next one, collision check of the trajectory
from current position to the next waypoint generated by
Relexxes is carried out. If it is obstacle-free, the local
target switches to the next waypoint; otherwise, the local
target keeps unchanged. The ﬂow chart of the local target
generation is given in Figure 4. In the local target generation,
collision checking of trajectory is the most important task.
The following steps are used to carry it out:
Step 1: Reconstruct the reference trajectory using the
variables provided by Reﬂexxes. The reference trajectory

Step 2: Discrete the reference trajectory to obtain a serial

Step 3: Check if the cubes where these discrete points

the cubes are obstacle-free, it indicates that the reference
trajectory is collision-free; otherwise, it indicates that there
is obstacle on the reference trajectory.

As the update rate of the 3D on-line path planner relies on
the update rate of the 3D occupancy grid map which requires
long processing time and the 3D Reﬂexxes trajectory genera-
tor has to provide commands for low-level motion control of
UAVs in real-time, different time scales are required for the
path planning and trajectory generator. In this research, the
update rate of the path planning and local target generator are
set as 10Hz and that of Reﬂexxes trajectory generator is set
as 50Hz. All the software development including building of
occupancy grid map, SLAM, 3D path planning and 3D on-
line trajectory generation is under Robot Operating System
(ROS).
III. SIMULATION OF MOTION PLANNING
In this section, a VTOL UAV is used to demonstrate the
proposed motion planning algorithm in MatLab.
The simulated environment is a forest. The constraints
of the VTOL UAV are as follows: the maximum velocity
vxmax = vymax = vzmax = 1m/s, the maximum accel-
eration axmax = aymax = azmax = 0.5m/s2 and the
maximum jerk jxmax = jymax = jzmax = 1m/s3. The
start position is set at (25,5,1) and the target position is set
at (45,57,2.5).
Figures 5 and 6 show the reference trajectory (in green
line) and the real trajectory (in black line) of the UAV from
3D view and X-Y view, respectively, where the red cubes
represent the obstacles. The path generated by the 3D on-line
path planner is shown in blue line where the blue circles are
the way points that construct the path. It is observed from
Figures 5 and 6 that the path generated by the 3D on-line
path planner is an obstacle-free path. However, it is difﬁcult
for UAV to follow without stop at the way points as the
path consists of a serial of straight lines. It is also observed
that the reference trajectory (in green line) generated by the
3D trajectory generation subject to the kinetic constraints is
along the path (in blue line) and is collision-free. Moreover,
the real trajectory (in black line) of the UAV tracks the
reference trajectory well. Moreover, the reference velocity
and acceleration generated by the proposed algorithm and
the real velocity and acceleration of the UAV are shown in
Figure 7. This simulation demonstrates that the 3D motion
planning works well in the obstacle-rich 3D environment.

IV. FLIGHT TEST OF MOTION PLANNING
The VTOL UAV used for ﬂight test is shown in Figure 8.
It is with the three dimension size of 1.2m × 1.2m × 0.5m.
This quadrotor UAV is equipped with two LIDAR scanners
to detect obstacles in horizontal and vertical direction, one
ranger ﬁnder to measure the relative altitude, IMU to measure
Euler angles, angular rates and acceleration on the body
axis, Pixhawk to perform the low-level control, and on-board
processor Mastermind to perform high level control such as
path planning, trajectory generation and logic algorithms, etc.
The ﬂight test was carried out in a forest. Part of the
occupancy grid map representing the forest is shown in
Figure 9 where obstacle-occupied space is represented by
colourful pixels and free space by transparent pixels.
Fig. 9: Occupancy grid map of the forest environment
In the ﬂight test, the UAV ﬂied a loop from the start
position (0,0,1.2) to the destination at the same position
(0,0,1.2) via a serial given middle points. The reference tra-
jectory generated by the motion planning and the trajectory
estimated by SLAM are shown in Figure 10 where the red
squares represent the obstacles, the blue stars represent the
given middle points, the black line represents the trajectory
estimated by SLAM and the green line represents the trajec-
tory generated by the proposed motion planning algorithm.
It is observed from Figure 10 that the reference trajectory
generated by the proposed approach is collision-free and the
UAV tracks the reference trajectory very well in the ﬂight
test.

V. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, a decomposition hierarchic on-line motion
planning method is proposed for 3D motion planning of
UAVs in obstacle-rich forest environment. Since the pro-
posed motion planning forms a closed-loop with low level
control system by introducing the UAV position feedback, it
has some inherent, implicit robustness to uncertainties. The
simulation and experiment on a single UAV demonstrate the
effectiveness of the proposed on-line 3D motion planning
algorithm.
