Abstract-Almost all military systems and combat platforms
rely on GNSS to achieve military missions. However, they are
facing more and more intensive jamming and spoofing attacks.
Due to the rapid development of computer vision technology
and vision sensor, visual navigation and localization technology
have gradually revealed their advantages to solve this kind of
problem. In this paper, a new framework of visual localization
under a GNSS-denied environment is proposed. Firstly, a
novel
image
preprocessing method
based
on
information
entropy and granular segmentation is presented which can
significantly filtrate the Google tiles in the georeferenced area
to parsimony set to reduce VAV localization time. Secondly, we
introduce an end-to-end image matching method based on
convolutional neural network and image preprocessing to
realize VAV localization which has strong robustness and
localization
accuracy.
Finally,
the
proposed
localization
algorithm achieves experimental performance on the real
images captured by DJI Phantom 3 drone. The good accuracy
and fast execution time shown by experiments indicate that the
proposed localization algorithm can serve as a powerful tool to
keep the VAVs working continuously in the face of intensive
jamming and spoofing attacks.
Keywords-UAV; deep learning; visual localization; image
matching; image preprocessing
I.
INTRODUCTION
Safe navigation is the key issue for UAVs to complete the
combat
mission
and
improve
survivability.
Nowadays,
navigation for UAVs is usually using Global Navigation
Satellite System (GNSS) [I], [2]or integrated navigation
systems (INS) [3], typically composed of an INS and GNSS
receiver. Their data are jointly processed in an optimal or
suboptimal manner via some form of the Kalman Filter (KF)
[3], [4]. Although modern INS/GNSS integrated systems can
offer outstanding parameters and are adequate for numerous
UAV applications, their inherent weakness consists of the
lack of immunity of GNSS receivers to jamming. GNSS
systems are vulnerable to interference from other radio
signals, which results in lowered localization accuracy or
even is unable to locate the position completely. The problem
of jamming must be seriously considered especially for
military systems.
In the case of signal loss, GNSS data are prolonged
unavailable due to the jamming perpetually, which will
constantly reduce the accuracy of the navigation system.
Then the navigation for UAVs will only rely on INS.
However, the accumulated errors without GNSS correction

C.
2-channel network architecture and learning
With the availability of large scale data sets and powerful
computers for training, the vision community has recently
seen a surge in the use of convolutional networks for various
applications. It uses local receptive fields to extract local
features of images and reduce the training parameters of the
network, and it has the characteristics of weights sharing
which can detect the same features in different positions of
the image. What's more, it can remain the detected features
translation invariant.
The CNN architecture consists of three primary parts:
convolution layer, pooling layer and fully connected layer.
The convolution layer is a linear, shift invariant operation
that consists of performing local weighted combination
across the input signal. The goal of the pooling operation is
to control the amount of calculation by reducing network
parameters and prevent the phenomenon of over fitting in the
network. The fully connected layer is used to synthesize the
extracted local features to obtain the global information of
Images.
2-channel network architecture is a fast accurate CNN
[14], which is proposed by Simob. The input of 2-channel

V.
CONCLUSION
This paper
presented
a promising
image matching
framework based on deep learning for UAV localization.
The main contributions fall into two parts. First, using a
convolutional neural network to realize the matching of high-
resolution UAV images and low-resolution satellite images.
And an image preprocessing based on image entropy and
granularity segmentation methods is proposed to finish the
selection and formation of image matching pairs. The image
entropy can significantly decrease the UAV localization time
by eliminating satellite tiles with a larger information
entropy
gap with
the
UAV images. And
the
image
granularity was presented to solve the resolution gap
problem between UAV images and satellite tiles which can
improve UAV localization accuracy. Comparisons on the
experimental results with other localization methods, our
method has significant advantages in localization precision
and localization time. More efforts will be devoted to the
promotion of the UAV localization method in four seasons
(especially in winter) and night.
