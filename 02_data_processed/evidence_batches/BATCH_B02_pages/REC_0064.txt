Positioning and Navigation Approaches
using Packet Loss-based Multilateration
for UAVs in GPS-Denied Environments
ISSAREE SRISOMBOON AND SANGHWAN LEE
Department of Computer Science, Kookmin University, Seoul 02707 South Korea
Corresponding author: Sanghwan Lee (e-mail: sanghwan@kookmin.ac.kr).
ABSTRACT
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) technology has shown rising outstanding performance and has become one
of the essential innovations at present applications. In outdoor surveillance operations, such as geolocation
and navigation in restricted areas, an unmanned aerial vehicle such as a drone is able to accomplish those
missions efficiently with the assistance of a Global Positioning System (GPS). On the contrary, a drone
without that being said system is particularly limited to indoor surveillance or short-range applications due
to the absence of precise positioning capabilities. This research paper addresses the crucial challenge of the
non-GPS drone in outdoor surveillance scenarios and proposes a packet loss-based positioning approach,
wherein a drone estimates its current position by utilizing wireless packet loss from reference points packet
transmission by employing a multilateration technique. Moreover, we introduce three heuristic lightweight
navigation algorithms, leveraging packet loss-based positioning and enabling a drone to navigate toward its
intended destinations. The simulation results prove that by relying solely on packet loss computation, the
drone without a precise positioning system can identify its location and recover to its nearest destination
position without the necessity of complex sensors.

I. INTRODUCTION
The more technology constructively advances, the more it
becomes a crucial part of modern human life. An example
would be allowing a robotic machine to execute an imprac-
ticable task for human beings. Unmanned aerial vehicles
(UAVs), commonly known as drones or occasionally consid-
ered as flying robots, likewise have played a significant role
for humankind due to their extensive applications, marking
the progressive potential over the past decades [1] [2]. The
term flying robot emphasizes its distinct robot-like character-
istics as being capable of operating without a human pilot
onboard, either remotely controlled or fully autonomously
using predetermined flight paths. These remarkable features
enhance human life safety and improve flight efficiency,
leading to more promising productivity and cost savings in
aerial applications. Correspondingly, drone technology has
emerged in society as essential assistance, specifically when
the accomplishment by a sole human is very challenging [3].
Regarding a fully autonomous flight for UAVs, where
position information is crucial to operating, modern drone
technology employs precise geolocation accessibility from
the Global Positioning System (GPS) and offers beyond
ease [4]. By receiving real-time signals from GPS satellites,
drones’ precise position can be identified and enables secure
autonomous flight using the calculation of trilateration [5].
A capability to recognize its real-time location helps achieve
the feasibility of aerial operations such as rescue operations
and surveillance [6] [7]. Contrarily, drones without GPS are
limited to only close-range applications, mainly indoor oper-
ations where long-distance navigation is not a primary con-
cern. Similar to the case of traditional drones in a restricted
area where the GPS signals are either inaccessible or sig-
nificantly degraded. Signal interference from other sources
may weaken the signal strength and degrade quality, causing
unreliable real-time position information and impaired nav-
igation in UAVs. A familiar human experience is driving a

Previous studies have extensively remarked on this critical
concern of positioning and navigation in UAVs when GPS
signals are unreliable [8] [9] [10]. Universal approaches
including vision-based navigation techniques such as Visual
Odometry (VO) [11] and visual SLAM [12] [13], as well as
leveraging machine learning models [14] [15], have demon-
strated their effectiveness in GPS-denied environments, lead-
ing to improved localization accuracy and precision in nav-
igation and mapping [9]. However, both methodologies en-
counter challenges that can potentially hinder accurate po-
sition estimation. Machine learning approaches might face
difficulties in obtaining representative training data encom-
passing all UAV navigation scenarios, leading to performance
limitations in diverse environments. Additionally, visual-
based methodologies could encounter failures in recognizing
ambiguous image features and fail the reliability [16].
Another promising approach is through packet-based lo-
calization as it offers several advantages and shows the
potential of improving reliable robustness for GPS-denied
UAVs [17]. Communicating through wireless infrastructure
provides numerous advantages and demonstrates the poten-
tial to enhance reliable robustness for GPS-denied UAVs
[17]. For example, UAV communication enables direct wire-
less transmission between UAVs, eliminating the reliance
on infrastructure-based networks that may not be accessible
in such GPS-denied environments [18]. Kang et al. [19]
classify GPS-independent localization techniques for drone
fleets into categories and analyze the most suitable strategy.
The prior arrival times-based positioning technique, TDOA,
offers fairly positive results yet fails to promise precise
accuracy and confronts a considerable error [20]. In the
meantime, radio frequency (RF) fingerprinting is one of the
localization techniques utilizing communication signals [21]
[22]. However, RF fingerprinting typically necessitates the
utilization of sensor fusion such as LiDAR and has some
limitations [23] [24].
To overcome the limitations posed by GPS-denied en-
vironments for UAVs, we seek to develop an alternative
lightweight positioning and navigation methodology solely
relying on packet loss measurement. In contrast to conven-
tional signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) methods that often neces-
sitate sensor fusion and additional resources, our approach
prioritizes simplicity and efficiency as the packet loss rate is
more straightforward to measure and monitor in real-world
scenarios, thus eliminating the need for excessive complexity.
This design philosophy enables drone positioning and nav-
igation at the aerial level with minimal sensor equipment,
primarily focusing on utilizing packet loss information in
scenarios where ground-level access is challenging, i.e., dur-
ing rescue missions. In this manner, we anticipate achieving
a higher payload capacity, thereby offering benefits in terms
of design and maintenance since sensor integration and cali-
bration are no longer one of the complications.
This study builds upon prior work [25], exploring the
potential of packet-based positioning and navigation for
the GPS-denied drone and proposes sensors-free novel ap-
proaches. The concentration of this paper is on packet trans-
mission between the unknown-position drone and declared
references to identify the real-time position using packet
loss information. We hypothesize that packet loss infor-
mation retrieved through the data transmission procedure
can indicate an approximate distance between a drone and
each reference point, representing a distance measurement
similar to one with GPS satellite signals. Aligned with the
rapid decrease of signal strength with increasing distance,
our hypothesis is grounded in the theoretical understanding
that communication quality tends to degrade over greater
distances. Our assumption links packet loss rate and distance
as an increasing function, allowing us to estimate the pre-
cise distance based on the observed proportions of packet
loss rates. Respectively, by computing multilateration on the
measured distances to four known references, the estimated
position of the unknown drone can be determined.
For improving navigation capabilities in unique conditions
derived from packet loss-based positioning methodology,
i.e., the intermittent and unpredictable nature of packet loss,
where traditional methods may encounter limitations, we fur-
ther categorize GPS-denied navigational problems into two
scenarios: When a drone realizes its heading direction and
when entirely directional uninformed, and propose three dif-
ferent packet loss-based navigation approaches to ensure pre-
cision in surveillance: “Heading-Based Navigation (HBN)”:
Drone’s current heading reliance for the optimal navigation,
“Clockwise-Sequential Navigation (CSN)”: Sequential head-
ing adjustments to minimize distance differences, and “Pre-
Decision Navigation (PDN)”: Direction pre-computation to
minimize predicted differences. Subsequently, we compare
which delivers the optimal solution considering the naviga-
tion precision, the complexity of navigation, and the total
performance consumption time.
With the novelty of the proposed methodology of rely-
ing solely on packet loss calculation, we aim to offer an
alternative lightweight methodology for achieving precise
positioning and reliable navigation for UAVs in GPS-denied
environments. To support this objective, we address the fol-
lowing questions in this study:
• What are the advantages and limitations of using UAV
communication for positioning and navigation com-
pared to other existing approaches?
• How effective are the current packet-based positioning
and navigation techniques? Do they need any additional
sensor equipment?
• How is the performance of the proposed novel approach
under diverse environmental conditions?

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
The challenge of achieving reliable UAV localization without
the use of GPS systems remains a critical challenge. To
address such a problem, an approach like visual-based local-
ization has been explored and demonstrated to be practical
and effective. For example, Visual Odometry (VO) [11] [26],
and visual SLAM [12] [13] [27]. The effectiveness of precise
localization carried out by such methodologies has been
demonstrated. However, they often demand either complex
sensors or expensive computational algorithms, leading to
potential issues with power consumption and integration
complexity implications [28]. Similar to conventional ap-
proaches such as the extended Kalman filter (EKF) that
have demonstrated their efficacy in providing sufficiently
accurate localization. However, despite their effectiveness,
these methods inherently rely on the availability of sensors
[29] [30] [31]. Another viable approach, such as radio-based
localization utilizes radio frequency (RF) signal strength to
estimate the position of UAVs [32]. One radio-based lo-
calization is the RF fingerprinting technique employing the
analysis of RF signal characteristics for the localization [21]
[33]. Likewise, this approach offers numerous advantages,
yet RF fingerprinting faces some limitations. For instance,
achieving high accuracy with RF fingerprinting can be chal-
lenging, especially in environments with medium and high-
fading indoor wireless multi-path channels [34].
In modern wireless communication systems, localization
is achieved not only through the analysis of radio wave
characteristics but also by leveraging the transmission of
data packets within the communication network [35]. In the
realm of packet-based localization, this selective approach
has also drawn considerable attention due to its inherent
advantages in localization, similar to radio-based localiza-
tion, eliminating the necessity for additional infrastructure
while ensuring stable communication for precise positioning
and navigation via wireless communication [36]. The early
adaptation approaches employed data packets to estimate the
position of a transmitter [19] e.g., angle-of-arrival (AOA)–
Angle measurement at which the packet signal arrives at the
receiver, time-of-arrival (TOA)–Time measurement on the
packet transmission from a transmitter to a receiver, and time-
difference-of-arrival (TDOA)–Time difference measurement
of the arrival of packets at different receivers.
With the potential for precise localization through data
packet transmission, we investigate an alternative approach
that leverages packet loss information. Generally, the packet
loss phenomenon occurs in the wireless communication pro-
cess due to environmental factors, interference, and network
congestion, where these instances tend to have a detri-
mental impact on localization. Fu et al. [37] investigated
UAV communication latency and information packet loss
and analyzed the influence on the multiple UAV cooperation
localization, providing the relationship between packet loss
information and localization and valuable insights into its
feasibility and effectiveness. Specifically, numerous studies
have extensively investigated the phenomenon of packet loss,
contributing valuable understandings of its underlying causes
and characteristics [38]. In prior research, Aguayo et al. [39]
delved into factors such as distance and interference affecting
SNR, revealing a correlation with distance—though inconsis-
tently. Carpenter et al. [40] conducted a thorough analysis
of safety packet reception and loss in large-scale wireless
vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication, demonstrating a
decrease in packet reception ratio with an increase in the dis-
tance between the sender and the receiver. Similarly, recent
work by Tantayakul et al. [41] carried out an experimental
vehicular network topology, finding an average percentage
of packet loss that consistently increases for longer distances
within Wi-Fi network coverage. These findings provide evi-
dence, supporting our hypothesis of packet loss being propor-
tional to distance while behaving as an increasing function.
The novel packet loss-based reformation approach for
drone swarm is introduced in our earlier work [25]. The
objective of the original work is to leverage the character-
istics of packet loss information on the localization, similar
to the RF-based trilateration technique. By exchanging data
packets between drones in the swarm, we estimated the
formation using data packet loss and angular information
among drones. The hypothesis is that the angle between
drones and the estimated distance connecting drone swarms
can represent a drone formation. By comparing each angle
to the intended angle information, we could extract the drone
swarm formation that was the closest to what was planned.
However, the work only offers preliminary findings for the
proposed heuristic methods. And since it only addresses the
formation problem without defining the position precisely,
there is potential for improvement in localization.

A. PROBLEM FORMULATION
The novel concept of decentralized localization utilizing
packet loss information to estimate the distance between
drones was initially proposed in our previous study [25].
In this paper, we refine the objective more rigorously while
placing a primary focus on achieving precise positioning and
navigation for UAVs. To enable more accurate and reliable
positioning, we address the challenge of resolving unknown
positions by combining trilateration and triangulation, thus
so-called multilateration.
Fundamentally, packet loss refers to the failure of one
or more data packets to reach their intended destination
within a particular time frame during network transmission.
This failure of the transmitted packets occurs due to various
factors in general, such as network interference or limitations
in the network infrastructure [42]. In this paper, we focus on
distance as the primary influencing factor and utilize drones
positioned at the corner of a rectangular surveillance area as
reference points. Initially, a predetermined flight command is
given to a target drone, presuming it arrives at some position
based on flying time control. The reference drones establish a
wireless communication protocol, acting as transmitters and
broadcasting small packets to the target receiver. The packet
loss computation begins to estimate the distance once the
transmission is completed, for which a higher packet loss
value indicates a significantly longer distance. In this manner,
the estimated distance can be represented correspondingly
to a signal measurement from a GPS receiver. Respectively,
the estimated position of the target drone can be determined
by refining the estimations using the multilateration tech-
nique, using the intersection of spheres centered around each
reference point and the angle between the drone and each
reference point as represented in Fig. 1.
Once the estimation position has been defined, the naviga-
tion procedure can immediately proceed. We propose three
heuristic navigation algorithms to ensure that drones de-
liver to their desired destination: “Heading-Based Navigation
(HBN)”, “Clockwise-Sequential Navigation (CSN)”, and
“Pre-Decision Navigation (PDN)”. We consider two open-
conditional GPS-denied scenarios: one is when drones sense
their present directional orientation, i.e., drones equipped
with gyroscope sensors, and one is when drones thoroughly
have no direction information.

From (4) and (5), we express the multilateration approach
in a matrix form to solve an unknown position using linear

defined in (6). We have the matrix A in (2 · n) × 3 matrix

in 3 × 1 matrix representing ρ′(t) = (x′(t), y′(t)) we aim

the total number of matrix rows results in (2 · n) since the
number of cor(disti(t), PLRi(t)) and ∠i(t) are equivalent to
the number of reference points n.
The objective is to find a unique solution x satisfying the
linear system Ax = b. Theoretically, if a solution x exists
such that Ax = b for all n circle equations and n projection
onto line equations, there is at least one unique solution
determining the unknown position of the drone. In this paper,
we use Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to retrieve
corresponding (x′(t), y′(t)) [43]. Basically, we compute the
SVD of matrix A to retrieve the pseudo-inverse of the matrix
A, A† = (AT A)−1AT . By multiplying the pseudo-inverse
with vector b, x = A†b = (AT A)−1AT b, we obtain the
solution vector x representing the unknown point coordinates

C. NAVIGATION APPROACHES
To define the navigation proceeding more precisely, we adopt
a PID control approach referring to the control regulating
the difference δ′(t) of the target drone and subsequently
adjusting the control to minimize this difference. From
δ′(t) = ρ′(t) −ϕ, the difference δ′(t) can be represented
in two dimensional space as δ′(t) = (△x′(t), △y′(t)) =
((x−x′(t)), (y−y′(t))). In this paper, the velocity command
for each procedure is adjusted based on the accumulated dif-

δ′(t) for each round of navigation, where a small k offers a
small time consumption of algorithm computation.

A. ARCHITECTURE MODEL AND PARAMETERS
To capture the dynamics of the positioning and navigation
procedure in an ordered manner, we employ a discrete event
simulation (DES) model to simulate a demonstration. In
this manner, each is considered a discrete event and occurs
sequentially at a corresponding discrete time. Our earlier
work has previously implemented DES into a simulation
[25]. Thus, we refine the model more rigorously to satisfy the
purposes of this paper. The simulation workflow is illustrated
in Fig. 4, and four states of events based on the operation of
the drone are defined as follows:
• MoveEvent: Declare the initial moving of the drone
where it starts flying under the command C(t = 0 s, k =
1) without perceiving its current location.
• PositioningEvent: Packet transmission occurs be-
tween the drone and every reference point ∀i ∈P. The
distance correlation cor(dist(i, t), PLRi(t)) is employed
to resolve ρ′(t) with multilateration technique.
• NavigateEvent: Navigate the drone to the desired
destination by determining dc(t+tc) for each event from
our proposed algorithms: “HBN”, “CSN”, and “PDN”.
• DoneEvent: The navigation ends when the position of
the drones is satisfied by the threshold τ, ∥δ′(t)∥≤τ.

SVD is implemented through the Java Matrix (JAMA) linear
algebra library [45]. It is important to emphasize that the

V. DISCUSSION
This section discusses the limitations and challenges of rely-
ing solely on packet loss-based positioning and navigation.
Our focus includes discussing the constraints posed by the
limited range of packet transmission, the influence of envi-
ronmental factors, and the underlying assumption regarding
the correlation between packet loss information and the true
distance. Additionally, we explore practical considerations
and potential research extensions in future studies. This in-
volves emphasizing the significance of measuring reference
points’ positions and acknowledging the importance of accu-
rate range estimation in UAV applications.
A. LIMITATIONS AND CHALLENGES
Theoretically, a limited packet transmission range directly
impacts the effectiveness of position estimation where the
more disti(t) between any reference point i
∈
P, the
less accuracy of the reliable estimation of ρ(t). Given that
disti(t) > rmax, the estimation position ρ′(t) is no longer
considered accurate since rmax represents the range at which
packet loss is at 100% or all transmitted packets are assumed
lost such that PLRi(t) = 1. Therefore, the estimated distance
beyond rmax is not likely to be valid. For example, for
PLRi(t) = 1 where disti(t)̸ = rmax, denoting (1 · rmax) m
packet loss-based distancing, the corresponding radius of
circle equation in (1) is inaccurate. As a result, some solu-
tions x may yield two or more possible coordinate points,
indicating non-unique intersections on radius PLRi(t) for
∀i ∈P. Moreover, there may be some cases in which no
solution x satisfies the linear system Ax = b, indicating
there is no absolute intersection point coordinate. For this
reason, it is necessary to determine rmax wisely, resembling
the intricacies of communication systems in practical settings
and various environmental conditions.
In line with our underlying assumption, wherein the cor-
relation between packet loss information and actual distance
is characterized by the value of rmax for normalization, it
is important to highlight our intent to preserve the linear
relationship such that we can represent the packet loss rate
as an approximation of the actual distance. In future research,
employing linear regression techniques to model and validate
this correlation would be a promising approach as it provides
more robustness for estimation, potentially enhancing the ac-
curacy and reliability of positioning and navigation methods.
Furthermore, it is crucial to acknowledge that packet loss
is not exclusively determined by distance as it is subject to be
influenced by various environmental factors within the wire-
less communication environment, i.e., signal interference,
network propagation characteristics, or the level of network
congestion. Therefore, it becomes essential to recognize the
significance of these factors impacting packet loss rates as
they directly affect the precision and reliability of positioning
methods. Hence, it is apparent that our proposed approach,
while showing promise in simulations, may encounter prac-
tical challenges in real-world settings due to the complexities
introduced by the multifaceted nature of wireless communi-
cation environments. Consequently, there is a need for further
research and practical implementation to refine and adapt the
algorithm for real-world applications.
B. PRACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS
Fundamentally, the effective operation of the multilateration
approach hinges on the constant precision of the reference
points’ positions, as any deviation or loss of accuracy can
introduce errors in determining the target drone’s position.
Emphasizing the significance of positional accuracy of P, our
assumption of fixed αi for ∀i ∈P at every time t is given
to ensure optimal results. However, in real-world scenarios
marked by diverse conditions, it becomes critical to imple-
ment practical measures guaranteeing the consistent and ac-
curate positions of P, thus enabling more reliable positioning
and navigation for our approaches. In future developments,
incorporating strategies such as real-time monitoring or peri-
odic recalibration needs to be further explored to enhance the

VI. CONCLUSION
Locating UAVs in GPS-denied environments, such as indoors
or in highly dense areas, remains a critical challenge for
such a surveillance operation. To address this difficulty, this
paper emphasizes utilizing the packet loss pattern indication
to the correlation of distance between UAVs, introducing
an alternative novel packet loss-based positioning technique
with the principles of multilateration. By combining trian-
gulation and trilateration with solely packet loss information
reliance, we approximate the estimated position of the UAV
with no access to GPS and further propose three heuris-
tic navigation algorithms based on packet loss-based posi-
tioning: “Heading-Based Navigation (HBN)”, “Clockwise-
Sequential Navigation (CSN)”, and “Pre-Decision Naviga-
tion (PDN)”. Through our comprehensive simulations, the
results indicate the achievement of practical navigation where
“HBN” exhibits the best performance on navigation among
three alternatives due to its ability to utilize current heading
directional degree information. In scenarios where angular
information is unavailable, both “CSN” and “PDN” likewise
offer feasible navigation solutions. However, the effective-
ness depends on the proposed intention of operation involv-
ing significant trade-offs between the two methods, which we
have analyzed and discussed in detail in the study.
In conclusion, we achieved our objective of developing al-
ternative lightweight positioning and navigation approaches
for UAVs without GPS information to simplify the complex-
ity associated with UAV configuration, resulting in more effi-
ciency in such a surveillance operation. In future works, our
focus will shift towards investigating more robust techniques,
such as employing linear regression to enhance reliability and
robustness in establishing the relationship between packet
loss information and the actual distance, thereby contributing
to more accurate positioning and navigation methodologies.
Correspondingly, we aim to further enhance the practicality
of our novel positioning approach by implementing it in real-
world settings, considering influence factors such as line of
sight (LOS) and environmental necessities. Additionally, we
intend to apply the proposed heuristic navigation algorithms
to a variety of scenarios while considering realistic environ-
ments for a comprehensive evaluation of their effectiveness
and adaptability. By pursuing these future works, we hope
to anticipate making significant contributions to the field of
UAV positioning and navigation.
