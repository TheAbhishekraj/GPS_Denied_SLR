UAV Navigation With Monocular Visual Inertial
Odometry Under GNSS-Denied Environment
Haolong Luo, Guangyun Li , Danping Zou , Member, IEEE, Kailin Li, Xueqiang Li, and Zidi Yang
Abstract— In global satellite navigation system (GNSS)-denied
environments, unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation based
on visual–inertial odometry (VIO) has been widely studied.
However, existing VIO methods still suffer from some practi-
cal problems, such as image enhancement oversaturation and
unreasonable weighting in backend optimization. Therefore, this
article presents monocular VIO with point-line fusion and back-
end adaptive optimization to improve the positioning accuracy
and robustness of UAV navigation systems. In the front end,
we proposed an adaptive gamma image correction algorithm
for image preprocessing to avoid image oversaturation, which
is more conducive to image extraction and matching. Instead of
the traditional line segment detector (LSD) line feature extraction
algorithm, we employed an improved EDLines algorithm to
enhance the efficiency of line feature extraction, better meeting
the high dynamic real-time requirements of UAV. In the backend,
we proposed a tightly coupled nonlinear adaptive optimization
method based on a two-step approach to address the issue of
unreasonable static weights. In the first step, we established
a factor graph model and performed the first nonlinear opti-
mization based on a priori visual weights. In the second step,
we calculated the reprojection error and established a functional
model that examines the relationship between the reprojection
error and the information matrix. We updated the information
matrix using the reprojection error to adaptively adjust the
weights of the point features and line features in real time. Finally,
we performed a second nonlinear reoptimization. The proposed
method was compared with the monocular visual-inertial system
(VINS-MONO) (Qin et al. 2018) and point and line features
(PL)-VINS (Fu et al. 2020) methods, the experimental results
showing that the positioning accuracy of the proposed method
on the public EuRoc dataset (Burri et al. 2016) improved by an
average of 32.3% compared with the PL-VINS method, and by an
average of 33.8% in three real-world scenarios under changing
illumination, weak texture, and large-scale complex scenarios.
The results demonstrated that the proposed method exhibited
better robustness and higher positioning accuracy in various
complex environments.

I. INTRODUCTION
G
LOBAL satellite navigation system (GNSS) is widely
used in fields such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)
navigation, autonomous driving, smart cities, surveying, and
the military [1], [2], [3]. In outdoor environments with good
GNSS satellite signals, UAVs can achieve stable positioning
and navigation. However, GNSS signals can be rendered
vulnerable and unavailable in GNSS-denied environments such
as indoor and underground spaces. In recent years, UAV
navigation in GNSS-denied environments based on sensors
such as visual, inertial measurement unit (IMU), and ultra-
wideband (UWB) has been extensively studied [4], [5], [6].
However, visual sensors can exhibit certain disadvantages—
such as scale errors and sensitivity to light changes—and IMU
measurement errors can accumulate over time. Consequently,
multisensor fusion technologies have become the mainstream
for UAV navigation [7], [8], [9]. A visual sensor can correct
the cumulative errors of IMUs, and an IMU sensor can
provide accurate attitude information and scale recovery for
vision. Therefore, visual/inertial simultaneous localization and
mapping (SLAM) has been widely applied in UAV navigation
for UAVs. Among them, monocular visual-inertial SLAM
technology, such as monocular visual-inertial system (VINS-
MONO) [7], is widely used owing to its low cost and
low power consumption. However, monocular visual-inertial
SLAM technology based on point features is not particularly
robust and can be sensitive to illumination changes, making
it difficult to achieve high-precision reliable positioning and
navigation in weakly textured and complex environments.
Consequently, the point and line features-visual–inertial odom-
etry (PL-VIO) method introduced line features to enhance the
robustness of the system to a certain extent [10]. To improve
its real-time performance, the PL-VINS method could be
used to accelerate the line segment detector (LSD) algorithm
by changing the implicit parameters of the LSD algorithm
[8]. However, experiments have shown that the efficiency of
the EDLine algorithm is much higher than that of the LSD
algorithm [11]. Therefore, point-line constraints (PLC)-VIO
[12] used the EDLines algorithm to accelerate the efficiency
of line feature extraction. In addition, delayed marginalization
(DM)-VIO used a DM strategy to effectively improve the
positioning accuracy of the VIO [13].
Today, the main problems with VIO for UAV navigation
in GNSS denial environments are low accuracy and low

II. RELATED WORKS
A. Image-Correction Methods
Image quality can be greatly affected by lighting and noise,
which can affect feature extraction and matching. Thus, pre-
processing of the collected raw images is often necessary. The
oriented FAST and rotated BRIEF (ORB)-SLAM3 method
preprocesses images using Gaussian blur during scale pyramid
construction to reduce high-frequency noise and improve scale
adaptability [14]; the VINS-MONO method uses histogram
equalization to correct images and enhances contrast for
improved feature detection of points and lines [7]. However,
in most cases, histogram equalization can alter image bright-
ness and extend the pixel threshold range, resulting in image
overcorrection and gray-level merging, making it difficult to
control the corrective effect.
The adaptive gamma-based image-correction algorithm
enables nonlinear stretching of the image through the global
power-law transform, which has the disadvantage that the
parameter γ must be manually adjusted [15]. Huang et al.
[16] proposed an adaptive gamma correction technique with
weighted distribution by establishing the compensated cumu-
lative distribution function to correct parameters; the adaptive
hyperparameter α method was proposed, which was effective
in reducing image overcorrection and intensity saturation to
a certain extent [17]. Later, a neural-network-based gamma
correction algorithm was proposed, but it was difficult to
apply it to a visual-inertial SLAM system in real time [18].
In addition, there are many deep learning methods that have
been extensively investigated in image processing and hyper-
spectral image classification [19], [20], [21], [22]. As computer
computing power continues to increase, deep learning image
processing methods will be used more often in the field
of visual-inertial SLAM. In general, the adaptive gamma
algorithm can have a considerable effect on image correction.
However, its application to SLAM is relatively rare.
B. Frontend Visual-Inertial Fusion Methods
The feature-point method is widely used in the front end of
VIO [23], [24]. The method extracts key feature points based
on images, calculates camera poses using 2-D or 3-D feature
matching, and constructs maps. The MonoSLAM method uses
extracted sparse feature points for pose estimation and is
the first real-time visual SLAM system based on monocular
cameras [25]. It selects the most representative keyframes to
process feature points and combines bundle adjustment (BA)
with visual SLAM using a nonlinear optimization approach
to reduce computational complexity. The VINS-MONO per-
forms nonlinear optimization using point features and IMU
preintegration [7]. However, the robustness and accuracy of
point-feature-based VIO systems can be greatly reduced in
complex environments with weak textures and changing illu-
mination. Moreover, line features provide richer geometric
structure information than point features, so visual-inertial
algorithms with point-line feature fusion tend to be more
advantageous.
PL-VIO adopts the LSD algorithm to extract line features
and expresses them using Plücker coordinates and orthogonal
representation of lines, which has the advantage of com-
putational simplicity and compactness [10]. The PL-VINS
method adjusts the implicit parameters of the LSD to oper-
ational efficiency [8]. Compared to the LSD algorithm, the
EDLines algorithm takes less time to extract line features.
Consequently, the fast adaptive EDLines VIO method has
been proposed; however, it lacks closed-loop detection, and
trajectory errors tend to accumulate or even diverge [26].
To solve the problems of repeated line segment detection
and excessive segmentation using the EDLines algorithm, the
PLD-VINS method merges short lines and uses an optical flow
method to track the detected line segments [27]. The point-line
fusion and parallel-line fusion (PLF)-VINS method introduces
parallel line features based on line features, combining the
parallel line constraints observed from multiple perspectives

C. Backend Optimization Methods
Backend optimization methods primarily include Kalman
filtering and graph optimization methods [30], [31], [32],
[33]. Classical Kalman filtering methods include robust VIO
(ROVIO) and OpenVINS [34], [35]. ROVIO uses visual fea-
ture points as landmark points and handles photometric errors
in images using mean–variance filtering [34]. OpenVINS is a
multistate constraint Kalman filter (MSCKF)-based algorithm
for VIO systems and supports online and time-offset cali-
bration [35]. However, the Kalman-filter-based visual-inertial
SLAM algorithm performs poorly in complex environments,
becoming increasingly computationally intensive over time.
Thus, optimization-based methods are the dominant methods
today. Open keyframe-based visual-inertial slam (OKVIS)
associates the camera pose with a series of IMU states and
is a keyframe-based nonlinear optimization method. How-
ever, OKVIS does not support loop closure detection or
relocation [36]. VINS-MONO performs tight-coupling VIO
optimization based on sliding windows [7]. Subsequently,
VINS-FUSION for stereo VIO and GVINS for GNSS-visual-
inertial tight coupling have been successively proposed [37],
[38]. However, the disadvantage of VINS-MONO is that the
information matrix of the vision sensors is considered to be
a pre-estimated fixed value, which is not reasonable for real
VIO systems. The ORB-SLAM3 method assumes that the
covariance matrix of the visual constraint is fixed at each
pyramid level, which is also unrealistic [14]. Consequently,
when the image quality changes considerably, the results
deteriorate. Based on the VINS-MONO method, a method
for flexibly adjusting the point feature covariance matrix was
proposed based on the reprojection error of the point features
[39]. However, the method does not use line features and lacks
loopback threads, leading to poor robustness and error accu-
mulation. In short, because the image quality changes in real
time, effectively reflecting the change in sensor accuracy and
properly weighting the sensors during backend optimization
have become urgent problems to be resolved.
In general, the current problems of the VIO algorithm are
as follows.
1) The original image quality is greatly affected by light,
and the histogram equalization method may cause image
oversaturation. Consequently, how to reasonably prepro-
cess an image to facilitate the extraction and matching
of image features is a key problem.
2) Point feature-based monocular visual-inertial SLAM has
low positioning accuracy and robustness in complex
environments. Thus, the realization of the full fusion of
point-line features is worthy of further study.

III. METHODOLOGY
The overall framework of the proposed method is based
on the VINS-MONO method. In the front end, we used
the adaptive gamma correction algorithm for image correc-
tion, the Shi-Tomasi algorithm for feature-point extraction,
and the Kanade-Lucas-Tomasi (KLT) optical flow tracking
algorithm for feature-point matching. In addition, we adopted
the improved EDLines algorithm for line-feature detection,
and the line band descriptor (LBD) and KnnMatch algo-
rithms for line-feature description and matching. Based on
the principle of IMU preintegration, we preintegrated the
IMU observations and aligned them with visual information
in time.
In the backend, we proposed a tightly coupled nonlinear
sliding-windows-based optimization method to establish tar-
get cost functions based on a factor graph, achieving the
first nonlinear optimization based on priori visual weights.
We then calculated the root mean squared error (RMSE) of
the reprojection error for PL, based on the principle that the
higher the sensor accuracy, the greater the sensor weight,
updated the information matrix that adaptively adjusted the
weights of the PL to realize the second nonlinear opti-
mization. Fig. 1 illustrates the framework of the proposed
method.

B. Point-Line Feature Fusion Method
This study followed the VINS-MONO initialization method,
performing short-term initial structure from motion (SFM)
recovery based on visual measurements, and combining IMU
measurements for short-term visual-inertial coarse alignment.
We estimated the initial state—such as the gyroscope bias,
velocity, gravity, and scale—to ensure that the VIO system
could operate under a set of excellent initial values. Because
the original observation data of the IMU are angular veloc-
ity and linear acceleration, we used the IMU preintegration
principle to implement IMU preintegration and synchro-
nize the timestamp with the visual information. In addition,
we adopted the Shi-Tomasi algorithm to extract feature points,
used the KLT algorithm for feature tracking. We improved
the EDLines algorithm and performed line-feature extraction,
which improved its efficiency compared to the LSD algorithm.
To solve the problems in which short lines can be difficult
to track, and approximate lines can lead to mismatching,
we optimized the line features using the strategy of short-line
merging and approximate line rejection, used the LBD and
KnnMatch algorithms to describe and match the line features,
finally rejecting the mismatching lines based on the random
sampling consistency (RANSAC) algorithm.

V. CONCLUSION
To solve the problem of low accuracy and weak robust-
ness of UAV navigation in a GNSS-denied environment,
we proposed a monocular VIO with point-line feature fusion
and adaptive nonlinear optimization in the backend for UAV
navigation. In the front end, we corrected the image using an
adaptive gamma algorithm to facilitate the extraction of visual
features, then rapidly extracted line features using an improved
EDLines algorithm. Moreover, we used the IMU preintegration
principle for IMU preintegration and time alignment with
visual observations. In the backend, we proposed an adaptive
weighting nonlinear optimization method based on a two-
step approach. In the first step, we established a factor graph
model and performed the first nonlinear optimization based
on a priori visual weights. In the second step, we calculated
the reprojection error and established a functional model that
examines the relationship between the reprojection error and
the information matrix. We updated the information matrix
using the reprojection error to adaptively adjust the weights
of the point features and line features in real time. Finally,
we performed a second nonlinear reoptimization.
We compared the proposed method to the VINS-MONO
and PL-VINS methods. The experimental results showed that
the positioning accuracy of the proposed method on the public
EuRoc dataset [9] improved by an average of 32.3% compared
to the PL-VINS method and by an average of 33.8% compared
to the PL-VINS method in three real-world scenarios. This
demonstrated that the proposed method was more robust
and achieved better positioning accuracy in various complex
environments.
Although the proposed method achieved relatively good
results in different environments, the proposed method still
suffers from inaccurate scaling and error accumulation. On the
one hand, the scaling factor in the monocular vision inertial
odometer may change in different environments, resulting
in inconsistent global scaling and poor positioning accuracy.
