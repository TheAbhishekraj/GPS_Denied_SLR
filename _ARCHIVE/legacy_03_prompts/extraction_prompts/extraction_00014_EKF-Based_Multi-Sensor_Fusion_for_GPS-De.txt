ABSTRACT: This paper presents an EKF-based multi-sensor fusion framework for GPS-denied localization of an autonomous ground vehicle. The proposed system integrates measurements from an inertial measurement unit, wheel encoders, and LiDAR to estimate the vehicle pose in terms of position and heading. Wheel encoder and IMU data are used for continuous motion prediction, while LiDAR-based localization provides environmental observations to correct accumulated drift. The framework is implemented on a compact Ackermann-steering robotic platform using a modular navigation architecture that integrates mapping, localization, obstacle avoidance, and motion control. Experimental validation in GPS-denied indoor environments demonstrates that the proposed fusion approach improves pose estimation consistency compared with dead-reckoning-based localization and reduces drift during trajectory tracking. The results confirm the suitability of the proposed framework for autonomous navigation applications where GPS signals are unavailable or unreliable, such as indoor inspection, search and rescue, and autonomous logistics.
