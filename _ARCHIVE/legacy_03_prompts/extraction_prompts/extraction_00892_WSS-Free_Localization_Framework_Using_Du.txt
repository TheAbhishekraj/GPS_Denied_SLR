TITLE: WSS-Free Localization Framework Using Dual-GNSS and Motor RPM for Autonomous Ground Vehicles
AUTHORS: K. R. Kumar

DOI: 10.1109/ICCAR69571.2026.11549754
ABSTRACT: Autonomous ground vehicles in GNSS-challenged environments typically rely on Wheel Speed Sensors (WSS), introducing hardware complexity and calibration requirements. This paper presents a unified WSS-free localization framework for electric drive-by-wire vehicles integrating motor RPM from the CAN bus with drift-free dual-antenna GNSS heading. The proposed approach fuses RPM-derived velocity and GNSS heading within an adaptive Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) incorporating HDOP-based GNSS update rejection and an RPM-triggered Zero Velocity Update (ZUPT) with heading freeze. Unlike conventional ZUPT methods constraining only velocity, the proposed mechanism explicitly mitigates gyroscope-induced yaw drift during stationary GNSS outages.Experimental validation on a retrofitted electric Tomcar demonstrates robust performance under open-sky, multipath, and GNSS-denied conditions, achieving 0.0236m trajectory RMSE under full GNSS availability, limiting stationary multipath drift to 0.05m compared to 2.8m from a commercial GNSS/INS/WSS system, and reducing RMSE from 5.30m to 2.64m during a 71-minute GNSS outage, representing one of the longest real-world GNSS outage validations reported for a WSS-free electric vehicle localization framework.
