TITLE: Design, Implementation & Optimization of Indoor Autonomous Mobile Robotic System Using RGBD Camera and 2D Lidar
AUTHORS: M. Singh; N. Joshi; H. D. Joshi; K. Singh; R. K. Duvedi
YEAR: 2025
VENUE: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT
DOI: 10.1109/STPEC66316.2025.11490608
ABSTRACT: Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) are increasingly deployed across various industries, yet their performance is often limited by reliance on a single perception sensor such as LiDAR or RGB-D. This paper presents an optimized sensor fusion approach combining a Kinect V2 RGB-D camera and RP LiDAR A2 for enhanced indoor navigation and mapping. The proposed framework addresses key limitations of single-sensor systems: LiDAR's inability to process vertical obstacles and RGB- D's susceptibility to lighting variations. The system is implemented in a prototype $A M R (60 \times 40 \times 40 ~\text{cm})$ and experimentally validated in a $15 \times 12 ~\mathrm{m}$ indoor environment with varying obstacle densities (2-8 obstacles per trial). Using the RTAB-Map algorithm, robust 3D mapping is achieved by integrating visual and depth data. Localization is performed via Adaptive Monte Carlo Localization (AMCL), with navigation handled by the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) and A* algorithm. The system is implemented on a prototype AMR and experimentally validated, demonstrating accurate pose estimation and reliable point-to-point navigation in complex indoor environments. System operates at 10 Hz localization frequency with 45W average power consumption on affordable hardware (Intel i58250U). This work establishes a practical, cost-effective framework for deploying AMRs in real-world applications requiring precise and autonomous operation in semi-structured indoor environments.
