TITLE: Drift-Resilient Indoor 3D Mapping in GPS-Denied Environments Using RKO-LIO with Velodyne-Pixhawk Integration in ROS 2
AUTHORS: A. M. N. S. Alqadhi; M. A. M. S. Bajhaw; M. El Muhammed; A. Ucar
YEAR: 2026
VENUE: NOT_IN_ABSTRACT
DOI: 10.1109/ICHORA69329.2026.11537209
ABSTRACT: Indoor 3D mapping in GPS-denied environments requires drift-resilient LiDAR-IMU fusion to ensure geometric consistency over extended trajectories. This work integrates a Velodyne VLP-16 3D LiDAR and a Pixhawk Cube Orange+ IMU on a TurtleBot Waffle Pi running ROS 2 Humble, and evaluates RKO-LIO, a LiDAR-inertial odometry method that avoids sensor-specific modeling via a physics-based kinematic motion model with adaptive scan-to-map regularization. Geometric accuracy is validated against roll-meter groundtruth measurements across five indoor test objects, achieving an RMSE of 0.026 m, MAE of 0.022 m, and mean error of 0.69 % (range: $\mathbf{0. 0 3 \% -} \mathbf{- 1. 4 7 \%}$). A controlled simulation comparison replays an identical sensor bag through RKO-LIO, LIO-SAM, and FAST-LIO in three Gazebo indoor environments. FASTLIO achieves the lowest trajectory error in all scenes; LIO-SAM is accurate in feature-rich environments but diverges catastrophically in the bookstore scene (APE 21.7 m, map destroyed); RKO-LIO produces bounded-error trajectories across every environment (APE $\leq 0.494 ~\mathrm{m}$) without any perscene parameter tuning. These results demonstrate that worstcase robustness-not peak accuracy-is the decisive property for real GPS-denied indoor deployment.
