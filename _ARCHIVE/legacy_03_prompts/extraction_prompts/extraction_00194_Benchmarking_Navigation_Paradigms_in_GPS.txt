ABSTRACT: The widespread adoption of autonomous mobile robots has highlighted the significance of accurate localization as a key challenge in the industry, particularly in environments where GPS signals are degraded or completely lost (e.g., the urban canyon, a tunnel, or indoors). This research represents the first complete assessment of 9 navigation techniques by comparing them across three categories using 8.75 hours of data from multimodal sensors in the CARLA simulator.Our results show that HistGradientBoosting exceeded those of all the other methods tested with a mean RMSE of 126.8 meters, which is 37.5% better than using dead reckoning for navigation. The second best-performing method, Linear Regression, produced an RMSE of 196.5 meters. The remaining methods tested (LSTM, GRU, CNN) were found to be performing similar to the baseline dead reckoning performance with RMSE errors of between 201 and 204 meters. Overall, it was determined from the data collected during the study that all of the tested methods struggled more to predict along the Y-axis than they did on the X-axis, with an average of 27.4% greater error on the Y-axis than on the X-axis. HistGradientBoosting handled this imbalance best, reducing it to just 8% (122 m X-axis vs. 132 m Y-axis), while other methods showed roughly 177 m X-axis error versus 227-231 m on the Y-axis.
