ABSTRACT: Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have shown increasing potential in indoor applications such as inspection, automated logistics, and smart environment management. However, the absence of reliable GPS signals in indoor environments poses significant challenges to precise localization and autonomous navigation. To address this issue, this paper presents the design and implementation of an indoor navigation system for UAVs based on Vision-Inertial Odometry (VIO) and depth sensing. The proposed system integrates a RealSense T265 tracking camera for robust VIO-based localization and a D435i depth camera for obstacle detection and environment perception. A path planning module employing the A* algorithm generates feasible routes, while a Jetson Orin NX onboard computer performs sensor fusion, control computation, and command execution through the UAV's autopilot. Experimental validation in indoor test environments demonstrates that the system achieves reliable localization, stable path following, and real-time obstacle avoidance. These results confirm the feasibility of VIO-and depth-sensing-based solutions for UAV navigation in GPS-denied environments. © The 2026 International Conference on Artificial Life and Robotics (ICAROB2026).
