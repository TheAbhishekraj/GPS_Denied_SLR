ABSTRACT: Accurate absolute localization is essential for the reliable and autonomous mission execution of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). However, GNSS-based navigation may be disabled under electronic warfare, physical obstruction, or spoofing. In this study, absolute position is estimated in GNSS-denied environments by matching UAV camera images with open-source satellite imagery. The proposed visual localization pipeline combines deep matching with SuperPoint–LightGlue, satellite–camera alignment via homography, Efficient Perspective-n-Point (EPnP)-based absolute pose estimation, and Kalman fusion with an optical-flow backup. Compared with SIFT and ORB, it achieves higher matching success (mAP) and lower horizontal error. In real flights validated against RTK GNSS, a positioning error of 10 m (MAE) and 14 m (RMSE) in the X–Y plane is obtained at approximately 500 m altitude, running at approximately 5 Hz on a Jetson Orin Nano.
