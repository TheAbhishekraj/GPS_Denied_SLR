ABSTRACT: Hospitals require frequent and reliable surface disinfection to reduce the spread of infectious microorganisms while ensuring continuous patient care and emergency responsiveness. Conventional manual sanitization is labor-intensive, exposes healthcare workers to infection risks, and is difficult to scale during high-demand situations. This paper presents the design and implementation of a low-cost autonomous hospital assistance robot that integrates hybrid disinfection and patient assistive technologies within a distributed embedded architecture. The proposed robot performs autonomous indoor navigation using ultrasonic sensors and wheel encoder feedback combined with lightweight grid-based mapping, enabling obstacle detection and collision avoidance without requiring expensive LiDAR-based SLAM systems. A hybrid disinfection mechanism combining ultraviolet (UV) irradiation and sanitizer spraying is employed to improve surface sterilization coverage. In addition to sanitization, the robot provides patient-assistive services, including scheduled medical reminders and an emergency SOS alert system that notifies caregivers through a web-based monitoring dashboard. The system is implemented using two ESP32 controllers and an Arduino Uno, enabling concurrent navigation, actuation, and service management on resource-constrained embedded hardware. Experimental evaluation in indoor environments demonstrates reliable navigation, continuous hybrid disinfection operation, accurate reminder triggering, and successful SOS alert transmission. The proposed system demonstrates that integrated hospital assistance functions can be achieved using low-cost distributed embedded platforms suitable for scalable healthcare deployment.
