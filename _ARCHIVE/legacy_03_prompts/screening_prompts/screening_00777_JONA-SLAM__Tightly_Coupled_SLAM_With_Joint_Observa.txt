TITLE: JONA-SLAM: Tightly Coupled SLAM With Joint Observation and Normal-Aware Loop Closure
AUTHORS: Q. Jian; Q. Ma; X. Wang; S. Ullah
YEAR: 2026
ABSTRACT: In the field of autonomous navigation, accurate localization and mapping are of paramount importance, especially for autonomous vehicles operating in global navigation satellite system (GNSS)-challenged or denied environments. This article presents joint observation and normal-aware (JONA)-simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), a tightly coupled light detection and ranging (LiDAR)-visual-inertial SLAM system designed to enhance state estimation robustness for autonomous driving platforms in complex scenarios such as urban canyons and tunnels. Building upon our previous research on point-line feature extraction, the proposed system strengthens spatial constraints by constructing a LiDAR-visual joint observation factor. This factor enables the tight coupling of LiDAR-derived geometric information with visual texture features at the feature level, effectively addressing the observation degradation common in GNSS-denied areas. Furthermore, a normal-aware hybrid loop closure detection method is proposed, incorporating point cloud normal consistency verification to significantly improve loop identification reliability and suppress false positives in challenging semistructured scenes. Evaluation results on 22 public sequences and a self-collected campus dataset demonstrate that JONA-SLAM achieves superior or competitive performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. Across 23 experimental test cases, the proposed method reduces the trajectory error [root mean square error (RMSE)] by an average of 35.02%. While maintaining real-time performance, the system demonstrates superior environmental adaptability and global consistency, providing a high-precision and robust localization solution for autonomous driving and versatile mobile platforms in degraded environments.
