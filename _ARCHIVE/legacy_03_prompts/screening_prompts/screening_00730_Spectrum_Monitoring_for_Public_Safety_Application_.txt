TITLE: Spectrum Monitoring for Public Safety Application in UAV GPS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: C. H. Huang; W. L. Chen
YEAR: 2025
ABSTRACT: At present, the Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) (e.g., Global Positioning System, GPS) has become the primary reference for the navigation and positioning of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). However, during disaster rescue operations or wartime scenarios, GPS signals can be blocked, jammed, or spoofed, potentially rendering high-value UAV assets unrecoverable and leading to significant economic losses and strategic risks. To address this issue, this study proposes a low-cost system based on the Doppler Effect and real-time signal processing in spectrum monitoring for public safety applications. Unlike conventional multi-antenna receiver approaches, the proposed system employs only a single receiver combined with a rotatable platform to perform azimuth estimation through 360° horizontal and 90° elevation scanning. The system integrates Signal Doppler Frequency (SDF), Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI), and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) for analysis, applies the Savitzky-Golay (SG) filter to mitigate environmental noise, and utilizes, Binary Phase-Shift Keying (BPSK) and Quadrature Phase-Shift Keying (QPSK) for feature enhancement to improve frequency-shift recognition to realize Interference Detection & Localization and Spectrum Occupancy Monitoring. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed system is capable of real-time estimation of UAV azimuth and elevation angles under GPS-denied conditions, highlighting its dual potential for disaster relief and defense applications.
