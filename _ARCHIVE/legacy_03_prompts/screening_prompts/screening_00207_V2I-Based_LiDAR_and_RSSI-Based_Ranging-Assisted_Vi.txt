TITLE: V2I-Based LiDAR and RSSI-Based Ranging-Assisted Visual-Inertial Odometry for Robust Ego Vehicle Localization in GPS-Denied Environments
AUTHORS: D. Kim; B. Jung; J. Nam; S. Lim
YEAR: 2026
ABSTRACT: This paper proposes a two-dimensional localization framework that integrates Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) and roadside units (RSUs) equipped with LiDAR sensors and vehicle-to-infrastructure (V2I) communication, while opportunistically incorporating GPS measurements when available. The proposed method first estimates the local pose of the vehicle using VIO based on onboard IMU and camera sensors. Then, vehicles broadcast Basic Safety Messages (BSM). While BSMs are not explicitly designed for localization, their periodic beaconing is opportunistically reused as a signal carrier. RSUs share this signal data via backhaul communication to estimate coarse vehicle positions using RSSI-based trilateration. These estimates are refined by correlating with object data from RSU-mounted LiDAR sensors, enabling accurate ego vehicle identification and localization even in multi-vehicle scenarios. The corrected ego vehicle position is transmitted back to the vehicle using Sensor Data Sharing Messages (SDSM), compliant with the SAE J2735 standard. Subsequently, this RSU-based position is adaptively fused with VIO and available GPS measurements using dynamically assigned weights based on sensor reliability. This approach effectively mitigates VIO drift, ensuring seamless and robust positioning performance across both GPS-available and GPS-denied environments. The framework was implemented and validated in the CARLA simulator under varying RSU spacing, GPS shadowing, and inter-vehicle distances. Experimental results show that the weighted-average fusion method (VIO+GPS+RSU) reduces localization errors by up to 77.5% compared to standalone VIO and 96.4% compared to standalone GPS, maintaining accuracy within 3 m. Ego vehicle identification performance was strongly influenced by RSU density and inter-vehicle distance, reaching up to 97.7% with 50 m RSU spacing and decreasing to approximately 20–45% at 150 m depending on inter-vehicle distance. Wider inter-vehicle distances generally yielded more stable identification.
