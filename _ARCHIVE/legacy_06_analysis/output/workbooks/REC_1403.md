Simless GSM positioning for navigation
in GPS-denied environments
Inmaculada Soldado Serrano, José García Doblado, Ignazio Federico Finazzi and María Ángeles Martín Prats
Electronics Engineering Department, University of Seville, Sevilla, Spain
Abstract
Purpose – Until now, air navigation systems have mainly relied on global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) on the worldwide spread global
positioning system. However, the so-called GNSS-denied environments open a new research line which pursues the development of alternative
technologies which will cover this gap in positioning systems’ services.
Design/methodology/approach – This paper presents the possibility of using positioning systems based on global system for mobile
communications (GSM) signal. This approach developed in a standalone device will provide real-time information. The presented algorithm pursues
a new methodology for providing useful information.
Findings – Among all the different technologies aimed at giving a navigation solution in the absence of any kind of GNSS in which this paper is
based, it advocates for the use of the signals of opportunity, particularly the usage of GSM.
Practical implications – Technology is currently immersed in an era of continuous progress and expansion of navigation systems. These are
evolving toward high performance systems, offering precise, efﬁcient and safe air navigation. In addition, the growing demand for unmanned aerial
vehicles increases the level of exigency on this activity even more. Therefore, in the context of the development of unmanned navigation
technologies, the aim is to implement positioning systems that will allow high precision even in though environments.
Originality/value – Referencing the SIMless concept, a SIM-free system is described in this paper. The SIM-free system is supported by open data
bases and permits the positioning based on the information sniffed from the signals broadcast by a set of several nearby base station of the GSM
network. Hence, it provides same and in some cases even better accuracies than the already developed techniques, not being necessary to
synchronize the link between the mobile terminal and the base station transceiver (BTS).
Keywords GPS-denied environment, GSM positioning, Navigation aid, SIMless
Paper type Research paper
1. Introduction
The recent advances in Avionic technologies are improving the
demanding navigation performances which are required in the
aeronautic sector nowadays. In this term, the global navigation
satellite systems and a large number of alternative techniques
based on satellite positioning, aided inertial navigation and
beacons are under development. Furthermore, even a larger
number of research lines are focusing on the development of
positioning systems based on the signals of opportunity
(SoOP). The main goal of these researches is providing an
alternative global positioning system (GPS) support when these
fundamental signals for navigation are not available, i.e. indoor
spaces (Varshavsky et al., 2007) or urban scenarios.
The SoOP are radio frequency (RF) signals which main
purpose is not navigation. A high density of SoOP can be found
in urban areas. Hence, they can be used for navigation purposes
in case the GPS is not available (Raquel and Martin, 2008).
Furthermore, an additional infrastructure is not necessary for
their usage, as the current one can be reused.
Nevertheless, the development of a hardware and software
SoOP receiver is a challenging goal which is hardly pursued by
engineers. The receiver’s drawbacks are given by three
principal factors. The ﬁrst one is related with the non-
navigation purpose of the received RF signals. The second one
bases this system’s functionality in the uniform extension and
distribution of these signals around the world. The last one
makes it necessary to know where the signal transmitters are
allocated, even their transmission direction in case the user
receiver is far away from those BTS. Although some multipath
problems are also to be taken into account, the SoOP research
focused on AM radio, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and DVT signals is
providing good results in terms of positioning estimation
(Mautz, 2012). Hence, they are being developed to add
support to the current navigation systems. Even in a higher
level in this term, it has to be mentioned the global system for
mobile communications (GSM), whose research is also
providing good results to the above mentioned development
(Mautz, 2012). A simple algorithm for positioning based on
GSM is presented in this paper.

The
GSM
is
a
worldwide
standard
for
the
digital
communication between mobiles that was developed by the
European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI)
(ETSI,
2016).
There
are
numerous
GSM
navigation
techniques for locating a user terminal whose algorithms are
used for positioning depending on the BTS signal range. Well-
known techniques for passive emitter localization are, for
example, time difference of arrival, time of arrival (Torrieri,
1984; Ho and Chan, 1993), frequency difference of arrival,
angle of arrival (Schell and Gardner, 2018; Weiss, 2004) or the
combination of two or more of those methods (Mušicki and
Koch, 2010), among other methods (Mautz, 2012).
However, the majority of these techniques require a GSM
station link and challenging algorithms. This article advocates
for the use of a less difﬁculty algorithm based on obtaining the
Cell ID which provides information about where the BTS is
geographically allocated. This Cell identiﬁcation is related with
a radio range where the user can be situated taking into account
the theoretical BTS signal strength. This coverage can vary
from 100 m in urban areas up to 35 km in rural areas where the
BTS density is signiﬁcantly lower. In addition, the user
terminal does not have to establish a communication with the
base transmission station (BTS) and, therefore, it does not
need to have a SIM. This concept, which is called SIMless,
allows the user terminal to receive signal from all the near BTS
no matter the phone company they belong to. Consequently, it
is possible to overlap coverage areas to obtain more information
for the cell identiﬁcation. Thus, this article is based on a
MATLAB
algorithm
implementation
which
allows
the
extraction of broadcast downlink GSM information for static
positioning purposes.
2. Algorithm description
The pursued ﬁnal GSM positioning system is able to acquire
and post-process the GSM signals, offering a positioning
solution. Thus, the whole development can be divided into two
fundamental steps which are described in detail in the two
following subsections. The ﬁrst one is related to the downlink
broadcast GSM signal snifﬁng and decoding. The second one
is referred to the positioning algorithm implementation.
The extraction of the needed GSM information is done via
the two ﬁrst mentioned steps. Then, after using the USRP-
E310 for snifﬁng the downlink broadcast GSM signals and
after decoding these signals with the GNU Radio software, the
Cell ID information is obtained from the logical BCCH
message [GSM 04.03]. The BTS identiﬁcation consists of two
messages, the location area identity, which consists of a mobile
country code, the mobile network code and de location area
code, and the Cell ID itself. They unequivocally identify each
BTS after their veriﬁcation with a station data base where each
BTS is placed in its geographical coordinates. Then, these
coordinates are given as inputs to the developed MATLAB
algorithm. Finally, the algorithm implements the technique
that will be presented in the second subsection providing a
positioning solution.
The block diagram illustrated in Figure 1 shows the process
which is followed by the GSM signals and has been described
above.
2.1 Snifﬁng and decoding global system for mobile
communications signals
These processes are supported by the USRP-E310. This device
is an Ettus Research Software Deﬁne Radio which allows the
RF signals processing. In the ﬁrst step, the broadcast GSM
signals are sniffed in the range of 921 MHz up to 960 MHz.
After an ADC conversion, GNU radio allows the BCCH
decoding. The simplest way to run this process is using GNU
radio companion (GRC), a python code generation tool used to
generate the blocks diagram which allows the decoding of the
determined information. Figure 2 shows the diagram that has
been used for snifﬁng and decoding GSM signals.
The information extracted from all the near BTS is given as
inputs to the MATLAB algorithm following described.
2.2 Proposed algorithm
This subsection presents the algorithm description. The
proposed
algorithm
uses
the
MATLAB
toolbox
image
processing to obtain mask information as it is explained as
following.
The ﬂow diagram in Figure 3 is divided into four different
blocks which are necessary for the correct extraction of the user
positioning. Following, they will be explained.
First, the BTS LLA coordinates, i.e. latitude and longitude
referred to the World Geodetic System 84 (WGS84), are
obtained by validating the Cell ID information with the BTS
data base provided as input. It is necessary to assign an initial
radio of coverage for all the BTS whose signals are received in
the user terminal. It is also pre-determined the number of ﬁnal
stations, i.e. the stop condition, that the algorithm calculates
the position with. In this paper, that number is three. After that,
the mask is built in relation with the map dimensions which are
needed for locating all the near BTS.
Second, the LLA coordinates are transformed into the
Universal Transverse Mercator (UMT) 2D coordinate system.
Apart from this, it is necessary to transform the deﬁned point in
the UMT matrix using a lineal transformation to obtain the
correct index in the built mask.

The third block comprises the iterative process that allows for
the decrease of the BTS radio coverage until a single point is
determined. In addition, it takes into account the algorithm
stop condition for breaking the process. The main purpose of
that process is to corroborate whether after a established radio
decrease it exists a non-connected BTS to the rest. In case this
decision condition is true, the isolated BTS is deleted from the
following iteration. If there are more than one in this situation,
the farthest BTS is ﬁrst removed and the radio is reduced to
continue the process. In case this decision point is false, the
radio decreases again. When the minimum number of BTSs
which have shared areas is reached, the mask pixels which are
commune for the ﬁnal BTS are calculated.
Finally, the mean point is assumed to be the positioning
result, providing its transformation into LLA coordinates the
positioning estimation.

5. Conclusions
The algorithm implements a low complexity functional
positioning technique code that provides good values of
precision for positioning in urban scenarios. In addition, the
ﬁnal system is expected to use downlink broadcast GSM signals
which
belong
to
a
global
communication
standard
infrastructure. Thus, it is a SIM-free (SIMless) system with
independency from interaction with this infrastructure. Then,
it is possible an indoor and GPS-denied scenarios positioning
due to the GSM signals availability.
Summarizing, the pursued ﬁnal GSM positioning system is
able to acquire and post-process the GSM signals. This
positioning solution is truly useful in GPS-denied environment,
i.e. indoor or urban areas where good values of positioning
estimation are obtained. Because of the fact that GPS is the
most precise system in terms of positioning, the alternative
positioning method based on GSM signals pursues the backup
usage for guaranteeing the everywhere positioning. The less
lack of availably signals for the position calculating, the more
precise, efﬁcient and safe aerial navigation.
