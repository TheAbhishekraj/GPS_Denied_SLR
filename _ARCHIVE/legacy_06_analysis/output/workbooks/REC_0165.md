Abstract—UAV navigation often relies on GNSS, failing in
denied environments like urban canyons. We propose an end-to-
end cross-view geo-localization framework mitigating geometric
and appearance discrepancies. It achieves 93.59% Recall@1
on University-1652 and 93.56% on DenseUAV, outperforming
baselines. Additionally, two efficient real-time inference strategies
reduce the average continuous tracking error from 9.68 m to 0.95
m, demonstrating high-precision potential in GNSS-denied areas.
Index Terms—Cross-view Image Retrieval, Hard Sample Min-
ing, Metric Learning, UAV, Visual Geo-localization
I. INTRODUCTION
UAV navigation often fails in GNSS-denied environments
such as urban canyons. To address this issue, cross-view geo-
localization matches UAV and satellite images for positioning.
We propose a framework integrating feature learning and hard
sample mining to improve robustness and deployability under
viewpoint and appearance variations.
II. METHODOLOGY
A. Feature Extraction Network
ConvNeXt-Base is adopted as the backbone to improve
spatial feature representation under large viewpoint variations.
Global Average Pooling (GAP) and L2 normalization are
applied to generate stable embeddings for cosine-similarity
retrieval.

IV. EDGE COMPUTING PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE AND
SIMULATION SCENARIO SETUP
A. Edge Computing Hardware and System Architecture
To validate real-world feasibility, the proposed model was
deployed on a Raspberry Pi 5 Model B with 8 GB RAM. The
DenseUAV dataset [5], with continuous trajectories, was used
to estimate the upper bound of localization performance.
B. Baseline Inference Scenario and Challenge Analysis
In the baseline (GLOBAL mode), each UAV image is
matched against the entire satellite database, achieving 94.59%
R@1, with an average error of 9.68 m. Although accurate,
global retrieval introduces high computational cost and may
cause drift due to visually similar distant scenes.
V. OUTLIER REJECTION AND TRAJECTORY OPTIMIZATION
Real UAV trajectories exhibit strong spatiotemporal conti-
nuity, while global retrieval alone may introduce mismatches
from visually similar scenes. To address this, geospatial con-
straints and indexing are applied to reduce search space and
improve stability.
A. Multi-Hypothesis Tracking and Dynamic Geofencing
Instead of single-point tracking, a Top-K Multi-Hypothesis
Tracking (MHT) strategy preserves multiple high-confidence
candidates as search centers. During inference, the Haversine
distance filters gallery images within a union-based geofence.
Candidates are pruned if their score difference exceeds 0.15
or distance exceeds 150 m.
A confidence-based state machine monitors tracking. If sim-
ilarity remains above threshold, tracking continues; otherwise,
after K=3 low-confidence frames, rescue mode is triggered,
resetting MHT and reverting to global retrieval. The threshold
is set to 0.6, and buffering reduces false resets.
B. Overlapping-Boundary Indexing and Adaptive Retrieval
To accelerate rescue mode, an overlapping grid-based in-
dexing structure is constructed offline. Each grid includes
boundary overlap to avoid truncation, and K-Means clustering
with multiple representatives improves robustness.
Online retrieval adopts a two-stage framework: coarse
matching against representative points with spatial filtering,
followed by fine matching within selected clusters. This re-
duces computation while maintaining accuracy.
C. Edge Deployment and Performance Evaluation
Three modes are evaluated: GLOBAL,MHT, and INDEX.
As shown in Table III, INDEX reduces error from 9.68 m to
0.95 m, lowers computation time, and maintains 95.80% R@1,
demonstrating improved accuracy and efficiency.

VI. CONCLUSION
This study proposes a cross-view UAV localization sys-
tem for GNSS-denied environments. By integrating a Con-
vNeXt backbone, dynamic sampling, and the InfoNCE loss,
the method improves feature representation and matching
performance. Geofencing and hierarchical semantic indexing
alleviate the limitations of static retrieval, enabling accurate
trajectory tracking with controllable latency.
