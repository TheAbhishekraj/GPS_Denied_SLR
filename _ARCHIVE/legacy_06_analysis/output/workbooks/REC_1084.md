and Kolmogorov‑Arnold Networks for non‑visual 
UAV localization in GNSS‑denied environments
Imen Jarraya1*   , Fatimah Alahmed1, Mohamed Abdelkader1, Khaled Gabr1, Muhammad Bilal Kadri1 and 
Wadii Boulila1 
Abstract 
Visual localization has shown promise for accurate Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) navigation in GNSS-denied envi-
ronments due to its high spatial resolution. However, performance declines in low-light or texture-scarce conditions 
and incurs high computational costs. In contrast, non-visual sensors offer a lightweight, low-complexity alternative 
for localization under such conditions. This work introduces CLAK (CNN-LSTM-Attention-KAN), a deep learning frame-
work that estimates global UAV positions (latitude, longitude, and elevation) using non-visual sensors such as LiDAR, 
barometric altitude, and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU). CLAK combines Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) 
for spatial encoding, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layers for temporal modeling, attention for feature prioritiza-
tion, and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) for flexible nonlinear regression. The model is trained on synthetic 
UAV flight data generated using a ROS2 simulation framework that integrates Gazebo for 3D environment simulation 
and PX4 for autopilot control, with QGroundControl (QGC) managing mission planning and monitoring. Elevation 
data is extracted from a Digital Elevation Map (DEM) of the Taif region in Saudi Arabia, while ground truth positions 
are derived from simulated onboard Global Positioning System (GPS) outputs. Simulation results across different 
flight trajectories show CLAK can achieve up to 78.35% Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and 75.40% Root Mean Squared 
Error (RMSE) reduction compared to the LSTM baseline, while maintaining an average coefficient of determination 
( R2 ) of 0.998. These results demonstrate the scalability and precision of CLAK for UAV localization in GNSS-denied 
conditions.
Keywords  UAV localization, Non-visual fusion sensors, GNSS-denied environments, Deep learning, Kolmogorov-
Arnold network, Digital elevation map

Introduction
Accurate localization remains a critical capability for 
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating in envi-
ronments where Global Navigation Satellite Systems 
(GNSS) are unreliable or unavailable, such as urban 
canyons, tunnels, forests, and indoor facilities (He et al., 
2023; Alghamdi et al., 2025). Conventional GNSS-based 
positioning is prone to signal blockage, multi path effects, 
and spoofing, limiting its effectiveness in such challeng-
ing scenarios. To address these limitations, a variety of 
alternative localization techniques have been proposed 
in the literature, including visual-based, non-visual, and 
multimodal sensor fusion approaches. Beyond naviga-
tion, accurate geolocation also underpins UAV-based 
smart-city perception (e.g., traffic monitoring and urban 
analytics), where sensed data must be consistently geo-
referenced for reliable downstream Artificial Intelligence 
(AI) inference (Benjdira et al., 2022).

Visual methods employ camera imagery in conjunc-
tion with IMU data to reconstruct UAV trajectories with 
high precision and full six-degree-of-freedom pose esti-
mation  (Ye et al., 2024). A leading example is Monocu-
lar Visual-Inertial System (VINS-Mono), which operates 
using a single monocular camera and a low-cost IMU, 
representing the simplest viable sensor configuration for 
such tasks. The system begins with robust on-the-fly ini-
tialization and failure recovery routines. Core processing 
uses a tightly integrated sliding-window nonlinear opti-
mization that combines IMU preintegration and visual 
feature observations. Loop detection enables efficient 
relocalization, and a four-degree-of-freedom pose-graph 
optimization corrects accumulated drift. VINS-Mono 
also supports efficient map saving, loading, and merg-
ing, and demonstrates reliable performance across pub-
lic datasets, micro aerial vehicle flights, and mobile 
platforms such as iOS devices  (Qin et al., 2018; Li et al., 
2023b). Notable extensions include VINS-Fusion, which 
enhances VINS-Mono to support stereo and RGB-D 
cameras while retaining its optimization framework and 
adding online spatial and temporal calibration. Oriented 
FAST and Rotated BRIEF-Simultaneous Localization and 
Mapping 3 (ORB-SLAM3) further broadens applicabil-
ity by accommodating monocular, stereo, RGB-D, and 
visual-inertial inputs. It employs intelligent keyframe 
selection, robust feature tracking, Bag-of-Words loop 
detection, relocalization, and multi-map handling to 
deliver centimeter-level accuracy in diverse indoor and 
outdoor environments (Zhang et  al., 2024a; Jiao et  al., 
2023).
Recent developments focus on reducing reliance on 
calibration and improving runtime efficiency. Self-Super-
vised Deep Mmonocular Visual-InertialOdometry and 
Depth Estimation (SelfVIO) adopts a self-supervised 
approach to jointly learn depth and motion, eliminating 
the need for extrinsic calibration and outperforming tra-
ditional VIO methods on datasets like KITTI and EuRoC 
(Almalioglu et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2025; Chen and Pan, 
2024). Optical Flow VINS-Mono minimizes latency by 
offloading optical flow computation to on-sensor ASICs 
and achieves 50 Hz performance on ARM Cortex-A72 
hardware (Jarraya et  al., 2025a). Notably, Lidar Inertial 
Odometry via Smoothing and Mapping (LIO-SAM) and 
Lightweight and Ground-Optimized Lidar Odometry 
and Mapping (LeGO-LOAM) merge Light Detection and 
Ranging (LiDAR) depth data and IMU measurements 
with camera information. This multimodal fusion offers 
a balance between geometric precision and visual texture 
robustness, resulting in more reliable localization in com-
plex and dynamic settings. Similarly, SaliencyI2PLoc ena-
bles GNSS-free localization by aligning camera images 
with pre-constructed LiDAR maps through contrastive 
learning and feature saliency enhancement  (Li et  al., 
2025).
Visual and hybrid localization systems offer high-pre-
cision performance but face significant challenges when 
deployed on lightweight and cost-constrained UAV plat-
forms. These methods depend on high-resolution camera 
inputs, continuous feature extraction and matching, and 
nonlinear optimization processes, all of which require 
substantial computational power and memory capacity. 
Evaluations on embedded platforms such as NVIDIA 
Jetson TX2, Nano, and Xavier NX have shown that even 
highly optimized algorithms like VINS-Mono and ORB 
SLAM often struggle to achieve real-time performance 
unless they operate in maximum power modes. This 
compromises energy efficiency and increases thermal 
constraints, limiting the practical use of such systems in 
energy-restricted UAV applications. In addition to com-
putational limitations, the performance of these systems 
is highly sensitive to environmental conditions. Situa-
tions involving poor lighting, motion blur, low-texture 
surfaces, or adverse weather conditions often result in 
degraded image quality, negatively affecting localization 
accuracy and reliability. Moreover, these systems often 
require additional sensors and involve strict calibration 
and synchronization procedures, further complicating 
their integration into compact, resource-constrained 
aerial platforms (Zhang et al., 2024b; Jarraya et al., 2025a; 
Kramarić et al., 2025; Zhan et al., 2025).
These limitations underscore the need for localization 
strategies that reduce reliance on high-end hardware 
while maintaining high accuracy under challenging con-
ditions. LiDAR-IMU frameworks such as Fast LiDAR-
Inertial Odometry (FAST-LIO) and FAST-LIO2 provide 
powerful yet efficient alternatives. FAST-LIO fuses raw 
LiDAR scans with IMU measurements using a tightly 
coupled iterated extended Kalman filter and a parallel 
kd-tree for map updates, enabling robust and fast odom-
etry in cluttered or fast-motion environments. Its succes-
sor, FAST-LIO2, eliminates explicit feature extraction by 
registering raw points directly to the map and employs 
an incremental kd-tree for efficient updates. This archi-
tecture achieves real-time operation at rates above 100 
Hz on both Advanced RISC Machine (ARM) and Intel 
processors. Empirical comparisons demonstrate that 
FAST-LIO frameworks match or exceed the localization 
performance of more complex visual-LiDAR systems 
such as LIO-SAM and LeGO-LOAM, while significantly 
reducing computational overhead and sensor dependen-
cies (Huaman et al., 2025; Zhan et al., 2025; Chen et al., 
2024).
These findings underscore a growing transition toward 
lightweight, sensor-efficient localization models that 
meet the performance and integration requirements of

Related works
Reliable localization of unmanned aerial vehicles in envi-
ronments where Global Navigation Satellite System sig-
nals are unavailable or significantly degraded remains a 
critical challenge in the fields of robotics and autonomous 
navigation. To address this problem, recent research 
has advanced along multiple methodological direc-
tions. Traditional sensor fusion techniques, particularly 
those based on Kalman filtering, remain foundational 
by integrating data from inertial measurement units, 
barometric sensors, magnetometers, ultra-wideband 
systems, or wheel odometry to maintain accurate state 
estimation. Building on these methods, hybrid frame-
works have been proposed that incorporate deep learn-
ing components, such as Convolutional Neural Networks 
(CNNs), long short-term memory networks, and atten-
tion mechanisms, into classical estimation models, such 
as the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) or Factor Graph 
Optimization (FGO). These hybrid approaches aim to 
combine the generalization capability of learning-based 
models with the consistency and physical interpretability 
of model-based estimators. In parallel, fully data-driven 
methods have emerged, relying exclusively on end-to-
end neural architectures that learn to infer UAV position 
and orientation directly from raw sensor inputs. These 
methods eliminate the need for explicit filtering or opti-
mization. Table  1 summarizes recent non-visual UAV 
localization methods, highlighting their implementation 
details, performance under GNSS-denied conditions, 
and main limitations.
Hybrid learning-filter models have shown promis-
ing results by combining the representational power 
of deep networks with the probabilistic rigor of clas-
sical estimators. For instance, Wang et  al. (2025) pre-
sented a Self-Attention LSTM (SALSTM) fused with an 
EKF, leveraging IMU, barometric, and magnetometer 
data to reduce velocity and position errors by over 50% 
compared to conventional LSTM-only models. Simi-
larly, Zhengsong Wang et al. (2025) developed a multi-
stage architecture that integrates CNN-LSTM-Attention 
modules with FGO. Their approach processes inertial 
and odometric time-series data using CNNs for spatial 
encoding and LSTM units for temporal modeling, while 
an attention layer dynamically prioritizes salient features. 
The learned representations are then optimized via FGO 
to enforce trajectory smoothness and long-term consist-
ency. Empirical evaluations on GNSS-denied vehicular 
datasets reported Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) 
reductions ranging from 34.62% to 46.58%, substantially 
outperforming both classical inertial navigation systems 
and standalone deep learning baselines.

Other studies have explored alternative fusion schemes 
that incorporate deep learning with adaptive filtering. 
Tang et  al. (2022) proposed a Gated Recurrent Unit-
Adaptive Kalman Filter (GRU-AKF) hybrid framework, 
where GRUs are trained to predict pseudo-GNSS meas-
urements during signal loss. These predictions are then 
filtered by an Adaptive Kalman Filter to correct inertial 
drift. The system demonstrated a reduction in RMSE 
from 157 m to 27 m and a decrease in maximum posi-
tion error from 380 m to 91 m over 180-second GNSS 
outages in urban driving scenarios. While effective, the 
model’s performance hinges on the stability of GRU pre-
diction quality and appropriate AKF tuning. Moreover, 
the results are reported under a single experimental con-
figuration, suggesting the need for broader multi-envi-
ronment validation. Xu et  al. (2022) proposed a hybrid 
learning-based localization model called Motion-Con-
strained Backpropagation (MC-BP), which integrates a 
motion-constrained Kalman filter with a backpropaga-
tion neural network. Their approach explicitly incorpo-
rates kinematic motion priors into the filtering process 
to address the problem of drift in inertial-based systems. 
Experimental results in GNSS-denied simulated envi-
ronments demonstrated that MC-BP reduces the aver-
age positional error by 37.8% compared to conventional 
Kalman filtering, underscoring the value of combining 
model-based constraints with data-driven learning.
In contrast, deep learning-only methods focus on 
directly modeling sensor dynamics from data, typically 
using recurrent or convolutional architectures. Fang 
et al. (2020) and Dai et al. (2020) showed that LSTM and 
RNN-based models can significantly outperform tradi-
tional inertial navigation systems during GNSS interrup-
tions. Narkhede et al. (2021) introduced an incremental 
LSTM-based learning framework that enables real-time 
attitude estimation via online weight updates, improving 
adaptability to dynamic motion patterns.
El Sabbagh et  al. (2023) proposed a cascaded neural 
architecture that combines CNN and Bidirectional LSTM 
(BiLSTM) modules to estimate vehicle positions dur-
ing GPS outages. Their method inputs filtered IMU data 
into CNN layers for spatial feature extraction, followed 
by BiLSTM layers for temporal modeling. Evaluated on 
GNSS-denied driving scenarios, their system achieved a 
mean RMSE of 2.94 m and demonstrated strong gener-
alization across diverse trajectories, without reliance on 
classical filters or visual sensors.
More recently, Zheng et al. (2025) proposed a hybrid 
passive radar localization approach that combines Accel-
erated Atomic Norm Optimization (AANO) with KANs. 
The method first performs high-resolution delay-Dop-
pler estimation to resolve closely spaced targets, followed 
by a KAN model that maps signal parameters to position 
estimates. It achieves up to 38.2% error reduction over 
Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) baselines with sub-meter 
accuracy in complex scenarios. The KAN module con-
verges rapidly within 20 epochs and generalizes well. 
However, performance may degrade under high noise 
or dense target conditions, and the framework requires 
careful signal preprocessing and remains computation-

These data-driven learning strategies reduce reliance 
on handcrafted models and extensive sensor calibration 
procedures. However, they typically necessitate large-
scale training datasets and robust regularization tech-
niques to mitigate overfitting and ensure generalization.

door UAV Dataset Generation (OS-RFODG) frame-

denied environments. The dataset generation pipeline 
incorporates a high-fidelity simulation stack that emu-
lates realistic UAV flight dynamics and terrain inter-
actions. PX4 serves as the flight control middleware, 
Gazebo provides a physics-based simulation environ-

reproduces the fine-scale geographic complexity of the 
real-world landscape. Flight scenarios were simulated 
over the desert and mountainous terrain of the Taif 
region in western Saudi Arabia, an area distinguished 
by steep escarpments and elevated plateaus.

In our ROS 2-based system, the preprocessing and syn-
chronization of multimodal data from the LiDAR, IMU, 
and barometric sensors are managed through a dedicated 
pipeline. The overall preprocessing pipeline is summa-
rized as follows:
•	 LiDAR preprocessing: Although a 3D LiDAR sensor 
was used, its output was converted into a single 1D 
beam representation. This was achieved by selecting 
one horizontal scan line (corresponding to the cen-
tral elevation layer) and extracting the distance value 
along that beam. The objective was to simplify spa-
tial perception into a compact, range-based feature 
for fusion while preserving the temporal characteris-
tics of the LiDAR stream. The resulting 1D distance 
measurement was then normalized for subsequent 
processing.
•	 IMU preprocessing: The raw IMU data provided 
linear acceleration and angular velocity along 
three axes. Each signal was bias-corrected and fil-
tered using a low-pass Butterworth filter to remove 
high-frequency noise. A short sliding window 
of 50-100  ms was applied to compute statistical 
descriptors such as mean and variance, preserving 
the short-term motion context.
•	 Barometric preprocessing: The barometric sensor 
operated at a lower sampling rate (approximately 
5  Hz). Its altitude estimates were interpolated 
according to the synchronized ROS 2 timestamps, 
ensuring that each LiDAR-IMU pair had a corre-
sponding barometric value. Temperature and pres-
sure-drift compensation were applied prior to nor-
malization.
•	 Temporal alignment: Because the three modalities 
differ in sampling frequency (LiDAR ≈ 10 Hz, IMU 
≈  200  Hz, barometer ≈  5  Hz), the closest times-
tamped samples were aligned across modalities. All 
synchronized readings were concatenated into a 
17-dimensional feature vector representing spatial 
(LiDAR beam), inertial (IMU), and environmental 
(barometric) cues.

reduction in position and velocity errors by leveraging 
IMU, barometer, magnetometer, and airspeed data. Yet, 
it requires frequent online retraining to adapt to envi-
ronmental changes. Similarly, the CNN-LSTM-Atten-
tion + FGO method  (Wang et al., 2025) reports RMSE 
reductions of 34.6% to 46.6% on vehicular datasets, but it 
relies heavily on factor graph optimization and lacks the 
robustness and generalizability of CLAK across varied 
aerial trajectories.
Both the AANO plus KAN method (Zheng et al., 2025) 
and the CLAK framework demonstrate the utility of 
Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks in improving localization 
accuracy through spline-based approximations and edge-
defined activations. The AANO plus KAN approach, 
developed for passive radar-based time difference of 
arrival localization, achieves a 38.2 percent reduction in 
error compared to multilayer perceptrons. This result 
illustrates the strength of KANs in modeling high-dimen-
sional delay-to-position relationships in static ground-
based geolocation tasks. However, the AANO plus KAN 
method is limited by its reliance on fixed sensor geometry 
and the absence of complementary sensing inputs. This 
constraint reduces its adaptability to mobile or aerial 
platforms and affects robustness in environments with 
multipath propagation, occlusions, or elevation changes. 
In contrast, the CLAK framework integrates KAN into 
a multimodal fusion pipeline that combines LiDAR, 
inertial measurements, barometric pressure, and digital 
elevation models. This design enables accurate three-
dimensional localization across diverse terrains and con-
ditions where GNSS is unavailable. While both methods 
benefit from the expressive capacity of KANs, CLAK 
addresses a broader set of challenges and provides higher 
robustness in UAV localization scenarios.
Vision-based localization methods such as PPP com-
bined with INS, vision, and LiDAR (Li et  al., 2023) 
achieve high accuracy in satellite-assisted conditions. 
However, their performance deteriorates during GNSS 
outages. For example, they accumulate around 3  m of 
error during a 60-second tunnel blackout. In contrast, 
CLAK exhibits lower 3D drift, averaging 1.92 m with a 
range of 1.62 to 2.40 m across seven GNSS-denied UAV 
flights. This outperforms the CNN-LSTM-Attention 
model, which records 2.30  m of drift, and the baseline 
LSTM model, which records 5.48 m of drift.
A key advantage of CLAK lies in its lightweight, real-
time operational capabilities. It operates at 2000 Hz, 
meeting the strict latency requirements of UAV control 
loops. Unlike vision-aided filters that depend on GPU 
acceleration and high-resolution image processing (Gha-
nizadegan and Hashim, 2025; Li et al., 2023a; He et al., 
2023), CLAK functions entirely on non-visual sensor 
data, including IMU, LiDAR, and barometric inputs. This 
enables near-instantaneous inference without the need 
for high-power onboard computation.
For example, vision-based systems like GLVL (Li et al., 
2023a) achieve 2.39 m of accuracy in 0.48 s under sparse 
visual conditions, and FoundLoc (He et  al., 2023) pro-
vides sub-meter accuracy in dynamic scenes. However, 
both methods rely heavily on visual data and computa-
tional resources, limiting their responsiveness and suit-
ability for embedded UAV platforms. In contrast, CLAK 
offers a more efficient and resilient solution for real-
time navigation in visually degraded or GNSS-denied 
environments.
Among CLAK’s strengths is its implicit map-matching 
capability enabled by digital elevation priors. By compar-
ing LiDAR range and barometric altitude with pre-loaded 
digital elevation models, CLAK effectively reduces ver-
tical drift, corrects local elevation biases, and preserves 
spatial accuracy even over steep escarpments and rug-
ged terrain. This capability enhances the system’s robust-

To further improve scalability and resilience, future

•	 Designing lightweight KAN variants with quantized

CLAK establishes a practical benchmark for GNSS 
denied UAV navigation by combining high-accuracy 
localization, millisecond-scale inference, and minimal 
sensor requirements. Its ability to generalize across ter-

Conclusion
This paper presented CNN-LSTM-Attention and Kol-
mogorov-Arnold Networks for non-visual UAV locali-
zation in GNSS-denied environments, a novel deep 
learning framework for UAV localization in GNSS-
denied environments, integrating convolutional encod-
ing, LSTM-based temporal modeling, attention pooling, 
and spline-based KAN layers. Through extensive trajec-
tory-level evaluations, CLAK consistently outperformed 
baseline models in both accuracy and robustness, achiev-
ing an average gain of 62.7% in RMSE and 65.2% in MAE 
compared to LSTM across seven real UAV trajectories. 
To enhance real-time applicability, future work will 
explore lightweight KAN variants and implement model 
compression techniques such as pruning, quantization, 
and knowledge distillation. Advances in neuromorphic 
and quantum edge hardware are also expected to facili-
tate CLAK’s integration on resource-constrained UAV 
platforms. In summary, CLAK offers a scalable, accurate, 
and self-contained solution for non-visual UAV localiza-
tion, with strong potential for real-time deployment sup-
ported by emerging edge hardware technologies.
