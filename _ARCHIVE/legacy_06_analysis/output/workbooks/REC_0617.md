Visual-Inertial Fusion for Indoor Autonomous Navigation of a Quadrotor 
Using ORB-SLAM
Seyed Jamal Haddadi, Eugenio B. Castelan
Department of Automation and Systems, Federal University of Santa Catarina, Florianopolis, Brazil
* Corresponding Author Email: Seyed.haddadi@posgrad.ufsc.br
Abstract— This brief addresses the accuracy of the pose 
estimation of a Low-Cost Quadrotor using visual SLAM and 
sensor data fusion for making an autonomous indoor navigation 
in a known GPS-denied environment. To perform this approach, 
two kinds of modules are employed. For the former, a 
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) system is used. 
This SLAM system uses Oriented Fast and rotated BRIEF (ORB) 
features, also known as ORB_SLAM. The second module is 
Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that is applied for combining the 
obtained pose from ORB_SLAM and Inertial Measurement Unit 
(IMU). Since in this paper the SLAM process is performed by the 
frontal monocular camera of Quadrotor, so developing scale 
factor estimation in order to calculate the scale of the map with 
minimum error is necessary. In addition, a Proportional Integral 
Derivative (PID) is utilized to conduct the maneuver of the robot. 
All of these processes are done by sending data via Wi-Fi to a
ground station in order to perform the data processing. To show 
the performance of the proposed algorithm, two experiments are 
carried out on an AR-Drone 2.0 quadrotor. The first one is 
position holding that shows the robustness of the estimated 
position of the system in hovering situation after take-off. The 
second one is an indoor trajectory tracking that illustrates the 
difference between the downward camera output position and the 
estimated position.
Keywords— Quadrotor, ORB-SLAM, Scale Factor Estimation,
Sensor Fusion, Pose Estimation
I.
INTRODUCTION 
An impressive interest in controlling the Unmanned Aerial 
Vehicles (UAVs) such as Quadrotors are significantly 
increasing among researchers. Applications of these UAVs that 
can autonomously operate in outdoor/indoor environments 
have been widely defined in tasks such as traffic monitoring, 
rescue operations, surveillance, transportation, and hobbyist. In 
addition, for all these applications an imperative for UAV 
autonomy is the ability of self-localization in the environment. 
Indeed, a robust accurate localization is crucial in order to 
achieve high-performance flight and to interact with the 
environment. Also, due to deny the GPS in the indoor 
environment and lack of self-localization, the autonomous 
maneuver of Quadrotor is more challenging.
Up to now, many researchers have reported the solutions for 
indoor navigation challenge with quadrotor by using LIDAR 
scanner [1-2], Red Green Blue and Depth (RGBD) [3], Motion 
Capture (MoCap) System and [4] Monocular cameras [5]. In 
[6], an autonomous indoor flight of quadrotor using a
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) system was 
provided. In this paper, in order to achieve accurate 3D 
positions and velocities, an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) has 
been employed for sensor fusion that fuses the measured 
navigation data with the obtained pose from the monocular 
camera. Also, a fuzzy visual servoing approach for controlling 
a Quadrotor is presented in [7], which allows performing take-
off, hovering, and landing without a marker. In their method, 
the forward-facing camera is used to obtain visual pose 
estimates using Parallel Tracking and Mapping (PTAM) based 
on SLAM.
Besides the pose estimation, estimating map scale factor and 
horizontal drifting elimination are other challenges that have 
been explained in [8]. Concerned on the same subject, J. Engle 
et al in [9] presented an autonomous visual navigation of a 
commercial quadrotor using PTAM. In this paper, they used 
absolute scale factor estimation of generated map and EKF for 
sensor fusion in order to have accurate visual odometry and 
elimination of falsely tracked frames. 
The main objectives of this study are: firstly, to improve the
pose estimation accuracy of Quadrotor and to make an 
autonomous indoor navigation in a known GPS-denied 
environment; and secondly, to implement the appropriate map 
online scale factor estimation of monocular SLAM that leads to 
have an accurate map and pose.
The two differences between this work and, [8] and [9] are; 
i: to change the input of EKF from PTAM to ORB-SLAM. ii:
To use the integration of horizontal accelerations in order to 
obtain horizontal velocities, whereas, in [8] and [9], horizontal 
velocities had been acquired by the downward camera.   
To this end, the lightweight frontal camera of an AR-Drone 
2.0 quadrotor is used for pose estimation. So that the quadrotor 
broadcasts the data of camera as the video stream using Wi-Fi
to the computer in the ground station in which the ROS 
(Robotic Operation System) software framework is already run. 
In this step, the video stream is processed by ORB-SLAM in 
order to determine the 3D position of quadrotor and the 3D 
spare map of the flight environment. To achieve a more 
accurate visual SLAM, calculating and applying a scale factor 
in ORB-SLAM measurements has to be done. To this end, the 
raw image of this frontal camera should be sent to ORB 
Wrapper node and by combining the output of ORB-SLAM and 
altitude (by Sonar) in scale estimator, the map scale factor can 
be 
online 
calculated. 
Using 
EKF, 
the 
ORB-SLAM 
measurements are fused with IMU as a navigation onboard 
sensors measurement of Quadrotor due to reach the more 
accurate measurements.

ODOMETRY BASED ON VISION
A. Monocular ORB-SLAM
The ORB-SLAM is a feature-based monocular Visual 
SLAM system that, as the main idea behind of it, uses a binary 
descriptor called ORB [14]. This ORB binary descriptor is very 
fast, rotation invariant and resistant to noise based on the 
estimation of the key-point orientation using the intensity 
centroid of the image patch. In the ORB-SLAM algorithm, the 
features are directly extracted from the imagery. Such an 
algorithm is different from other monocular SLAM systems 
such as Large-Scale-Direct SLAM because it does not extract 
features from the imagery but directly works on pixel intensity 
gradient of imagery. ORB-SLAM can operate in real time and 
also in various environments such as indoor, outdoor, small and 
wide. In addition, robustness against intense motions, re-
localizing and full automatic initialization are other advantages 
of this system.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This paper addressed an improvement of pose estimation 
accuracy and visual-inertial navigation of a Low-Cost 
Quadrotor to make an autonomous indoor navigation in a 
known GPS-denied environment. This algorithm is performed 
by using a SLAM-based approach that also is known 
ORB_SLAM, and also EKF. In addition, we calculated the 
scale factor that is obtained as online and it is one of the major 
challenges in using monocular SLAM. Meanwhile, a PID 
control is used to control the robot during the test. To verify the 
proposed algorithm, two kinds of experimental tests are 
accomplished that the first one is position holding and the 
second one is trajectory tracking. The tests results showed that 
the combining the ORB_SLAM data and IMU data by EKF 
could be an appropriate method to improve the position 
accuracy of quadrotor, especially in indoor trajectory tracking.
