Study on angle-only relative navigation for
unmanned aerial vehicle formation in
GPS-denied environment
Zixuan Zheng1, Sha Wang2, Baichun Gong2, Erlong Su3 and
Hefei Huang2
Abstract
To satisfy the autonomous relative navigation demand of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) formation in a GPS-denied
environment, an angle-only relative navigation algorithm is proposed. First, the relative states in 3-dimension space are
decoupled into relative motion along the horizontal plane and altitude by applying the relative azimuth between the north-
east-down global frame and the body-ﬁxed horizontal frame belonging to UAVs. Second, a relative dynamic and mea-
surement model considering the measurement deviation of sensors are established. This solves the problem of colored
noise in a majority of sensors used in practical engineering. Third, a consensus constraint model of relative states is
established by introducing a geometric constraint among formation members. Additionally, a consensus-extended Kalman
ﬁlter is designed. Finally, the angle-only relative navigation method is validated using standard Monte Carlo simulations. The
simulation results show that all the states of the system are observable when the observability criterion is satisﬁed. The
relative position accuracy achieved using gyros with a drift rate of 50°/h, accelerometers with 1 mg deviation, and optical
cameras with 1° Gaussian white noise is within 10 m (3σ). Additionally, the method proposed in this study can be used to
estimate the sensor measurement deviations effectively. Furthermore, it can be potentially applied to improve the absolute
navigation accuracy of UAVs.
Keywords
Unmanned aerial vehicle, consensus-extended Kalman ﬁlter, angle-only navigation, observability analysis

Introduction
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) formation, a set of aerial
robots, that is, drones that work together to achieve
a speciﬁc goal, has attracted increased attention in
military and civilian applications in recent decades.1,2
Compared to a single UAV, distributed intelligent sys-
tems based on networked autonomous UAVs are ex-
pected to be more capable, ﬂexible, and robust in many
applications,
including
mapping,
inspection,
and
detection.3–5 For example, in a ﬂight mission, the failure
of a single UAV directly lead to the failure of the mission,
but a redundancy in the UAV formation design allows
a backup UAV to complete the mission when a member
of the formation fails. The ﬂight mission of a UAV
formation is relatively complex, and it is necessary to
control the relative position and orientation of the for-
mation members in the coordinated ﬂight process so that
the entire formation can maintain a certain geometric
construction.6 However, the premise of completing
formation control is precise relative navigation among
the formation members.
Accurate relative navigation information can be ob-
tained in a short time using the inertial navigation
equipment (inertial measurement unit, IMU) carried by the
UAV, but the relative navigation error diverges over time
as the IMU’s error drifts. Presently, most UAVs use IMU
combined with GPS localization, which has been widely
adopted and researched owing to the low-cost, high-rate
output frequency, and high reliability. However, satellite
navigation information is prone to interference and
shading.7 Therefore, it is vital to ﬁnd a relative navigation

The range-only relative navigation algorithms for

decade. Bradley8 presented a range-only relative

Mohammed9 improved the observability of a range-

communication beacons on a single UAV. However,

actual size of the UAV. Therefore, this scheme is more
suitable for relative navigation under short-range

relative positioning method of UWB ranging. Helm

range information of the camera optical ﬂow method.
However, the velocity measurement accuracy of the
optical ﬂow method is greatly affected by the envi-

measurement information is unavailable in some cases.
Gong et al.16 presented a range-based collaborative
relative navigation algorithm for ﬁxed-wing UAV

wideband and displacement information of each air-
craft from each origin measured by V-SLAM.

achieve relative navigation for the formation may still be

The main contribution of this study is to develop an
angle-only relative navigation method for UAV for-
mation. Although this method is independent of GPS
location, camera-based velocity measurement, and
multi-antenna ranging, it can provide sufﬁciently ac-

control by integrating angle-measuring cameras and
other commonly used low-cost sensors, such as IMU,
altimeters, magnetometers, and pitot tubes. Unlike in
previous research, the sensor deviations estimated by
this angle-only relative navigation method can also be

presented in the last section.
Problem statement and relative model
Problem statement
In this study, a tri-UAV formation ﬂight mission in a GPS-
denied environment is considered. Assuming that each
UAV is capable of absolute measurements from IMU,
altimeter, pitot tube, and magnetometer and relative
measurement from optical camera, the key problem solved
in this work is to integrate absolute and relative meas-
urements to achieve an acceptable estimation of relative
position and velocity among UAV formation.
Reference frame deﬁnition
The reference frames used in this study are shown in Figure
1. The global reference frame, denoted as n, considers the
north-east-down (NED) geographic frame, which is as-
sumed to be an inertial frame. The origin of the body-ﬁxed
frame, denoted as b, is located at the center of gravity of the
UAV, and the directions of the three axes point front-right-
down. The origin of the body-ﬁxed horizontal frame, de-
noted as h, is also located at the center of gravity of the
UAV, but the x and y axes are always in the horizontal
plane, whereas the z-axis points toward the ground. The
frame h is deﬁned in the process of rotating the frame n to
coincide with the frame b according to the 321 rotation
sequence, and the Euler angle relative to the frame b is the
azimuth φ (north-east is a positive direction). Euler angles
relative to the frame n are the pitch angle θ and roll angle γ.

Conclusion
A new angle-only cooperative relative navigation algo-
rithm for UAV formation, which fuses an optical camera
with other commonly used low-cost sensors, such as in-
ertial measurement units, altimeters, magnetometers, and
pitot tubes, was analyzed in this study. Accurate relative
navigation for a formation composed of at least three
UAVs was achieved with relatively light equipment, low
power consumption, and low processing cost in a GPS-
denied environment. The research work of this dissertation
includes four main aspects.
(1) An accurate relative dynamic model and measure-
ment model between UAVs in the horizontal plane
were established, and sensor measurement deviations
were considered to realize the whitening of non-white
Gaussian noise.
(2) The Lie derivative criterion was introduced to ana-
lyze the observability of the angle-only relative
navigation system, and sufﬁcient conditions for the
system to be observable were obtained. First, the
velocity and acceleration of a single UAV cannot be
parallel to each other. Second, the relative position
and velocity between UAVs cannot be parallel.
(3) A decentralized estimation strategy based on the
CEKF was designed, and a consensus algorithm was
constructed using multiple physical constraints.
(4) The validity of the angle-only relative navigation
algorithm and the superiority of the consensus-
extended Kalman ﬁlter were validated using stan-
dard Monte Carlo simulations. All the states are
observable when the trajectories of the UAVs satisfy
the observability criterion.
The angle-only relative navigation algorithm proposed
in this study was based on three UAVs. When more UAVs
will participate in the formation, the decentralized strategy
and nonlinear estimation algorithm will become more
complicated. This will be the focus of future research.
