Abstract— This paper presents an innovative approach to 
enhance the navigation of UAVs in GNSS denied environments. 
Considering the limited space, power, and size of small UAVs, the 
proposed approach does not require any sensors on the UAV. The 
typical repeated dynamic patterns of such UAVs are related to the 
actuators to offer a useful information  for estimating the UAV 
navigation states. Machine learning (ML) classifier has been 
employed to detect these repeated dynamic patterns, then 
according 
to 
the 
detected 
pattern, 
an 
appropriate 
constraint/update is utilized to enhance the navigation solution 
through  EKF to obtain a better estimate of the UAV states. 
Different test scenarios where conducted to verify the ability of the 
proposed approach to aid the INS solution during GNSS signal 
outages. The solution after fusing the Vheicle Model (VM) is 
enhaced by 98% compared to low cost stand-alone IMU solution. 
 
Index Terms— Extended Kalman Filter (EKF), vehicle 
modeling (VM), machine learning (ML), classification, Global 
Navigation Satellite System (GNSS), Inertial Measurement Unit 
(IMU), and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs). 
I. INTRODUCTION 
ECENTLY, small or micro unmanned aerial vehicle 
(UAV) plays an important role in different fields of our 
daily life, as the applications utilizing UAVs to save effort, 
time, and human life is massively increasing. Regardless of the 
assigned task, the UAV must be able to operate autonomously 
which requires accurate, precise, and robust navigation system. 
Most of the commercial UAVs depend on the fusion of Inertial 
Navigation Systems (INS) and Global Navigation Satellite 
Systems (GNSS) to get an appropriate estimation of its states 
(position, velocity, and attitudes) [1]. The small scale of these 
small and micro UAVs imposes some limitations on the utilized 
sensors due to the limited space, size, weight, power, and cost 
[2].  The small scale of these UAVs enabled  operation in more 
challenging environments like caves, tunnels, urban and natural 
canyons, and downtown areas. Regardless of the UAV scale, 
the GNSS plays a vital role in bounding the drift exhibited by 
the IMU and supply the vehicle with its absolute position. The 
problem arises when the GNSS signal is blocked, as the 
navigation solution will be deteriorate due to the exhibited drift 
in the IMU sensros, especially when the utilized IMU is a low-
cost MEMS-based sensor. Therefore, using additional sensors 
to aid the INS and limit the navigation solution drift during 
GNSS signal blockage is necessary to bound the drift and aid 
the navigation solution. 
II. RELATED WORK 
In order to compensate for the GNSS signal loss, and mitigate 
the stand-alone INS solution drift, different sensors/methods 
have been utilized. One of the alternative solutions to replace 
GNSS during blockage is the Signal of Opportunity (SoOP) [3]. 
SoOP mainly depends on signals from stationary beacons that 
act as GNSS satellites, these signals are mainly not intended for 
navigation, like  Radio Frequency (RF) signals [4] [5] [6]. 
Cameras also are considered one of the mainly utilized sensors 
during GNSS signal outage periods, either in Vision Based 
(VB) [7], or Vision Aided [1] approaches, because of the 
lightweight, low power consumption and suitable cost. Despite 
these specifications which make cameras suitable for such task, 
it still suffers from lack of features, small base distance when 
using stereo cameras, or environmental changes, which may 
deteriorate

states. 
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) [8] [9] [10] 
is another method to substitute the GNSS signal unavailability, 
which mainly depends on the simultaneous estimation of the 
vehicle states and map formation. SLAM can be implemented 
with the aid of different sensors like cameras [11], sonar, or 
LIDAR [12].  LIDAR based SLAM suffers from some 
disadvantages if it's utilized with small and micro UAVs, which 
is the weight, cost, size, and power consumption. Radars also 
can be used to bound the IMU drift during GNSS outage [13]. 
Although Radars suffers from the same drawbacks as LIDAR, 
but it is more practical than cameras or LIDAR in some aspects, 
as it is less sensitive to environmental changes [14]. Due to the 
recent development of Radar manufacturing, the development 
of micro Radars suitable for small and micro UAVs navigation 
became feasible. To proof the great potential of using small and 
micro Radar in UAVs Navigation, [14] [15] utilized micro 
pulsed wave and continuous wave Radar respectively to 
enhance navigation performance where the Radar showed great 
potential in navigation enhancement.

sensors to replace the GNSS during signal outage periods, and 
mitigate the IMU drift, they share some similar drawbacks 
when utilized with small/micro UAVs. All of the aiding sensors

The authors in [16] proposed a new approach to enhance the 
navigation solution and bound the IMU errors without adding 
any extra load to the UAV. This new approach mainly depends 
on the additive information which can be gained from the 
vehicle dynamic model (VDM). Such approach exploits the fact 
that the INS is an independent system that did not require any 
knowledge about the vehicle dynamics. Also, the vehicle 
dynamics relates the body accelerations and rotations to the 
commands from the autopilot to the actuators. During their 
research they took advantage of the VDM to estimate the 
navigation states (positions, velocities, and attitudes). Their 
approach fuses two systems through extended Kalman filter 
(EKF). The first system which is the main system model is the 
strap down inertial navigation system (SDINS) that depends on 
low-cost IMU (accelerometers and gyroscopes) to estimate the 
navigation states. The second system (aiding/update) is the 
VDM that estimates the navigation states resulting from the 
variation of forces and torques acting on the UAV airframe, 
calculated from the dynamic model state equations. The results 
of this fusion are the navigation states, the IMU biases and the 
VDM coefficient errors. In order to use VDM as an aiding 
system, the UAV dynamic parameters must be modeled, which 
require special equipment and massive effort to model every 
part of the UAV including any attachments. At the beginning, 
they assumed a perfect dynamic model which made the VDM 
not effective to enhance the navigation solution. So, they 
expanded the filter states (28 states) to include the aerodynamic 
parameters. By increasing the filter states in [16], they increased 
also the required computations and filter complexity. 
 To reduce the computations, the authors in [17] used unified 
Kalman filter instead of EKF. Unified Kalman filter permits 
two system models for state propagations each with its own 
noise and level of confidence. With the aid of this technique, 
the duplicate states are eliminated, and the filter complexity and 
computations are decreased. Following the same track, the 
authors in [18] tried to benefit from the useful information 
gained from the VDM while decreasing the computations and 
complexities. So, they did not include all the VDM state 
equations, in order to decrease the computations and to decrease 
the filter states. Although they were required to model 688 
different part to be able to utilize the helicopter VDM as an 
aiding system. Similarly, in [19] and based on their preceding 
work in [17], a massive effort is required to be able to identify 
and model the vehicle parameters. So, instead of including all 
the state equations, they utilize only the translation state 
equations. To verify their approach through simulation, they 
assumed no wind and perfect vehicle modeling process. Then 
they carried out a hardware experiment on MikroKopter 
quadrotor in both indoor and outdoor environments. As a 
reference for the indoor experiment they used Hokuyo 
URG04LX laser range finder, while for the outdoor they took 
the GPS as the reference solution. In both experiments, the 
result of the integration between the VDM and INS shows 
enhancement in the navigation states compared to IMU solution 
alone.  
The authors in [20] took different configuration for the EKF 
as the VDM act as the main system model, while the GNSS or 
the IMU when available, acts as an update for the filter. Their 
filter was expanded to include vehicle dynamic parameters and 
wind parameters. Their configuration was tested through 
simulation with an error in the modeling parameters around 
10%. 
III. SYSTEM OVERVIEW 
Despite the potential of utilizing the VDM as in aiding system 
to enhance the navigation solution and bound the INS drift 
during GNSS signal outages, the VDM as an aiding system still 
faces some drawbacks/difficulties to be an effective aid during 
the GNSS signal blockage. These difficulties are related to the 
requirement for special equipment to be able to model the 
vehicle parameters, the massive effort required during the 
modeling process, and the unrealistic assumptions made during 
the modeling process. Moreover, the existing perturbations 
during modeling of these parameters may lead to the 
ineffectiveness of the VDM as an aiding system. These errors 
lead to unbounded drift in the VDM solution, similar to the drift 
exhibited by the IMU as a stand-alone system, because of the 
two required integration processes of the dynamic state 
equations to calculate the navigation states. 
   The proposed approach in this paper limits these 
drawbacks/difficulties regarding the unrealistic assumption 
during the modeling process (unbalanced weights, CG shift, un-
modeled effects, same efficiencies for similar parts, etc.), 
special equipment required, and modeling perturbations. The 
proposed approach adopts a machine learning algorithm to 
recognize the repeated dynamic pattern of the UAV during the 
GNSS signal availability. As the UAV typically carry out 
different dynamic patterns during the mission that can be 
related to the actuators action according to the vehicle model 
(VM). Some of these dynamic patterns are repeated massively 
in a typical flight mission. According to these detected patterns 
different updates/constraints can be applied to the navigation 
system to enhance the estimation of the navigation states. 
While the VDM approach utilizes the vehicle dynamic state 
equations (angular velocity state equations, kinematic Euler 
state equations, velocity state equation, and position state 
equations) to estimate the navigation states, the proposed 
approach identify the useful dynamic patterns using the 
actuators that can aid the navigation estimation instead of 
directly estimating the navigation states. Such approach limits 
the struggling exhibited during the modeling process, avoid 
unrealistic assumptions and also avoids the error accumulating 
double integration procedure. The main challenges in the 
proposed approach are the appropriate choice of the ML inputs 
(actuators combinations) that efficiently typifies the vehicle 
dynamic states. The second challenge, is how to employ these 
detected dynamic patterns towards enhancing the navigation 
states estimation. 
The proposed approach was verified through quadcopter 
simulation environment [21].

The proposed approach was tested through quadcopter 
simulation environment (MATLAB). One of the main 
challenges in the proposed algorithm is the selection of 
appropriate inputs during the learning process that will aid in 
accurately detecting and identifying the repeated patterns 
during the flight. It is noticed that during mission either in 
simulation or in a real environment, the quadcopter movement 
can be classified according to its motor behavior into a set of 
repeated patterns shown in figures (6, and 7). 
 These patterns were identified and detected by two machine 
learning classifiers for each direction (X, Y, and Z). One for 
detecting if there is angular rotation or not, and the other for 
detecting if there is velocity or not. The output of these two 
classifiers is of binary format. If the output from the classifier 
is 0, this means there is no rotation/velocity in this given 
direction, while if the output is 1, this means there is 
rotation/velocity in this given direction.  
 The adequate choice of inputs to the machine learning 
module greatly affect its performance. As the modes to be 
classified are not mainly dependent on the instantaneous motor 
speed values but rather on their differences, averages and 
derivatives, such differences should be fed also as inputs to the 
classifier to enable it to build a fast and reliable mapping 
between the inputs and the output and to help the classifier to 
avoid deep trees while short ones can represent the 
classification decision more accurately. 
After choosing the appropriate inputs that enable the 
classifier to detect these modes, the second challenge arises 
which is how this information from the classifiers will aid to 
enhance the INS solution during GNSS outage? and how it's 
going to be fused with the IMU through EKF?. The outputs 
from the two classifiers are combined together in each direction 
and it is dealt with as a truth table with four possible outcomes, 
(1,1), (1,0), (0,0), and (0,1) each outcome represents a specific 
mode as shown in table (1). 
Generally, the ML-VM will act as velocity update. Zero 
velocity update will be imposed when the zero-velocity mode 
is detected. When constant velocity mode is detected, the 
velocity at time t will be updated with the same velocity at t-1.

VII. CONCLUSION 
Small and micro-UAVs mainly rely on GNSS system to 
bound the drift in the IMU to achieve an acceptable estimate of 
the navigation states (position, velocity, and attitudes). During 
GNSS outage, the INS solution will be deteriorated massively 
and the UAV cannot navigate autonomously during this period. 
Different aiding sensors were utilized to replace the GNSS 
system during GNSS outage to limit this drift and enhance the 
navigation solution. The small scale of these micro UAVs 
imposes a limitation on the aiding system, due to the limited 
space, power, weight and cost of this kind of UAVs. Vehicle 
modeling as an aiding approach considerate this limitation but 
requires special equipment/software and massive effort to be 
able to model every single part of the UAV to be able to reach 
an appropriate model to enhance the navigation solution. Also 
perturbations in the modeling parameters may lead to massive 
drift that resembles the IMU drift during GNSS outage. 
To be able to benefit from the knowledge that can be gained 
from the VM to enhance the navigation solution without the 
need for special equipment/software, massive effort, and time, 
machine learning vehicle model approach is presented in this
