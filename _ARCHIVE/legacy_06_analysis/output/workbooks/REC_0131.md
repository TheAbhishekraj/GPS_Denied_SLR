Abstract—A digital twin (DT)-based framework for autonomous drone nav-
igation in GPS-denied indoor environments is presented in this letter.
A real-time virtual replica of the drone enables precise control, trajec-
tory optimization, and feedback. A 224 cm × 224 cm arena with ArUco
markers deﬁnes the coordinate system, while an overhead camera and
OpenCV provide vision-based localization. The ESP32-controlled drone
uses the YOLOv11-nano model for obstacle detection and a lightweight
transformer model, Depth Anything (Lihe-Young-small-hf), for monocular
depth estimation—eliminating the need for LiDAR or stereo sensors. De-
tected obstacles are mapped into a 3-D grid for Dijkstra-based path planning. Real-time synchronization between
the physical drone and its DT is achieved via message queuing telemetry transport (MQTT) within a robot operating
system–Gazebo environment. The proposed DT system achieves an RMS trajectory deviation of approximately 0.015 m,
representing an order-of-magnitude improvement compared with DT-based uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) navigation
studies under similar experimental conditions, and maintains stable detection accuracy (mean average precision ≈0.994)
throughout the maneuver. The proposed system offers a scalable low-cost solution for indoor UAV auton-
omy with potential applications in warehouse automation, disaster management, and intelligent surveillance.
Index Terms—Sensor applications, ArUco marker, Digital Twin (DT) synchronization, Dijkstra’s algorithm, Robotics and Digital Twin, You
Only Look Once (YOLO).
I. INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of autonomous systems has accelerated the
adoption of Uncrewed Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) for diverse applications,
such as industrial inspection, environmental monitoring, and indoor lo-
gistics. Despite this progress, achieving precise ﬂight control, efﬁcient
obstacle avoidance, and reliable real-time decision-making remains
challenging, particularly in GPS-denied or cluttered environments. To
addresstheseissues,thisworkintegratesadigitaltwin(DT)framework
with the UAV’s control and perception system, creating a synchronized
virtual replica that continuously mirrors the drone’s physical state. The
DT enables predictive trajectory planning, real-time error correction,
and situational awareness through sensor fusion and artiﬁcial intel-
ligence (AI)-based control algorithms. This hybrid system enhances
both safety and autonomy by allowing ﬂight conditions and navigation
strategies to be simulated and optimized before execution in the real
world. Furthermore, the proposed architecture reduces the dependence
on high-cost sensors, such as LiDAR or stereo cameras, making it
adaptable to lightweight and resource-constrained UAV platforms. By
focusing on real-time synchronization and embedded intelligence, this
study contributes to the growing intersection of DT technology and
UAV systems. The proposed framework establishes a foundation for
reliable indoor navigation and scalable applications in smart manufac-
turing, warehouse automation, and disaster response.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
The application of DT technology to UAVs has attracted signiﬁcant
research interest due to its potential to enhance autonomy, reliability,
and control intelligence. Previous works have demonstrated how
DTs can enable fault diagnosis [1], predictive maintenance, and
system optimization across industrial and aerospace domains [2].
Wang and Du [3] discussed entity virtualization for real-time data
exchange and predictive control, while Demirbaga et al. [4] utilized
Bayesian-network-based DTs to improve predictive fault detection
and safety monitoring in UAV communication networks. These studies
collectively underscore DTs’ ability to link virtual and physical UAV
models; however, most focus on isolated functionalities rather than
an integrated real-time control loop. A major limitation in the
existing literature is the fragmented nature of DT implementations.
Many works emphasize speciﬁc use cases, such as predictive
maintenance [5], [6], but lack uniﬁed frameworks supporting
closed-loop synchronization and adaptive control in dynamic indoor
environments. In addition, challenges, such as high computational
costs, nonstandardized architectures, and limited scalability, hinder
the deployment of lightweight DTs for UAVs. Zhou et al. [7]
highlighted advancements in DT development for manufacturing and
embedded control systems, yet these approaches remain constrained
by insufﬁcient real-time bidirectional data exchange and limited
Internet of Things (IoT) integration, both crucial for UAV autonomy.
Similarly, federated DT frameworks [8] have been proposed to

perception and control, which are vital for aerial robotics. Recent

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSIONS
A. System Model
This section presents the hardware architecture, software stack,
control algorithms, and the overall system workﬂow that enables the
autonomous functionality of the drone, as depicted graphically in
Fig. 1. The key objectives of this project are as follows.
1) Design and develop the embedded system for autonomous drone
control: Build a robust embedded system capable of managing
autonomous ﬂight in indoor environments.
2) Create a DT for real-time simulation and testing: Develop a
high-ﬁdelity virtual model of the physical drone that allows for
continuous real-time synchronization and testing.
3) Optimize drone navigation and obstacle avoidance for indoor
environments: Use advanced control algorithms and AI-based
models to enhance the drone’s ability to autonomously avoid
obstacles and optimize its ﬂight path.
B. Software Stack
The software stack for this DT architecture is designed to ensure
seamless real-time interaction between the drone and its virtual coun-
terpart, enabling continuous synchronization and effective control:
1) Robot Operating System (ROS2): Middleware for component
communication and data exchange reliably;
2) Gazebo simulator: Real-time indoor environment and behavior
simulation for precise simulation and testing of ﬂight scenarios;
3) You Only Look Once (YOLO): Deep learning algorithm
for real-time object detection to aid obstacle avoidance;
LifeYoung/depth-anything-small-hf model is a lightweight
transformer-based monocular depth estimation framework in-
tegrated into the proposed system for real-time 3D perception
4) MQTT protocol: Low-latency data transmission for synchro-
nization;
5) Apache Kafka: It ensures robust data streaming for real-time
updates or streaming and processing using open-source libraries
6) Prometheus and Grafana: They monitor and visualize drone
metrics.

C. Simulation and Analysis
By leveraging simulation tools such as Gazebo and real-time data
synchronization between the physical drone and its DT, the system’s
efﬁciency, reliability, and robustness are validated before deployment
in real-world scenarios. Our simulation involved testing the YOLOv8
model for object detection in an indoor environment, where it identiﬁes
obstacles and warehouse objects for autonomous navigation. In addi-
tion, a Fusion 360 CAD model of the drone was also created to ensure
design compatibility with indoor navigation requirements. Simultane-
ous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms are employed for
real-time mapping and localization, ensuring accurate movement and
obstacle avoidance. Initially, Turtle-Bot3 is used to simulate navigation
with Dijkstra’s algorithm for path planning, validating strategies in a
2-D maze. The results from this simulation, as shown in Fig. 2, are used
to reﬁne the approach, which is then adapted for drone navigation in
3-D environments.

While the results demonstrate strong tracking precision and reliable
perception, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the eval-
uation is based on an indoor, visually controlled environment where
ArUco markers provide stable metric references; performance in out-
door or partially textured environments may exhibit higher localization
drift. Second, the trajectory reconstruction is derived from video-based
detection rather than direct onboard ﬂight logs, which may introduce
small temporal or pixel-level uncertainties despite YOLOv8 nano’s
high detection accuracy. Third, the dynamics modeled in the DT do
not incorporate full aerodynamic disturbances or actuator saturation
effects, and therefore, the reported RMS of 0.015 m should be inter-
preted as the accuracy achievable under low-disturbance conditions.
Future work will integrate onboard sensing, more detailed quadrotor
dynamics, and hardware-in-the-loop testing to validate the framework
across larger scale and higher dynamic scenarios.
IV. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
This work presents a lightweight DT-assisted indoor navigation
framework for GPS-denied UAVs that integrates YOLOv8-nano per-
ception, ArUco-based metric precise localization (224 × 224 cm
arena), and a synchronized virtual–physical control loop. The sys-
tem integrated YOLOv11 nano for real-time obstacle detection and
the Depth-Anything-small-hf transformer model for monocular depth
estimation, enabling 3-D perception without LiDAR or stereo sen-
sors. A ROS2–Gazebo-based DT was implemented to achieve real-
time simulation, control, and path planning, facilitating autonomous
ﬂight using an ESP32-based controller. Experimental reconstruction
of drone trajectory from recorded experimental video shows that the
proposed system achieves an RMS trajectory deviation of approx-
imately 0.015 m, representing an order-of-magnitude improvement
compared with DT-based UAV navigation studies that typically report
RMSE values in the range of 0.07–0.10 m [11], [12] for quadrotors
under comparable planning and tracking tasks. The framework further
reduces latency-induced drift, achieves smooth trajectory execution
around cluttered obstacles, and maintains stable detection accuracy
(mAP ≈0.994) throughout the maneuvers, in comparison to mAP of
0.65 on the VOC 2007 dataset [16] conﬁrming high detection accuracy.
These results highlight that the proposed method provides high-ﬁdelity
trajectory reproduction and robust navigation performance in compact
GPS-denied indoor environments, while also offering a repeatable
evaluation pipeline that can be extended to validate perception, plan-
ning, and control modules in future UAV systems.
