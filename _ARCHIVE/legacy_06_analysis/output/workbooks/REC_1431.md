Abstract — This research introduces a novel approach to 
enhance the robustness of autonomous UAV target localization 
in GPS-denied environments. Typically, localization in such 
environments relies on utilizing received signal strength (RSS) 
transmitted by the target and employing the Q-learning 
algorithm to determine agent actions. Usually, there are two 
approaches to defining Q-learning's state, both using only a 
single variable as the Q-learning’s state. One of those approaches 
is potentially hard to converge, while the other is susceptible to 
unrealistic assumptions in a real-world deployment. This study 
proposes an improved Q-learning algorithm by incorporating 
multiple variables as states, addressing the limitations of existing 
approaches that use a single variable. In this research, it will be 
proven that if the assumptions don’t hold, the performance of 
the proposed method by another research will be messed up. In 
addition, it's proved that a new approach is proposed in this 
research able to outperform the approach using only single-
variable as Q-learning state in terms of speeds of convergence 
and robustness, even when the assumptions are not met. 
I. INTRODUCTION

grown significantly. The global UAV market is valued at over 
$7.7 billion and is projected to reach $17.5 billion by 2028, 
with a compound annual growth rate of 14.7% from 2022 to

control systems, making them adaptable to a wide range of 
applications. One such application is search and rescue (SAR) 
operations, which are crucial for assisting people in danger or 
distress, particularly during natural disasters.

immediately. The problem with search operations is that they

rescue team localize the target or the victim. However, another 
problem that often occurs is the need for knowledge of the 
rescue team in the environment in which natural disasters 
occur. There are also some situations where the GPS is 
inaccessible for the rescue team. Those facts motivate many

II. RELATED WORKS 
Since the main focus of our research is improving the 
performance of target localization problems using the Q-
learning algorithm, we will only discuss other research that 
uses Q-learning for their target localization based on the RSS. 
In typical target localization using RSS based on Q-learning, 
there are two different approaches how defining the Q-
learning state. The first approach involves dividing the UAV 
search area into grids of a specific size, with each grid 
represented by a set of RSS values. This approach assumes 
that no two grids have the same RSS value, which may be an 
unrealistic assumption [8], [9]. This approach is also not in 
accordance with the nature of the propagation of RF waves, 
which propagate uniformly in all directions, which means that 
all points that have the same distance from the propagation 
source will have the same RSS value [11]. The second one is 
the state defined by some intervals of RSS range (e.g., state 1 
for RSS value < -50 dBm, state 2 for RSS value between 72 
and 50 dBm, etc.) [6], [7]. Worth knowing that those two 
approaches only use a single variable to define the Q-learning 
state. Using these two approaches may result in a problem: 
representing multiple distinct real-world locations with a 
single Q-learning state. Those approaches limit the agent to 
having the same action for multiple locations since a single 
Q-learning state only represents those multiple locations. This 
can prevent the agent from finding the optimal policy as 
various locations may require different actions to reach the 
target.

research proposes using two variables as the Q-learning state.

VI. CONCLUSION 
Our research examined the approach used in previous 
studies for RSS-based target localization. These studies 
employed a single-variable Q-learning state using only RSS, 
which challenged the algorithm's convergence due to the 
uniform propagation of RF waves in all directions. 
Consequently, the RSS values were identical for locations 
equidistant from the target, making it difficult for the agent to 
determine its actual relative position. To overcome this 
limitation, our paper proposes the incorporation of multiple 
variables to define the Q-learning state. The proposed 
algorithm 
surpasses 
the 
single-variable 
approach's 
performance in reducing the number of steps required for the 
UAV to reach its target from the starting position. 
Additionally, we examined the assumption made by some 
existing research when creating simulation environments 
where no two locations have the same RSS value. In such 
environments, our proposed algorithm performs comparably 
to the Q-learning algorithm utilizing a single-variable Q-
learning state.
