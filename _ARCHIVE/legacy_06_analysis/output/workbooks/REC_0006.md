Abstract—Reliable
autonomous
navigation
of
Unmanned
Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) in GPS-denied environments remains a
significant challenge due to signal attenuation, spoofing, and
jamming. Indoor environments such as warehouses and industrial
facilities suffer from GPS degradation caused by structural occlu-
sions, while outdoor scenarios including forests and flat terrains
lack reliable localization cues. To address these challenges, this
paper presents a multi-sensor fusion framework for robust UAV
navigation in GPS-compromised environments.
The proposed approach employs a dual-strategy navigation
framework: (i) Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping
(VSLAM) with 3D point cloud generation for structured indoor
environments, and (ii) a VSLAM-based 2D digital twin repre-
sentation for outdoor environments, integrating visual data with
inertial and altitude measurements. Software-in-the-loop (SIL)
simulations using ROS and RTAB-Map validate autonomous
navigation with obstacle avoidance in warehouse scenarios, while
Hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) experiments on an in-house devel-
oped UAV platform demonstrate accurate indoor mapping and
localization.
The results demonstrate that the proposed vision-centric
framework enables reliable navigation without dependence on
GPS, offering a scalable and resilient solution for UAV operations
in GPS-denied environments, with applications in inventory
management, surveillance, and search-and-rescue missions.

I. INTRODUCTION
Autonomous navigation of Unmanned Aerial Vehicles
(UAVs) in GPS-denied environments remains a critical chal-
lenge for both civilian and defense applications. Indoor en-
vironments such as warehouses, underground facilities, and
dense urban areas suffer from severe GPS signal attenuation
due to structural occlusions, while outdoor scenarios including
forests and open terrains are vulnerable to GPS spoofing, jam-
ming, and atmospheric disturbances. These conditions result
in unreliable localization, motivating navigation solutions that
do not rely solely on satellite-based positioning [1].
Most autonomous navigation systems depend heavily on
GPS for precise localization and waypoint execution [2]. The
accuracy of GPS-based positioning is influenced by the Dilu-
tion of Precision (DOP), which depends on satellite visibility
and geometry [3]. Although RTK-based methods can achieve
Fig. 1. In-house developed GATHI platform used for HIL testing
high accuracy, their dependence on satellite availability limits
their effectiveness in GPS-compromised environments.
Prior work has investigated robust navigation under GNSS-
denied conditions. GNSS-free navigation and control frame-
works leverage onboard sensing and adaptive control to
maintain stable flight in dynamic environments [4], while
recent studies address GPS spoofing vulnerabilities using
visual odometry-based mitigation techniques [8]. However,
these approaches primarily focus on low-level localization and
control, offering limited support for mission-level navigation
and operator-driven goal specification.
In this work, we propose a vision-centric navigation frame-
work for GPS-denied UAV operation that integrates percep-
tion, planning, control, and human-in-the-loop interaction.
The framework employs a depth camera for Visual SLAM
(VSLAM) to build a spatially consistent map, associates visual
information with navigation nodes for contextual understand-
ing, and provides a web-based GUI for real-time visualization
and command-based navigation through bidirectional commu-
nication with the UAV.

III. NAVIGATION SOLUTIONS
Based on the demonstrated vulnerabilities discussed in
Section II, a robust alternative navigation framework for GPS-
denied environments is required. To address these limitations,
a comprehensive multi-scale navigation approach is developed
by integrating Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping
(VSLAM) with sensor fusion algorithms, drawing inspiration
from prior work.
For indoor environments, the framework employs VSLAM
using RTAB-Map for real-time 3D point cloud generation
and feature tracking. An Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is
used to fuse Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data with
visual odometry, while stereo camera systems provide depth
perception for reliable obstacle avoidance [6].

the basis for navigation and path planning.

RTAB-Map node, an RGB image is captured from the depth
camera and processed using a YOLO-based object detection
model. Detected objects are extracted as bounding-box images
and semantically associated with their corresponding map

both static obstacles (storage racks and support columns) and
dynamic elements (moving cranes, forklifts, and personnel).

Despite its effectiveness indoors, VSLAM-based navigation
relying on dense 3D point clouds is computationally expen-
sive and less suitable for large-scale outdoor environments,
while also being sensitive to lighting variations and altitude
changes [8], [10]. Simplified 2D digital twin approaches re-
duce computational load but suffer from degraded performance
under poor illumination and feature-sparse conditions [9].
Future work will focus on developing a unified digital twin
framework that integrates complementary sensing modalities,
including thermal imaging and electromagnetic-wave-based
sensors, to provide robust and lighting-independent navigation
cues. By generating and fusing visual, thermal, and electro-
magnetic representations, the proposed tri-modal framework
aims to eliminate single-point failures and enable resilient
UAV navigation across diverse GPS-denied operational en-
vironments. Further validation will be conducted on next-
generation UAV platforms with optimized onboard sensor
fusion and adaptive learning-based sensor weighting.
