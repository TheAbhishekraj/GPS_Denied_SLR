Abstract
The role of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in time-sensitive missions such as low-altitude
reconnaissance and disaster rescue has gained increasing significance. To address the
challenge of visual localisation for UAVs operating in complex terrains under Global Nav-
igation Satellite System (GNSS)-denied environments, this paper proposes an improved
adaptive gradient-damped Newton approach to mitigate the trade-off between terrain
non-convexity and computational real-time performance. The proposed approach incorpo-
rates a terrain-gradient-based dynamic step-size adjustment mechanism that adaptively
captures non-linear terrain characteristics in real time and effectively reduces the numerical
oscillations typically observed in steep regions when using the standard Newton method.
In addition, a tightly coupled vision–geometry framework was developed to constrain
cumulative drift during long-range flight. Monte Carlo simulation results demonstrate that
the proposed algorithm maintains submeter localisation accuracy while achieving approxi-
mately a three-fold improvement in computational efficiency compared with traditional
grid-based methods, and a 27.4% increase in convergence speed relative to the standard
Newton method. Experiments conducted under high-noise conditions and highly undu-
lating terrains indicate that the approach exhibits strong convergence stability, offering a
computationally efficient and robust solution for UAV navigation.
Keywords: monocular vision-based geolocation; unmanned aerial vehicle navigation;
GNSS-denied environment; adaptive gradient-damped newton method; digital elevation
model (DEM); tightly coupled vision–geometry framework
1. Introduction
In recent years, the role of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) in time-sensitive missions
such as low-altitude reconnaissance and disaster rescue has become increasingly promi-
nent [1–3]. Although Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) are widely utilised, their
reliability drops sharply in GNSS-denied environments due to electromagnetic interference
or urban canyon effects [4]. Therefore, monocular visual geolocalisation technology, which
relies on matching airborne imagery with digital elevation models (DEM), has emerged as
a research focus in the navigation field because of its robust anti-interference capability and
passive detection characteristics [5,6].

With advancements in research, significant progress has been made in visual feature

lishing spatial correspondences between UAVs and satellite imagery through perspective
projection to achieve absolute matching localisation in complex environments. Wang
et al. [8] employed deep convolutional neural networks to extract implicit geometric fea-
tures from images, significantly improving feature-matching accuracy under dynamic
lighting scenarios. Chen et al. [9] summarised recent advancements in deep learning for
visual localisation, noting that feature-matching networks incorporating attention mecha-
nisms, such as Transformer models, can effectively overcome feature association failures in
extremely textureless environments. Although these methods deliver outstanding accuracy,
their substantial computational overhead makes it difficult to meet the stringent power-
consumption constraints of miniature airborne edge computing platforms. When it comes
to micro UAVs, integrating high-performance GPUs often severely restricts their payload
capacity and inevitably curtails flight endurance. Given this practical bottleneck, there is a
pressing demand within the engineering community to develop lightweight mathematical
optimization strategies that can simultaneously guarantee low latency and minimal energy
consumption [10,11].
To overcome this computational challenge, some studies have focused on improv-
ing backend non-linear optimisation algorithms to accelerate the solution process. For
instance, Zhang et al. [12] introduced a non-linear least-squares framework with a robust
kernel function into backend optimisation for dynamic localisation, effectively accelerating
the update of the system state. Saxena et al. [13] proposed a unified theoretical frame-
work for non-linear optimisation that significantly enhanced system solution efficiency
by reconstructing state variables within a sliding window. Boche et al. [14] developed a
multi-sensor tightly coupled framework supporting dense depth, effectively controlling
optimisation time complexity in large-scale scenes by combining submap alignment with
marginalisation strategies.
Despite the remarkable progress made in accelerating backend optimisation and
improving noise robustness, existing algorithms nevertheless struggle to balance low com-
putational latency with robust convergence when dealing with severely undulating and
unstructured terrains, High degrees of non-convexity often invalidate the local smoothness
and quadratic convexity assumptions that underpin traditional nonlinear optimization
frameworks. Within these intricate geometric manifolds, the Jacobian matrix of the ob-
jective function remains highly susceptible to degeneracy or near-singularity, particularly
where the terrain gradient undergoes abrupt shifts. This vulnerability, in turn, tends to
trigger step-size expansion in conventional search operators [15,16] largely driven by the
computational limits of airborne platforms [17,18]. Traditional grid-search methods can
theoretically approximate the global optimum; however, their computational load surges
exponentially as resolution increases [19]. Contemporary UAV dynamic localisation sys-
tems require both sparse reconstruction and high-frequency computational efficiency [20],
and the resulting surge in computational overhead makes it difficult for these methods
to meet the demands of high-frequency dynamic navigation. Consequently, the indus-
try has increasingly turned to the standard Newton method, which features a simple
single-iteration structure. However, its fixed-step iterative mechanism is highly sensitive to
non-linear terrain characteristics [21]. In extreme environments, this method is prone to
numerical overshoot, which can cause iterative oscillations at sharp gradient transitions,
and ultimately trapping the localisation solution in local optima within regions of abrupt
terrain variation [22,23]. This persistent challenge of non-linear optimisation in complex
three-dimensional spaces has been repeatedly corroborated in both the robustness evolu-

failing to authentically represent the physical smoothness of the geomorphology [34].

method, making targeted improvements to the classical standard Newton’s method to ad-

terrains. This approach mitigates the numerical divergence inherent in traditional optimi-
sation algorithms when dealing with non-convex terrains, thereby significantly reducing
computational latency. Building upon this, a tightly coupled optimisation architecture is
constructed employing airborne sensor states as time-domain constraints, providing an
effective mechanism for decoupling the error propagation between single-frame visual
failures and low-frequency inertial drift.

2.3. Terrain Surface Modelling
Unlike traditional planar assumptions or first-order linear approximations, the real ter-
rain encountered during low-altitude UAV flights often exhibits pronounced non-convexity
and strong local undulations. Essentially, the input DEM is a discrete two-dimensional spa-
tial grid. However, the adaptive-damped Newton method proposed herein relies heavily
on accurate sensing of high-frequency gradients on the terrain surface.
To establish a smoother mathematical transition between discrete terrain grids and
high-order continuous optimization, this study constructs a terrain manifold reconstruction
mechanism. By primarily leveraging local nonlinear mapping, this method transforms
discrete two-dimensional elevation data into an analytical surface characterized by second-
order continuous differentiability. Such a mathematical construct holds substantial sig-
nificance for the overall performance of the algorithm. At the physical level, it assists in
significantly mitigating the truncation errors typically prone to emerge at the boundaries of
conventional discrete grids. At the mathematical level, it provides a reasonably reliable
safeguard for the continuity of the objective function’s Jacobian matrix and the stability of
local curvature features. Overall, this reconstruction mechanism lays a theoretical founda-
tion enabling the subsequent adaptive damping operator to better perceive high-frequency
spatial gradients, thereby mitigating the risk of numerical degradation to a certain extent.

3.2. Adaptive Damping Strategy
In non-convex manifold optimization, traditional damped Newton methods or trust-
region methods predominantly rely on rigorous mathematical iterations to update step-size
constraints. Such complex matrix operations are highly computationally intensive, often
exceeding the load capacity of micro onboard platforms, thereby constraining the real-time
responsiveness of the system. To resolve the aforementioned theoretical bottlenecks, this
study constructs a mapping mechanism between physical terrain features and mathemati-
cal optimization boundaries and proposes a novel nonlinear damping operator. Specifically,
this operator embeds the geometric fluctuations of the local terrain as a physical prior into
the analytical construction of the penalty factor, thereby inducing an inherent adaptive trun-
cation mechanism within the algebraic structure. This design not only effectively reduces
the computational burden on micro-UAV platforms, but also achieves a structural exten-
sion at the level of manifold optimization theory, objectively enhancing the convergence
robustness of the system when solving within complex non-convex spaces.

3.4. Visual-Geometric Tightly Coupled Model
Non-linear optimization in traditional UAV visual localization predominantly relies
on the independent solving of single-frame images or loosely coupled strategies. These
methods are essentially static approximations of the local spatial manifold. during long-
distance flights, if homogeneous terrain features or degraded observation quality are
encountered, they are highly prone to falling into local minima, thereby inducing low-
frequency cumulative drift that is difficult to eliminate. To effectively suppress degeneration
boundary effects, this study constructs a visual-geometric tightly coupled architecture. This
architecture introduces the high-frequency kinematic propagation state as a temporal a
priori regularization term, essentially accomplishing the mathematical reshaping of the
visual elevation residual manifold. By imposing dynamic constraints in the time dimension
during the gradient optimization process of the cost function, the system acquires a reliable
mechanism for escaping local optima. Compared to conventional decoupled schemes,
the proposed architecture safeguards the theoretical consistency of state estimation and
the robustness of the underlying kinematics, even under extreme operating conditions
characterized by severely sparse observational features.
To bridge the inherent disparity in sampling frequencies between the onboard camera
and the accompanying inertial or barometric sensors, we opted for a temporal alignment
approach anchored primarily to the visual frame timestamps. Provided the system operates
under a hardware-level global clock, we can denote the exact exposure instant of the k-th
image frame as tk. When attempting to determine the a priori state at this precise moment
tk, our strategy involves extracting the sequence of high-frequency sensor measurements
falling within the interval [tk−1, tk]. Subsequently, by applying the governing state evolution
equations, we meticulously integrate these measurements to propagate the system state
forward to the target time tk.

4.3. Comprehensive Performance Evaluation
Given the stringent constraints on computational resources and real-time performance
in micro-UAVs, this study introduces the grid search method, the standard Newton’s
method, and the trust-region algorithm (serving as a high-order nonlinear optimization
baseline) for a quantitative comparison. By analyzing the limitations of these classical
algorithms in complex terrains and weighing the trade-off between solution accuracy and
computational time, this paper aims to verify the practical performance of the proposed
algorithm in improving system latency and numerical stability, thereby evaluating its
potential for engineering applications.
To objectively evaluate the computational efficiency and convergence performance of
the proposed algorithm, comparative analyses were conducted under a unified simulation
environment. As illustrated in Figure 3, the convergence trajectories of the objective

In the benchmark tests for positioning accuracy, the proposed algorithm achieved bet-

the standard Newton’s method in numerical accuracy and achieves a level comparable
to that of the high-order trust-region algorithm. However, when considering the aver-
age execution time, the trust-region algorithm incurs considerable computational latency
due to the frequent evaluation of the Hessian matrix and the solving of subproblems. In
comparison, the algorithm proposed herein effectively reduces the time required for a
single computation while maintaining an equivalent level of convergence accuracy. These
results suggest that the proposed architecture alleviates the computational bottlenecks
of traditional high-order solvers to a certain extent, offering a viable trade-off between
accuracy and real-time performance for micro-UAV systems.
However, it is imperative to rigorously distinguish between the solver’s “numerical
precision” and the system’s “actual physical positioning accuracy.” Physical accuracy is
inherently bounded by the observational limits of the underlying hardware, such as the
discrete resolution of DEM. Conversely, the sub-micron numerical precision discussed
herein primarily characterizes the optimization solver’s capacity to approximate extrema
within the mathematical residual space.
While pursuing extreme numerical precision cannot transcend the lower error bounds
dictated by sensor physics, the engineering significance of this design lies in provisioning
substantial computational redundancy. Crucially, this redundancy mitigates the detrimen-
tal coupling between algorithmic nonlinear truncation errors and underlying physical
observation noise. Consequently, cumulative errors during long-range navigation tasks are
robustly constrained to a tight regime bordering the hardware’s physical limits.

6. Conclusions
To address the localisation challenges of micro-UAVs in complex terrain under GNSS-
denied environments, by deeply coupling the classical nonlinear second-order optimization
framework with the underlying physical constraints of the UAV, this paper proposes an
improved adaptive gradient-damping Newton method integrated with terrain gradients
and a visual–geometric tightly coupled architecture.
From a theoretical perspective, the adaptive damping mechanism dynamically per-
ceives the non-linear characteristics of local terrains and constructs a smooth iterative
law that approximates the trust region. This approach effectively mitigates the oscillatory
behaviour inherent in the standard Newton method when navigating non-convex regions,
thereby significantly broadening the algorithm’s convergence domain and enhancing the
stability of the numerical approximation process.
Without significantly increasing computational complexity, the mechanism improves
the numerical convergence depth, providing numerical redundancy to counter the phys-
ical sensor noise. At the architectural level, the visual–geometric tightly coupled model
mitigates error accumulation in low-cost sensors during long-endurance missions. By
introducing a time-series regularisation penalty term, the architecture achieves state–space
smoothing between terrain constraints and inertial extrapolation effectively restricting
trajectory drift during simulated long-range flights.
In summary, the core academic value of this research is primarily manifested in the
profound integration of the system architecture and a rational trade-off concerning com-
putational resources. Rather than pursuing fundamental breakthroughs within the purely
mathematical theoretical dimension, this study comprehensively accounts for the objective
physical constraints of micro-UAV platforms. By constructing a mapping mechanism
between physical spatial states and mathematical optimization boundaries, it partially
mitigates the numerical degradation phenomena frequently induced by traditional fixed
step-size algorithms when solving across complex non-convex manifolds. Grounded in
realistic engineering conditions, this trade-off strategy not only assists in circumventing
the cumbersome subproblem resolution burdens inherent to high-order optimization al-
gorithms but also holds the promise of providing a highly feasible technical evolution
pathway for autonomous perception and high-frequency state estimation tasks on resource-
constrained platforms operating within extreme geomorphological environments.
Future work will prioritize deploying the proposed algorithm onto physical UAV
platforms to conduct rigorous field flight tests in real-world complex terrains, thereby
thoroughly verifying and refining its practical engineering applicability.
