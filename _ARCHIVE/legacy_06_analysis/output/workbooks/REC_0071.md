Abstract—Global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) such as 
GPS are the de-facto standard for absolute and reliable un-
manned aerial vehicles (UAV) localization due to their global 
availability and the maturity and wide spread of the underlying 
technology. However, as the spread and reliance upon autono-
mous UAV systems increases, the risk of jamming becomes a 
substantial safety problem. The area where jamming attacks are 
most likely to occur is the close vicinity of the UAV landing sta-
tion. Hence, to ensure reliable operations, an alternative localiza-
tion technology for the approach and landing phase is needed. 
This paper presents a localization system for 3D position estima-
tion based on 24 GHz FMCW cooperative radar. It has very low 
infrastructure requirements thanks to the combination of rang-
ing and direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation techniques em-
ployed in the ground station. Jamming resistance is improved 
due to the large bandwidth of 250 MHz. A measurement cam-
paign was conducted to prove the feasibility of the proposed 
concept. 
Keywords—wireless localization; 3D position estimation; sec-
ondary radar; UAV; GNSS jamming 
I. INTRODUCTION 
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are on their way to 
transform multiple industries in the next years and decades. 
While already common in the military sector, there is great 
research interest in UAVs for civil applications. Among others, 
these include parcel delivery [1], surveillance [2], and automat-
ed warehouse inventory [3]. Ensuring robust and safe operation 
is of utmost importance for the wide commercial adoption of 
UAVs. One of the most sensitive topics in this regard is UAV 
localization. 
In this paper we present a wireless local positioning system 
(WLPS) for the 3D position estimation of a UAV comprising a 
single ground-based radar station (GBRS) and a miniaturized 
radar unit (MRU) on the aircraft (Fig. 1). The system can 
replace high-accuracy GNSS techniques for precision landings 
on indoor and outdoor static or moving platforms, while 
providing better jamming resistance. 
II. APPLICATION SCENARIO 
A. Application 
One scenario for realizing commercial UAV parcel deliv-
ery services suggests automated aircraft flying between fixed 
parcel delivery stations, where end customers can receive and 
send their shipments. Parcels are automatically loaded in the 
UAV and then flown to the receiving station. The concept was 
demonstrated during the ParcelCopter 3.0 project in the Ba-
varian Alps [1]. Automatic landing on the parcel delivery 
stations requires reliable highly accurate 3D localization of the 
UAV with respect to the platform [4]. 
A common way of achieving such accuracy is by using 
differential GNSS techniques – like real-time kinematic 
(RTK) satellite navigation – as it offers excellent 3D localiza-
tion accuracy in the range of 1 cm as well as heading estima-
tion. GNSS services are globally available, however their 
accuracy can be considerably reduced in high multipath envi-
ronments like urban canyons.  Furthermore, GNSS satellite 
signals as well as differential GNSS correction data streams 
can be maliciously blocked by using illegal, but commercially 
available jamming equipment. Spoofing is also a threat, which 
is less likely to occur for commercial UAVs, but cannot be 
ruled out. 
The critical flight phases for GNSS localization failure are 
during takeoff and landing as those take place near ground

Considering the fact that localization service failure may

more robust localization methods becomes apparent. Substan-
tial research has been done in the field of GNSS anti-jamming 
resulting in better interference mitigation algorithms and con-

and expensive hardware. 
In the scenario considered here, high-accuracy localization 
is required only in the vicinity of the landing platforms. There-
fore, additional infrastructure in the form of positioning radar 
can be integrated in the landing stations to complement and in 
the case of failure replace GNSS. Using the proposed system, 
landings on roofed or indoor platforms can be enabled as well. 
B.  Maverix Tiltwing Demonstrator 
The Maverix tiltwing demonstrator (see Fig. 1 and Fig. 2) 
built at the RWTH Aachen University was used as a carrier 
platform for the MRU and the RTK system. A tiltwing is a 
hybrid aircraft configuration which is capable of vertical take-
off and landing as well as of wing-borne aerodynamic flight. 
This is facilitated by tilting the main wing from the vertical to 
the horizontal position and vice versa. The demonstrator has a 
wing span of 1 m and a takeoff weight of 2 kg. It is equipped 
with a Novatel OEM617D dual-antenna dual-frequency 
GPS/GLONASS receiver using correction data from a dedi-
cated base station. 
III. 3D LOCALIZATION SYSTEM 
To ensure reliable approach and landing, a cooperative fre-
quency-modulated continuous-wave (FMCW) radar was used. 
It operates in the worldwide freely available 24 GHz ISM band 
with a bandwidth of 250 MHz. 
A. Jamming 
A variety of GNSS jammers is presented and analyzed in 
[1]. While low-cost devices for as little as $5 emit CW signals, 
more sophisticated ones costing a few hundred dollars use 
sawtooth signals with a bandwidth of up to 100 MHz. As 
GNSS signals have a relatively low bandwidth of ≤ 25 MHz 
and a signal power in the range of −130 dBm at the Earth sur-
face, they are highly susceptible to jamming. 
FMCW signals, on the other hand, are much harder to jam 
due to the large bandwidth and modulation scheme employed 
[6]. To effectively interfere with the signal, a potential attacker 
would need to estimate its parameters (sweep duration and 
bandwidth) and to synchronize to it in time and frequency with 
sufficient accuracy. As the proposed system uses a cooperative 
active transponder, the signal power is much higher 
(≈ −73 dBm at a range of 100 m considering free-space path 
loss, legally limited Tx power of 13 dBm, and patch antennas). 
Therefore, a much more advanced device would be needed to 
cause failure of the proposed system. 
B. Multilateration vs. RTOF/DOA Combination 
Based on the experience with GNSS, most available WLPS 
use multilateration and the time difference of arrival (TDOA), 
time of arrival (TOA), or round-trip time of flight (RTOF) 
measurement principles [7]. Obtaining an unambiguous 3D 
position estimate with this approach requires simultaneous 
measurements to at least 4 ground-based stations distributed 
across a relatively large area (see also Section III D). Instead, 
we propose a combination of the RTOF and direction of arrival 
(DOA) measurement techniques [8]. This allows a single 
ground-based radar station to determine the 3D position of the 
airborne radar unit with a single measurement. 
C. Hardware and Operating Principle 
The GBRS used was an 8-Rx-channel single-input multi-
ple-output (SIMO) 24 GHz FMCW radar [9]. It uses a two-
way synchronization-measurement operating principle to esti-
mate the RTOF and thus the distance between two cooperative 
radar nodes as described in [10]. It is equipped with a 2D 
sparse planar antenna array comprising 8 patch antennas [11] 
enabling it to simultaneously estimate the azimuth φaz as well 
as the elevation ϑel angles. The MRU, a smaller, lighter, and 
more energy-efficient radar node with 2 Rx channels based on 
the same operating principle was mounted on the aircraft as 
shown in Fig. 1. The measurement results of the GBRS were 
transmitted to the UAV over an FSK link in the 24 GHz band, 
which also contributes to the security of the concept.

V. CONCLUSION AND OUTLOOK 
In this paper, a radar-based system for 3D UAV positioning 
in GNSS-denied scenarios was presented. As derived theoreti-
cally, the proposed RTOF/DOP combination is superior to 
multilateration systems in terms of accuracy and system com-
plexity. In a next step, a Kalman filter can be implemented to 
smoothen the positioning result and filter outliers. A higher Rx 
channel count and a higher bandwidth will further increase the 
accuracy. An antenna array with hemispherical field of view 
will allow using a single radar as landing aid independent on 
the UAV approach direction. The airborne radar node can be 
further miniaturized and extended with multiple Rx channels to 
enable orientation estimation of the UAV [16].
