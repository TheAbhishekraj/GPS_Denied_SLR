Abstract—This paper discussed cost-effective vision-based ob-
stacle avoidance for Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) operating
in GPS-denied environments. The system combines the You Only
Look Once (YOLO) architecture with stereo vision cameras
(OAK-D Lite), a Raspberry Pi computer, and a ﬂight controller
unit (Pixhawk-Cube). Navigating safely through obstacles be-
comes challenging for UAVs in GPS-denied environments. To
address this issue, the drone is conﬁgured in altitude hold mode,
and the system is trained on the Common Objects in Context
(COCO) dataset, enabling it to recognize objects and analyze
the surrounding areas to identify free spaces. By doing so, the
drone can traverse an obstacle-free path. The detected obstacle
information is then utilized to generate avoidance trajectories,
allowing the UAV to navigate around obstacles safely. Real-time
testing of the proposed technique demonstrates its efﬁcacy in
detecting and avoiding obstacles within a threshold distance of
2 meters, with an error rate of 10%. The drone’s relative speed
is conﬁgured at 2 m/s during these tests.
Index Terms—Autonomous Navigation, Convolution Neural

I. INTRODUCTION
Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are highly versatile
robots that ﬁnd applications in various domains, such as
surveillance, search and rescue, inspections, and monitoring
[1] [2] [3] [4]. Industries like construction, trafﬁc systems,
agriculture [5], and cinematography have adopted UAVs for
their speciﬁc needs, automating niche applications and reduc-
ing human errors. However, navigating UAVs in environments
where GPS signals are limited or unreliable, such as indoor or
urban environments, presents challenges for safe and efﬁcient
operations. So, obstacle avoidance is critical to UAV naviga-
tion to prevent collisions and ensure safe operations. Vision-
based obstacle avoidance has emerged as a promising solution
to address this challenge as it leverages onboard cameras and
advanced object detection algorithms to detect obstacles in
real time and autonomously avoid them [6] [7]. Some of the
approaches have discussed on drone racing that is autonomous
and uses deep reinforcement learning [8], [9] about sensor-
based navigation which uses onboard computing. There are
still challenges remaining in terms of obstacle avoidance
accuracy, utilization of system resources, and optimized path
planning that needs to be addressed. The motivation behind
this novel technique is to overcome the limitations of GPS and
provide UAVs with the capability to navigate safely and au-
tonomously in GPS-denied environments by relying on visual
perception. This paper introduces an end-to-end pipeline for
obstacle avoidance in GPS-denied environments speciﬁcally
designed for UAVs. The proposed solution leverages a stereo
vision camera and a Raspberry Pi onboard unit. It offers a
low-cost solution that is compact and efﬁcient. By utilizing
the YOLO (You Only Look Once) object detection algorithm
[10], this approach efﬁciently detects and classiﬁes objects
in real time, allowing for obstacle avoidance by considering
the surrounding space and adjusting navigation based on
velocity. The stereo vision cameras provide depth information
for obstacle detection, and the onboard computing enables
real-time processing and decision-making for safe obstacle
avoidance. The contributions of this paper are as follows:
• Developed an end-to-end pipeline for detecting and
avoiding obstacles, utilizing YOLO to determine unoc-
cupied and occupied space.
• Developed velocity-based navigation system speciﬁcally
designed for GPS-denied environments.
• Design of an obstacle avoidance system that continuously
monitors the availability of free space along the drone’s
path to its destination.
• In addition, integrated a probability module that assesses
the likelihood of the detected objects. This pipeline also
incorporates a free space assessment module.

II. RELATED WORK
Challenges persist in aerial autonomous navigation regard-
ing precision, sensor errors, noise, and wind-induced data vari-
ation. Goals include enhancing stability, reliability, simplicity,
and cost-effectiveness. Extensive research has been conducted
on vision-based autonomous navigation for Unmanned Aerial
Vehicles (UAVs) in unknown environments, as demonstrated
by various authors [18]–[22]. Early approaches utilized re-
active heuristic collision avoidance techniques and extracted
obstacle representations from camera images for autonomous
navigation [18], [19]. One author even employed a cylinder
concept for obstacle detection and reactive avoidance using
a monocular camera [19]. However, these methods often
required computationally intensive image processing on off-
board ground computers. Alternatively, some methods relied
on Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) solutions,
where UAVs create maps of their environments and make
decisions based on them [21], [22]. [23] proposed a patch-
based method for accurate obstacle detection. Zhihai et al.
[11] discussed video-based motion ﬁeld estimation for UAV
navigation and obstacle avoidance. Du et al. [12] explored the
use of reinforcement learning for global path planning and ob-
stacle avoidance, considering its complexity and computational
cost [13], [14]. J. Hu [15] presented a technique for obstacle
avoidance using a stereo vision camera to measure depth,
showing the accuracy, reliability, and localization capabilities
of UAVs. When a camera is unable to detect obstacles,
ultrasonic sensors are used as an alternative L. Keyu, [16].
Obstacle avoidance algorithms such as A*, APF (Artiﬁcial
Potential Field Method), and RRT (Rapid Extended Random
Tree Method) are widely used [17]. Reinforcement learning is
also employed for path planning and collision avoidance. In
recent efforts, a 3D occupancy global map of the environment
was constructed from image data and used in conjunction with
the VFH+ (Vector Field Histogram) algorithm for collision
avoidance planning in UAVs. This paper introduces a new
vision-based obstacle avoidance method for UAVs in GPS-
denied environments. The system combines YOLO architec-
ture with stereo vision cameras, trained on the COCO dataset,
to detect objects and navigate safely around obstacles, enabling
obstacle-free drone paths.
III. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
Figure 1 illustrates the system conﬁguration for vision-
based obstacle avoidance in drones. The drone is equipped
with an external camera facing forward to capture a frontal
view. The ﬂight controller used in the system is the Pixhawk
Cube. Additionally, a Raspberry Pi 4B with 4 GB RAM, 32
GB memory card, 40-pin GPIO, a powerful quad-core with
processing speed of 1.2 GHz, and a 64-bit CPU is employed as
a secondary onboard controller. The Raspberry Pi is connected
to the ﬂight controller, power is supplied by a universal battery
eliminator circuit (UBEC) connected to the same 5000 mAh,
12 V, 30 Amp lithium-polymer (LiPO) battery that powers
the ﬂight controller via a Pixhawk cube power module 2.
Serial communication is employed to send control commands
between the Raspberry Pi and Pixhawk telemetry. The camera
data is transmitted to the Raspberry Pi as a range value, which
is then relayed to the Pixhawk system for effective collision
avoidance. In this range, a threshold sensor value of 4m is
set, and if the drone detects an obstacle approaching within
this threshold, it immediately initiates appropriate avoidance
measures to ensure a safe ﬂight. The system incorporates an
in-house-built drone with a payload capacity of 1kg. It has
an endurance of 10-15 minutes, making it ideal for various
applications. This cost-effective and reliable drone system is
designed to meet the demands of diverse tasks with efﬁciency
and effectiveness.
IV. METHODOLOGY
This section discusses obstacle avoidance and detection,
ensuring safe navigation from the source to the destination,
as elaborated below.
A. Obstacle avoidance
Figure 3 illustrates our vision-based obstacle avoidance and
navigation system. The system commences with a stereo-
vision camera, capturing a real-time video stream serving as
input for our Raspberry Pi processing unit. This video stream
contains visual data essential for obstacle detection, avoidance,
and navigation instructions for the drone’s ﬂight controller.
Obstacle avoidance is a two-step process: initially, the system
detects nearby obstacles and reduces the drone’s speed to
prevent collisions. We employ the YOLO (You Only Look
Once) object detection algorithm due to its swift and precise
real-time object detection capability.
YOLO partitions an input image into N cells of equal
dimensions, as shown in Figure 4. Each cell is responsible for
detecting bounding box coordinates relative to its coordinates
and predicting the object label and the probability of the object

B. Navigation
The system’s navigation aspect involves reducing the speed
and adjusting the drone’s direction to reach its ﬁnal destination
safely. The drone is operated in altitude hold mode and is
navigated using velocity mode estimated from the IMU sensor,
which is required to guide the drone for navigation. The pre-
dictions from the YOLO model can be used to determine free
space for navigation. The model has a prediction parameter
that assigns the probability of the class object being present.
When an object is detected, the cells surrounding the object are
examined to identify available space for the drone to navigate.
When an obstacle is detected within 2 meters, the system
prompts a slight change in the drone’s direction, typically to
the right. Following this direction adjustment, the obstacle
avoidance algorithm is executed again to evaluate the presence
of a clear path. This iterative process of obstacle avoidance
and direction adjustment continues until the drone successfully
reaches its intended destination. The steps for this approach
are illustrated in algorithm 1 Overall, our vision-based obstacle
avoidance and navigation system integrates the capabilities of
YOLO for real-time object detection, disparity information for
obstacle distance estimation, and adaptive navigation strategies
to ensure safe and efﬁcient drone operation in challenging
environments.

VI. CONCLUSION
This paper introduces a UAV obstacle avoidance system
utilizing a stereo vision camera with YOLO module, and depth
information for navigation. Design an obstacle avoidance
system that provides continuous monitoring of available free
space along the drone’s path toward its destination. The system
effectively navigates the UAV by inspecting the space around
objects and utilizing the YOLO model for object presence
prediction and bounding box estimation. The proposed sys-
tem performs well in low-resource settings, leveraging the
Raspberry Pi compute unit and OAK-D Lite stereo camera,
with a detection rate of 90% and a relative speed of 2 m/s
for the drone and obstacle avoidance speed of 1 m/s. Future
research in this area will focus on 3D lidar-based autonomous
navigation. This will further enhance the capabilities of UAVs
for safe and efﬁcient navigation in complex and dynamic
environments.
