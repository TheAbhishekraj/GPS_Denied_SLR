that the proposed framework achieves 95.95% localization 
coverage and 100% valid-frame node accuracy, showing strong 
robustness and interpretability for indoor UAV localization and 
navigation.

I. INTRODUCTION  
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have attracted increasing 
attention in indoor applications such as building inspection, 
emergency rescue, security patrol, infrastructure maintenance, 
and digital twin management[1]. Compared to ground robots, 
UAVs offer superior mobility and accessibility in narrow or 
complex indoor spaces[2]. However, reliable indoor UAV 
operation fundamentally depends on robust localization and 
navigation capabilities. Unlike outdoor environments, indoor 
scenarios typically suffer from unavailable or severely degraded 
Global Positioning System (GPS) signals[3]. In addition, 
repetitive corridors, visually similar rooms, weak textures, 
dynamic occlusions, and illumination variations further increase 
the difficulty of UAV localization in indoor environments[4]. 
Existing indoor localization approaches mainly focus on 
recovering continuous metric coordinates and performing 
navigation on geometric maps[5]. Chen et al. [6]transformed 
BIM semantic and geometric information into a graph structure 
for UAV navigation, improving the interpretability of indoor 
path planning. However, the approach primarily focused on 
navigation planning and assumed that the UAV location was 
already known, failing to address localization ambiguity caused 
by repetitive corridors and visually similar rooms in GPS‑denied 
indoor environments. Radwan et al. [7]reduced dependence on 
purely geometric localization through semantic mapping and 
multi‑level graph reasoning. However, the framework mainly 
relied on SLAM‑based scene reconstruction and did not fully 
utilize BIM semantic topology as a unified localization state 
space. To address these issues, this paper proposes a 
BIM‑assisted UAV topological localization and navigation 
method for GPS‑denied indoor environments. The proposed 
framework 
transforms 
BIM 
into 
a 
lightweight 
semantic‑topological 
graph 
and 
converts 
UAV 
image 
observations into candidate BIM node sets using spatial 
semantic recognition and OCR‑based textual cues. A 
topology‑aware recursive localization mechanism is then 
introduced, integrating BIM adjacency constraints and historical 
states to continuously infer the most probable topological node. 
Furthermore, semantic‑cost A* path planning is employed to 
verify whether the estimated topological locations can 
effectively support indoor UAV navigation tasks.

III. EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN 
A. Experimental Setup 
The experiments were conducted on a desktop workstation 
equipped with an Intel Core i7-12700 CPU, 32 GB RAM, and 
an NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU under Ubuntu 22.04. The proposed 
framework was implemented in Python using PyTorch for image 
semantic classification and NetworkX for BIM topology 
construction and path planning. UAV perception input was 
simulated using monocular image sequences collected in a real 
university teaching-building environment containing repetitive 
corridors, room entrances, and staircase regions. The BIM 
semantic-topological graph was manually constructed from the 
floor-plan BIM model, resulting in 16 semantic nodes and 15 
navigable edges. The dataset consisted of 721 indoor images, 
including 349 Corridor images, 319 Room images, and 53 Stair 
images, with an 8:2 training-testing split.

IV. CONCLUSION 
This paper proposed a BIM-assisted UAV topological 
localization and navigation framework for GPS-denied indoor 
environments. By transforming BIM data into a semantic-
topological graph, the proposed method converted UAV image 
observations into candidate BIM nodes using spatial semantic 
recognition and OCR-based textual cues. A topology-aware 
recursive localization mechanism was introduced to infer the 
most probable indoor location through BIM adjacency 
constraints and historical states. In addition, semantic-cost A* 
planning was employed to validate the navigation capability of 
the 
estimated 
topological 
nodes. 
Experimental 
results 
demonstrated that the proposed framework achieved high 
localization coverage and 100% valid-frame node accuracy in 
real indoor environments containing repetitive corridors, rooms, 
and staircase regions. To quantitatively validate the proposed 
framework, future work will compare it with representative 
visual localization methods, including feature-based approaches, 
visual SLAM, and deep-learning-based place recognition. The 
evaluation will consider localization accuracy, robustness, 
computational efficiency, and navigation consistency in GPS-
denied indoor environments. Compared with conventional 
coordinate-based methods, the proposed framework is expected 
to provide better interpretability and robustness through BIM 
semantic-topological constraints.
