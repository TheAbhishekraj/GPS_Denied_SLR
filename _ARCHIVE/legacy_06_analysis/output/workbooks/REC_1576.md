1. Introduction 
UAV autonomous docking has been a hot topic in recent years. Generally, it includes two 
stages: approaching phase and landing phase. First of all, UAV needs to locate the target 
relatively. The target can be a ﬁxed landing point, manned or unmanned ground vehicles, or 
ships on the sea, etc. Secondly, the navigation is carried out according to the navigation rules 
when the relative position is determined. When the target enters the UAV’s view of vision, the 
UAV will land on the set target area. Traditionally, because on-board vision system can provide 
rich and accurate position information, it is widely used in navigation and positioning [1–7] . 
Therefore, the landing problem is relatively mature using mainstream visualization methods, 
and most of the works are carried out in the approaching phase. In many cases, when GPS 
is available, the approaching capability can be achieved by simply transmitting GPS data 
between the UAV and target. For example, the obtained position and attitude in a multi- 
channel environment can be completed by RTK-GNSS technology [8] . Recent studies have 
shown that by integrating GPS and visual data, UAVs can land on vehicles with a speed of 
50km/h [9] , or ﬁxed-wing UAVs can successfully dock with aerial targets [10] . However, this 
kind of method primarily depends on whether there is GPS coverage, and the accuracy also 
depends on the strength of the signal. In fact, GPS is not accessible in many places, such as 
indoor, canyon, forest, cave or deep well. As such, other methods have been proposed in the 
case of GPS-denied environments. 
For example, [11] developed a model for autonomous navigation and collision avoidance 
of UAVs in a corridor environment with GPS constraints. Reference [12] proposed a local- 
ization method for a UAV, replacing the on-board GPS with noisy or unreliable GPS signals 
by aligning the images captured by a downward-facing monocular RGB camera with existing 
satellite images at ﬂight positions. References [13,14] proposed an integrated relative posi- 
tion estimation and control scheme to achieve two objectives simultaneously: only using the 
distance and odometer measurements for relative positioning, and navigating the UAV to the 
required position by designing an appropriate bounded control law. Through a large number 
of outdoor ﬂight tests, it is shown that UAV can approach a moving unmanned ground vehicle 
(UGV) from 50m away and then accurately land on a 1 . 5 m × 2m large platform. 
Although the above works can complete the UAV autonomous landing task in many cases, 
it requires the vehicle to have GPS position information or vehicle odometer position infor- 
mation. Note that accurate odometry is generally difﬁcult to obtain, especially for UAVs with 
low grade IMU. Therefore, this paper focuses on UAV docking on MP without the vehicle 
odometer in GPS-denied environments. 
The main contents are as follows: an autonomous navigation framework is proposed, which 
is divided into three stages: hovering, approaching and landing. In the hovering stage, a speed 
and relative position estimation method is proposed based on distance measurement using 
geometric tools and extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF). In the approaching stage, an estimation

3. Integrated relative positioning and navigation algorithm 
As mentioned above, the proposed autonomous landing process consists of three phases: 
hovering phase, approaching phase and landing control phase. In the hovering phase, assuming 
that the UAV hovers at altitude h at a point far away from the MP, the velocity and direction 
of MP and the relative position of the UAV to MP are calculated by the distance measurement 
based on UWB and the displacement of the UAV. In the approaching phase, a recursive least 
squares optimization algorithm based on adaptive genetic factors is proposed. The distance 
and relative displacement measurements are used to estimate the position of the UAV to the 
target. With this estimation, the UAV can approach MP. Once the UAV is located near MP 
and within the view of the UAV camera (i.e., landing phase), the system will switch to the 
camera-UWB-vision combination mode to ensure a high level of pose accuracy. In the ﬁnal 
phase, vision-based landing is performed to complete advanced guided maneuvers to ensure 
a safe vertical landing on the mobile platform.

5. Conclusion 
An autonomous docking scheme based on distance and displacement measurements was 
proposed for GPS-denied environments and MP without on-board odometers. We proposed 
an integrated relative positioning and navigation scheme to achieve autonomous docking. 
Simulation and experiment veriﬁed the effectiveness of the proposed docking scheme. The 
feasibility of the proposed scheme was veriﬁed in the GPS-denied environment.
