Recursive Extended Finite-Memory Positioning
Algorithm for Robust UAV Positioning in
GNSS-Denied Environment
Sang Su Lee , Jung Min Pak , Peng Shi , Life Fellow, IEEE, and Choon Ki Ahn , Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Recently, the operational domain of uncrewed aerial
vehicles (UAVs) has expanded from outdoor environments to
indoor spaces such as factories, power plants, and tunnels.
In these environments, where the Global Navigation Satellite
Systems (GNSS) is unavailable, and the risk of collision with
surrounding structures is high, a positioning system with high
accuracy and fast update rates is essential. In this article, we
propose a novel 3-D positioning algorithm for UAVs operating in
a GNSS-denied environment. The proposed algorithm is based
on sensor fusion of the inertial measurement unit (IMU) and
ultra-wideband-based wireless sensor network (UWB-WSN). The
proposed algorithm, referred to as the recursive extended ﬁnite-
memory positioning (REFMP), features a unique ﬁnite-memory
(FM) structure that estimates the current position using only
a limited set of recent information. This FM structure endows
the algorithm with robustness against measurement model errors,
linearization errors, and uncertainties in initial position informa-
tion. We evaluated the performance of the proposed algorithm
through both simulations and real-world experiments under
various challenging conditions, including UAV collisions with
walls, aggressive maneuvers inducing sensor measurement errors,
and scenarios with uncertain initial position information. By
comparing the proposed algorithm with state-of-the-art UAV
positioning algorithms based on IMU and sensor fusion, we
demonstrate the superior positioning accuracy and robustness
of REFMP under harsh conditions.
Index Terms—Finite-memory (FM) structure, inertial measure-
ment unit (IMU), uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV), wireless sensor
network (WSN).

Various sensors have been considered for UAV positioning

that obtain acceleration and angular velocity measurements
regardless of the external environment, they can compensate
for the problems of external sensors that obtain measurements
through interaction with the environment [9], [10], [11]. In
addition, the update rate of IMU is very high, which is suit-
able for positioning UAVs ﬂying fast in complex trajectories.
Therefore, the sensor fusion using IMUs for UAV positioning

various nonlinear ﬁlters. The extended KF (EKF) is based on
the linearization of a nonlinear system model through a Taylor
series expansion [13]. In contrast, the unscented KF (UKF) and
PF are based on the approximation of probability distributions
using sigma points and random samples, respectively [14],

compared with UKF and PF, it is computationally eﬃcient in
systems with many state variables due to its simplicity and is
superior in real-time processing. Therefore, algorithms based
on EKF with improved performance have been developed and
applied to UAV positioning in recent years.
The error-state EKF (ESEKF) [16] has recently attracted
attention in IMU-based positioning. It has been demonstrated
to be eﬀective for dealing with the orientation expressed in
quaternions and the angular velocity measurements from IMU.
In the ESEKF algorithm, the true state is decomposed into
the nominal state and the error state deﬁned in the local
space around the nominal state. Based on this decomposition,
the error-state system is formulated. Subsequently, the true
state can be obtained through the composition of the nominal
state and the estimated error state. This approach oﬀers many
advantages over traditional EKFs in IMU-based positioning,
including a reduction of linearization errors and overcoming
the singularity problem in the estimation error covariance
matrix when utilizing quaternions [17], [18]. Iterated ESKF
(IESKF) [19] also uses the error-state model, although it
performs the iterative correction using measurements. Since
IESKF is highly dependent on measurements, it can outper-
form ESEKF when the measurements are accurate. However,
it can be inferior to ESEKF when the measurements are
inaccurate. Most EKFs assume that the measurement noise is
Gaussian distributed. However, the G-forces from the UAV’s
rapid maneuvers and the shocks from collisions introduce non-
Gaussian noise into the IMU measurements. This leads to
errors in the measurement noise model, which degrades the
position estimation performance. To address the uncertainty of
the noise model, a tightly coupled adaptive EKF (TC-AEKF)
[20] that estimates the variation of noise statistics for UAV
positioning was proposed.
Recursive Bayesian estimators, including EKFs, UKFs, and
PFs, update the previous estimate to produce the next estimate,
and they have an inﬁnite memory (IM) structure where past
information accumulates. In the IM structure, errors due to
incorrect noise models accumulate and degrade estimation
performance. In addition, the ﬁlters with an IM structure
require an initial value to be set at the beginning of the
estimation, and if the initial value is signiﬁcantly diﬀerent from
the true value, the estimation will take a long time to get closer
to the true value. To address this issue, we propose a novel 3-D
positioning algorithm with a ﬁnite memory (FM) structure
based on the error-state system model. The FM structure can
prevent the error accumulation by using only a ﬁnite window
of recent information [21], [22], [23], [24]. The proposed
algorithm fuses IMU and UWB-WSN sensor measurements,
which enables the position estimation at a very fast rate and
is available even in GNSS-denied environments. The ﬁlter
gain in the proposed algorithm is derived by reducing the
Frobenius norm of the error term, which makes the algorithm
robust against various errors that degrade positioning accuracy.
Moreover, the proposed algorithm is designed to have a recur-
sive FM structure, which makes it computationally eﬃcient
and enables real-time processing. The proposed algorithm,
termed recursive extended FM positioning (REFMP), has
superior robustness against various uncertainties that degrade
positioning accuracy compared with state-of-the-art UAV posi-
tioning algorithms, such as ESEKF, IESKF, and TC-AEKF.
Simulations and experiments on the 3-D positioning of a
UAV using IMU and UWB-WSN demonstrate the superior
performance of the proposed REFMP.
The rest of this article is organized as follows. In Section II,
we describe the system models for UAV 3-D positioning using
IMU and UWB-WSN. In Section III, we describe the proposed
REFMP algorithm and the observability analysis. Section IV
presents the results of the UAV 3-D positioning simulation
and experiment to validate the performance of the proposed
REFMP. Finally, the conclusion and future work are discussed
in Section V.

II. UAV POSITIONING SCHEME AND PROBLEM
FORMULATION
UWB-WSN provides high resolution, allowing for precision
down to tens of centimeters, which has led to its extensive
use in indoor RTLS. The fusion of UWB-WSN with an
IMU in indoor environments has the potential to enhance the
positioning accuracy. We considered a system that fuses UWB-
WSN and IMU, the most commonly used sensors for indoor
UAV positioning. In a manner analogous to the utilization
of state estimation algorithms, including KFs, in GNSS and
INS fusion, a state estimation algorithm was also employed
for the fusion of UWB-WSN and IMU. In order to estimate
the position of UAV through the implementation of a state
estimation algorithm, it is necessary to employ a state-space
model that incorporates the position (coordinates) as a state
variable. The state-space model for position estimation com-
prises a motion model, which delineates the time-dependent
transition of the target’s position and velocity, and a measure-
ment model, which articulates the relationship between sensor
measurements and position. This section ﬁrst delineates the
process of fusing UWB-WSN and IMU measurements with a
state estimation algorithm to estimate the indoor position of a
UAV. Subsequently, we formulate the problem to be solved in
UAV localization based on state estimation algorithms.

D. Problem Formulation
Given a motion model (18) and a measurement model
(24), which are state-space models based on error states, the
UAV’s position can be estimated by applying state estimation
algorithms such as ESEKF, IESKF, and TC-AEKF. These
algorithms are based on the EKF, and recursive Bayesian
ﬁlters like EKF, UKF, and PF all have an IM structure.
The state estimation algorithm for the IM structure sets
an initial estimate and then iteratively performs prediction
and correction steps at each time step, using the state-space
model and sensor measurements to update the state estimation.
Therefore, past information is fully reﬂected in the current
estimate, and the inﬂuence of various errors, such as modeling
errors or measurement errors, accumulates over time, poten-
tially decreasing the estimation accuracy or even causing the
estimate to diverge. Also, in the IM structure, initial estimates
need to be set, and if this information signiﬁcantly diﬀers from
the actual values, the error will continuously aﬀect subsequent
time steps, degrading the estimation performance. In the case
of UAVs, they are easily aﬀected by wind in the air, and
indoors, and they can collide with objects or cause errors in
sensor readings when making rapid, high-speed maneuvers.
These disturbances, sensor errors, and others introduce errors
into the state-space model for UAV position estimation, and
these errors can accumulate, degrading position estimation
performance. In summary, existing algorithms for 3-D UAV
localization are not robust to errors arising from the IM
structure in UAV ﬂight environments, as well as errors in
initial estimates. In this study, we develop a new FM structure-
based localization algorithm based on error states to solve
this problem. The algorithm being developed is robust to
initial position estimation errors, motion model errors caused
by wind or collisions with objects, and sensor measurement
errors. It maintains consistent position estimation accuracy
under various conditions and has a computational load that
allows for real-time processing.

IV. VALIDATION
To validate the performance of the proposed positioning
algorithm, we considered three scenarios: 1) hovering; 2) col-
lision; and 3) complex maneuvers. The UAV collision with
a structure was only simulated to avoid damaging UAV, and
the complex maneuver scenario was only experimented with.
The hovering scenario was both simulated and experimented
with to validate the reliability of the results through cross
comparison.
We compar the proposed REFMP algorithm with state-of-
the-art algorithms: ESEKF [16], IESKF [19], and TC-AEKF
[20]. The design parameters for the comparison algorithms
were set as follows.
1) Covariance of ng (23): 0.002 I3.
2) Covariance of na (23): 0.05 I3.
3) Covariance of nwg (23): 0.0001 I3.
4) Covariance of nwa (23): 0.001 I3.
5) Covariance of vk (23) and (26): 0.01 I3 for k ∈κ1 and
0.05 I4 for k ∈κ2.
The design parameters of REFMP were set as Nhorizon = 50,
α = 0.5, θn = 1 for n ∈[i, k −1], and ξn = 1/(k −n + 1)0.5 for
n ∈[i, k].

3.3 ms, allowing for fusion with sensors operating at around

comparison algorithms in terms of accuracy. The relationship
between computation time and horizon size for REFMP can be
approximated as T(Nhorizon) ≈0.143N + 0.438 (in ms), which
follows a time complexity of O(Nhorizon) in Big-O notation.
V. CONCLUSION
In this article, we propose the REFMP algorithm, which
enables robust UAV positioning even in GNSS-denied envi-
ronments such as indoor spaces by fusing UWB-WSN and
IMU data. REFMP was designed to maintain high accuracy
by leveraging the advantages of the FM structure, even under
conditions with inaccurate initial information or high uncer-
tainty in the system model—both of which are commonly
encountered during UAV operation. The performance of the
proposed algorithm was evaluated and compared against state-
of-the-art methods (ESEKF, IESKF, and TC-AEKF) through
both simulations and real-world UAV experiments. REFMP
achieved improvements in APE of at least 82.38 %, 40.07%,
and 91.99 % over ESEKF, IESKF, and TC-AEKF, respectively.
In addition to accuracy, the proposed algorithm is capable of
real-time operation by fusing high-rate IMU measurements,
supported by an error-state system model and a recursive
solution structure. This recursive formulation is the ﬁrst of
its kind among FM-based estimation algorithms that rely on
linearization. Experimental results demonstrated the feasibility
of integrating high-frequency sensor data, conﬁrming the real-
time capability of REFMP. Therefore, the proposed algorithm
has been validated in terms of accuracy, robustness, and
computational eﬃciency. We plan to extend this research to
address robot localization and multi-UAV positioning in harsh
environments.
