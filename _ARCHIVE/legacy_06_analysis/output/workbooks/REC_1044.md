A B S T R A C T
A new visual-inertial odometry (VIO) algorithm for fixed-wing unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) operating in 
GNSS-denied, low to high altitude (50–800 m) environments is proposed. The method builds upon the Fast Multi- 
State Constraint Kalman Filter (FMSCKF) framework, introducing key innovations such as a dynamic feature 
extraction strategy, specialized handling of data for slow-motion imagery, selective feature utilization during 
filter updates, and magnetometer integration for improved heading estimation. Emphasis is placed on practical 
implementation and rigorous flight-testing on manned and unmanned multi-rotor vehicles. The proposed system 
is validated on a custom dataset obtained from a 15 km long-range flight via a conventional fixed-wing UAV in a 
desert-like environment. Experimental comparisons of results with standard Multi-State Constraint Kalman Filter 
(MSCKF), a semi-direct visual odometry approach with smoothing (SVO+GTSAM), and the VINS-Mono algo­
rithm demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed system in terms of long-duration navigation, accuracy, and 
convergence while the other methods diverged. In addition, quantitative evaluation of the proposed integrated 
system using relative and absolute trajectory error (RTE and ATE) metrics confirms its robustness and accuracy 
under challenging conditions.
1. Introduction
Visual inertial odometry (VIO) has gained considerable attention in 
the past few years as a means of high-accuracy autonomous navigation, 
especially in GNSS-denied environments. Various versions of VIO are 
being increasingly utilized for robotics and augmented reality applica­
tions, where the basic idea is to fuse vision-based sensor data with that of 
the inertial sensors for real-time pose estimation (PE) of autonomous 
vehicles. By integrating information from diverse sensor modalities, VIO 
systems can transcend some limitations associated with using vision or 
inertial sensors in isolation. In this respect, high-resolution images from 
vision sensors can serve for visual feature extraction and environment 
tracking, while the motion measurements picked up by the inertial 
sensors are generally insensitive to many factors affecting the environ­
ment like changes in the light. As such, their combination provides the 
basis needed to determine robust and accurate solutions for localization 
and mapping tasks of VIO systems, even in difficult complex environ­
ments where a single sensor modality fails.
A comprehensive overview of various existing SLAM and VIO tech­
niques is covered in [1] and [2], in which the authors have presented 
different visual-based navigation methods in detail. In addition, some 
comparisons among various algorithms as well as issues and challenges 
found within autonomous systems are discussed. Stochastic VIO ap­
proaches typically utilize a form of nonlinear Kalman filter, such as EKF, 
for pose estimation via fusion of vision and inertial data to effectively 
process sensor noise in an updated framework that continuously updates 
the state with new measurements to give real-time state estimates. 
Fortunately, most nonlinear EKF-based methods are of high computa­
tional efficiency and thus suitable for real-time applications in 
resource-constrained environments.
Multi-State Constraint Kalman Filter (MSCKF) is another basic VIO 
approach that uses the Kalman filter to predict the pose of a moving 
vehicle via the utility of monocular camera observation data that is 
aided with inertial measurement unit (IMU) data. The key novelty of 
MSCKF is its potential capability to process visual measurements 
without explicit estimation of 3D landmark positions. This in turn re­
duces the computational complexity of MSCKF and thus makes it 
attractive for real-time applications [3,4].

Some studies focus on MSCKF enhancement in various scenarios. PO- 
MSCKF (Photometric Odometry MSCKF) combines photometric errors 
with MSCKF to improve robustness in low-altitude environments [6]. 
The RD-VIO algorithm combines the results of MSCKF with the estimates 
obtained from the direct visual inertial odometry method to ensure 
better performance under challenging lighting conditions [7].
The broad robustness of ROVIO is against challenging scenarios like 
dynamic lighting or low-feature conditions. It extends the Kalman filter 
update with a direct photometric error minimization approach that 
enhances performance without relying on complicated feature extrac­
tion and matching processes [8].
In general, optimization-based VIO methods improve PE by solving a 
nonlinear optimization problem that is often based on the minimum of 
reprojection errors from visual features or even inertial data in­
consistencies. In these scenarios, both bundle adjustment and factor 
graph optimization allow one to perform joint optimizations over 
several frames for enhanced robust estimates. Though optimization- 
based VIOs are usually more computationally intensive, they achieve 
higher precisions. So, they are well suited for prolonged applications 
needing very accurate long-term localization within complex 
environments.
Semi-Direct Visual Odometry (SVO) combined with GTSAM is 
another technique focused on minimizing photometric inaccuracies, 
using a library dedicated to graph-based optimization. This integration 
significantly improves resilience against drift and inaccuracies across 
extensive trajectories by utilizing factor graph optimization [9,10].
KIMERA extends VIO with capabilities such as metric-semantic 
mapping and planning. It integrates state estimation with dense 3D 
reconstruction and semantic information. As such it is ideal for appli­
cations requiring environment identification beyond mere localization 
[11].
VINS-Mono is a robust monocular VIO system that integrates visual- 
inertial state estimation with loop closure for global consistency. It uses 
a tightly coupled optimization approach and includes failure detection 
mechanisms, making it versatile for various applications [12].
High-altitude flights using VIO concepts for pose estimation face 
specific challenges of their own related to operation in conditions of 
reduced visual features and environmental variability, joined with 
precise sensor fusion. Difficulties with a high-altitude flight scenario 
include the provision of robust initialization and state estimation that 
should be performed for both sparse visual data and challenging atmo­
spheric conditions. Initialization in high-altitude scenarios via the utility 
of a spline-based approach for added robustness is performed [13], due 
to sparse feature distribution.
An automatic re-initialization and failure recovery strategy for 
aggressive flight is investigated by integrating a monocular camera 
attached to a quadrotor to estimate its pose via tracking the visual fea­
tures of the surrounding environment [14]. It can be concluded that PE 
for low-feature-density conditions still undertakes comprehensive 
research in the methodologies of sensor fusion with sparse visual cues 
that permit persistent state estimation [15].
Some of the above-mentioned ideas are extended into practical 
application for fixed-wing UAVs flying at a high altitude, showing 
promising performance relative to VIO PE in GNSS-denied environments 
based on monocular visual cues in conjunction with IMU measurements 
[16].
There are other studies by Hinzmann et al. [17] and Furgale et al. 
[18] that discuss multi-sensor system calibration and optimization is­
sues for high-altitude flights. They showed that to realize high accuracy 
in long flights, precise calibration between vision and inertial sensors is 
of crucial importance.
Recent developments in VIO methods [19–22] have largely focused 
on general applications and do not specifically target high-altitude flight 
situations. This gap emphasizes the need for specialized algorithms that 
address the unique challenges of high-altitude navigation.
Other recent studies on VIO have attended to AI-based feature ex­
tractors, descriptors, and trackers that have outperformed traditional 
handcrafted methods reflected in [23,24] and [25]. Although AI-driven 
techniques potentially guarantee much better robustness and adapt­
ability to boost accuracy and reliability in VIO, their scarce utility for 
airborne systems is due to their high computational requirements 
against limited onboard resources.
A comparison between stereo VO and mono VIO revealed that stereo 
VIO provides the best accuracy while flying 40–60 m above the ground 
surface. The latter verifies a crucial strand in robust localization solu­
tions for autonomous flying drones, particularly Beyond Line-of-Sight 
flying, where GNSS reliability may be compromised [26].
While numerous VIO approaches have demonstrated strong perfor­
mance on benchmark datasets and low-altitude UAV platforms, experi­
mental validation under extended high-altitude (>300 m), long-range 
(>10 km) fixed-wing GNSS-denied flight remains limited. In such re­
gimes, reduced parallax and heading drift accumulation present unique 
challenges that are not explicitly addressed in most existing studies.
Given the existing published literature, an improved algorithm is 
proposed, implemented and experimentally validated in the current 
paper for fixed-wing high-altitude UAVs with several improvements and 
innovations that include: 
• High-altitude low-parallax adaptation within FMSCKF: A regime- 
aware feature management strategy designed to improve triangula­
tion conditioning under reduced parallax conditions encountered at 
300–800 m altitude.
• Motion-informed feature management: A feature extraction and 
retention strategy tailored to fixed-wing forward motion that en­
forces longer feature tracks and reduces unstable short-track 
updates.
• Selective feature usage during update: A measurement selection 
mechanism that prioritizes sufficiently long-lived feature tracks to 
improve update conditioning and stability.
• Magnetometer-aided heading stabilization: Integration of magne­
tometer updates within the FMSCKF framework to enhance long- 
range heading stability in GNSS-denied high-altitude flight.
• Real-flight validation: Experimental evaluation on extended fixed- 
wing flight data (up to 15 km) under visually sparse desert 
conditions.
The above-mentioned improvements have been experimentally 
demonstrated to enhance robustness and accuracy of the state-of-the-art 
fixed-wing UAV PE designed for long-duration, high-altitude missions in 
a GNSS-denied or GNSS-degraded environment.
The remainder of the paper is structured as follows. Section 2 details 
the methodological contributions corresponding to the points listed 
above: Section 2.1 describes the motion-informed feature management 
and selective update strategy, while Section 2.2 presents the magne­
tometer integration. Section 3 describes the experimental setup, and

2.1.1. Enhancement in image acquisition based on UAV motion
In forward fixed-wing flight, pixel flow is predominantly downward 
in a nadir-facing camera configuration due to forward translation. 
Features located near the horizon or upper image region may remain 
visible for longer durations compared to features near the bottom, which 
exit the field of view more rapidly. Therefore, the proposed method 
avoids aggressive spatial restriction of feature extraction and instead 
prioritizes feature longevity across frames.
2.1.2. Improvements in the update process
In the baseline FMSCKF algorithm, the update process would trigger 
the use of all currently available features in an image, whenever the 
number of features exiting the field of view drops below a predefined 
threshold. This condition implies that the number of trackable features 
across consecutive frames had diminished, thereby necessitating new 
feature extraction from the latest frame.
In contrast, the proposed algorithm introduces a more refined cri­
terion. While it still considers the number of exiting features, this alone 
is no longer sufficient to trigger a full feature update. Instead, an addi­
tional condition is initially checked, which refers to the number of 
surviving features shared between the initial frame (where the feature 
was first detected) and the latest frame. Next, if the number of exiting 
features falls below the threshold, but the number of surviving 
commonly observed features exceeds a certain value, the algorithm re­
frains from using the exiting features during the update step due to their 
insufficient quantity.
This modification in the update strategy significantly improves both 
the stability and computational efficiency of the system in real-world 
flight testing. The increased stability arises from a higher average 
number of observations per feature across multiple frames, an effect 
explained in detail in the Section 3. Furthermore, the reduced compu­
tational load stems from fewer feature extraction operations. Since 
feature extraction is among the most time-consuming components in 
VIO pipelines, this change not only enhances performance over the 
original MSCKF baseline, as demonstrated previously for FMSCKF, but 
also offers further efficiency gains in the current system by minimizing 
the reliance on feature extraction during runtime, as evidenced in the 
implementation section.

2.3. Initialization procedure
The proposed estimator follows a structured multi-stage initializa­
tion procedure designed to ensure stable convergence under high- 
altitude low-parallax conditions.
2.3.1. Static initialization and gravity alignment
At system startup, the estimator performs a short static initialization 
phase. During this period (approximately 3–5 s), IMU measurements are 
averaged to estimate the gravity vector and initialize accelerometer and 
gyroscope biases. The initial orientation is computed by aligning the 
body-frame accelerometer average with the gravity direction in the 
global frame. This provides roll and pitch initialization. Yaw is initial­
ized using magnetometer measurements after bias compensation.
The initial velocity is set to zero, and the initial position is defined as 
the origin of the local navigation frame.
2.3.2. Minimum motion requirement
To avoid degenerate initialization due to insufficient excitation, vi­
sual updates are enabled only after a minimum translational motion is 
detected. Specifically, the norm of accumulated velocity change must 
exceed a predefined threshold Δvmin. This ensures that sufficient geo­
metric baseline exists for reliable triangulation.
2.3.3. Delayed triangulation condition
In high-altitude flight, reduced parallax can lead to poorly condi­
tioned early depth estimates. To mitigate this, feature triangulation is 
delayed until sufficient temporal observation is achieved. A feature is 
considered eligible for triangulation only if its track length satisfies:

4.3. Limitations and failure modes
The following factors are among the limitations and processes that 
may cause the presented algorithm to lose its effectiveness: Visual 
texture less terrains, excessive magnetic disturbances and altitude 
beyond tested range and aggressive maneuvers. These issues of the 
subject of future research directive being undertaken by the current 
authors.
5. Conclusion
A robust new visual-inertial odometry (VIO) algorithm designed for 
fixed-wing UAVs operating in GNSS-denied, high-altitude environments 
is proposed and validated. The proposed system enhances the baseline 
FMSCKF algorithm by introducing innovations in feature extraction, 
selective feature usage during updates as well as integration with the 
magnetometer data. These improvements have led to significantly better 
performance, demonstrating the system’s ability to navigate over long 
distances (up to 15 km) in environments with sparse visual features with 
good reliability.
Experimental results show that our method outperforms traditional 
VIO algorithms such as VINS-Mono, MSCKF, and SVO+GTSAM, espe­
cially in high-altitude scenarios. The system maintains stable and ac­
curate trajectory estimation, even in environments where visual and 
inertial data alone could cause estimator instability. Furthermore, by 
reducing the dependency on computationally expensive feature extrac­
tion, our approach achieves notable computational savings, making it 
highly effective and efficient for real-time applications.
