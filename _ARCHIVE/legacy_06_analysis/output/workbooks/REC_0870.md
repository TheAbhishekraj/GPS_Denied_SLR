Abstract— This paper presents a structured survey and 
comprehensive synthesis of influential research on autonomous 
drones, focusing on their development, applications, and 
performance evaluation. The studies span multiple domains, 
including medical delivery, agricultural automation, military 
surveillance, swarm coordination, urban navigation, and last-mile 
logistics. Techniques such as deep learning, fuzzy logic, 
reinforcement learning, SLAM, and LLM-based adaptive 
navigation have been applied and evaluated using both 
simulations and field deployments. Representative parameters 
include navigation accuracy, latency, battery life, payload 
capacity, flight time, and delivery efficiency. To complement this 
analysis, we highlight real-world drone deployments such as 
Zipline (medical logistics), Wing (urban parcel delivery), and 
Matternet (hospital supply chains), thereby linking experimental 
findings to operational viability. Emerging trends include the rise 
of AI-driven autonomy, integration with mobile and cloud 
applications, GPS-denied navigation, and use in fire detection, 
infrastructure inspection, and healthcare delivery. However, gaps 
remain in privacy-preserving data usage, AI responsibility, 
benchmarking standards, and commercial viability assessments. 
This survey identifies opportunities for future research toward 
scalable, context-aware, and ethically aligned deployment of 
autonomous drones.

I. INTRODUCTION
The evolution of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), 
commonly known as drones, has dramatically reshaped the 
landscape of modern automation across sectors such as logistics, 
agriculture, healthcare, military, and urban monitoring [1]. As 
drones transition from manually controlled platforms to fully 
autonomous systems, there has been an exponential increase in 
research investigating their capabilities, reliability, and 
integration into real-world ecosystems [2]. 
Autonomous drones leverage a fusion of technologies that 
enable them to perceive, decide, and act with minimal human 
intervention. Techniques such as deep reinforcement learning 
allow for adaptive decision-making in unfamiliar environments, 
while fuzzy logic systems facilitate nuanced responses in 
dynamic or uncertain contexts [3]. Simultaneous Localization 
and Mapping (SLAM) algorithms, including ORB-SLAM2, 
empower drones to construct and update maps in real time, 
enhancing navigation precision [4]. Recent advancements in 
large language models (LLMs) have also introduced novel 
approaches to adaptive mission planning and high-level human-
drone interaction[5]-[9]. 
The practical applications of these systems span a wide 
spectrum. In agriculture, drones are utilized for crop monitoring, 
pesticide dispersion, and yield estimation[10]. In healthcare, 
field deployments such as Zipline and UPS Flight Forward 
deliver vaccines, blood units, or medical kits to inaccessible 
regions. Military implementations range from surveillance to 
tactical 
reconnaissance, 
while 
urban 
usage 
includes 
infrastructure inspection, traffic management, and delivery 
logistics[11]-[12]. Drone swarms, enabled by coordinated AI 
strategies, offer scalability and resilience for complex missions 
such as fire detection or search-and-rescue operations[13]. 
Despite the considerable promise, several barriers inhibit the 
seamless adoption of autonomous drones. Key challenges 
include real-time obstacle avoidance, energy constraints due to 
limited battery life, secure communication in swarm networks, 
and privacy concerns regarding collected data and responsible 
AI decision-making. Regulatory uncertainties related to flight 
permissions and airspace integration further constrain adoption. 
The variability in environmental conditions also affects drone 
performance, necessitating clear benchmarking methods and 
distinction between simulation and field testing results to ensure 
reliability[14].

V. PROBLEM FORMULATION 
The rapid growth in UAV adoption across defense, 
agriculture, logistics, and public safety has driven significant 
research activity. However, much of the literature remains 
fragmented and narrowly focused on isolated technical 
problems (e.g., navigation, perception, energy optimization), 
without offering a consistent comparative framework across 
application domains. The absence of standardized metrics and 
benchmarking methods makes it difficult to evaluate trade-offs, 
replicate findings, or align simulation results with real-world 
deployments. This lack of structured synthesis limits the ability 
of researchers and practitioners to design optimal, scalable, and 
regulation-ready autonomous drone solutions.

VII. CONCLUSION & FUTURE DIRECTION 
This structured survey highlights significant progress in 
autonomous drone research, with AI/ML, sensor fusion, and 
swarm coordination driving autonomy. Applications span 
agriculture, logistics, defense, and disaster response, with field 
deployments (e.g., Zipline, UPS Flight Forward, Wing, DJI 
enterprise models) validating selected use cases. 
However, most reported advancements remain simulation-
heavy, with limited translation to field-tested, regulation-
compliant systems. Real-world deployment faces barriers of 
battery limitations, environmental unpredictability, fault 
tolerance, and privacy/ethical issues in data collection and AI 
decision-making. 
The study emphasizes a transition from simulation-focused 
experimentation 
to 
benchmark-driven, 
field-validated 
deployments. Future research should incorporate: 
x 
Standardized benchmarking frameworks (as proposed in 
this paper). 
x 
Cost-effectiveness and commercial viability assessments 
for scaling. 
x 
Ethically aligned AI models with responsible data handling. 
x 
Regulation-aware designs supporting safe integration into 
national airspaces. 
Ultimately, the path forward lies in developing autonomous 
drones that are scalable, trustworthy, commercially viable, and 
socially responsible, enabling wide adoption across critical 
sectors.
