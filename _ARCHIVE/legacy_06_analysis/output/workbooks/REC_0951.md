cycle management. Traditional manual methods are
labor-intensive and hazardous, particularly for high-rise struc-
tures and conﬁned spaces, and unmanned aerial vehicles
(UAVs) have emerged as a transformative alternative by
enabling rapid, noncontact data acquisition [1], [2]. Accurate
and real-time state estimation is fundamental to UAV-based
inspection, directly determining navigation safety and mapping
quality [3]. LiDAR-inertial odometry (LIO), which tightly

However, practical deployment demands not only high-ﬁdelity

that challenges resource-constrained onboard processors [6].
The past decade has witnessed remarkable advances in
LIO systems. Filter-based approaches built upon the extended
Kalman ﬁlter and its variants [7], [8], [9] constitute the
mainstream. FAST-LIO2 [7] pioneered direct point registration
with an incremental k-d tree, achieving up to 100 Hz odom-
etry. Faster-LIO [10] further accelerated computation using
incremental voxels, and iG-LIO [8] integrated generalized ICP
constraints for improved registration accuracy. Despite these
advances in frame-based LIO, per-frame processing introduces
inherent latency, limiting responsiveness during aggressive
maneuvers [11]. Point-by-point update frameworks [12], [13],
[14] address this by processing each LiDAR point at its true
sampling instant, achieving 4–8 kHz output and eliminating
in-frame motion distortion. However, processing every point
individually incurs substantial computational overhead, partic-
ularly in building environments where planar surfaces provide
redundant geometric constraints, straining onboard resources.
To improve robustness under challenging sensing condi-
tions, intensity-based methods [15], [16] enhance constraints
in

Multisensor

visual-inertial SLAM with deep feature extraction [18],
[19], adaptive noise estimation for visual-inertial odome-
try [20], [21], and GNSS-RTK coupling [22] have also
been explored to address complementary sensing modalities.
Beyond sensor-level enhancements, structural priors inherent
in man-made environments oﬀer an orthogonal avenue for
improving estimation accuracy. The Atlanta World general-
ization [23] relaxes strict Manhattan orthogonality for linear
SLAM formulations, and LIO-BIM [24] couples odometry
with building information models. However, these approaches
require environments to conform to predeﬁned structural tem-
plates, limiting applicability to buildings with nonstandard
geometry. Ground surface constraints such as VBRO [25]
suppress accumulated errors through spatiotemporal ground
modeling, but are designed for ground vehicles and not directly
transferable to UAV platforms with intermittent ground obser-
vations.
From a computational eﬃciency perspective, eﬃcient obser-
vation selection and map representation strategies have been
proposed to reduce redundancy. IO-LIO [26] introduced
information-oriented voxel mapping, and UA-LIO [27] mod-
eled measurement uncertainty to improve match quality
awareness. FI-SLAM [28] employs scalar information metrics
based on subspace vector similarity for feature selection, but
evaluates each point through a single aggregated score that
cannot distinguish pose and dynamics subspaces. VoxelMap++
[29] and C3P-VoxelMap [30] achieve plane merging for
improved map accuracy and memory eﬃciency, but do not
extract the learned planar structure as constraints for state
estimation. Overall, existing methods either focus on mapping
eﬃciency or observation selection in isolation, lacking a uni-
ﬁed framework that jointly exploits geometric predictability,
directional information allocation, and structural priors to
balance computational cost and estimation quality.
Despite substantial progress, existing LIO systems exhibit
three key limitations for building inspection. First, neither
frame-based nor point-by-point methods exploit the geomet-
ric predictability of planar surfaces prevalent in buildings,
resulting in unnecessary computational overhead. Second,
observation selection methods, including recent information-
based approaches [28], employ scalar metrics that conﬂate
information across state components, risking observability
degradation when directional constraints are systematically
eliminated. Third, structural regularities remain underutilized;
plane merging methods [29], [30] improve map quality but
do not feed learned structure back into state estimation,
while even the more ﬂexible Atlanta World generalization
[23] requires environments to conform to predeﬁned structural
templates that fail in buildings with irregular geometry. These
limitations lead to either excessive computation or degraded
accuracy.
To address these limitations, this article proposes PSS-LIO.
The main contributions are as follows.
1) A prediction-based coarse ﬁltering mechanism that
bypasses observations conforming to geometric pre-
dictions, reducing computational load while preserving
informative points.
2) A ﬁne selection strategy via directional Fisher Infor-
mation decomposition, enabling multiresolution updates
that adapt to observation informativeness with direc-
tional coverage guarantees.
3) A structure-prior guided update mechanism that learns
dominant surface orientations online and incorporates
them as adaptive soft constraints for improved rotational
accuracy.
4) Comprehensive experiments on public datasets and
real-world UAV deployments demonstrating consistent
accuracy improvement and computational reduction over
state-of-the-art methods.
The three mechanisms operate sequentially: prediction-
based ﬁltering reduces point volume by exploiting geometric
predictability,
information-driven

ensures

structure-prior update leverages learned regularities to enhance
estimation accuracy. This hierarchical architecture achieves
both computational eﬃciency and estimation precision tailored
for building inspection, freeing onboard resources for concur-
rent inspection algorithms.

tion II details the proposed methodology. Section III presents
experimental evaluation and ablation studies. Section V con-

D. Structure-Prior Guided Update
Building environments exhibit strong structural regularities,
including vertical walls, horizontal ﬂoors, and orthogonal
intersections. These patterns are not exploited by conventional
LIO frameworks that treat each observation independently. A
class of voxel mapping methods, represented by VoxelMap++
[29] and C3P-VoxelMap [30], identify dominant planar struc-
tures through voxel-level merging to improve plane ﬁtting
accuracy and memory eﬃciency. While such plane merging
approaches share the high-level goal of exploiting environmen-
tal regularity, the learned structure remains conﬁned to the map
representation layer and is not exploited as constraints for state
estimation. Inspired by Schema Theory in cognitive science
[32], a mechanism is proposed that learns dominant structural
directions online and incorporates them as soft constraints
during state update. Unlike Manhattan World assumptions
[33] that impose hard orthogonality constraints, this approach
learns environment-speciﬁc patterns and applies adaptive con-
straints, accommodating buildings that deviate from strict
orthogonality. The key distinctions from the aforementioned
class of plane merging methods are: 1) clustering operates in
the normal direction space to extract global structural patterns
rather than local spatial coplanarity, 2) the learned directions
directly participate in state update as residual constraints
rather than merely improving map quality, and 3) the adaptive
weighting mechanism prevents degradation in nonorthogonal
or unstructured environments where rigid planar assumptions
do not hold.

III. METRICS EVALUATION
This section evaluates PSS-LIO through experiments on
public datasets, covering accuracy (Section III-B), eﬃciency
(Section III-C), ablation studies (Section III-D), and parameter
sensitivity (Section III-E).
A. Experimental Setup
The proposed PSS-LIO is evaluated on four public datasets
covering diverse environments and LiDAR types. The M2DGR
dataset [34] provides multisensor sequences collected on
a ground robot in campus environments. The lili dataset
[35] contains structured indoor and outdoor sequences with
Livox Horizon solid-state LiDAR. The utbm dataset [36]
provides urban driving sequences recorded with two Velodyne
HDL-32E LiDARs. The M3DSS dataset [37] oﬀers indoor
building sequences captured by a quadrotor UAV platform
characterized by high vibration and aggressive maneuvers.
Among these, M2DGR, M3DSS, and lili feature building-
rich scenarios aligned with the target application, while utbm
evaluates generalization capability in unstructured outdoor
environments. Five LIO baselines are selected: FAST-LIO2
[7], Faster-LIO [10], Point-LIO [12], iG-LIO [8], and
LIO-SAM [4].
All experiments were run on an ASUS NUC 14 Pro (Intel
Core Ultra 5, 16 GB RAM, Ubuntu 20.04). Key parameters:
prediction thresholds τl = 1.0, τh = 2.5, ρ∗= 0.4, α = 0.1;
information thresholds τp = 0.01, τd = 0.005, κmin = 0.1,
W = 500; structure learning Nb = 2000, K = 3, λ0 = 0.5, γ0 =
200, ϵ fit = 0.05 m, σobs = 0.02 m. The map uncertainty σ2
map, j
(7) is computed adaptively and requires no manual tuning.
Sensitivity analysis is provided in Section III-E.
B. Accuracy Evaluation
Absolute trajectory error measured as root-mean-square
error against ground truth serves as the primary evaluation
metric. Table I presents an accuracy comparison across 12
sequences. PSS-LIO achieves optimal performance on eight
sequences, with particularly strong results in building-rich
environments. On structured indoor environments including
M2DGR gate 1, hall 1, room 1, and M3DSS sequences,
PSS-LIO consistently achieves the lowest or comparable
RMSE, beneﬁting from the structure-prior mechanism that
exploits dominant orthogonal directions. On the lili dataset fea-
turing structured building environments, PSS-LIO outperforms
all baselines across all three sequences, demonstrating eﬀective
integration of predictive ﬁltering and structural constraints. For
less structured outdoor scenarios such as M2DGR street 3
and utbm sequences, PSS-LIO maintains competitive accuracy
while exhibiting superior robustness. Notably, several base-
lines suﬀer signiﬁcant degradation on challenging sequences,
whereas PSS-LIO delivers consistent performance through the
adaptive weight mechanism.
Table II reports end-to-end drift on sequences. On building-
centric sequences lili 6 and lili 7, PSS-LIO achieves the
lowest drift among all methods, conﬁrming eﬀective drift
suppression through structure-prior constraints. On utbm
long-distance urban sequences, PSS-LIO remains compet-
itive with leading methods. The results validate that the
three-stage pipeline provides both accuracy improvement in
target building scenarios and robust generalization to diverse
environments.

C. Eﬃciency Evaluation
For practical UAV-based building inspection, computa-
tional eﬃciency directly determines whether suﬃcient onboard
resources remain available for concurrent inspection tasks
such as defect detection and semantic segmentation. Table III
compares computational eﬃciency across three representative

memory consumption among all evaluated methods.
For processing time, PSS-LIO reduces computation by
25 %–40 % compared to leading lightweight methods includ-
ing Faster-LIO and Point-LIO. The improvement is most
signiﬁcant on M3DSS 2 featuring dense indoor point clouds,
where the prediction-based ﬁltering eﬀectively bypasses
redundant planar observations. On lili 2 and utbm 4,
PSS-LIO maintains consistent eﬃciency gains while all base-
line methods exhibit increased processing time due to higher
point density. LIO-SAM incurs substantially higher computa-

For memory consumption, PSS-LIO achieves 30 %–40 %
reduction across all sequences. The three-stage ﬁltering
pipeline processes only 30 %–45 % of original points for
full state update, signiﬁcantly reducing memory allocation for
map maintenance and covariance computation. This eﬃciency
advantage makes PSS-LIO particularly suitable for resource-
constrained UAV platforms in building inspection applications.
These eﬃciency gains are critical for resource-constrained
UAV platforms: the reduced computational footprint not only
ensures real-time odometry but also preserves substantial pro-
cessing capacity for concurrent inspection algorithms, enabling
integrated localization-and-inspection workﬂows essential for
practical building inspection deployment.
D. Ablation Study
Table IV quantiﬁes individual contributions of the three
proposed mechanisms across diﬀerent environments. Dis-
abling prediction-based ﬁltering increases processing time by
30 %–40 % on all sequences while maintaining compara-
ble accuracy, conﬁrming its primary role in computational
eﬃciency through bypassing redundant observations. Remov-
ing information-driven selection results in a moderate time
increase and slight accuracy degradation on lili 2 and
M3DSS 1, validating its contribution to preserving geo-
metrically informative constraints. Disabling structure-prior
leads to 10 %–50 % accuracy degradation on building-
rich sequences lili 2 and M3DSS 1 but yields marginal
improvement on utbm 1, demonstrating that this mechanism
is speciﬁcally designed for exploiting structural regularities
prevalent in building environments. The full PSS-LIO achieves
optimal trade-oﬀfor building inspection applications, with
each component addressing distinct aspects: prediction-based
ﬁltering for eﬃciency, information-driven selection for con-
straint quality, and structure-prior for accuracy enhancement.
This synergistic design ensures that PSS-LIO delivers high-
accuracy state estimation while minimizing computational
overhead, thereby enabling reliable deployment on resource-
constrained platforms with suﬃcient capacity reserved for
real-time inspection tasks.

V. CONCLUSION
This article presented PSS-LIO, a computationally eﬃ-
cient LIO framework for UAV-based building inspection.
The proposed approach integrates prediction-based coarse
ﬁltering to bypass redundant observations, information-driven
ﬁne selection with theoretically grounded directional cover-
age analysis for multiresolution updates, and structure-prior
guided update that learns dominant orientations as adaptive
soft constraints feeding back into state estimation. Compre-
hensive evaluation on four public datasets demonstrated that
PSS-LIO achieves the best accuracy on 8 out of 12 sequences
while reducing processing time by 25 %–40 % and mem-
ory consumption by 30 %–40 % over leading lightweight
baselines. Ablation studies further conﬁrmed that the three
mechanisms address complementary aspects: prediction-based
ﬁltering primarily contributes to computational eﬃciency,
information-driven selection preserves geometric constraint
quality, and structure-prior guided update speciﬁcally enhances
accuracy in building-rich environments. Real-world UAV
deployments across four representative inspection scenarios
validated that PSS-LIO achieves accuracy improvements of
32 %–56 %, processing time reduction of 33 %–56 %, and
memory reduction of 34 %–66 % relative to all baseline
methods. Comparisons with VoxelMap++ and C3P-VoxelMap
conﬁrmed
the
architectural
advantage
of
the
proposed
structure-aware state estimation over map-level plane merging
alone.
Current limitations include the quasi-static environment
assumption and reduced eﬀectiveness in scenes lacking
dominant planar structures. Future work will explore visual-
inertial fusion for geometrically degenerate scenarios, adaptive
structure-prior learning for dynamic environments, and multi-
UAV collaborative mapping with shared structural priors.
