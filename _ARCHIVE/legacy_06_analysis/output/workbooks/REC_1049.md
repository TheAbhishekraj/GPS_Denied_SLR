Abstract— This paper presents a novel approach for mobile
target tracking using a UAV ﬂying in a cluttered and GPS-denied
environment that occludes the target partially and momentar-
ily. The problem is modelled as a sequential decision process
under uncertainty using the Partially Observable Markov De-
cision Process framework (POMDP). The motion of the UAV is
planned as sequences of high-level actions in order to optimise a
multi-objective mission. A modular system executes the motion
planning. The UAV and target dynamics models are included
in the POMDP transition function as a black-box simulator.
The target is detected using a front facing camera onboard the
UAV. A model of the projection of the target and obstacles into
the image plane of the camera is presented and used in the
POMDP transition function. This model enables the POMDP
solver to include the occlusions in the Field of View (FOV) of
the camera caused by the obstacles in the scenario. Results in
simulation indicate that the developed system is able to guide
the UAV towards locations in which the target tracking duration
is maximised whilst avoiding collisions with obstacles.
This
research has the potential to be used below the forest canopy,
in fault or GPS-denied environments, underground mines and
urban canyons.

1. INTRODUCTION
UAV are becoming an efﬁcient tool for a variety of ap-
plications including site and infrastructure inspection, re-
mote sensing, exploration, ecology and biodiversity assess-
ment [1], [2], [3], [4], [5]. There are several areas of de-
velopment including path planning, design and their use [6],
[7], [8].However, there are certain scenarios in which these
type of missions are challenging such as in places where there
is a partial or no GPS signal and there are obstacles that
must be avoided. Such places may include, below the tree
canopy, natural and urban canyons, indoor environments and
underground mines [9], [10].
This paper presents a novel approach to solving a multi-
objective problem of ﬁnding tracking and following a mobile
target with a UAV.
The main contributions of this paper are:
1. A novel approach for modelling a multi-objective UAV
mission under uncertainty to track occluded targets in clut-
tered and GPS-denied environments. This approach models
the onboard camera FOV and projection of the target and
possible occlusions by obstacles in the scenario in order to
maximise the time the UAV detects and follows a moving
target.
2. This research provides novel approaches for generating
the reward functions for the UAV Target Tracking POMDP
formulation and provides results of tests in simulation and
ﬂight trials.
3. This research has the potential to be used as a method for
collecting data in challenging environments and for remote
sensing and monitoring in the ﬁelds of urban search and
rescue, underground mining, biodiversity and ecology. For
example, tracking of wildlife in natural cluttered environ-
ments.
2. BACKGROUND
POMDP
A POMDP is a mathematical framework to model sequen-
tial decision process under state, transition and observation
uncertainty [11], [12]. It consists of the following elements
(S, A, O, T, Z, R, γ) where S is the set of states in the
environment, A is the set of actions the agent, in this case
the UAV, can execute, O is the set of observations, T is the
transition function between states after executing an action,
Z is the distribution function describing the probability of
observing o from state s after taking action a, R is the set
of rewards for every state and γ is the discount factor. A
feature of POMDPs is that the state of the process is not
deﬁned by a single value at any given moment, but instead,

Problem description
In this paper we present a target ﬁnding and tracking mission
that consists on a quad-copter UAV ﬂying in a 3D space with-
out access to external GPS localisation and in the presence
of obstacles.
The UAV must search and detect a moving
target with an onboard front facing camera and follow it for as
long as possible. Figure 1(a) shows the ﬂying environment,
a visible target and the obstacles. The target initial location
is uncertain. It can start moving from four possible zones.
The target moves on the ground using one of three different
modes which are explained in section 3. Figure 1(b) shows a
partially occluded target. The occlusion of the target caused
by the obstacles is modelled in section 3.
After take off, the UAV hovers for a few seconds to initialise
its orientation with readings from its on-board sensors. There
is some initial drift produced in this initial hovering position
and a normal probability distribution (see Eq. (1)) with mean
value around the take off position is used to model this initial
uncertainty.
The UAV navigation system needs an alternative source of
localisation in order to reduce the growing estimation error
over time due to the uncertainty in the sensing of the heading
angle and the visual odometry calculation error. In order to
solve this problem, unique landmarks are introduced into the
scenario. Each of the obstacles has a label which is used as
a landmark. In our experiment, an augmented reality (AR)
tag that can be detected by the front camera (see Fig. 1(b)) is
used.
Once the UAV’s front camera faces the AR tag, a ground
station AR reader program gives an accurate estimation of
the UAV position with respect to the obstacle.
A frame
transformation is then calculated in order to obtain the UAV
pose in the world frame and reset the error in the localisation.
The landmark detection using the front camera is modelled in
the POMDP Target Tracking formulation, so that the motion
planning not only incorporates actions for ﬁnding the target
but also incorporates sequences of actions to guide the UAV
towards the landmarks in order to reduce the uncertainty in
the UAV localisation.

3. POMDP MODELLING
State Variables (S)
The state variables considered in the POMDP formulation are
the quad-rotor position and heading Pr = (xw, yw, zw, Ψw),
the target planar position and heading PT = (xT , yT , ΨT ),
target forward velocity VT and the UAV’s forward velocity
˙xb and lateral velocity ˙yb in the body frame. The wind speed
Vw and orientation Wψ are all measured in the world frame.
It is important to note that aerodynamic effects which may af-
fect the UAV dynamics are neglected since the UAV mission
is carried out under the following conditions:
▶The UAV navigates in an indoor environment where the
wind disturbances are minimal. The UAV is also ﬂying above
the ground at a minimal altitude of 0.6 m where ground
effects can be neglected.
▶The obstacles are enlarged in the map to increase the
safety distance and to avoid affecting the aerodynamics of
the UAV as the propeller wash may interact with the obstacle
creating unwanted disturbances.

Actions (A)
The UAV actions are designed to be the set-points for the four
PID controllers, thus the UAV actuates in four state variables,
namely, forward ˙xb and lateral ˙yb velocities, heading angle
Ψb and altitude zb.
The set of actions consists of seven actions. An action to keep
the aircraft hovering, i.e. ˙xb, ˙yb = 0 m/s; two actions to go
forward and backward, with current heading angle Ψw, lateral
velocity ˙yb = 0 m/s, and forward velocity, ˙xb = 0.6 m/s
and ˙xb = −0.6 m/s, respectively. Actions Up and Down,
increase or decrease altitude zw in 0.3 m, respectively, with
multi-rotor velocity ﬁxed at 0 m/s, and two actions to roll
left and right with current heading angle Ψw and forward
velocity at ˙xb = 0 m/s, and lateral velocity at ˙yb = ±0.6
m/s, respectively.
Observations (O)
An observation is composed of the following variables: 1) the
UAV position in the world frame Pw, 2) the target’s position
and heading if it is detected by the front looking camera PTw,
and 3) boolean variables indicating whether the target has
been detected and whether or not an AR tag is detected by
the front camera.
Perception uncertainty—The system receives an odometry
reading from the perception module with an uncertainty that
is caused by the accumulating error and drift in the yaw angle
reading. This uncertainty is represented by adding noise to
the UAV position using a gaussian distribution as in equation
(1), with mean value µ around the perceived position and
standard deviation σ = 0.5 m.
This uncertainty can be reduced, i.e. σ = 0.05m if the UAV
ﬂies in front of an obstacle where it can detect an AR tag
and can reset the error inherent to the odometry and onboard
sensors.
If the target is detected by the onboard camera,
the detection system provides the target position within the
image. This position is transformed into a position in the
world frame. This uncertainty is modelled as deviations from
the measured speed and accounted for in the parameters of
the controlled states ˙xbd, ˙ybd, ψbd and zbd.

UAV motion Uncertainty—Another source of uncertainty for
the target tracking mission is in the dynamics and the motion
of the UAV. That is, there is uncertainty in the fact that a
commanded action may not result in the desired response.
Four PID controllers where designed to control four states
of the UAV. Characteristic step responses in time where
obtained in order to include them in the transition model as
the dynamic model of the UAV.
The dynamic response to the actions generated by the
POMDP motion planner are included as discretised look-up
tables. The standard deviation of the UAV step responses for
each of the controlled states were measured in ﬂight trials and
characterised using a Gaussian distribution.

=

target could be located on the ground in any location within

as a Gaussian distribution (Eq. 1).
Target motion modes—The target moves on the x −y plane
with a constant height and its motion is modelled using two
modes:
▶The target moves in a loop fashion visiting different zones
in the ﬂying area in a clockwise direction.
▶The target tries to escape in the opposite direction away
from the UAV with a probability P(TE) of 0.5 and a probabil-
ity to continue with the same heading P(TC) of 0.5.
Target speed uncertainty—The other source of uncertainty in
the target motion is its speed. The target has a probability
P(TM) of 0.9 of moving with constant speed and a probability
P(TS) of 0.1 of remaining in the same position.
These
probabilities model the effect that the terrain has on the target
motion which gets affected by the use of tiles on the ground
in order to simulate texture of real scenarios.

4. RESULTS
In order to test the target ﬁnding and tracking problem formu-
lated as a POMDP, we implemented in hardware and software
one of the fastest on-line POMDP algorithms to the authors
knowledge, ABT [16]. This algorithm has been tested previ-
ously in simulations and onboard a UAV for navigation [17]
and target tracking [18], [19] and target ﬁnding [20], however
this paper presents a novel target tracking formulation, mod-
elling the UAV front camera for target projection and obstacle
occlusions, so that the UAV can maximise the time tracking
the target with the front camera in cluttered and occluded
environments.
The target tacking formulation was tested in simulation,
running 50 episodes for each of the reward functions. Each
episode duration is of maximum 60 time-steps, approxi-
mately 60 seconds.
The UAV starts the episode from a
hovering position with the ﬁrst objective of ﬁnding the target.
Once the target has been found the UAV should maximise the
time for tracking the target, i.e. the total time that the target
AR tag has been detected by the onboard camera. A tracking
time ratio RT = 100(ttrack/tepisode) is calculated as the time
the target is tracked over the duration of the episode in order
to evaluate the performance of the system and each reward
function structure.

structures evaluated in term of the tracking time ration and the
average discounted return for the two target motion modes.

5. CONCLUSIONS
An online POMDP model for mobile target ﬁnding and
tracking with a UAV cluttered, occluded and GPS-Denied
environments was presented and demonstrated in simulation.
The formulated online model is able to ﬁnd, detect and track
a target with a UAV ﬂying within a conﬁned space using
an onboard front looking camera and in the presence of
obstacles.
The formulation achieves higher tracking time ratio when the
target’s motion model is mostly deterministic. However, this
ratio decreases for the cases where the target is escaping from
the UAV and its motion has a higher stochastic component.
