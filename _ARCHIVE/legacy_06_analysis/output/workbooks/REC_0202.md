I.
INTRODUCTION
Umnanned aerial vehicle plays an important role in fields
such as military, civil and scientific research. However, the
traditional VAV positioning and navigation system cannot
respond to the fault in time, especially in the field search and
rescue, battlefield reconnaissance
and other scenes.
The
shielding from mountains and forests, as well as the complex
and changeable battlefield enviromnental, pose challenges to the
traditional UAV positioning technology which highly relies on
global positioning system.
To address this challenge, researchers have proposed
different solutions, which can be divided into two main
categories: autonomous localization method relying on single
UAV and cooperative localization (CL) method relying on
ground anchors or multi-UAVs.
Autonomous localization methods mainly include map
matching localization and dead reckoning (DR) technology [1-
3]. For example, common visual positioning technology are
based on map matching positioning methods. Such methods
perceive the surrounding environment by sensors and match
positioning with the built-in map. Due to the high complexity,
they
are almost impossible to deploy on small UAVs.
Meanwhile, dead reckoning technologies, including the fast-
developing simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) in
recent years, have no approach to eliminate the problem of
accumulated errors in positioning [4].With the increase of flight
time, the accumulation of positioning errors becomes more and
more obvious.
The cooperative localization method is mainly based on
static anchors or other intelligence agents [5,6]. Static anchor
points, which need to be arranged in advance, are not suitable
for complex dynamic scenes. The mainstream cooperative
localization relays on multiple agents with accurate location
ability to coordinate GPS fault nodes, which requires the GPS of
the cooperative agent to be available and are not suitable for the
enviromnent where GPS signals are completely blocked.
This
paper
focuses
on
the
cooperative
positioning
technology ofUAV cluster system in GPS-denied enviromnent.
We use infonnation provided by labeled UWB station to
calculate a predicted position vector and describe acceleration
vector in ground-coordinate system with acceleration measured
by IMU and Euler angle provided by the other module installed
in UAV The data collected and processed by sensors are passed
to the control system for filtering. The control system obtains
higher accuracy position data by fusion processing.

VI. CONCLUSION
In order to reduce the requirement of sensor accuracy for
VAV and realize the use of low-cost and low precision sensors
for positioning system, this project uses a multi-sensor fusion
method to fuse the data collected by IMU and UWB, and uses
the fused higher precision data as the basis for positioning
system. The simulation results show that the data obtained by
this method greatly
improves the measurement accuracy
compared with the original data. In the near future we will use
this positioning system on our UAVs, This method greatly
reduces the need for sensor accuracy and enables the design of
a small cost UAV positioning system, which is of great
significance for reducing the cost ofUAVs.
