Abstract— The paper focuses on the movement of a drone without a GPS (Global Positioning
System) signal. For this purpose, diﬀerent types of sensors and their output data are described.
In order to realize a ﬂight without GPS, several steps need to be done such as data description and
analysis and its correlation with GPS coordinates during the research. For data analysis, data
from airspeed sensor, barometer, compass, GPS sensor, gyroscope sensor, accelerometer sensor
and rangeﬁnders sensor were used. Based on the correlation dependencies obtained, software for
autonomous ﬂight without GPS was designed.
1. INTRODUCTION
Nowadays, drones are used more and more, both in civilian and military operations. Indeed, the
most common uses we know of these machines are for leisure or military operations, for example.
However, drones are used in many other ﬁelds that we do not necessarily think of, such as agri-
culture, cinema, surveillance, or even ﬁrst aid, to name but a few. Drones therefore occupy an
important place in society that will continue to grow in the coming years.
As with all advanced technology, drones carry sensors for real-time monitoring and control. As
drones operate in stressful conditions for the on-board electronics, it is essential to ensure that
their sensors can take over from each other in the event of a failure. Thus, drones must be able
to continue to function if one of the sensors fails, relying on the other on-board sensors. This
problem can be related to instrumentation ﬂight in aviation. Indeed, when a ﬂight data is wrong
or insuﬃcient, pilots must know how to use the other data to continue the ﬂight. The same applies
to drones. In the event of a GPS failure, the drone must be able to continue its ﬂight to meet its
objectives, especially when these are critical objectives.
Also, there are many situations where drones cannot rely on GPS signals to maintain their
route. Indeed, in secure areas where GPS signals are jammed, or indoors, where the signal is weak,
ensuring ﬂight viability is a major issue for drone specialists.
Finally, GPS is an old technology. Launched in 1995 by the United States, the ﬁrst experiments
with GPS date back to the early 1970s. The missions carried out by drones require more and
more precision. However, GPS is reaching its technical limits, which no longer match the accuracy
required by drones.
2. MATERIAL AND METHODS
It is essential to improve the monitoring and control of a drone at distance. A drone has many
sensors, all with diﬀerent purposes.
However, coupling data from several types of sensors can
provide similar results to those given by other sensors.
Some sensors, such as GPS, are based on sometimes outdated technology, with an accuracy that
may be considered adequate in some use cases, but insuﬃcient in critical operations requiring high
accuracy. Furthermore, in the event of a failure of one or more sensors, creating redundancy is
one of the most important issues in aeronautics, to be able to continue ﬂying in the event of a
failure [1–3].
Thus, our team was able to ask questions around various issues: “How UAVs interacts with
their environment to compute ﬂight data?”.

2.2. Data Collection
To carry out this project, we needed to collect a lot of data. Indeed, we needed to compare data
from diﬀerent sensors of a drone, during a complete ﬂight. To do this, we carried out a test ﬂight,
during which all the data from all the drone’s sensors were recorded. Among the sensors that make
up our drone, we ﬁnd:
• Airspeed: sensors that measures the speed of an aircraft relative to the surrounding air, using
the diﬀerential between the pressure of still air and that of moving air compressed by the craft
forward motion;
• Barometers: sensors that detect atmospheric pressure;
• Camera: optical instrument that captures images of the environment. Coupled with IMU,
a camera can achieve visual odometry, which is the process of determining the position and
orientation of the drone by analyzing the associated images;
• Compass: sensor designed to detect and respond to the magnetic ﬁeld of the Earth. Items
in this family are designed for dead reckoning, digital compass, or a digital magnetometer in
the X, Y, Z axis;
• GPS (Global Positioning System): receivers with antennas that use a satellite-based
navigation system with a satellite constellation in orbit around the earth to provide position,
velocity, and timing information;
• Gyroscope: advanced accelerometers that can measure and maintain the orientation and
angular velocity of an object. These sensors can measure the tilt and lateral orientation of
the object whereas accelerometer can only measure the linear motion;
• IMU (Inertial Measurement Unit): device composed of multiple sensors that can measure
and report speciﬁc gravity and angular rate of an object to which it is attached. It consists
in the addition of gyroscopes and accelerometers sensors;
• Rangeﬁnders: also called distance sensors, these sensors work on the principle of reﬂected
light waves. An infrared light is sent from an infrared remote or beacon, then reﬂected. The
reﬂected light is detected and then an estimate of distance is calculated between sensor and
object.
Our drone has many other sensors on board. However, analyzing the data from the other sensors
will not be of any interest in our project. Therefore, we will focus on the sensors mentioned above
throughout the project. All these sensors are linked to an on-board computer, which operates as
the brain of our drone. The drone does not require high computational capabilities. However, the
on-board computer needs to be very liable, small, lightweight and energy saving. So, we have opted
for a NVIDIA Jetson which is often used on drones and other radio commanded aircrafts. It is very
similar to a RASPBERRY Pi. At the end of our test ﬂight, all the data provided by the sensors
was recorded on the on-board computer. We were able to retrieve this data using a log ﬁle. This
is a ﬁle extension that can be compared to the logbook of a ship’s captain. All the data from all
the sensors are listed in a raw form.

Before doing these analyses, it is important to understand what a sensor is and what its pur-
pose is. A sensor is a device that detects and responds to some type of input from the physical
environment. The input can be light, heat, motion, moisture, pressure, or any number of other
environmental phenomena. The output is electrical voltage or intensity. Once we have retrieved
the electrical output values, we can analyze them and determine correlations between the environ-
mental variables, i.e., the physical values at the sensor input, and the output variables, i.e., the
electrical current or intensity.
2.3.1. Airspeed Sensor Data Analysis
Airspeed sensors are used to calculate the speed of a drone, in addition to the measurements given
by the GPS sensor. The sensor is made of a tube placed on the drone, parallel to the ground and
in the same direction as the front of the drone. When the drone is in ﬂight, and moving, pressure
is applied to it. This pressure enters the tube. The principle is to compare the pressure inside the
tube with the pressure on the rest of the drone. Thus, we can estimate the speed of the drone
according to the pressure delta. The higher the delta of pressure is, the faster the device goes.

2.3.3. Compass Sensor Data Analysis
The compass role is to measure the orientation of the drone in relation with the magnetic north of
the Earth. To do this, it is composed of a ﬁxed magnetic bar, bathed in a ﬂuid that prevents any
resistance in the medium. Thus, when the drone changes direction, the magnetic bar of the compass
reorients itself according to the angle with the magnetic north. Thus, the direction of the drone
can be known in real time via three coordinates along the X, Y , and Z axes in a three-dimensional
reference frame.

2.3.4. GPS Sensor Data Analysis
GPS location requires a satellite constellation. These satellites send the time of transmission by
microwave which travels at the speed of light. Knowing this speed and the time diﬀerence between
the transmission and reception, the distance between the receiver and the satellite is determined.
GPS sensors requires a minimum of 3 satellites from the constellation. Thus, we can estimate the
distance to each satellite and make a triangulation, knowing that the satellites are geostationary.
The coordinates are given according to two variables, latitude, and longitude. Accuracy is between
5 and 10 m. This accuracy can be improved with the help of IMU and camera sensors to make
visual-inertial odometry, as explained in [7], see Figure 4.
2.3.5. Gyroscope Sensor (IMU) Data Analysis
A gyroscope is a device that measures the orientation and movement of the drone by detecting
changes in its orientation. It usually consists of a rotor that rotates at high speed and is mounted
on a rotating shaft. When the gyroscope is in motion, the rotational force of the rotor tends to
keep its axis of rotation in a constant direction, regardless of the movements of the object on which
it is mounted. This property is called “gyroscopic stability” and allows the gyroscope to maintain
a ﬁxed orientation in space, even if the object it is mounted on is moving. Thus, the movement
of the drone can be known in real time via three coordinates along the X, Y , and Z axes in a
three-dimensional reference frame.

2.3.6. Accelerometer Sensor (IMU) Data Analysis
The accelerometer is a sensor capable of measuring, in three dimensions, the linear accelerations of
an object as well as the vibrations. The accelerometer then converts the vibration energy into an
electrical signal proportional to the instantaneous acceleration of the object. Thus, the movement
of the drone can be known in real time via three coordinates along the x, y, and z axes in a
three-dimensional reference frame, see Figure 6.

2.3.7. Rangeﬁnders Sensor Data Analysis
Rangeﬁnders are sensors, but also actuators.
As we have seen previously, a sensor is a device
that detects and responds to some type of input from the physical environment. An actuator does
the exact opposite. Indeed, actuators are devices that are responsible for moving and controlling
a mechanism or system, converting electrical voltage or intensity, into a physical environment
variable. Firstly, the rangeﬁnder emits an infrared wave. This signal will reﬂect oﬀan obstacle
and back to the rangeﬁnder. Thus, the rangeﬁnder acts as a transmitter, as it is an actuator, but
also as a receiver, as it is a sensor. The main goal is to calculate the time between emission and
reception of the IR wave. The time is divided by two since the wave travels back and forth between
the drone and the obstacle.
