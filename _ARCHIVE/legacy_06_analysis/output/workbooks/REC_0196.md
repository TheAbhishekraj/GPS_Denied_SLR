Navigation of Simultaneous Localization and Mapping by Fusing
RGB-D Camera and IMU on UAV
Xi Dai1, Yuxin Mao1, Tianpeng Huang1, Binbin Li1, Deqing Huang1
1. Institute of Systems Science and Technology, Southwest Jiaotong University, Chengdu 610031, P. R. China
E-mail: elehd@home.swjtu.edu.cn
Abstract: Simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) is one of the key components of the navigation system of unmanned
aerial vehicles (UAVs). In this paper, a novel system that enables a UAV to navigation in a GPS-denied environment with an
RGB-D (Red, Green, Blue, and Depth) camera and Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) is presented. Such a system is realized via
a host computer on a UAV, in which the IMU information read from the ﬂight controller and the image information read from the
RGB-D camera are fused using extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF). Experiments are designed to test the feasibility of the proposed
navigation scheme. Accurate assessment of our idea shows a good response when rapidly moving and rotating the UAV.
Key Words: Simultaneous localization and mapping; UAV; RGB-D camera; Inertial Measurement Unit

Introduction
With the rapid development of unmanned aerial vehicle
(UAV) technology, UAV has been widely used in military
and civilian ﬁelds including intelligent detection, battleﬁeld
rescue, earthquake relief, and ﬁre alarm monitoring. Ow-
ing to the small and unmanned characteristics, UAV is re-
garded as the ideal platform for autonomous navigation in
complex and conﬁned environment to accomplish tasks such
as exploration, inspection, and interaction with the environ-
ment. In particular, intelligent UAV with autonomous posi-
tioning functions has become an essential trend in all aspects
of applications, where the technology of simultaneous lo-
calization and mapping (SLAM) is frequently used. SLAM
is considered to be the critical technology for autonomous
positioning and navigation of robots in unfamiliar environ-
ments. By observing the surrounding environment based on
SLAM algorithm with sensors carried by the robot, the robot
is able to estimate its position and move as expected in real
time, thus constructing an environmental map.
There are many works that have demonstrated the abili-
ties of SLAM algorithm in UAV navigation by using laser
rangeﬁnders [1, 2]. Nevertheless, the considerable weight
and power consumption of the laser range ﬁnder pose a prob-
lem for UAVs with stringent payload and power limitations.
In addition, laser rangeﬁnders cannot provide height and
distance information outside the sensing 2D plane, which
makes them less efﬁcient in complex 3D scenes. As a re-
sult, it is difﬁcult to localize a UAV due to its variation in
altitude and motion dynamics. In particular, the positioning
error will increase speedily when UAV ﬂies in the environ-
ment with sparse features.
Recently, along with the progress of multi-view geom-
etry theory in computer vision, simpler and more conve-
nient stereo camera, depth camera and monocular cameras
are gaining popularity among researchers. In consequence,
many mature SLAM frameworks are reported, e.g., ORB-
SLAM [3] and RGBD-SLAM-V2 [4]. Within these frame-
works, the mapping relationship between images is calcu-

sensor with IMU measurements with EKF [8].
It is worth pointing out that any mismatch in 3D compo-
sition will render to positioning error of the RGBD-SLAM
algorithm[3, 4]. Therefore, although the point cloud map
may be generated by using RGBD-SLAM, the quality of the
map could be degraded when the feature points in the ex-

robust in navigation, it is necessary to fuse with other sen-
sor’s information to identify the accuracy of the constructed
SLAM point cloud map, which further facilitate the precise
control of UAV.
In this paper, based on the improved RGB-D SLAM, a
virtual state estimation law is proposed for UAVs, where the
low-precision IMU information is combined with RGBD-
SLAM via extended Kalman ﬁlter (EKF) to realize real-time
estimation of RGBD-SLAM point cloud map as well as the
spatial position of UAV. Experiments have been conducted to
test the feasibility of the proposed navigation scheme. Com-
pared with pure RGBD-SLAM, the positioning accuracy of
UAV has been improved remarkably owing to the output fu-
sion of RGBD-SLAM and IMU.

2.2.1
Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM)
As one of classical graph-based SLAM systems, RGB-D
SLAM can be divided into three modules: frontend, back-
end, and ﬁnal map representation. In the following, more
details on three of them will be given one after another.
In the frontend, the feature points are extracted to deter-
mine the geographic relationship of two RGB images by us-
ing ORB feature point [17] and RANSAC algorithm [10].
Due to the inherent uncertainties existed in the RGB-D cam-
era data, the backend is used to construct the relationship and
ambiguities in a graph, and then g2o algorithm [13] is em-
ployed to optimize this graph so as to achieve a maximum
likelihood solution of the motion trajectory of UAV. Final
map representation is a process to create a 3-D probabilistic
occupancy map of the environment by the location obtained
from frontend or backend.
The SLAM in this work, which is used to calculate the
location of UAV by RGB-D images, is based on RGBD-
SLAM proposed by Endre [4]. At the beginning of SLAM,
feature extraction and feature point matching are conducted
to calculate the transformation between two consecutive im-
ages, which implicitly represents the movement and position
of UAV. Considering the high requirement in real-time con-
trol of UAV, ORB has been chosen as the feature extraction
algorithm, where the RGB data of each pixel of image is
fully used. Feature points and feature descriptors are the two
main parts of the structure of feature extraction [17]. Fea-
ture points refer to those points that can reﬂect the essential
characteristics of the image, and can be taken out by fea-
ture detector process from each image captured by camera.

Coordinate system transformation and estimation
The coordinate systems involved in this paper can be di-

IMU frame. Since the ﬂight controller is right on the cen-

computer programs. It provides libraries and tools to help

2.3.5
Communication and solution method
To make sure all sensor data we fused are in the same pe-
riod, all incoming information are time-stamped, and the in-
formation can only be used if they are measured in the same
time. Here, multiple processes are introduced to reduce the
time, and pthread-mutex is used to avoid multiple threads
accessing the task queue at the same time. Except for ser-
vices and topics of ROS, the remote desktop control is used
as the way of connection between host computer and ground
station.
