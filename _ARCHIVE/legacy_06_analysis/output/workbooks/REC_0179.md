Abstract— A novel concept for ad-hoc landing of unmanned 
aerial vehicles (UAV) in GNSS-denied environment is presented in 
this paper. In our concept at the beginning of the landing phase 
the UAV drops several active beacons down to the landing site. It 
enables the UAV to localize itself via distance and angle 
information to the deployed beacons. The presented wireless local 
positioning system is based on 24 GHz secondary radar sensors 
and allows for accurate and robust 3D-positioning even in harsh 
weather conditions. The specially designed antennas with large 
angular coverage assure UAV localization disregarding the 
beacon’s position and orientation. The developed beacon design 
has small size, low weight and power consumption that perfectly 
suits the application goal. Measurements of localization and 
landing scenario using four beacons and the radar unit installed 
on the drone are shown. The 3D-positioning of the UAV with the 
RMSE under 36 cm using only a single beacon and 31 cm using 
four beacons were achieved. These results verify the application of 
the proposed system as an accurate and redundant UAV landing 
solution.

I. INTRODUCTION  
In last years a rapid growth of unmanned aerial vehicles 
(UAV) applications in the civil sector can be observed. There 
are examples of UAVs successfully accomplishing different 
tasks in logistics, surveillance, agriculture and other fields [1]–
[2]. Safety is a key issue in UAV applications, especially while 
operating close to human beings. Therefore, accurate 
positioning is a vital aspect in secure UAV operation. 
In most of the examples [1]–[2], systems based on 
differential GPS are employed for this task. A high positioning 
accuracy up to 1 cm can be achieved using a real-time kinematic 
approach [3]. However, there are scenarios where a GPS signal 
is not reliable due to shadowing by obstacles or jamming. This 
uncertainty in localization is most critical during the UAV 
landing phase [4]. Moreover, there are several applications 
where a local positioning system is required. Among them are 
localization and landing of a UAV relative to a moving platform 
and extra-terrestrial applications. In the VaMEx Initiative of the 
German Aerospace Center, a concept for an exploration 
mission of Valles Marineris on Mars employing UAVs was 
developed [5].  
Accounting mentioned GNSS-denied scenarios, a demand 
for an alternative wireless positioning system for a safe UAV 
operation has emerged. Comparing different sensor types, the 
use of optical sensors such as laser scanner or camera are not 
reliable enough as their performance depends on weather and 
lightning conditions. Inertial sensors exhibit a drift over time, 
which prohibits their use for accurate positioning. Radar 
technology becomes a promising solution here as it enables 
high positioning accuracy even in harsh environmental 
conditions. Secondary radars for ranging between transponders 
have been shown as a reliable base for localizing mobile robots 
[6] and UAVs [7]–[8]. 
In this paper we propose a novel concept of controlled UAV 
localization and landing via radar-based wireless local 
positioning system (WLPS). In our system, the UAV equipped 
with the secondary radar having hemispherical angular 
coverage defines its position based on measurements to 
miniaturized transponders on the ground (Fig. 1). This WLPS 
provides accurate, robust and redundant 3D-positioning of 
UAVs. The system concept differs to the published earlier 
secondary-radar WLPSs [6]–[11] by a specially designed 
sparse antenna array with large angular coverage and the use of 
multiple transponders. Moreover, the new system configuration 
enables a self-organizing landing that is especially important 
for extra-terrestrial application.

II. WIRELESS LOCAL POSITIONING SYSTEM FOR UAV LANDING 
In the proposed WLPS, the UAV defines its position based 
on measurements to the beacons. Here, beacons equipped with 
radar transponder play the role of ground-based reference. They 
can be pre-installed in the landing zone. Alternatively, 
miniaturized beacons can be deployed just before the landing 
begins, by throwing them down from the UAV. Certainly, 
considerations on aerodynamic properties of the beacon 
housing should be made to ensure landing with antenna in up-
right position (Fig.1). The self-organization feature makes the 
proposed WLPS concept especially attractive for UAV 
applications in remote areas or other planets. On the other side, 
this property introduces challenges for the system configuration 
as additional requirements on redundancy and versatility of 
WLPS emerge.  
The local positioning system is constructed as following. 
The UAV is equipped with a radar sensor enabling direction and 
distance estimation to the beacons. A multi-channel secondary 
radar is used in our system. The antenna array exhibits a large 
angular coverage to ensure angle-of-arrival (AOA) estimation 
disregarding the UAV position relative to the beacons. The 
antennas of the main radar unit and the transponders are circular 
polarized to work in any relative orientation of the WLPS 
participants. For the ad-hoc landing application scenario, size, 
weight and power consumption of transponders play a crucial 
role. Therefore, a miniaturized active transponder design 
presented in [8] with reduced power consumption is used. 
III. SYSTEM DEMONSTRATOR 
In order to verify the proposed localization concept, a 
WLPS demonstrator based on 24 GHz secondary radar was 
developed. The minimal configuration of this system combines 
a multi-channel radar sensor and one active transponder. In 
following, the role of each system component and its structure 
are considered with more details. 
 
A. Radar unit with dome array 
The single input multiple output (SIMO) FMCW radar 
sensor that enables AOA and distance estimation is a core 
component of our wireless local positioning system. The radar 
unit works at 24 GHz ISM band. Its configuration and 
measurement principle is detailed in [9].  
In this work, the radar unit combines 16 receive channels 
facilitating an antenna array design with a large angular 
coverage. The dome array specially designed for UAV 
applications was presented in [10]. In this array, antennas are 
distributed over a hemispherical surface enabling an angular 
coverage of 360° in azimuth and up to 140° in elevation plane. 
Antenna positions of this array are optimized to achieve fairly 
constant AOA estimation performance inside the large angular 
region. Using conventional Bartlett beamformer, robust 
localization where 68% of the AOA estimation errors do not 
exceed 2° has been achieved [10]. A further enhancement in 
AOA accuracy and angular coverage in elevation plane is 
possible by increasing the number of receive channels. 
The radar unit with the integrated dome array (Fig. 2) has 
the dimensions 28 x 29 x 28.5 cm3 and weighs 1.78 kg. The 
height and the weight of the unit can be reduced by optimizing 
the interconnection between antennas and radar channels. A 
redesign of the proposed WLPS concept at higher frequencies, 
for example, 60 GHz, would yield a substantial miniaturization 
of the whole system.  
The AOA and distance estimation algorithms are 
implemented on the DSP enabling UAV localization in a real-
time. 
B. Beacon 
The role of the active transponder unit is to receive, 
modulate and send back the signal received from the main radar 
unit. In order to increase the system range, it is desired to 
rescatter an amplified signal. Therefore, an active backscatter 
transponder using the switched injection-locked oscillator 
(SILO) principle was used in our system. A detailed description 
of the configuration and operation principle can be found in 
[11]. The transponder has a small size of 35 x 15 mm2 (Fig. 3). 
The maximal power consumption does not exceed 500 mW. 
A circular polarized patch with specially formed metal 
reflector was used as a transponder antenna. Such reflector 
substantially increases the 3dB-beamwidth of a patch up to 140°. 
Antenna size does not exceed 10 mm in all dimensions. The 
reflector can be manufactured using plastic 3D printing

D. UAV Localization Demonstrator Setup 
To verify the application of the system concept for UAV 
localization, the radar unit equipped with 16-element dome 
array was mounted on the drone (Fig. 4) developed at the 
Institute of Aerospace Information Technology of Julius-
Maximilians University of Würzburg, Germany. This non-
commercial UAV based on the system presented in [13] has a 
footprint of 50 x 50 cm2 and a maximum load of up to 8.5 kg. 
The energy of 240 Wh stored in two accumulators enables a 
flight time of approximately 5 minutes. 
The SILO transponders were placed in the aluminum case 
and, together with a battery, fixed on a mounting structure.  
IV. MEASUREMENTS AND EVALUATION OF LANDING SCENARIO 
In order to investigate the performance of the proposed 
localization concept, field measurements in Würzburg were 
conducted. The measurement scenario is depicted in Fig. 5.  
Four transponders were distributed randomly with a distance of 
several meters. The UAV was flying in a triangle-formed 
trajectory for about 100 s and then landed between the beacons.  
The Leica TS30 total station was used as a reference.  An 
optical 360° prism was placed close to the dome array and has 
been tracked during the flight. The accuracy of the reference 
drone position here is limited to the prism size that lies under 
15 mm. As only a single prism could be tracked it was not 
possible to measure the drone tilt and rotation, causing 
additional uncertainty between measured and reference results. 
In Fig. 6 the cumulative error of angle and distance 
estimation to each beacon is presented. Almost identical 
performance for each transponder was achieved. The mean 
value of their 1σ-values of cumulative distribution function 
(CDF) is less than 2° for angular distance and 20 cm for range 
estimation errors.  
 In order to reduce influence of outliers and fuse results from 
different transponders, a Kalman filter with a constant velocity 
dynamic model was employed [14]. In Fig. 7 the UAV positions 
evaluated with information to the one and all four transponders 
are depicted. The applied filter was able to detect outliers and 
obtained trajectories correspond well to the reference points. If 
only a single transponder is used the RMSE of 3D-positioning 
equals 36 cm. Certainly, more accurate and robust localization 
with RMSE of 31 cm was achieved using all four beacons. A 
better reference incorporating drone tilt and rotation during 
flight could reduce the uncertainty in estimated positioning 
accuracy.

V. CONCLUSION 
We presented and verified a concept of UAV localization 
and landing in GNSS-denied environments. The wireless local 
positioning system comprised of a secondary radar unit with 
large angular coverage and miniaturized beacons was employed 
for this task. The accurate and redundant 3D-positioning 
solution is based on AOA and distance estimation between 
UAV unit and multiple beacons. The self-organization property 
of the proposed system makes it especially attractive for remote 
and extra-terrestrial applications.  
The concept was verified with measurements of a 
localization and landing scenario using a drone and four 
beacons. Robust 3D positioning with RMSE of less than 31 cm 
has been shown. Comparing positioning results using single 
and multiple beacons, we have shown the redundancy of the 
presented localization concept.  
For further system improvement, the number of radar 
receive channels can be increased. It would allow to expand the 
angular coverage of the dome array up to 180° or more in 
elevation plane and enhance the AOA accuracy. By doing so, 
UAV landing on complex non-planar terrain can be realized.
