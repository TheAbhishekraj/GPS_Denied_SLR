LD3DGS-SLAM: Long-Distance Monocular SLAM
With 3-D Gaussian Splatting and GNSS-Aided
Localization for UAVs
Dongdong Li , Tao Yang , Member, IEEE, Yilin Wang, Haidong Qin , Shixiong Fan, Shuanghan Zhang,
Yidan Zhang , and Jing Li , Member, IEEE
Abstract—Integrating neural rendering with simultaneous
localization and mapping (SLAM) has shown great promise
for achieving high-precision localization and photorealistic scene
reconstruction. However, the dynamic perspectives of uncrewed
aerial vehicles (UAVs), compounded by Global Navigation Satel-
lite System (GNSS) signal interference and the accumulation of
localization errors, pose signiﬁcant challenges to existing monoc-
ular SLAM methods in complex environments. To address these
limitations, we propose LD3DGS-SLAM, a novel framework that
integrates monocular SLAM, GNSS, and 3-D Gaussian splatting
(3DGS) to enhance UAV-based localization and mapping. The
system integrates traditional geometric feature-based localiza-
tion with the multiview synthesis capability of 3-D Gaussian
rendering, achieving a breakthrough in accuracy while enhanc-
ing robustness. First, we construct a multisensor fusion graph
optimization model that tightly integrates GNSS and monocular
vision data, eﬀectively mitigating the cumulative drift typical
of traditional SLAM pipelines. Afterward, we introduce a 3-D
Gaussian-based mapping strategy that incrementally reﬁnes a
dense scene representation using SLAM-generated point clouds,
fusing geometric and texture information to reconstruct highly
accurate and detailed maps. To further enhance localization
performance in GNSS-denied scenarios, we develop a dynamic
frame interpolation and tracking approach based on 3-D Gaus-
sian rendering. By synthesizing novel viewpoints, this method
improves observation matching and strengthens loop closure
detection, enabling robust relocalization in large-scale environ-
ments. To validate the eﬀectiveness of the proposed framework,
we implement a UAV test system and evaluate its performance
on both the publicly available TUM dataset and a self-collected
aerial dataset spanning several kilometers. Experimental results
show that LD3DGS-SLAM achieves a state-of-the-art average
localization error of only 0.8 m using just a monocular cam-
era, even during long-range ﬂight missions exceeding tens of
kilometers and altitudes up to 500 m. Overall, LD3DGS-SLAM
eﬀectively addresses the limitations of monocular SLAM in
aerial scenarios, providing a robust, accurate, and cost-eﬀective
localization solution for urban air mobility (UAM) and Aerial
Internet of Things (IoT) applications.
Index Terms—3-D Gaussian splitting, long-distance visual
simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM), UAV visual
navigation, vertical take-oﬀand landing (VTOL) aircraft.

I. INTRODUCTION
W
ITH the rapid advancement of uncrewed aerial vehi-
cles (UAVs) and electric vertical take-oﬀand landing
(eVTOL) technologies, urban air mobility (UAM) is emerging
as a transformative solution for intelligent urban transportation
systems. By integrating intelligent aerial vehicles into city traf-
ﬁc networks, UAM establishes a secure, eﬃcient, and scalable
low-altitude airspace infrastructure, enabling new modes of on-
demand air travel, emergency response, and logistics services
[3], [4]. In this context, the Global Navigation Satellite System
(GNSS) serves as a core technology for accurate positioning
and navigation, ensuring the reliable execution of ﬂight mis-
sions. These ﬂight missions typically operate within predeﬁned
aerial corridors, relying on GNSS signal coverage, rather than
in GNSS-denied environments such as indoors or in canyon-
like areas.
However, UAVs face dual security challenges arising from
complex electromagnetic environments and intentional sig-
nal interference during actual operations. Zhang et al. [5]
pointed out that the positioning accuracy of traditional wireless
simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) relies on the
quality of the propagation path, which is highly susceptible
to environmental factors. Unlike indoor environments, where
reconﬁgurable intelligent surfaces (RISs) can be employed to
optimize the communication channel, high-altitude UAVs can
only passively adapt to complex propagation conditions. More-
over, as edge nodes, UAVs are also exposed to communication
security and privacy threats [6], [7].
Owing to the inherently weak nature of GNSS signals, they
are highly susceptible to interference and spooﬁng attacks,
posing signiﬁcant challenges to system security [8]. Interfer-
ence can be either intentional—such as jamming by malicious
actors using strong signals to overwhelm legitimate GNSS
transmissions, leading to position loss and route deviation—or
unintentional, caused by dense urban radio environments. Both
types of interference can severely compromise ﬂight safety
and mission success. Spooﬁng, on the other hand, involves
hackers forging location data to mislead UAVs into deviating
from their intended path or entering dangerous areas, directly
endangering passenger safety and cargo integrity and resulting
in economic and reputational damage.

gation technologies are inherently local, providing positioning
only within a local coordinate frame and lacking global
navigation capabilities. Although global positioning can the-

A. Cross-Modal Observation Scale and Noise Diﬀerences
The GNSS provides global-scale information but suﬀers

absolute scale; and 3DGS [1] maps represent scenes as con-
tinuous Gaussians but entail high computational complexity.
Improper fusion of these heterogeneous observations can lead
to nonconvergence in optimization or a degradation in local-
ization accuracy.
B. Data Management and Matching Eﬃciency of Hybrid
Maps
Conventional point cloud maps support fast matching but
struggle to represent continuous scene details, whereas 3-D
Gaussian maps provide ﬁne-grained representations at the cost
of higher computation. Combining the two into a hybrid map
requires eﬃcient management and dynamic loading of map
blocks under UAV storage and computation constraints while
ensuring real-time matching and loop closure detection.
C. Real-Time Performance and Matching Under Varying
Altitudes
UAVs during high-altitude missions experience large view-
point and height variations, limiting the eﬀective matching
range of traditional visual maps. To increase the number of
valid matching points, hybrid maps must employ dynamic
intermediate-frame generation and layerwise matching strate-
gies while balancing computational eﬃciency to maintain
high-accuracy localization.
To address these challenges, we introduce a weighted
balancing strategy in factor graph optimization to enable
cross-modal observation fusion, implement block-based and
dynamic loading management for the hybrid map, and adopt
dynamic intermediate-frame rendering during localization to
enhance matching points, reducing the iteration count and
computational load.
Compared with existing GNSS-augmented visual SLAM
methods, our system uniquely enables photorealistic mapping,
global-scale relocalization, and online adaptability, all within a
lightweight and cost-eﬀective framework suitable for deploy-
ment in real-world UAM scenarios using UAVs and eVTOLs.

II. RELATED WORKS
For vertical take-oﬀand landing (VTOL) electric aircraft
operating at high altitudes in outdoor environments—such as
during cargo transportation or passenger missions—reliable
real-time kinematic GNSS (RTK GNSS) signals are generally
available. However, these signals can be maliciously interfered
with or lost during operation, necessitating maps to provide
continued global localization information. At high altitudes,
the depth information provided by stereo and RGB-D cameras
becomes largely ineﬀective. This section focuses on monocular
visual SLAM, providing a brief review of methods capable of
preserving and reusing maps.
A. Graph Solver Versus Neural Solver
Traditional visual SLAM methods can be broadly catego-
rized into feature-based and direct approaches. Feature-based
methods such as ORB-SLAM [9], [10], [11] and OpenVSLAM
[12] rely on keypoint detection, loop closure, and pose graph
optimization. Although eﬀective, they often yield sparse and
discontinuous maps. Direct methods such as LSD-SLAM
[13] and DSO [14] leverage photometric consistency for
semidense or dense mapping. Semidirect approaches such as
SVO [15] balance eﬃciency and robustness by combining
both strategies. However, they typically suﬀer from signiﬁcant
accumulated drift over long trajectories (tens of kilometers),
particularly in GNSS-denied environments.
Recent advances have explored GNSS-visual SLAM frame-
works to achieve global localization in large-scale outdoor
environments. Chu et al. [16] proposed a loosely coupled
GNSS-monocular SLAM method using pose graph optimiza-
tion. However, the system does not model GNSS uncertainty
and performs poorly in environments with weak or unavail-
able GNSS signals. Systems such as VINS-FUSION [17],
GVINS [18], and IC-GVINS [19] integrate GNSS obser-
vations into visual-inertial odometry to suppress long-term
drift. However, these methods typically generate sparse point
cloud maps that lack dense surface geometry, texture infor-
mation, and photorealistic detail, which limits their utility in
map-based localization and relocalization scenarios. G-VIDO
[20] improves map consistency through GNSS-constrained
keyframe alignment and feature matching but still relies
heavily on point features while ignoring dense appearance
cues, reducing robustness under signiﬁcant viewpoint or illu-
mination variations. Moreover, these systems generally focus
on real-time state estimation and do not emphasize robust
map construction or relocalization strategies. Consequently,
their performance degrades signiﬁcantly under extreme con-
ditions, such as GNSS spooﬁng attacks or complete signal
loss—challenges that remain open in the ﬁeld. In addition,
most existing frameworks utilize stereo vision to obtain
an absolute scale. While eﬀective at low altitudes, stereo-
based depth estimation suﬀers at high altitudes because
of the negligible disparity between distant scene elements,
resulting in considerable depth uncertainty. Although increas-
ing the stereo baseline may improve accuracy, it imposes
physical design challenges for lightweight UAV platforms.
Furthermore, texture-sparse environments such as grasslands,
water surfaces, or roads further degrade stereo-matching per-
formance, often resulting in incomplete depth maps and
unreliable localization.
Deep learning-based SLAM methods such as DROID-
SLAM [21], DeepVO [22], and UnDeepVO [23] have shown
promise in improving robustness to challenging visual condi-
tions. Learned features such as SuperPoint [24] and LoFTR
[25] further enhance data association. Nonetheless, these
methods often require massive training datasets, GPU-level
computing power, and a lack of interpretability, making them
impractical for lightweight, real-time UAV platforms.
In contrast, our proposed approach leverages GNSS-
constrained monocular SLAM to construct dense and photore-
alistic environmental maps using 3DGS [1], enabling accurate
relocalization even in the presence of GNSS loss or spoof-
ing. Unlike previous sparse or point-feature-based mapping
systems, our method preserves scene geometry, texture, and
lighting continuity and supports long-range visual localization
across altitude changes. This makes it well-suited for UAV

B. Explicit Representation Versus Implicit Representation
With the development of neural radiance ﬁelds (NeRFs)
[26], NeRF-based SLAM methods have garnered signiﬁcant
attention. iNeRF [27] proposed camera pose estimation by
inverting NeRF, while iMAP [28] introduced NeRF as a map
representation in SLAM, achieving camera pose tracking and
map construction. However, iMAP is limited to small-scale
scenes. NICE-SLAM [29] improved this by introducing a
hierarchical feature grid, enabling larger scale scene handling,
and was followed by OBEZEE-SLAM [30] and VolBA [31].
NICER-SLAM [32] further enhanced NICE-SLAM [29] by
incorporating monocular depth and standard estimation meth-
ods. NeRF-SLAM [33] decoupled pose and depth estimation
to improve reconstruction quality, and GO-SLAM [34] incor-
porated loop closure detection and bundle adjustment (BA),
reducing cumulative error.
In contrast, 3DGS [1] replaces ray marching with diﬀer-
entiable rasterization, greatly improving the rendering speed.
GS-SLAM [35] was the ﬁrst to apply 3-D Gaussian representa-
tion to SLAM, followed by MonoGS [36], SplaTAM [37], and
CG-SLAM [38], which optimize Gaussian point clouds and
camera poses for eﬃcient map construction and localization.
POTHO-SLAM [39] combines visual SLAM with a super
primitive map representation for improved accuracy and real-
time image rendering on embedded platforms. SGS-SLAM
[40] integrates semantic information to enhance scene recon-
struction. Methods such as Reduced-GS [41] and Scaﬀold-GS
[42] optimize memory for large-scale SLAM.
Notably,
MonoGS
[36],

POTHO-SLAM [39] represent state-of-the-art SLAM frame-
works that employ 3DGS [1] or hybrid neural representations,
demonstrating the signiﬁcant potential of neural rendering
in SLAM. MonoGS [36] achieves eﬃcient construction of
high-ﬁdelity maps through tightly coupled pose tracking and
3-D Gaussian point cloud optimization. Building on this,
OBEZEE-SLAM [30] incorporates hierarchical optimization
and semantic priors to enhance system robustness and support
incremental map updates. POTHO-SLAM [39] adopts a hybrid
approach, combining traditional SLAM front ends with super-
primitive representations to balance geometric structure and
neural rendering advantages, enabling accurate relocalization
and real-time rendering on embedded platforms. However,
these methods have limitations: MonoGS [36] lacks global
loop closure detection and optimization mechanisms, mak-
ing it prone to local tracking loss and error accumulation;
OBEZEE-SLAM [30] relies on implicit NeRF-based neural
rendering, which restricts its scalability in dynamic scene
expansion and incremental map updates; although POTHO-
SLAM [39] constructs high-quality 3-D Gaussian rendering
maps, it does not integrate them into joint camera pose
optimization, resulting in limited localization accuracy and
robustness. Moreover, all these methods generally lack global
localization information, which hinders the optimization of
pose and map building. The monocular camera’s scale ambi-
guity, combined with the absence of global positioning
sensors, leads to severe cumulative errors over long-distance
ﬂights spanning tens of kilometers, causing map distortion
or even failure. In summary, while these approaches demon-
strate excellence in neural rendering–enhanced SLAM, further
improvements are necessary to achieve more stable, eﬃcient,
and adaptable mapping and localization in complex dynamic
environments.
To address the limitations above, this article proposes a
hybrid SLAM method tailored for long-range, high-altitude
localization scenarios involving drones and VTOL aircraft.
The proposed method innovatively integrates the RTK GNSS
into the monocular SLAM mapping phase to mitigate cumu-
lative errors and enable global localization. It also introduces
a hybrid map representation that combines 3DGS [1] with
traditional point clouds, balancing the geometric structure
and rich scene details. This representation supports global
loop closure keyframe detection as well as incremental map
expansion and updating, signiﬁcantly enhancing localization
accuracy and extending the eﬀective operational altitude range
in complex aerial environments. During the localization phase,
the system synthesizes novel-view images by combining
monocular camera data with the map, enabling more pre-
cise matching between current observations and the historical
map, thereby improving localization accuracy. Compared with
existing methods, our approach not only resolves the issues
of cumulative error and a lack of global optimization but
also strengthens map updating capabilities in dynamic environ-
ments, providing a more stable and eﬃcient SLAM solution
for high-altitude UAV applications.
Speciﬁcally, our method belongs to the graph-based SLAM
paradigm and employs an explicit 3-D Gaussian map repre-
sentation. This design oﬀers the following advantages.
1) Optimization Consistency and Flexibility:
The inte-
gration of graph optimization enables joint adjustment of
visual and GNSS constraints within a uniﬁed optimization
framework, ensuring globally consistent pose estimation and
facilitating modular fusion of multisource information.
2) Flexible Scalability and Chunked Management: The
explicit map can be ﬂexibly partitioned and loaded, supporting
dynamic updates and large-scale environmental mapping. It
is also suitable for resource-constrained onboard embedded
platforms, enabling optimized computation and storage.
3) Resource and Power Eﬃciency: The manuscript further
details system resource consumption and power performance,
demonstrating that the explicit map mode can operate eﬃ-
ciently on actual UAV deployments, balancing map accuracy
with platform resource limitations.
4) Comparison With Neural Network Maps: Explicit 3-D
Gaussian maps are more eﬃcient for tasks such as relocal-
ization, loop closure detection, and local updates, allowing
direct access and modiﬁcation of scene data. In contrast,
neural network maps typically require full network inference
to update or query information, incurring higher computational
costs.
Moreover, explicit maps facilitate the integration of tra-
ditional SLAM features and GNSS information, achieving
uniﬁed cross-modal representation and enhancing both robust-
ness and measurability.

B. Accompanying-Flight 3-D Gaussian Map (Af3Dgs Map)
We propose the AF3DGS map, which integrates both tra-
ditional ORB feature-based mapping and photorealistic 3-D
Gaussian-based rendering to support robust localization and
global mapping.
The map primarily comprises two components: ORB feature
points and 3-D Gaussian feature points.
ORB feature information is primarily used for feature-
based matching and relocalization. The ORB map points
include the spatial positions of the sparse point cloud along
with their corresponding feature descriptors. Keyframes store
pose information, visual descriptors, and covisibility rela-
tionships with other keyframes. A DBoW2 bag-of-words
vocabulary tree is employed for loop closure detection and
relocalization, signiﬁcantly accelerating feature matching dur-
ing keyframe retrieval.
3-D Gaussian feature point information is primarily used
to render photorealistic images for downstream localization
and map optimization modules. The scene is modeled as a
set of 3-D Gaussians G, where the ith Gaussian is deﬁned
by its 3-D position Pi, covariance matrix Σi, opacity oi, color
coeﬃcients S Hi represented using spherical harmonics, and a
densiﬁcation ﬂag indicating whether the Gaussian is derived
from a matched ORB feature. During optimization, new Gaus-
sians are generated through gradient-based densiﬁcation and
do not participate in relocalization.
Although the 3-D Gaussians are stored explicitly, their
ability to synthesize novel views from arbitrary camera poses
allows them to serve as a lightweight implicit representation
of the scene. Unlike traditional sparse ORB maps, which
are restricted to discrete points and geometric matching, the

the pose of the current keyframe, the poses of the primary

aforementioned keyframes, other keyframes in the map set that

the cost function. Therefore, these keyframes are included in
the optimization process, although their poses remain ﬁxed.

D. Online Localization Process
This module primarily uses the established high-precision
visual map to provide global positioning information in the
absence of the GNSS, thus improving the online positioning
accuracy of the aircraft. The process is illustrated in Figs. 1
and 2.
The viewpoints and ﬂight altitudes of drones are relatively
ﬂexible and variable. However, traditional visual maps are
typically limited in their operational range because of the
eﬀects of parallax and the limited descriptive power of feature
descriptors. For example, a visual map established at an
altitude of 300 m can only eﬀectively match within a range of
60 m. When the drone reaches an altitude of 500 m, it falls
outside the map coverage area, preventing the use of the map’s
prior information to improve localization accuracy. However,
establishing maps at multiple altitude layers increases the
data collection time and the resource consumption of the
maps, aﬀecting the eﬃciency of on-site operations. Therefore,
our new map and tracking localization strategy eﬀectively
addresses this issue. For real frames, the pose estimation
relies on the existing map. The rendered frames are generated
on the basis of an accurately optimized map derived from
GNSS-based global optimization, ensuring that the synthe-
sized images are both photorealistic and geometrically precise.
Therefore, the rendered frame poses can serve as landmarks,
acting as constraint conditions to optimize the current frame’s
pose estimation.
1) Initial Pose Estimation: The current pose is initially
estimated by visual odometry. In this stage, the pose of the
current frame is estimated sequentially through velocity model
matching, keyframe matching, and brute-force matching.
2) Local Map Expansion for Pose Optimization:
The
pose is optimized by expanding the local map through a
hierarchical keyframe querying strategy. By retrieving ﬁrst-
and second-level covisible keyframes, additional map point
correspondences are established. Subsequently, local BA is
performed to reﬁne the camera pose using the matched map
points.
3) Conditions for Rendered Frame Generation: The fre-
quency of frame generation is determined by checking whether
the distance since the last rendered image involved in the
optimization exceeds N frames. In our experiments, we set
N to 20. A too-low frame insertion frequency reduces the
constraint imposed by the observed points, leading to lower
positioning accuracy. Conversely, frame insertions that are too
frequent negatively impact the positioning tracking speed. The
number of generated poses determines whether the elevation
diﬀerence from the map layer exceeds the valid range of
M meters. The drone primarily performs localization with
a downward-facing camera, which minimizes the need for
variations in rotational angles. However, uniform sampling
along the vertical (height) dimension is essential for ensuring
suﬃcient viewpoint diversity. In our experiments, we adopted
a vertical interval of 10 m between sampling heights.
4) Rendering Frame Image Acquisition:
The rendering
image thread accepts the set of rendered frame poses and
generates a sequence of rendered images, which are then
returned to the localization thread. In this process, the ROS
communication framework is used.
5) Rendering Frames for Map Tracking and Expansion:
The rendered frames extend the original 3-D map by increas-
ing the number of 3-D points and updating their associated
descriptors. This enhancement enables eﬀective matching
between the current real frame and the expanded map, thereby
establishing a robust covisibility relationship between the map
and the live image.
6) Tracking of the Current Frame on the Expanded Map:
The current frame is further tracked, and its pose is esti-
mated on the expanded map. Map 3-D points are obtained
through feature matching and camera pose estimation using
the perspective-n-point (PnP) method.

C. Comparative Analysis of Experimental Methods
In outdoor high-altitude, long-distance ﬂight scenarios
(100–500 m) for drones, both stereo and RGB-D cameras
are ineﬀective because of their limited range. Therefore,
this study focuses on monocular SLAM algorithms. In the
experimental design, we carefully balanced algorithm repre-
sentativeness and reproducibility by selecting several publicly
available and academically recognized methods for compari-
son. This approach ensures the reliability and practicality of
the experimental results. Speciﬁcally, ORB-SLAM2 [10] and
ORB-SLAM3 [11] serve as classical benchmark algorithms in
visual SLAM and are widely adopted in leading conferences
and journals because of their mature multimap fusion and
relocalization capabilities, thereby oﬀering strong reference
value. OPENVSLAM [12] is another comprehensive open-
source visual SLAM framework that supports map loading
and relocalization, featuring good reproducibility and an active
user community. VINS-FUSION [17] represents a multisensor
fusion solution that combines visual-inertial navigation with
the GNSS and is suitable for long-range tracking and localiza-
tion tasks; thus, it is included as a baseline in the experiments.
MonoGS [36] prioritizes uniﬁed joint optimization of poses
and maps. OBEZEE-SLAM [30] integrates a feature-based
SLAM frontend (ORB-SLAM2 [10]) with an NeRF built
on the instant-ngp framework [45], achieving breakthroughs
in scalability and semantic awareness. POTHO-SLAM [39]
focuses on practical deployment by combining ORB-SLAM3
[11], 3DGS technology [1], and the LibTorch framework
to establish an explicit–implicit hybrid mapping paradigm.
This supports real-time 3-D Gaussian rendering and novel
view synthesis on embedded platforms such as Jetson AGX
Orin. The diversity of these technical approaches provides a
comprehensive benchmark system covering multimodal neural
SLAM paradigms, thus underpinning the evaluation of our
method.

G. Computation Time and Resource Consumption Analysis
We provide a detailed discussion considering the charac-
teristics of eVTOL platforms, the division of onboard and
ground tasks, and the tradeoﬀs between latency and power
consumption.
1) Characteristics of eVTOL Platforms:
Commercially
deployed eVTOL aircraft can be broadly classiﬁed into two
categories: cargo transportation and passenger transport (as
illustrated in Fig. 9). Cargo-type eVTOLs typically have a
payload range of approximately 2.5–250 kg and a ﬂight
distance of 10–35 km, whereas passenger-type eVTOLs have
a maximum takeoﬀweight between 600–880 kg and a ﬂight
distance of approximately 20–30 km, usually accommodating
two passengers. The latest hybrid-wing ﬂying car to receive
a civil airworthiness certiﬁcate combines the vertical takeoﬀ
and landing capability of multirotor aircraft with the eﬃcient
cruising performance of ﬁxed-wing aircraft. It is capable of
carrying both passengers and cargo, with a maximum ﬂight
speed of 250 km/h, a range of 200 km, and a maximum takeoﬀ
weight of 1700 kg. These eVTOL aircraft feature substantial
payload capacity and spacious interior, making them well-
suited for carrying a variety of onboard equipment to perform
diverse missions.
2) Impact of Operational Scenarios on Design Paradigms:
At present, visual online localization for uncrewed aerial
vehicles (UAVs) mainly operates in two modes: fully onboard
processing and onboard–cloud collaborative processing. For
instance, Meituan’s delivery UAVs adopt an onboard–cloud
cooperative architecture, in which the onboard unit performs
lightweight visual localization. In contrast, the cloud model
provides precise correction and returns the result within one
second. To address the limited onboard computational capa-
bility, Zhang et al. [46] proposed an integrated air–ground
edge–cloud collaborative framework, where UAVs serve as
edge nodes responsible for data collection and small-scale
model inference. In contrast, the ground cloud performs large-
scale model computation and periodic updates. This approach
features lightweight computation and low power consumption,
making it particularly suitable for small UAV missions in
resource-constrained scenarios with stable network coverage.
Notably, our system is designed to handle even more adverse
scenarios. Unlike ground vehicles, UAVs may encounter
extreme conditions such as a complete loss of RTK GNSS
signals or communication disruptions. In such cases, the
system relies on onboard visual maps to perform autonomous
emergency navigation, ensuring safe ﬂight operations. To
support this capability, tasks are carefully divided between
onboard devices and ground servers according to compu-
tational requirements: the onboard unit executes real-time
visual/GNSS fusion estimation, prioritizing rendering speed
for live localization and tracking, while more resource-
intensive map training and updates are oﬄoaded to the ground
server. This task separation underpins the system’s overall
operational workﬂow.
During the map construction phase, onboard devices cap-
ture images and receive real-time kinematic (RTK) GNSS
data—for instance, via China’s Qianxun service, which uti-
lizes RTCM32 diﬀerential signals at a rate of 0.5–0.7 KB/s.
Sparse visual maps are trained online, whereas Gaussian map
optimization is completed oﬄine on the server, which is not
time-critical—for instance, training on the XAMIT-Map300
dataset (523 keyframes, 1805 × 1023 px, 390k points) takes
about 51 min for 30k iterations, as summarized in Table IX.
During the online localization phase, only the map of the
mission area (typically spanning 5–30 km) is loaded into
memory to reduce resource usage. Throughout operation, the
system can rely entirely on the visual map without requir-
ing any data communication with servers or reception of
radio signals—including GNSS data. The system dynamically
adjusts processing frequency: when GNSS is stable, visual
updates are minimized to save power; when GNSS is weak
or unavailable, the onboard visual and Gaussian map modules
ensure continuous localization at meter-level accuracy.

V. CONCLUSION
We present LD3DGS-SLAM, a novel framework that com-
bines monocular SLAM, GNSS, and 3DGS [1] to enhance
localization and mapping in complex environments. By explic-
itly modeling the geometric structure and dense appearance
with 3DGS, our system generates richer and more continuous
maps compared to traditional sparse ORB-based point clouds.
The proposed approach has been validated on both public
indoor datasets and large-scale dynamic outdoor datasets. The
experimental results demonstrate that integrating GNSS into
the mapping and optimization process signiﬁcantly reduces the
cumulative drift inherent in pure monocular SLAM, reducing
localization errors from tens of meters to the meter level in
long-range, cross-altitude UAV ﬂights. Moreover, our system
achieves performance comparable to or superior to that of
stereo and GNSS fusion methods. The incorporation of loop
closure detection and global optimization further enhances
the stability and accuracy of localization. The proposed map
representation enables reliable localization at altitudes up to
500 m, eﬀectively addressing the challenges of viewpoint
and altitude variations in aerial mapping tasks. Compara-
tive evaluations against classical and state-of-the-art SLAM
systems conﬁrm the improved accuracy and robustness of
our approach. With a high-ﬁdelity prebuilt map supporting
online dynamic updates, LD3DGS-SLAM can operate inde-
pendently of the GNSS, ensuring reliable global localization
in GNSS-degraded or denied environments. This capability
makes LD3DGS-SLAM a promising solution for safe and
precise autonomous navigation in Aerial Internet of Things
(IoT) scenarios, including UAVs transporting critical payloads
and eVTOL aircraft operating in UAM settings.
Future work will focus on multimap fusion strategies for
larger scale environments, enabling dynamic local map loading
during ﬂight. We also plan to explore the integration of seman-
tic scene understanding and object-level perception to enhance
environmental awareness. In addition, further optimization
eﬀorts will focus on reducing memory and computational
overhead, as well as improving deployment on resource-
constrained embedded platforms. These advancements will
strengthen the practical applicability of LD3DGS-SLAM in
real-world operations.
