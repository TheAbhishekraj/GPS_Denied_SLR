Abstract
This survey presents a systematic analysis of recent advancements in autonomous unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), focusing
on their navigation and control systems. The paper provides a critical overview of the key technologies in navigation and
control systems that enhance UAV autonomy. It focuses on three main aspects: perception and state estimation (navigation,
target tracking, GNSS-denied environments); path planning (obstacle avoidance, autonomous takeoff/landing); and operating
conditions (wind disturbances, night efﬁciency). Our ﬁndings highlight that critical developments, including vision-based
and hybrid navigation frameworks, reinforcement learning methods, and energy-aware planning, are signiﬁcantly advancing
UAV capabilities, especially in terms of navigation in GPS-denied environments, path efﬁciency, energy optimization, and
multi-UAV communication. The contributions of this survey include a uniﬁed taxonomy linking the perception, control, and
energy subsystems, the identiﬁcation of essential open research gaps, and the provision of structured recommendations to
guide future research. Furthermore, by synthesizing a wide range of technologies, the survey identiﬁes current challenges and
points to future research opportunities for achieving safe, efﬁcient, and fully autonomous UAV operation.

architectures and control frameworks discussed throughout
thissurvey.Weﬁrstpresentaclassiﬁcationofplatformsbased

2.1 Classification of UAV types
Accurate modeling of UAV dynamics is crucial but is often
overlooked by the emphasis on controller design. Using
methods such as Newton–Euler and Euler–Lagrange [14],
alongwithaccountingforaerodynamiceffectsanduncertain-
ties, signiﬁcantly improves stability, maneuverability, and
overall control performance. As shown in Table 2, UAVs
are categorized into heavier-than-air and lighter-than-air
systems. Each subtype (A–J) corresponds to a speciﬁc con-
ﬁguration.
Given the variety of conﬁgurations [15], advanced multi-
sensor setups, such as stereo vision-based systems [16],
facilitate precise navigation, obstacle avoidance, and 3D
mapping, even in GPS-denied environments.
2.1.1 Heavier-than-air UAVs
Heavier-than-air UAVs can be divided into the following
types:
Wing-type UAVs: Fixed-wing UAVs (A) resemble tra-
ditional airplanes, known for longer ﬂight times and energy
efﬁciency during cruise. They generate lift through wings but
lack hovering capabilities, requiring runways or catapult sys-
tems for takeoff and landing [17]. Flying-wing UAVs (B) are
a variation of ﬁxed-wing aircraft with a blended wing-body
conﬁguration, offering aerodynamic efﬁciency and stealth,
often used in military and surveillance applications [18].
Flapping-wing UAVs (C) are inspired by birds and insects,
generating lift and propulsion through wing ﬂapping. Used
in bio-inspired research, they offer advantages in maneuver-
ability and stealth operations [17].
Rotor type: Rotor-based UAVs rely on single or multiple
rotors which provide lift and stability. All of these, especially
quadcopters (E) (four-rotor drones), are popular platforms
with accurate hovering, fast and agile ﬂight, and vertical take-
off and landing capabilities [17]. An example is in wind ﬁeld
surveying, where quadcopters equipped with sensors survey

2.1.2 Lighter-than-air UAVs
These include blimps (I) and balloons (J) which get their
lift from buoyant gases like helium instead of aerodynamic
forces. Although they generally have limited maneuverabil-
ity and speed compared to heavier-than-air platforms, they
offer greater endurance. They are usable for atmospheric
study, air observation, and long-duration monitoring [17, 22].
2.2 Control module
Control strategies can be divided into three main categories:
linear, nonlinear, and intelligent [10, 23]. Table 3 presents a
comparison of these modules [10, 24, 25].
2.2.1 Linear controllers
This category includes PID controllers, linear quadratic reg-
ulators (LQR), and H∞controllers. Among them, the PID
controller is one of the most widely used due to its simplicity
and ease of implementation. It provides reliable performance
byadjustinggainsandisextensivelyusedformaintainingsta-
bility. However, due to nonlinear dynamics, PID controllers
alone may not be suitable for all scenarios. Other linear
controllers like LQR and H∞are also employed for path opti-
mization and handling external disturbances, but they require
precise modeling [10].
2.2.2 Nonlinear controllers
Many UAVs exhibit nonlinear dynamics with multiple
degrees of freedom. Common methods include feedback
linearization and backstepping control. Feedback lineariza-
tion transforms the nonlinear system into an equivalent
linear system, allowing linear control theory to be applied.
Backstepping control stabilizes subsystems progressively to
control the overall system [10].
2.2.3 Intelligent controllers
This category includes model predictive controllers (MPC),
fuzzy logic, and artiﬁcial neural networks (ANNs). These are
suitable for complex applications such as path planning in
unknown environments. Neural network-based system iden-
tiﬁcation offers an effective approach to modeling dynamics.
Types such as multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) and radial basis
function (RBF) networks can approximate complex, highly
coupled dynamics without relying on detailed physical mod-
els [23].
3 Flight features
The development of UAVs for various applications focuses
on improving key features to increase performance and reli-

3.1 Navigation
Navigation is a fundamental ﬂight feature that enables reli-
able operation across a broad range of applications [26].
Signiﬁcant approaches are categorized into deep learn-
ing navigation, reinforcement learning, sensor fusion, and
Transformer-based approaches.
3.1.1 Deep learning navigation
Recent advancements in artiﬁcial intelligence have allowed
UAVs to operate autonomously in defense scenarios, where
they must detect, classify, and respond to potential threats. A
combination of deep learning techniques, including convo-
lutional neural networks (CNNs) and generative adversarial
networks (GANs), allows UAVs to interpret visual data, pre-
dicthazards,andautonomouslynavigatethroughchallenging
terrains [26].
The paper [27] predicts the real-time position and velocity
trajectories of UAVs in a 3D environment using histori-
cal data. To address this, they use sequence-based neural
networks,speciﬁcallygatedrecurrentunits(GRU).Theaccu-
racy of the model decreases as they extend forecast horizons.
The work in [28] explores a similar approach, implementing
a CNN combined with long short-term memory (LSTM) to
preprocess the sensor data before the Kalman ﬁlter process.
The CNN extracts spatial features while the LSTM models
temporal dependencies.

3.4 Path planning
Path planning is a pivotal ﬂight feature of autonomous UAVs
[53].
3.4.1 Classical algorithms
A* and Dijkstra’s algorithm can be used to ﬁnd the shortest
path. Rapidly exploring random trees (RRT) are useful for
planning in high-dimensional spaces. Particle swarm opti-
mization (PSO) ﬁnds optimal paths by simulating the social
behavior of birds ﬂocking [54]. Enhanced genetic algorithms
(GAs), when combined with clustering methods, ensure ﬂy-
able and collision-free paths [55].
3.4.2 Reinforcement learning
Reinforcement learning can be used for path planning by
allowing the UAV to learn optimal paths through reward-
based learning [5]. DRL models can generate paths in
real time for unknown 3D environments [56, 57]. Recent
advancements introduce explainable deep reinforcement
learning (XDRL) to address the challenges of opaqueness
in DRL models [58].

3.4.4 Multi-UAV cooperative planning
In GNSS-challenging environments, multi-UAV cooperative
path planning leverages a “leader–follower” structure, where
“leader” UAVs maintain line-of-sight and broadcast posi-

Recent research focuses on safe reinforcement learning

Automationoflandingishighlychallenging[66].Autonomous

when aiming for a complex condition with moving platforms

3.7.1 Reinforcement learning
Autonomous landing has been tackled using DRL for both

Marker detection uses visual markers (e.g., ARTag, ArUco)

3.8 Handling wind disturbances
One of the signiﬁcant challenges in UAV ﬂight is dealing
with wind disturbances. Wind can divert the ﬂight path and
jeopardize stability [2].
3.8.1 Wind estimation models
Many methods use Kalman ﬁlters to estimate wind speed
and direction. In [71], a two-stage Kalman ﬁlter (TSKF) is
developed, which includes a wind speed estimation algo-
rithm using an EKF. Another method introduces an invariant
extended Kalman ﬁlter (IEKF) [72]. Model-based wind esti-
mation uses dynamic models to estimate wind and improve
stability [2].
3.8.2 Robust control algorithms
H∞Controllers handle disturbances like wind gusts [10]. In
[73], a generalized extended state observer (GESO) is devel-
oped to estimate interference forces and torques caused by
wind.
3.9 Efficiency at night
For full autonomy, a UAV system must ensure reliable per-
formance at night [29].
3.9.1 Low-light imaging
Traditional algorithms for low-light image enhancement
include histogram-based [74] and variational-based [75]
methods. Recent publications have suggested learning-based
enhancement techniques. LighterGAN, for example, is an
unsupervised illumination enhancement GAN [76].

3.9.2 Thermal imaging
A thermal camera captures infrared radiation of warm
objects. Thermal images can recognize heat-emitting objects,
including humans, animals, and machinery [77]. Angkhem
et al. used a thermal camera with YOLOv3 to improve night-
time operations [78].
3.10 Edge AI and resource-constrained inference
Deploying advanced AI models on UAVs presents a signiﬁ-
cant challenge due to limited computational power [79]. Key
approaches include model quantization, network pruning,
and knowledge distillation [80].
3.11 Fuel, energy, and battery management
Battery management systems (BMS) monitor the health of
batteries and optimize energy utilization [5, 81]. Recent
efforts aim to extend UAV mission longevity by predicting
battery health and optimizing recharge timing. Integrating
batteryhealthintomissionplanningviaMarkovdecisionpro-
cesses (MDPs) improves energy allocation [82]. Along with
the growing popularity of IoT, Digital Twin (DT) technology
creates a real-time simulation from the physical object [83].
4 Future research directions and open
challenges
The applications of UAVs have greatly increased in a variety
of domains. This survey highlights important aspects of UAV
effectiveness under difﬁcult circumstances. As summarized
in Table 4, future research should push toward ﬂexible and
cooperative strategies that optimize energy use. The associ-
ated KPIs provide clear metrics to assess progress and guide
future development toward resilient, safe, and scalable auton-
omy.
5 Conclusion
This survey has presented a comprehensive analysis of
autonomous UAV navigation and control systems, empha-
sizing the critical technologies that enable safe ﬂight in
challenging environments. The ﬁndings demonstrate that
vision-based SLAM consistently outperforms LiDAR-based
approaches in GPS-denied environments. Reinforcement
learning provides more efﬁcient solutions than classical
methods for path planning, and intelligent energy manage-
ment systems address key endurance limitations. Future
work should focus on enhancing computational efﬁciency,
