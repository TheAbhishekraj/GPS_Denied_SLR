## 2. Problem Statement
"Autonomous inspection of civil structures using unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) offers a safer and more efficient alternative to manual methods; however, in GPS-denied environments, the absence of global positioning makes reliable navigation challenging and requires the use of onboard sensing." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Bridges and other forms of civil infrastructure are vital for economic development and public safety, yet they inevitably degrade with age and use." [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
Method name: Parametric GBDTpose
Category: VISION_OBJECT
"This study presents an updatable Graphics-Based Digital Twin (GBDT) that provides a globally referenced structural prior and generates synthetic image-pose datasets for training Siamese neural pose regressors for absolute pose estimation." [p.1]

## 6. System Architecture
- Platform: custom-built drone
  "Experiments on a custom-built drone demonstrate stable flight and improved global localization" [p.1]
- Sensors: Intel T265 fisheye camera
  "These camera parameters are derived from the Intel T265 fisheye camera" [p.5]
- Compute: Edge device on UAV + base station PC
  "a bidirectional wireless communication protocol is established between the edge device on the UAV and a base-station PC using ROS" [p.19]
- Communication: Wireless (ROS-based)
  "images from the UAV's onboard camera are wirelessly transmitted to the base station... The resulting poses are then sent back to the edge device on the UAV" [p.19]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
"To systematically evaluate the contribution of each component in the Parametric GBDTpose framework, several model variants were trained for the KLB bridge case study. These components include the GBDT-generated dataset, the YOLOv8-based isolation block, the INAF-enabled novel view synthesis module, and the geometric constraint loss." [p.17]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"scaling this approach to very large structures presents significant challenges. These challenges arise from the expanded range of possible images that must be mapped to distinct poses, as well as the increased preprocessing and training times required to develop a reliable pose regressor." [p.19]
"another limitation of the proposed framework is its reliance on wireless internet connectivity between the base station and the UAV." [p.19]

## 11. Future Work
"A more practical solution involves dividing the physical twin into smaller regions and training a dedicated pose regressor for each region." [p.19]
"One potential solution is to equip both the UAV and the base station with independent cellular network access and establish communication through a virtual private network (VPN)." [p.19]

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
"Intel T265 fisheye camera" [p.5]

### 18.2 Best algorithm
"Siamese network that maps both GBDT-generated and real images to a shared latent space, incorporating a geometric constraint loss" [p.3]
"ResNet101 backbone" [p.13]

### 18.3 Best accuracy
MAE X: 0.102 m (Scenario 1, pure lateral translations) [p.15]

### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration

### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy

### 18.6 Trade-offs
"Running large parametric regressors on edge devices such as Jetson Nano... poses a challenge due to limited memory capacity." [p.19]
"Although this setup introduces latency during pose estimation... its impact is minimal for a camera operating at a frame rate of 30 Hz" [p.19]

### 18.7 Generalization limits
"The reduced accuracy of the Parametric GBDTpose model in outdoor settings can be attributed to the increased spatial extent and visual variability of the environment" [p.18]
"the outdoor smart bridge structure is over ten times larger than the indoor laboratory bridge, so that the 500 templates used in this case are likely insufficient relative to the increased scale of the environment" [p.18]
