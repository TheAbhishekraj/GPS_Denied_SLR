## 1. Bibliographic Metadata
Shpagin, P., & Panchenko, T. (2026). NaviLoc: Training-Free Trajectory-Level Aerial-to-Satellite Localization for GNSS-Denied UAV Navigation. *Drones*.

## 2. Problem Statement
"Aerial-to-satellite visual localization enables GNSS-denied UAV navigation, but the appearance gap between low-altitude (50-150 m) UAV imagery and nadir satellite tiles makes per-frame Visual Place Recognition (VPR) unreliable." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Global localization is a prerequisite for long-horizon GNSS-denied UAV autonomy. Applications such as environmental monitoring, search-and-rescue, and autonomous delivery increasingly require operation in environments where GNSS signals may be unavailable, jammed, or spoofed." [p.1-2]

## 5. Proposed Method
Method name: NaviLoc
Category: VISION_OBJECT
"We introduce NaviLoc, a training-free three-stage trajectory-level estimator that treats VPR as a noisy measurement source and exploits Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) as a relative-motion prior." [p.1]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
"Each stage provides substantial improvement (Table 2). Stage 1 alone achieves 9× improvement by finding the correct global transform. Stage 2 refines local errors, and Stage 3 leverages VIO constraints while excluding outliers." [p.9]
"Performance is stable across moderate changes, while overly permissive local search radii or underconstrained refinement settings degrade accuracy." [p.9]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"VIO dependency. NaviLoc assumes VIO provides accurate relative motion over short horizons. Significant VIO failures (e.g., from aggressive maneuvers or texture-poor environments) would propagate to the final estimate." [p.12]
"our evaluation is limited to a single 2.3 km trajectory" [p.12]

## 11. Future Work
"Future work could jointly estimate VIO scale and bias." [p.12]
"Extending to SE(3) would support altitude variations and complex terrain, potentially leveraging 3D point cloud or mesh references." [p.12]
