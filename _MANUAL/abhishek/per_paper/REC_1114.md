## 2. Problem Statement
"Accurate geolocation remains challenging in areas with limited Global Positioning System (GPS) availability, such as dense forests, rural settings, urban canyons, and disaster zones. To overcome this, we introduce Inverse Navigation (I-NAV), a geolocation framework explicitly designed for decision-making in GPS-denied environments." [p.1]

## 3. Motivation
"Geolocation is fundamental to contemporary technological systems, underpinning applications from everyday navigation to critical emergency response operations. Despite its widespread utility, the effectiveness of the GPS significantly diminishes in environments with limited or obstructed satellite connectivity... Consequently, geolocation accuracy often deteriorates precisely when it is most critical, highlighting a notable gap in current navigation capabilities" [p.1]

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
MIXED
"GPS-denied, dynamic" environments including "dense forests, rural settings, urban canyons, and disaster zones" [p.1]

## 5. Proposed Method
Method name: I-NAV (Inverse Navigation)
Category: OTHER
"Unlike conventional methods that determine optimal routes from known origins to destinations, I-NAV inversely identifies probable user locations based on contextual data from questionnaires such as recently passed landmarks, travel-time constraints, and directional cues." [p.1]

- Platform: N/A (questionnaire/SMS-based; not a UAV onboard system)

## 7. Experimental Setup
- real_or_sim: REAL (validated against real taxi trajectories)
  "we validated the system using authoritative real-world mobility datasets and widely used routing engines" [p.10]
- environment: North Dallas, Texas (pilot); downtown San Francisco (evaluation); diverse geographic contexts
  "North Dallas, Texas" [p.6]; "downtown San Francisco" [p.15]
- trials: 175 real taxi trajectories + 120 ORS-generated trips for calibration; 94 held-out real taxi trajectories for evaluation
  "Parameter calibration was performed using a combined dataset of 175 real taxi trajectories from the EPFL mobility dataset and 120 additional trips generated using ORS" [p.10]
- baselines: Uniform reachable-road prior, KDE-over-Time, Particle-Filter-style
  "We compare I-NAV against three baselines: (i) a uniform reachable-road prior, (ii) a kernel density estimation (KDE) model based on travel time, and (iii) a particle-filter-style estimator." [p.10]
- dataset: CRAWDAD EPFL mobility dataset, OpenRouteService (ORS), OpenStreetMap (OSM)
  "the CRAWDAD EPFL mobility dataset [54], which contains a large collection of real urban taxi trajectories from San Francisco, together with travel-time queries from the OpenRouteService (ORS) routing engine" [p.10]

## 9. Ablation / Sensitivity
Travel-time noise injection: baseline median confidence level 0.242; ±10% noise → 0.388; ±20% → 0.738; ±30% → 0.764. Landmark perturbation: baseline mean 0.278; 100m → 0.389; 250m → 0.450.
"These results indicate a predictable reduction in localization tightness as anchor uncertainty increases." [p.18]

## 10. Stated Limitations
"Despite its strengths, cross-fusion currently depends on a precollected image database for online matching, which limits its applicability in dynamic scenarios where preempting is impractical." [p.7] (note: this quote belongs to REC_1110; actual quote from REC_1114:) "AGLM relies on user-reported cues such as travel direction and recently passed landmarks which may be collected via low-bandwidth channels like SMS. These inputs are typically sparse, subject to human error, and may introduce ambiguity due to recall bias or vague descriptions." [p.20]

## 18. Best Combination & Accuracy
### 18.1 Best sensor combination
"Questionnaire inputs (SMS-based landmarks, travel time, direction) + road network data from OpenStreetMap (OSM)" [p.4]

### 18.2 Best algorithm
"I-NAV with AGLM probabilistic modeling" [p.1]

### 18.3 Best accuracy
Mean confidence level: 0.234 (23.4% probability mass needed to include true endpoint) [p.18]

### 18.4 Baseline comparison at best configuration
"AGLM obtains the lowest NLL (9.119) and best CRPS (0.99986), indicating that it assigns higher probability to the true endpoint while maintaining well-calibrated distributions." [p.17]

### 18.5 Conditions of best accuracy
"94 held-out evaluation subset of real taxi trajectories" [p.14]

### 18.6 Trade-offs
"AGLM relies on user-reported cues such as travel direction and recently passed landmarks which may be collected via low-bandwidth channels like SMS. These inputs are typically sparse, subject to human error" [p.20]

### 18.7 Generalization limits
"Localization performance depends on the completeness and accuracy of the underlying road graph. In regions where road data are incomplete or outdated, inference quality may degrade proportionally." [p.20]
