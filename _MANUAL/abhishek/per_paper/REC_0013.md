## 2. Problem Statement
"The classical localization schemes fail in GPS-denied environments like the city canyons, indoor environments, dense woodland and intentional jamming, undermining swarm coordination and mission achievement [3], [4]." [p.1].

## 3. Motivation
"The autonomous swarms of drones have literally shaken the fences of the sphere of search and rescue operations, precision farming, and inspection of infrastructures. ... However, this is highly dependent on good real-time localization." [p.1].

## 4. GPS-Denied Context
MIXED

Urban canyons make the multipath extreme and aggravate the localization based on RF [19], [20], and the total inaccessibility of GPS signals in the indoor space, and this situation necessitates an alternative method [21], [22]." [p.1].

## 15. Notes
This paper is written in an unusually informal register for a technical publication, with colloquial phrasing throughout ("you know", "stuff", "thing is", "They went with", "kinda"). Section II contains two subsections labeled "A." (one on Classical Drone Swarm Localization, one beginning "A. Quantum Computing Applications in Navigation" on p.2), a numbering error in the source. Reference [11] is cited in-text (p.2, p.4) for visual-SLAM but the reference list entry [11] corresponds to "S. Krul et al., Visual SLAM for indoor livestock and farming..." which does not match the in-text usage; the author appears to be citing by the group of references, and this mismatch is preserved. The in-text citations frequently do not correspond to the listed references (e.g., [8],[9],[11] cited as distributed SLAM setups, but [8] is a quantum sensing paper and [9] is Kimera-multi). These citation inconsistencies are source anomalies and are not corrected. Figures 1-5 are referenced; note Fig. 3 is labeled inconsistently ("Computational Load Distribution in Classical vs. Quantum-Enhanced Systems"). The paper claims "cut localization error by 25-65 per cent" in the intro and "25 to 65 per cent" in the conclusion, consistent.
